To fix a clogged toilet, start by assessing the water level and avoiding flushing further. Often, a simple plunger can resolve the issue by creating suction to dislodge the blockage. For stubborn clogs, household items or a toilet auger can be effective solutions.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Toilet plunger (flange or cup style)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Dish soap or shampoo
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda and vinegar (optional)
- Toilet auger/snake (for tougher clogs)
- Bucket (for bailing water)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Prevent Overflow

Before you begin, put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. If the toilet bowl is nearly full, lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl and prevent an overflow. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any splashes or spills during the unclogging process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Never flush again if the water level is high; this will almost certainly lead to an overflow.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position a quality toilet plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down gently to release air, then plunge vigorously in and out, maintaining the seal. Perform about 15-20 strong plunges, then quickly pull the plunger up to break the suction. If the water drains, flush once to check if the clog is gone. For more detailed tips on plunging, you can refer to articles on [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Tips](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy).

> 💡 **Tip:** A flange plunger is typically more effective for toilets as it creates a better seal around the curved drain.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap (or shampoo) into the toilet bowl. The soap acts as a lubricant, helping the blockage slide down. Follow this with a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, allowing the soap and hot water to break down the clog. The combination of heat and lubrication can often dislodge organic matter.

> 💡 **Tip:** Boiling water can crack the porcelain, so always use hot tap water.

### Step 4: Consider Baking Soda and Vinegar

For a natural alternative or follow-up, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction can help break apart clogs. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for stubborn clogs. This method is particularly effective for organic blockages and can help deodorize the drain.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure there's enough room in the bowl for the fizzing reaction without overflowing.

### Step 5: Utilize a Toilet Auger or Snake

If the previous methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is the next step. This tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the auger into the drain hole and crank the handle to extend the cable until you feel resistance. Twist and push the auger to break up or hook the obstruction, then carefully withdraw it. For step-by-step instructions on using this tool, check out [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use a toilet-specific auger, as a standard drain snake can damage the toilet bowl.

### Step 6: Test and Clean Up

After successfully clearing the clog, flush the toilet once or twice to ensure the water flows freely and the clog is fully gone. If the water level still rises too high, the clog might not be completely removed, and you may need to repeat a method or consider professional help. Clean up any spills, disinfect the toilet, and store your tools.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider wearing gloves throughout the entire cleanup process for hygiene.


## Tips & Tricks

- Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, or feminine hygiene products.
- Keep a plunger readily available in each bathroom for quick response to minor clogs.
- Periodically pour a mixture of baking soda and vinegar down the toilet to help prevent buildup and keep drains clear.
- Educate household members, especially children, on what can and cannot be flushed down the toilet.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and release dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Never flush a toilet more than once if it's already clogged and the water level is high, as this will cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Avoid using excessive force with plungers or augers, which could damage the toilet's internal mechanisms or porcelain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a mixture of baking soda and vinegar can also help break down the blockage.

**What should I do if my toilet is clogged with poop?**

If your toilet is clogged with poop, first avoid flushing again to prevent overflow. Then, try the dish soap and hot water method to help break down the organic matter. If that doesn't work, a toilet plunger is usually effective, or for more stubborn situations, a toilet auger can directly address the blockage.

**How can I fix a severely clogged toilet?**

For a severely clogged toilet, start with repeated, strong plunging. If that fails, a toilet auger (or snake) is typically the most effective tool, as it can physically break through or retrieve stubborn obstructions deep within the drain. As a last resort, professional plumbing services may be required.

**Can hot water alone unclog a toilet?**

Hot water alone can sometimes unclog a toilet, especially if the blockage is soft organic material like toilet paper. However, it's more effective when combined with dish soap, which acts as a lubricant to help the clog slide down. Ensure the water is hot, but not boiling, to avoid damaging the toilet porcelain.

**How long should I let dish soap and hot water sit in a clogged toilet?**

For the dish soap and hot water method, you should let the mixture sit in the clogged toilet for at least 20-30 minutes. For more stubborn clogs, allowing it to sit for a few hours or even overnight can increase its effectiveness in breaking down the blockage.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber for a clogged toilet if you've tried multiple DIY methods (plunging, dish soap, auger) and the clog persists. Also, if you experience frequent clogs, multiple drains are backing up, or you suspect a deeper issue in your plumbing system, it's best to consult a professional.
