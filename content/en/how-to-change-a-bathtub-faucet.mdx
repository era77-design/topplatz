Changing a bathtub faucet involves removing the old fixture and installing a new one, which can refresh your bathroom's look or fix persistent leaks. If you're dealing with a minor leak and considering repair before full replacement, you might find useful tips in our guide on [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet-diy-guide). This DIY project typically requires basic plumbing tools and careful attention to detail to ensure a watertight seal.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet (spout, handles/cartridge)
- Adjustable wrench
- Screwdriver set (Phillips, flathead, Allen keys)
- Plumber's tape (PTFE tape)
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Utility knife or caulk gun (for sealant)
- Bucket and rags
- Safety glasses
- Work gloves
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning threads)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare Your Workspace and Shut Off Water

Before starting, gather all your tools and the new faucet. Place a towel or rag in the bathtub drain to prevent small parts from falling. Most importantly, locate and completely shut off the main water supply to your house or specifically to the bathroom. Turn on the old faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

### Step 2: 2. Remove the Old Faucet Spout

Inspect the underside of your bathtub spout for a small set screw. This usually requires an Allen wrench or a Phillips screwdriver. Loosen the screw, then twist the spout counter-clockwise to remove it from the pipe. If there's no set screw, the spout is likely threaded directly onto the pipe; simply twist it counter-clockwise until it detaches. Use an adjustable wrench if the spout is stubborn, but be careful not to damage the pipe.

### Step 3: 3. Remove Old Handles and Cartridges (if applicable)

For two-handle faucets, carefully pry off the decorative caps on top of each handle. Underneath, you'll find screws that secure the handles. Unscrew these and pull the handles off. Next, you'll need to remove the retaining nuts or clips that hold the cartridges in place. Carefully pull out the old cartridges. For single-handle faucets, remove the handle screw, pull off the handle, and then remove the escutcheon plate to access the cartridge.

### Step 4: 4. Clean the Area and Prepare for New Installation

With the old faucet components removed, thoroughly clean the wall and pipe connections. Use a wire brush to remove any old plumber's putty, sealant, or mineral deposits from the pipe threads. A clean surface ensures a better seal for your new faucet. Ensure the area is dry before proceeding to the next step.

### Step 5: 5. Install the New Faucet Spout

Apply plumber's tape clockwise around the threads of the pipe coming out of the wall (3-4 wraps are usually sufficient). If your new spout uses a set screw, apply a bead of plumber's putty or silicone sealant around the base of the spout where it meets the wall. Slide the new spout onto the pipe and tighten the set screw, ensuring it's level. For threaded spouts, carefully twist it clockwise onto the pipe until snug, being careful not to overtighten.

### Step 6: 6. Install New Handles and Cartridges

Insert the new cartridges into their respective valve bodies, ensuring they are oriented correctly. Secure them with any retaining nuts or clips provided. Place the new handles onto the cartridge stems, aligning them properly, and tighten the screws. Finally, snap on the decorative caps. For single-handle faucets, install the new cartridge, secure it, then attach the escutcheon plate and the handle.

### Step 7: 7. Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Carefully check all new connections for any signs of leaks. Run both hot and cold water through the new faucet, checking for proper flow and temperature. Test the diverter (if applicable) to ensure it correctly directs water to the showerhead. If you notice any drips or persistent leaks, re-check all connections. For troubleshooting specific leak types, refer to our detailed guide: [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet-diy-guide).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always choose a new faucet that is compatible with your existing plumbing setup (e.g., single-hole vs. widespread, spout connection type).
- Take photos of the old faucet during disassembly. These can be helpful references during the installation of the new one.
- Do not overtighten plastic nuts or connections, as this can easily cause cracking or stripping.
- Keep a bucket and plenty of rags handy for any unexpected water drips or spills during the process.
- Consider replacing the showerhead at the same time for a complete bathroom refresh, as access is often easier with the faucet removed.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris or splashes.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when dealing with old, corroded pipes, as they can be brittle and prone to breaking.
- ⚠️ Do not use excessive force when tightening connections, as this can damage pipes or the new faucet components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a plumber to change a bathtub faucet?**

While a plumber can certainly do the job, changing a bathtub faucet is a common DIY project that many homeowners can complete with basic tools and a bit of patience. This guide provides the necessary steps for a successful installation.

**What tools do I need to change a bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need an adjustable wrench, screwdrivers (Phillips, flathead, Allen keys), plumber's tape, plumber's putty or silicone sealant, a utility knife, a bucket, rags, safety glasses, and the new faucet itself.

**How do I know what type of bathtub faucet I have?**

Bathtub faucets can be single-handle, two-handle, or three-handle. The spout connection can be threaded or secured with a set screw. Inspect your current faucet and the existing plumbing behind the wall (if accessible) to determine the type and ensure compatibility with your new fixture.

**Can I replace just the spout or handles?**

Yes, in many cases, you can replace just the spout or the handles if the underlying valve body is still in good condition. However, ensure the new components are compatible with your existing valve type and brand to avoid fitment issues.

**What should I do if my new faucet leaks after installation?**

If your new faucet leaks, first re-check all connections to ensure they are properly tightened. Ensure plumber's tape was applied correctly to threaded connections and that any O-rings or gaskets are seated properly. Sometimes, disassembling and reassembling with fresh plumber's tape or sealant can resolve the issue.

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For a DIYer with some experience, changing a bathtub faucet typically takes 1 to 2 hours. However, this can vary depending on the complexity of your existing plumbing, the type of new faucet, and any unforeseen issues like corrosion.

**What's the difference between plumber's tape and plumber's putty?**

Plumber's tape (PTFE tape) is used on threaded pipe connections to help create a watertight seal and lubricate the threads, making them easier to tighten and loosen. Plumber's putty is a pliable, clay-like substance used to create a watertight seal around the base of fixtures where they meet a surface, like a faucet spout against the tub wall.
