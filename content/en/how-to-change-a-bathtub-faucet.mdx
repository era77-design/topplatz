Changing a bathtub faucet is a practical DIY project that can update your bathroom's look and improve water function. It involves turning off the water supply, carefully removing the old fixture, and installing the new one. With proper preparation and tools, you can successfully replace your bathtub faucet.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly (spout, handles, trim)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Phillips and flathead screwdrivers
- Plumber's putty
- Thread seal tape (Teflon tape)
- Utility knife
- Rags or old towels
- Bucket
- Safety glasses
- Cleaning supplies (e.g., scrub brush, mild cleaner)
- Silicone sealant (100% silicone caulk)

## Steps

### Step 1: Gather Tools and Shut Off Water Supply

Before beginning, collect all necessary tools and your new faucet assembly. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, or the specific valve for your bathroom, and turn off the water supply completely. Open a faucet downstairs to relieve any remaining pressure and drain the lines, then place a rag in the tub drain to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: Remove Old Faucet Handles and Trim

Most bathtub faucets have decorative caps covering screws on the handles. Pry these caps off gently with a flathead screwdriver or utility knife. Unscrew the handle screws, then pull the handles straight off. Next, remove any escutcheon plates or trim pieces by unscrewing them or prying them away from the wall, noting any old caulk that needs to be cut with a utility knife.

### Step 3: Disconnect and Remove the Old Faucet Spout

Bathtub spouts are typically held on by a set screw underneath (often requiring an Allen wrench or small screwdriver) or by threading directly onto a pipe. If there's a set screw, loosen it and pull the spout straight off. If it's threaded, twist the spout counter-clockwise to unscrew it from the pipe coming out of the wall. Have your bucket ready to catch any residual water.

### Step 4: Clean the Faucet Mounting Area

With the old faucet components removed, thoroughly clean the area around the pipes and faucet holes. Use a utility knife to scrape away any old caulk, plumber's putty, or mineral deposits. A mild cleaner and scrub brush can help remove grime, ensuring a clean, smooth surface for the new faucet to seal properly. This step is crucial for preventing future leaks.

### Step 5: Install the New Faucet Spout

Apply thread seal tape clockwise around the threads of the pipe extending from the wall if your new spout screws on. If your new spout uses a set screw, ensure the pipe is clean and smooth. Apply a thin bead of plumber's putty around the back edge of the new spout where it meets the tub wall, then push it onto the pipe and secure it. If it's a screw-on type, tighten clockwise; if it's a set-screw type, tighten the screw firmly but don't overtighten.

### Step 6: Install New Handles and Trim

Place the new escutcheon plates or trim over the faucet holes, ensuring they are correctly oriented. Secure them with their respective screws. Install the new faucet handles, aligning them properly and securing them with their screws. Avoid overtightening, which can crack the plastic or porcelain components. Apply a thin bead of silicone sealant around the edges of the escutcheon plates where they meet the tub or wall for a watertight seal.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Check for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the new bathtub faucet handles to allow air to escape and water to flow. Carefully inspect all connections, especially around the spout and handles, for any signs of leaks. Run the water for a few minutes, checking both hot and cold, and use a towel to dry the area and then re-check for drips. If you notice any leaks, tighten connections slightly or reapply thread tape/putty as needed.


## Tips & Tricks

- When choosing a new faucet, ensure it's compatible with your existing plumbing setup (e.g., number of holes, pipe type).
- Keep a bucket and plenty of rags handy throughout the process to manage any drips or spills.
- Take photos during disassembly to serve as a reference when installing the new components.
- For a cleaner finish, use painter's tape to create crisp lines before applying silicone sealant around the trim.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before starting any plumbing work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Avoid overtightening plastic or brass connections, as this can strip threads, crack components, or damage pipes.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance, rusty components, or complex plumbing beyond your comfort level, consider contacting a licensed plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

Changing a bathtub faucet typically takes about 1 to 2 hours for someone with basic DIY experience. The actual time can vary depending on the type of faucet, the condition of the old plumbing, and whether you encounter any unexpected issues during the removal process.

**What tools do I need to change a bathtub faucet?**

You'll primarily need an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, Phillips and flathead screwdrivers, a utility knife, plumber's putty, thread seal tape (Teflon tape), rags, a bucket, and safety glasses. Silicone sealant is also recommended for a watertight finish.

**Can I change a bathtub faucet without turning off the main water supply?**

No, it is crucial to turn off the main water supply to your home or at least to the specific bathroom before attempting to change a bathtub faucet. Failing to do so will result in significant water leakage and potential flooding.

**What's the difference between a diverter spout and a non-diverter spout?**

A diverter spout has a mechanism (usually a pull-up knob on top) that redirects water flow from the spout to the showerhead. A non-diverter spout simply allows water to flow out without the ability to switch to a shower. Ensure your new spout matches your existing setup if you have a shower.

**Do I need plumber's putty or Teflon tape?**

Both are used for sealing, but in different applications. Plumber's putty is typically used to create a watertight seal under the base of the spout or trim where it meets the tub surface. Thread seal tape (Teflon tape) is wrapped around the threads of pipe connections to prevent leaks. You will likely need both for a complete installation.

**What if my new faucet leaks after installation?**

If your new faucet leaks, first recheck all connections to ensure they are tight but not overtightened. Make sure thread tape was applied correctly to threaded pipes and plumber's putty was used under the spout base. If it's a persistent drip from the spout or handles, it might indicate an issue with the cartridge or seals, which you can often troubleshoot. For detailed guidance on fixing leaks, refer to our guide on [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet-diy-guide).

**When should I call a professional plumber for a faucet replacement?**

You should call a professional plumber if you encounter significant challenges like rusted or seized pipes, difficulties accessing the old faucet's connections, or if you need to replace the rough-in valve behind the wall. Additionally, if you're uncomfortable with plumbing tasks or if leaks persist after your DIY attempt, a professional can ensure a correct and leak-free installation.
