Changing a bathtub faucet can update your bathroom's look and improve functionality, especially if your old faucet is beyond repair or simply outdated. This guide will walk you through the process of safely removing your old faucet and installing a brand new one. If your faucet is simply dripping, you might be able to [fix a leaky bathtub faucet](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet-diy-guide) instead of replacing it entirely.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet (matching hole configuration)
- Adjustable wrench
- Phillips and flathead screwdrivers
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Utility knife or caulk removal tool
- Clean rags or towels
- Bucket
- Cleaning supplies (scrub brush, degreaser)
- Flashlight (optional, for dark spaces)
- Pipe thread tape (Teflon tape)
- Safety glasses

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare and Shut Off Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathroom or entire house. Locate the main shut-off valve, usually in your basement, utility closet, or near the water meter, and turn it off. Open the existing bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines, and place a bucket underneath to catch drips. Gather all your tools and the new faucet to ensure everything is within reach.

### Step 2: 2. Remove the Old Faucet

Carefully remove any decorative caps on the faucet handles to expose the screws. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove these screws, then pull off the handles. Next, use an adjustable wrench to loosen the nuts securing the faucet body and spout from underneath the tub or access panel. Disconnect the hot and cold water supply lines, being prepared for residual water to spill out into your bucket. Once all connections are loose, you can gently pull the entire old faucet assembly out.

### Step 3: 3. Clean the Mounting Surface

With the old faucet removed, thoroughly clean the area where the new faucet will sit. Use a utility knife or caulk removal tool to scrape off any old plumber's putty, caulk, or mineral deposits. A scrub brush and a mild cleaning solution can help remove stubborn grime and ensure a clean, smooth surface for the new faucet's seal. A clean surface is essential for preventing future leaks.

### Step 4: 4. Install the New Faucet Body and Spout

Apply a thin bead of plumber's putty or silicone sealant around the base of the new faucet body and spout where it will meet the tub surface. Carefully insert the new faucet components through the holes in your tub or deck. From underneath, secure the faucet body and spout using the provided mounting nuts and washers. Hand-tighten first, then use an adjustable wrench to tighten them securely, but avoid over-tightening which can crack the fixture.

### Step 5: 5. Connect Water Supply Lines

Apply pipe thread tape (Teflon tape) clockwise around the threaded connections of your new faucet's hot and cold water supply inlets. Connect the hot and cold water supply lines to their respective inlets on the new faucet, ensuring a snug fit. Use your adjustable wrench to tighten these connections, but again, do not overtighten. Make sure the connections are firm enough to prevent leaks.

### Step 6: 6. Test for Leaks and Finalize

Slowly turn the main water supply back on and check all connections carefully for any signs of leaks. Turn on the new faucet to ensure proper water flow and check for drips around the handles and spout. If you notice any leaks, turn off the water, tighten the problematic connection slightly, and retest. Once you're confident there are no leaks, apply a thin bead of silicone caulk around the base of the faucet and spout where it meets the tub, smoothing it out for a watertight seal. Allow the caulk to cure according to the manufacturer's instructions before heavy use.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always place a rag or towel in the drain opening to prevent small parts or tools from falling into the drain during the process.
- Take photos of the existing plumbing setup before you start dismantling it; this can be a helpful reference during reassembly.
- Ensure your new faucet's hole configuration (e.g., single-hole, widespread, centerset) matches your existing tub's drilling to avoid complications.
- If working in a tight space under the tub, a small mirror and a flashlight can be invaluable for seeing connections.
- Consider replacing old, stiff supply lines with new, flexible ones for easier installation and better peace of mind.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet replacement to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using a utility knife or sharp tools to avoid cuts.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts and bolts, as this can damage the new faucet or existing plumbing.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance, rust, or cannot reach connections, it may be best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why should I change my bathtub faucet?**

You might change your faucet for aesthetic reasons, to update your bathroom's look, or due to functionality issues like persistent leaks, corrosion, or difficulty operating. A new faucet can significantly improve your bathing experience.

**What type of faucet should I buy?**

The type of faucet depends on your tub's existing hole configuration. Common types include single-hole, centerset (3 holes, 4-inch spread), and widespread (3 holes, 6-16 inch spread). Always measure your existing setup before purchasing.

**Do I need special tools for this job?**

While most common household tools like screwdrivers and an adjustable wrench are sufficient, some specialized tools like a basin wrench (for tight spaces) or a caulk removal tool can make the job easier and more efficient.

**What if my new faucet leaks after installation?**

If your new faucet leaks, first turn off the water supply. Check all connections for tightness, ensuring they are snug but not overtightened. Make sure pipe thread tape was applied correctly and that plumber's putty or silicone created a good seal at the base. Sometimes, simply tightening a connection slightly will resolve the issue.

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For an intermediate DIYer, changing a bathtub faucet typically takes between 1.5 to 3 hours, depending on the complexity of the existing plumbing, accessibility, and your experience level. Allow extra time for unexpected issues.

**When should I call a professional plumber?**

You should call a plumber if you encounter significant issues like rusted or corroded pipes that won't disconnect, inaccessible plumbing, or if you're uncomfortable with any part of the process. Professionals can ensure the job is done correctly and safely.

**Can I replace just the faucet handles?**

Sometimes, yes. If your faucet body is in good condition and only the handles are broken or outdated, you might be able to find replacement handles that are compatible with your existing faucet model. Check the manufacturer's specifications for compatibility.
