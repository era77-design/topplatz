Changing a bathtub faucet can update your bathroom's look and improve functionality. This guide will walk you through the process, from removing the old fixture to installing the new one. With the right tools and a little patience, you can successfully complete this DIY project.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers (slip-joint or channel-lock)
- Screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead)
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Teflon tape (PTFE tape)
- Bucket and towels
- Wire brush or sandpaper
- Safety glasses
- Work light (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare and Shut Off Water Supply

Before starting, gather all your tools and the new faucet assembly. Clear the area around the bathtub for easy access. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, usually in the basement, utility closet, or near the water meter. Turn off the main water supply to prevent flooding during the replacement process. Open a nearby cold water faucet to relieve any residual pressure in the pipes.

### Step 2: 2. Remove Old Faucet Handles and Trim

Begin by removing the old faucet handles or knobs. Look for a small screw, often hidden under a decorative cap, which secures the handle. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove these screws, then pull the handles off. Next, carefully pry off the escutcheon plates or trim surrounding the handles and spout, using a utility knife or putty knife to break any old caulk seals. Be gentle to avoid damaging your tub or tile.

### Step 3: 3. Remove the Bathtub Spout

The method for removing the spout varies depending on its type. Many spouts are held on by a set screw located underneath the spout, which you can loosen with an Allen wrench or screwdriver. Once the screw is loose, the spout should slide off. Other spouts may simply unscrew counter-clockwise from the pipe extending from the wall. If it's stubborn, you might need an adjustable wrench to get it started.

### Step 4: 4. Access and Disconnect the Old Faucet Body

Accessing the main faucet body (valve) usually requires working from an access panel behind the wall, often in a closet or adjacent room. Once accessed, you'll see the hot and cold water supply lines connected to the valve body. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully disconnect these supply lines, as well as the shower diverter line if present. Be prepared with a bucket and towels to catch any residual water. Unscrew or unclamp the old valve body from its mounting bracket.

### Step 5: 5. Clean and Prepare the Mounting Area

With the old faucet components removed, take time to thoroughly clean the mounting surfaces. Use a utility knife or putty knife to scrape away any old plumber's putty, silicone sealant, or mineral deposits. A wire brush or sandpaper can help remove stubborn grime and ensure a smooth, clean surface for the new faucet. This step is crucial for achieving a watertight seal with your new installation.

### Step 6: 6. Install the New Faucet Body and Connections

Apply a thin bead of plumber's putty or silicone sealant around the gasket or mounting surface of the new faucet body, if recommended by the manufacturer. Insert the new valve body into the wall opening and secure it with its mounting screws or clamps. Connect the hot and cold water supply lines and the shower diverter line to the new valve. Apply Teflon tape to all threaded connections before tightening them with an adjustable wrench to ensure a watertight seal.

### Step 7: 7. Install New Spout, Handles, and Test for Leaks

Slide the new bathtub spout onto the pipe and secure it, either by tightening a set screw or by screwing it on directly. Attach the new handles or knobs, securing them with their respective screws. Apply a fresh bead of silicone sealant around the base of the spout and trim plates where they meet the tub or tile. Slowly turn the main water supply back on and check all connections carefully for any signs of leaks. Turn on the faucet to test hot and cold water flow, as well as the shower diverter function. If you notice any drips, tighten the connections as needed. For persistent leaks, refer to guides like [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: DIY Repair Guide](/en/how-to-fix-leaky-bathtub-faucet) for troubleshooting.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos of the existing setup before disassembling to help with reassembly.
- Keep a bucket and plenty of towels handy for any unexpected water spills.
- If working in a tight space, a headlamp or work light can greatly improve visibility.
- Always use Teflon tape on threaded pipe connections for an extra layer of leak prevention.
- Don't overtighten plastic connections, as this can crack them. Hand-tighten plus a quarter turn with a wrench is usually sufficient.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing work to avoid flooding.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris or splashes.
- ⚠️ If you encounter electrical wiring near the plumbing, exercise extreme caution or consult a professional.
- ⚠️ If you are unsure about any step, it's best to consult a licensed plumber to avoid potential damage or injury.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What tools do I need to change a bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need an adjustable wrench, pliers, screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), a utility knife, plumber's putty or silicone sealant, and Teflon tape. A bucket and towels are also essential for catching water.

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For an experienced DIYer, changing a bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 2 to 4 hours. If you're new to plumbing, it might take longer, so set aside a full afternoon.

**Do I need to turn off the main water supply?**

Yes, it is crucial to turn off the main water supply to your home before beginning. This prevents water from gushing out when you disconnect the old faucet lines and helps avoid potential flooding.

**What is plumber's putty used for?**

Plumber's putty is a soft, pliable sealing compound used to create a watertight seal around the base of faucets, drains, and other plumbing fixtures. It prevents water from seeping underneath the fixture and causing damage.

**Can I replace just the faucet handles?**

Yes, in many cases, you can replace just the faucet handles and trim kit if the internal valve body is still in good working condition. Ensure the new handles are compatible with your existing valve type and brand for proper fit.

**What if my new faucet leaks after installation?**

If your new faucet leaks, first recheck all connections to ensure they are tight. Make sure Teflon tape was applied correctly to threaded connections and that any gaskets or O-rings are seated properly. Sometimes, a slight adjustment is all that's needed.

**When should I call a professional plumber?**

You should call a professional plumber if you encounter unexpected complications, such as rusted or seized pipes, inaccessible plumbing, or if you're uncomfortable with any step of the process. A professional can ensure the job is done correctly and safely.
