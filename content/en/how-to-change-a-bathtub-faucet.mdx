Changing a bathtub faucet involves turning off the water supply, carefully removing the old fixture, and installing the new one. This DIY project can refresh your bathroom's aesthetic, improve water flow, and resolve persistent leaks. It typically takes a few hours depending on your experience and the type of faucet.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Teflon tape (PTFE tape)
- Utility knife
- Rag or old towels
- Bucket
- Safety glasses
- Flashlight or headlamp
- Wire brush (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Gather Tools and Turn Off Water Supply

Begin by gathering all necessary tools and your new faucet assembly. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific valve for your bathroom and turn it off completely. Open the existing faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes, and keep a bucket and towels handy to catch residual drips.

### Step 2: Remove Old Handles and Trim Plate

Use a screwdriver to remove the screws holding the faucet handles and trim plate in place. These screws are often concealed under decorative caps or behind the handles themselves. Carefully pull away the handles, trim, and any escutcheons, exposing the faucet's internal components. You might need a utility knife to score old caulk around the trim for easier removal.

### Step 3: Disconnect the Old Faucet Body

Depending on your faucet type, you may need to access the plumbing from behind the wall or directly from the front. Use an adjustable wrench or basin wrench to loosen the nuts connecting the faucet's hot and cold water supply lines. Be prepared for a small amount of residual water to drain into your bucket. Carefully detach the old faucet body from its mounting points and pull it free.

### Step 4: Clean and Prepare the Mounting Area

With the old faucet removed, thoroughly clean the area where the new faucet will be installed. Scrape away any old plumber's putty, caulk, mineral deposits, or rust using a utility knife or wire brush. A clean, smooth surface is essential for ensuring a watertight seal with the new faucet and preventing future leaks. Inspect the wall or tub surface for any damage that might need attention before installation.

### Step 5: Install the New Faucet Body

Apply a bead of plumber's putty or silicone sealant around the base of the new faucet body or trim plate, following the manufacturer's instructions. Carefully insert the new faucet into the mounting holes. From behind the wall or underneath the tub, secure the faucet body with the provided washers and nuts, tightening them with your wrench. Connect the hot and cold water supply lines to the new faucet, ensuring all connections are snug but not overtightened. Wrap the threaded connections with Teflon tape (PTFE tape) in a clockwise direction before attaching the supply lines to help prevent leaks.

### Step 6: Attach New Handles and Spout

Install the new faucet handles and spout according to the manufacturer's instructions. This usually involves securing them with screws and then attaching any decorative caps or trim. Ensure the handles operate smoothly and are properly aligned. Double-check that all components are firmly in place before proceeding.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Check for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water pressure is restored, thoroughly inspect all connections for any signs of leaks. Turn on the new faucet to check both hot and cold water flow. If you spot any drips, gently tighten the corresponding connections. If you encounter a persistent leak, sometimes [fixing a leaky bathtub faucet](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet) with new washers or O-rings can resolve the issue. Once confirmed leak-free, apply a thin bead of silicone caulk around the base of the faucet trim for a finished, watertight seal.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always read the manufacturer's instructions for your specific faucet model, as installation steps can vary.
- Take photos during disassembly; they can be invaluable references during the installation of the new faucet.
- Use Teflon tape on all threaded plumbing connections to help ensure a watertight seal.
- Don't overtighten nuts, as this can damage threads or components. Snug is sufficient.
- Consider replacing old shut-off valves if they are corroded or not functioning correctly, as this is a good time to do so.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the main water supply before starting any plumbing work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris or splashes.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when using utility knives or other sharp tools.
- ⚠️ If you encounter lead pipes or suspect asbestos, consult a professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For a DIYer with some experience, it typically takes 1.5 to 3 hours. Factors like the faucet type, access to existing plumbing, and unexpected issues can affect the total time.

**Do I need a plumber to change a bathtub faucet?**

No, many homeowners can successfully change a bathtub faucet themselves following a detailed guide. However, if you're uncomfortable with plumbing tasks or encounter significant complications, hiring a professional plumber is always a wise option.

**What's the difference between a bathtub faucet and a shower faucet?**

While often part of the same system, a bathtub faucet typically refers to the spout and handles for filling the tub. A shower faucet includes the showerhead and diverter, though many modern units are combined tub/shower faucets operated by a single valve.

**What tools are essential for changing a bathtub faucet?**

You'll definitely need an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), plumber's putty or silicone sealant, and Teflon tape. A utility knife, bucket, and rags are also very helpful.

**How do I know what type of faucet to buy?**

Before purchasing, identify your current faucet's configuration (e.g., single-handle, two-handle, widespread). Measure the distance between handle centers if applicable. Consider the number of holes in your tub or wall and choose a new faucet that matches those specifications.

**What should I do if my new faucet leaks after installation?**

First, check all connections to ensure they are snug but not overtightened. Sometimes, a gentle tightening can resolve a small drip. If the leak persists, you may need to reapply Teflon tape or verify the proper seating of washers and O-rings within the connections.

**Can I change just the spout of my bathtub faucet?**

Yes, in many cases, you can change just the bathtub spout if it's a slip-on or threaded type. This is a simpler task than replacing the entire faucet assembly and can often be done without turning off the main water supply, provided it's only the spout and not the internal valve that needs replacement.
