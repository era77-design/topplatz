Changing a bathtub faucet can update your bathroom's look and fix persistent leaks. This DIY guide provides clear steps to help you replace your old faucet with a new one, ensuring a fresh, functional update to your tub.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly
- Adjustable wrench
- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Allen wrench set (if needed for existing faucet)
- Plumber's putty or thread seal tape
- Cleaning rags
- Bucket
- Utility knife or scraper
- Flashlight
- Safety glasses
- Work gloves

## Steps

### Step 1: Gather Tools and Turn Off Water Supply

Begin by collecting all necessary tools and your new faucet assembly. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, usually in the basement, utility closet, or near the water meter, and turn it off completely. To relieve pressure and drain any remaining water from the pipes, open the old bathtub faucet and any other nearby faucets.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a bucket under the faucet area to catch any residual drips as you work.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Use a screwdriver to remove the screws holding the faucet handles in place, typically found under a decorative cap or on the side of the handle. Carefully pull off the handles and any escutcheon plates or trim. For the spout, look for a small setscrew on the underside near the wall; loosen it with an Allen wrench or screwdriver, then pull the spout straight off the pipe.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos as you disassemble the old faucet to help remember how components fit together, especially for reassembly.

### Step 3: Disconnect Water Lines and Remove Old Faucet Body

You'll likely need to access the back of the faucet from an access panel behind the tub, or sometimes by reaching through the front if it's a deck-mounted faucet. Using an adjustable wrench, carefully disconnect the hot and cold water supply lines from the old faucet body. Be prepared for some water to drip out, so have your bucket and rags ready. Once the lines are disconnected, unmount the old faucet body from its position, which is often secured by nuts on the back of the wall or deck. If you were replacing the faucet due to a persistent drip, understanding the cause of leaks can be helpful. Learn [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: A Simple DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-leaky-bathtub-faucet) before deciding on a full replacement.

> 💡 **Tip:** Label the hot and cold supply lines before disconnecting if they aren't clearly marked.

### Step 4: Clean and Prepare the Mounting Area

With the old faucet removed, thoroughly clean the mounting surface on your tub or wall. Remove any old plumber's putty, caulk, or mineral deposits using a utility knife or scraper. A clean surface ensures a proper seal for your new faucet, preventing future leaks. Carefully read the instructions for your new faucet to understand if plumber's putty or a rubber gasket is required for sealing the new components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the new faucet assembly fits the existing holes perfectly before proceeding with installation.

### Step 5: Install the New Faucet Body and Connections

Apply plumber's putty or thread seal tape to the threads of the new faucet body components as instructed by the manufacturer. Carefully insert the new faucet body into the mounting holes from the front of the tub or wall. From the access panel, secure the faucet body with the washers and nuts provided, tightening them snugly with your adjustable wrench, but avoid overtightening. Reconnect the hot and cold water supply lines to the corresponding inlets on the new faucet body, ensuring a tight, leak-free seal.

### Step 6: Attach New Handles, Spout, and Test for Leaks

Install the new faucet handles and spout according to the manufacturer's instructions, ensuring they are securely fastened and aligned correctly. Once all components are in place, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the new faucet and check carefully for any leaks around the handles, spout, and where the supply lines connect to the faucet body. Run the water for a few minutes to flush out any air or debris from the lines and re-check for drips.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wipe down all connections with a dry rag after turning the water on; any dampness indicates a potential leak that needs tightening.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always read the manufacturer's instructions for your specific faucet model, as installation steps can vary.
- Have a helper on hand, especially when working in tight spaces or needing an extra set of hands to hold components.
- Consider upgrading your shut-off valves under the tub if they are old or difficult to operate, as this makes future repairs easier.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before starting any plumbing work to prevent flooding and serious water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten connections, as this can strip threads, crack pipes, or damage the new faucet, leading to leaks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if I need to replace my bathtub faucet or just repair it?**

If your faucet is constantly leaking, dripping even after attempting repairs, or if the parts are severely corroded or difficult to find, replacement is often the better long-term solution. For minor leaks, you might only need to repair it. See our guide on [How to Fix a Leaky Faucet DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-leaky-faucet-diy).

**What type of faucet can I replace my old one with?**

You typically need to match the type of faucet (e.g., single-handle, two-handle, three-handle) and the spacing of the holes in your tub or wall. Measure the distance between the centers of the existing holes to ensure compatibility with your new faucet model.

**Do I need special tools to change a bathtub faucet?**

Most bathtub faucet replacements can be done with common household tools like screwdrivers, an adjustable wrench, and possibly an Allen wrench set. For connections in very tight spaces behind the tub, a basin wrench can be an extremely helpful specialized tool.

**How long does it typically take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For a DIYer with some experience, changing a bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 1 to 3 hours, depending on the complexity of the existing installation and the new faucet design. First-timers might take longer due to unfamiliarity.

**What should I do if I encounter a leak after installation?**

If you find a leak, immediately turn off the water supply. Carefully re-check all connections you made – especially the supply lines and any threaded parts – to ensure they are tight and properly sealed with plumber's tape or putty. Sometimes a connection just needs a quarter-turn more to seal correctly.

**Can I change a wall-mounted bathtub faucet to a deck-mounted one?**

This is usually not a straightforward replacement. Changing from wall-mounted to deck-mounted (or vice-versa) typically requires significant plumbing modifications, including rerouting water lines, and potentially modifying the tub or wall structure itself. It's often best left to a professional plumber.
