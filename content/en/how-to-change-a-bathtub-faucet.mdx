Changing a bathtub faucet involves turning off the water supply, carefully removing the old fixture, and installing the new one. This DIY project can refresh your bathroom's aesthetics or resolve persistent leaks, typically requiring basic tools and an hour or two of your time.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Plumber's putty or thread seal tape
- Utility knife or scraper (for old caulk)
- Bucket or towels
- Safety glasses
- Cleaning supplies (for residue)
- Silicone caulk and caulk gun (optional, for sealing)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Shut Off Water

Begin by clearing the area around your bathtub to allow ample working space. Lay down towels on the tub floor to protect the surface and catch any drips. The most crucial first step is to locate and completely turn off your home's main water supply, or the specific shut-off valves for your bathroom if available. Confirm the water is off by briefly turning on the old faucet.

### Step 2: Remove the Old Faucet Handles and Trim

Carefully remove any decorative caps or covers on the faucet handles. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove the screws holding the handles in place, then pull them off. Next, remove the escutcheon plates or trim that cover the wall openings, which will expose the internal valve components. Some trim pieces may simply pull off, while others are screwed on.

### Step 3: Detach the Old Faucet Spout

Bathtub spouts are typically secured in one of two ways: either by a set screw on the underside or by threading directly onto a pipe. If there's a set screw, loosen it with an Allen wrench or screwdriver, then pull the spout straight off. If it's a threaded spout, twist it counter-clockwise to unscrew it from the pipe stub-out. Have your bucket ready to catch any residual water.

### Step 4: Clean the Area and Prepare for New Installation

Once all old components are removed, thoroughly clean the wall and tub surfaces where the faucet was installed. Use a utility knife or scraper to remove any old caulk, sealant, mineral deposits, or grime. Ensure the area is dry and smooth, as this creates a better surface for the new faucet's seal and prevents future leaks. A clean foundation is key to a successful replacement.

### Step 5: Install the New Faucet Spout

Begin by applying plumber's tape clockwise to the threads of the pipe stub-out coming from the wall if it's a threaded connection. If it's a slip-on spout, apply a bead of plumber's putty around the back where it meets the wall. Slide or thread the new spout onto the pipe until it's snug and correctly oriented. If it has a set screw, gently tighten it to secure the spout, being careful not to overtighten.

### Step 6: Install New Handles and Trim

Follow the manufacturer's instructions for your specific new faucet model. Typically, you'll place the new escutcheon plates over the valve stems, securing them with their respective screws. Then, attach the new handles onto the stems, tightening their retaining screws. Ensure all components are aligned and firmly secured, but avoid excessive tightening that could damage the fixtures.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks and Finish Up

Slowly turn the main water supply back on and carefully inspect all new connections for any signs of leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water flow. If no leaks are present, apply a thin, even bead of silicone caulk around the base of the spout and trim plates where they meet the wall or tub for a watertight seal. If you find a leak, immediately turn off the water and recheck your connections; [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-leaky-bathtub-faucet) or [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet - DIY Repair Guide](/en/how-to-fix-leaky-bathtub-faucet) can provide troubleshooting tips if drips persist.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always read the manufacturer's instructions for your specific faucet model, as installation steps can vary.
- Keep a bucket and plenty of towels handy throughout the process to manage any water spills or drips.
- Before purchasing a new faucet, measure the distance between existing handle holes (if applicable) and the spout length to ensure compatibility.
- Take photos of the existing setup before disassembly; they can be helpful references during reassembly.
- Don't overtighten connections, as this can strip threads or crack components. Hand-tighten, then give a quarter-turn with a wrench if needed.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris or unexpected water spray.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when using utility knives or sharp tools to remove old caulk.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance, feel unsure at any point, or suspect issues with the internal valve, consider consulting a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why would I change my bathtub faucet instead of just repairing it?**

You might choose to change your faucet if it's severely corroded, aesthetically outdated, or if repeated repairs haven't resolved persistent issues. Sometimes, replacing an old, inefficient faucet with a new model can be more cost-effective than continuous part replacements.

**What tools do I need to change a bathtub faucet?**

Essential tools typically include an adjustable wrench, screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), plumber's putty or thread seal tape, a utility knife for old caulk, and a bucket and towels. Specific faucets might also require an Allen wrench or a basin wrench.

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

For an experienced DIYer, changing a standard bathtub faucet can take about 1-2 hours. If you're new to plumbing or encounter unexpected issues like seized parts, it might take longer to complete the project.

**Do I need to turn off the main water supply to change a bathtub faucet?**

Yes, it is crucial to turn off the main water supply to your house or at least the bathroom's shut-off valves before beginning any work. This prevents water from flowing while you're disconnecting the old faucet and installing the new one.

**What's the difference between a slip-on spout and a threaded spout?**

A slip-on spout slides over a copper pipe stub-out and is secured by a set screw on the underside. A threaded spout, in contrast, screws directly onto a threaded pipe nipple extending from the wall. Knowing which type you have is important for correct removal and replacement.

**Should I use plumber's putty or thread seal tape?**

Plumber's putty is typically used to create a watertight seal under the base of components like the spout or handles where they meet the tub or wall. Thread seal tape (Teflon tape) is applied to the threads of pipe connections to prevent leaks. Use both as appropriate for your specific faucet's connections.

**What if my new faucet doesn't fit the existing holes?**

If your new faucet doesn't align with the existing holes, you might have purchased an incompatible model. In some cases, minor adjustments can be made, but often it requires returning the faucet for one that matches your existing setup or undertaking a more extensive renovation to alter the wall behind the tub.
