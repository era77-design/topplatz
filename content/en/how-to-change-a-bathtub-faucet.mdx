Changing a bathtub faucet can significantly update your bathroom's look and improve water flow. If your old faucet is beyond repair or simply outdated, replacing it can give your bathroom a fresh feel. (Consider attempting to [How to Fix a Leaky Bathtub Faucet - Easy DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-leaky-bathtub-faucet) if the issue is just a leak and you prefer a repair over replacement.) This guide will walk you through the process, step by step.

## What You Need

- New bathtub faucet assembly
- Adjustable wrench
- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Utility knife
- Cleaning rags
- Bucket or pan
- Flashlight
- Safety glasses
- Plumber's tape (Teflon tape)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Gather Tools and Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting, ensure you have all necessary tools and your new faucet assembly ready. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or, if possible, just for the bathroom. Turn off the water supply completely. Open the existing faucet handles to drain any residual water from the lines.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a towel or cloth in the bathtub drain to prevent small parts from falling down and to protect the tub surface.

### Step 2: 2. Remove Old Faucet Handles and Spout

Carefully remove the decorative caps or covers on your old faucet handles, usually revealing screws underneath. Unscrew these and pull off the handles. For the spout, look for a set screw on the underside near the wall, or try twisting it counter-clockwise if it's a threaded type. Use a utility knife to score any caulk around the base of the handles or spout for easier removal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos of the existing setup before disassembly; this can be helpful during reassembly.

### Step 3: 3. Disconnect Water Supply Lines (if applicable)

For two-handle or single-handle faucets, you'll need to disconnect the hot and cold water supply lines. This often requires access from behind the wall or underneath the tub. Use an adjustable wrench to loosen the nuts connecting the supply lines to the faucet body. Have a bucket or pan ready to catch any remaining water.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider replacing old, corroded supply lines with new ones while you have everything disconnected.

### Step 4: 4. Remove the Old Faucet Body

Once the supply lines are disconnected (if applicable), locate the mounting nuts or screws that secure the faucet body to the tub or wall. Loosen and remove these fasteners. Carefully pull the entire old faucet assembly out from its mounting holes. Thoroughly clean the area around the faucet openings, removing any old caulk, grime, or mineral deposits.

> 💡 **Tip:** A putty knife or scraper can help remove stubborn caulk and debris.

### Step 5: 5. Install the New Faucet Body

Read the manufacturer's instructions for your new faucet. Apply plumber's putty or a thin bead of silicone sealant around the base of the new faucet body or onto the gaskets, as specified. Carefully insert the new faucet assembly into the prepared openings. From underneath or behind the wall, secure the new faucet body with the provided mounting nuts and washers, tightening them snugly but not excessively.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the faucet body is properly aligned and level before fully tightening the mounting hardware.

### Step 6: 6. Connect New Water Supply Lines and Spout

If your faucet has separate hot and cold connections, connect the water supply lines to the corresponding inlets on the new faucet body. Hand-tighten the connections, then use an adjustable wrench for a final snug turn, being careful not to overtighten. For the new spout, apply plumber's tape to the threads if it's a threaded type, or secure it with the set screw provided.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use plumber's tape on threaded connections to ensure a watertight seal and prevent future leaks.

### Step 7: 7. Test for Leaks and Finish Up

Slowly turn the main water supply back on, listening for any hissing sounds and carefully inspecting all connections for leaks. Operate the new faucet, checking both hot and cold water flow and handle function. Once you've confirmed there are no leaks and everything works correctly, apply a thin bead of silicone caulk around the base of the spout and handles for a finished, watertight seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wait at least 24 hours for silicone caulk to fully cure before exposing it to water.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always read the specific installation instructions provided with your new faucet, as designs vary.
- Keep a bucket and rags handy throughout the process to manage any drips or spills.
- If working in a tight space, a basin wrench can be invaluable for reaching nuts.
- Consider upgrading your water supply lines to braided stainless steel ones for durability if they are old.
- Take your old faucet with you when purchasing a new one to help ensure compatibility if unsure.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before beginning any plumbing work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten connections, as this can strip threads, crack pipes, or damage fittings, leading to leaks.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris, splashes, or accidental impacts.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant corrosion, rusted pipes, or plumbing beyond your comfort level, contact a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to change a bathtub faucet?**

Typically, changing a bathtub faucet takes between 1 to 3 hours, depending on your experience level, the type of faucet, and the complexity of accessing the existing plumbing.

**Do I need special tools to change a bathtub faucet?**

Most bathtub faucet replacements can be done with common household tools like an adjustable wrench, screwdrivers, and plumber's putty. Some specific faucet types might benefit from a basin wrench for hard-to-reach nuts.

**What's the difference between a bathtub faucet and a shower faucet?**

A bathtub faucet generally refers to the spout and handles that control water flow into the tub. A shower faucet controls water to the showerhead. Often, they are part of a combined tub/shower unit controlled by a single valve.

**Should I use plumber's putty or silicone sealant?**

Both can be used for sealing. Plumber's putty is good for non-porous surfaces and allows for repositioning. Silicone sealant offers a stronger, more permanent waterproof seal, especially for porous surfaces. Always check the faucet manufacturer's recommendations.

**Can I change just the bathtub spout?**

Yes, you can often change just the bathtub spout if it's a separate component. Spouts are typically either threaded onto a pipe or secured with a set screw underneath that holds them onto a smooth copper pipe.

**When should I call a professional plumber?**

You should call a professional plumber if you encounter severely rusted or corroded pipes, cannot access the plumbing behind the wall, or if you feel uncomfortable or unsure at any point during the installation process.

**What are common reasons to change a bathtub faucet?**

Common reasons include updating the bathroom's aesthetic, replacing a severely leaking or damaged faucet that is beyond repair, or improving water flow and efficiency. Sometimes a full replacement is chosen over repeated repairs.
