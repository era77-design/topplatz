To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, immediate action is crucial. Start by blotting up as much urine as possible, then apply a high-quality enzymatic cleaner designed to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor remover
- Spray bottle (optional, for vinegar solution)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Stiff brush or scrub pad (for carpets)
- Clean cloths
- Plastic wrap or foil (optional)
- UV blacklight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Assess the Area

Before you can treat the smell, you need to find all affected areas. Cat urine can soak into various surfaces, including carpets, upholstery, and even subflooring. Use your nose, or for older, dried stains, a UV blacklight can help illuminate hidden spots.

> 💡 **Tip:** A UV blacklight is invaluable for finding dried urine stains that are invisible to the naked eye, especially in low light.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is key. Don a pair of rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or an old, absorbent cloth to blot up as much urine as possible. Press firmly and replace towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. For quick and effective solutions, prompt action is always best, as detailed in [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: Quick & Effective Solutions](/en/how-to-remove-cat-pee-smell-quickly).

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a heavy object on top of the paper towels for a few minutes to maximize absorption.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Once excess urine is removed, saturate the affected area generously with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor remover. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals, which are the source of the persistent cat pee smell. Follow the product's instructions carefully for best results.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner saturates deep enough to reach all the urine, especially in carpets and padding, to truly eliminate the odor at its source.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit on the stain for the time recommended by the manufacturer, which is often 10-15 minutes or longer. For very old or strong odors, you might need to cover the area with plastic wrap or foil to prevent the cleaner from drying out too quickly, allowing the enzymes more time to work.

> 💡 **Tip:** Longer dwell times often lead to better odor elimination, especially for deeply set or aged stains.

### Step 5: Blot and Air Dry

After the dwell time, use clean paper towels or a fresh cloth to blot up any remaining cleaner and moisture. Again, press firmly without rubbing. Allow the area to air dry completely. You may want to place a fan near the spot to speed up drying and help dissipate any lingering odors.

### Step 6: Consider a Vinegar and Baking Soda Follow-up (Optional)

Once the area is completely dry, if a faint odor persists, you can try a homemade solution. Lightly mist the area with a 50/50 solution of white vinegar and water in a spray bottle, then sprinkle baking soda over the damp spot. Let it sit for several hours or overnight, then vacuum thoroughly. For a comprehensive approach to tackling persistent smells, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

> 💡 **Tip:** Baking soda is an excellent natural deodorizer that can absorb residual odors after the enzymatic treatment.

### Step 7: Address the Root Cause

While cleaning is essential, it's equally important to understand why your cat is urinating outside the litter box. Consult with your veterinarian to rule out any underlying medical issues, such as urinary tract infections. Addressing behavioral problems, like litter box aversion or stress, can prevent future accidents and ensure a lasting solution.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage to your surfaces.
- Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic urine and actually attract your cat back to the same spot, perpetuating the problem.
- For persistent odors, especially from old or deeply saturated stains, you may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process multiple times or consider professional cleaning services.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix different cleaning products, especially those containing bleach and ammonia, as this can create dangerous and harmful fumes.
- ⚠️ Ensure adequate ventilation when using cleaning products, opening windows or using fans.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and any cleaning product residue has been safely removed or absorbed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so difficult to remove?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that are highly resistant to water and common cleaners. These crystals re-crystallize when exposed to humidity, reactivating the strong, pungent odor over time, making it seem like the smell keeps coming back even after cleaning.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it recommended?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into harmless, odorless compounds, effectively eliminating the source of the smell rather than just masking it.

**Can I use household products like vinegar or baking soda to remove cat pee smell?**

While white vinegar and baking soda can help neutralize some odors and lift stains, they are generally not as effective as enzymatic cleaners for completely breaking down the uric acid crystals in cat urine. They can be good as a follow-up treatment after enzymatic cleaning to absorb residual odors.

**What should I do if the cat pee smell is in my carpet padding or subfloor?**

If urine has soaked into carpet padding or the subfloor, removing the smell becomes more challenging. You might need to pull up the carpet, replace the affected padding, and seal the subfloor with a specialized odor-blocking primer after enzymatic treatment to fully eliminate the odor.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box in the future?**

Prevention involves addressing the root cause. Ensure the litter box is clean and accessible, try different litter types, consider additional litter boxes, reduce stress, and consult your vet to rule out medical issues like UTIs that could cause inappropriate urination.

**Is it safe to use these cleaners around pets and children?**

Most enzymatic cleaners are formulated to be pet-safe once dry, but always check the product label for specific safety instructions. It's best to keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and any residue has been blotted up or vacuumed.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to completely disappear?**

The time it takes for the smell to disappear depends on the severity and age of the stain, as well as the effectiveness of the cleaning. Fresh stains can be removed in a few hours, while older, deeply saturated areas may require multiple treatments over several days or even professional help to fully eradicate the odor.
