A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but often it can be resolved with simple tools and techniques. Before calling a plumber, try these straightforward steps to clear the obstruction yourself.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Bucket or small trash can
- Old towels or rags
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Assess the Situation

The first rule of a clogged toilet is: do not flush again, especially if the water level is high. This can lead to an overflow. Lift the tank lid and close the flapper valve if the bowl is filling too rapidly to prevent spillage. Observe the water level to gauge the severity of the clog.

### Step 2: Protect Your Bathroom Floor

Before you begin, lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet. This will protect your floor from any splashes or overflows that might occur during the unclogging process. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

A good quality flange plunger is your best friend for most clogs. Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Plunge with strong, consistent up-and-down motions for about 15-20 seconds, then pull the plunger up quickly to break the seal. Repeat this process several times until the water drains. For more general tips on clearing clogs, see [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Quick & Easy Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work, or if you don't have a plunger, this method can often clear minor clogs. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured from waist height to add some force. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap acts as a lubricant and the hot water helps break down the clog.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs that resist plunging and the dish soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is the next step. This tool is specifically designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the auger's tip into the drain, crank the handle to feed the cable, and push through the clog. Once you feel resistance, rotate the auger to break up or hook the obstruction, then carefully withdraw it.

### Step 6: Consider Chemical Drain Cleaners with Caution

As a last resort before calling a professional, you can try a chemical drain cleaner designed for toilets. Always follow the product instructions meticulously, wear appropriate protective gear, and ensure good ventilation. Be aware that these chemicals can be harsh on pipes and the environment, and if they don't work, they can make the subsequent plumber's job more hazardous.

### Step 7: Know When to Call a Professional Plumber

If you've tried all the above methods and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged, or if you experience frequent clogs, it's time to call a professional plumber. There might be a deeper issue in your plumbing system, such as a main drain blockage, which requires specialized equipment and expertise to resolve safely and effectively.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets, as its design creates a better seal than a cup-style plunger.
- Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. 'Flushable' wipes are a common culprit for clogs.
- Regularly clean your toilet to prevent mineral buildup that can contribute to clogs.
- Keep a dedicated bucket nearby for a quick water source if you need to add more water to the bowl for plunging, rather than flushing again.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different chemical drain cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes or reactions.
- ⚠️ Avoid using a regular plumbing snake (drain auger) in your toilet, as it can scratch or damage the porcelain finish; always use a toilet-specific auger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of very hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a toilet auger (snake) can effectively clear the obstruction.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, the methods are generally the same as for any other clog. Start with a good quality flange plunger, ensuring a tight seal and plunging vigorously. If that doesn't work, try the dish soap and hot water method, or use a toilet auger to break up and remove the obstruction.

**What causes a toilet to clog easily?**

Toilets often clog easily due to flushing non-flushable items like wipes, paper towels, excessive toilet paper, or feminine hygiene products. Low-flow toilets can also be more prone to clogs if not enough water pressure is used to clear the trap. Blockages in the main drain line can also lead to frequent toilet clogs.

**How long does it take for a clogged toilet to unclog itself?**

While some minor clogs, especially those caused by organic matter, might eventually dissolve and clear on their own, it's not reliable to wait for a toilet to unclog itself. This could take hours or even days, and in the meantime, the toilet is unusable and a potential overflow risk. It's usually best to take active steps to clear it promptly.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

Chemical drain cleaners should be used with extreme caution and only as a last resort in toilets. Ensure the product is specifically designed for toilets and follow all instructions, including wearing protective gear and ensuring ventilation. Overuse or improper use can damage pipes, harm the environment, and pose safety risks.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all DIY methods (plunger, dish soap, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, contact a plumber if you experience frequent clogs, multiple drains in your home are backed up, or if you suspect a deeper issue with your main sewer line.

**Can hot water fix a clogged toilet?**

Yes, hot water can help fix a clogged toilet, especially when combined with dish soap. The hot water helps to soften and break down organic matter, while the dish soap acts as a lubricant. Remember to use hot, but not boiling, water to avoid damaging the toilet porcelain.
