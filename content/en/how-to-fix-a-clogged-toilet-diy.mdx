Fixing a clogged toilet often requires simple tools and techniques that most homeowners can perform. By following a few straightforward steps, you can clear common blockages and restore your toilet's function quickly, preventing further issues.

## What You Need

- Flange plunger
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket (optional)
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Bathroom

Before attempting to clear the clog, lay down old towels or newspapers around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from potential splashes or overflows. Put on a pair of rubber gloves for hygiene and protection. If the toilet bowl is completely full, you might want to remove some water using a small cup or bucket to prevent overflowing when you start plunging or adding solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure good ventilation in the bathroom before you start any cleaning or unclogging efforts.

### Step 2: Plunge with Purpose

Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal around the opening. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction, which helps to dislodge the clog. Repeat this vigorous up-and-down motion 10-20 times without breaking the seal. After several plunges, quickly pull the plunger up to see if the water level drops, indicating the clog is clearing.

> 💡 **Tip:** A flange plunger, which has an extended rubber cup, is ideal for toilets as it creates a better seal than a standard cup plunger.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Trick

If plunging doesn't work, or if you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. The soap helps to lubricate the clog, allowing it to slide down more easily. Follow this by carefully pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, allowing the soap and hot water to work on breaking down the blockage.

> 💡 **Tip:** Boiling water can crack porcelain due to extreme temperature changes, so always use hot tap water for this method.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For persistent clogs that resist plunging and the soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is a highly effective tool. Carefully insert the auger's end into the toilet drain, feeding the cable while rotating the handle. The auger's tip will either break up the clog or hook onto it, allowing you to pull it out. Be gentle to avoid scratching the porcelain of the toilet bowl.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use a toilet-specific auger, which typically has a protective plastic sleeve to prevent damage to the bowl's finish.

### Step 5: Flush and Observe Carefully

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, the clog is gone, and you can proceed to clean up. If the water level rises too high, quickly close the tank lid and lift the flapper to stop the water flow, preventing an overflow. You may need to repeat one of the earlier steps or try a different method if the clog persists after the initial attempt.

> 💡 **Tip:** Have a bucket ready to quickly scoop out water if an overflow seems imminent after flushing.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Sanitize

After successfully clearing the clog, thoroughly clean your plunger, auger, and any other tools used with an antiseptic cleaner. Dispose of soiled towels or newspapers properly to prevent the spread of germs. Wipe down the toilet and surrounding floor with a disinfectant spray to maintain hygiene and ensure your bathroom is fresh and clean.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using disposable gloves and wiping down surfaces with disinfectant wipes for quick and effective sanitization.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger for toilets; its design creates a better seal than a standard cup plunger.
- Never use harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes and porcelain, and pose health risks.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Be mindful of excessive toilet paper use.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet repeatedly if it is clearly clogged or overflowing, as this can worsen the overflow situation and cause water damage.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water to clear a clog, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain bowl, leading to costly repairs.
- ⚠️ Never mix different cleaning chemicals, especially bleach and ammonia-based products, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can often clear a clogged toilet using the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate and the hot water to break down the clog, then try flushing carefully.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop in it?**

A clogged toilet with poop in it can often be fixed using the same methods for other clogs. Start with a plunger, ensuring a good seal and strong thrusts to dislodge the waste. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method is particularly effective for organic clogs, helping to break down and lubricate the waste for easier passage.

**What causes a toilet to clog easily?**

Toilets often clog easily due to flushing non-flushable items like 'flushable' wipes, cotton balls, paper towels, or feminine hygiene products. Excessive use of toilet paper can also lead to blockages, as can a weak flush from an older or low-flow toilet model that lacks sufficient water pressure.

**Can hot water alone fix a clogged toilet?**

Hot water alone can sometimes help dislodge minor clogs, especially those caused by organic matter like toilet paper. However, it's significantly more effective when combined with dish soap, which acts as a lubricant to help the clog move. Remember to use hot tap water, not boiling water, to prevent damage to the toilet bowl.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all DIY methods (plunging, soap/water, auger) and the clog persists, or if multiple toilets and drains in your home are backing up, indicating a main sewer line issue. For more advanced troubleshooting or if these quick solutions don't work, you might find additional guidance in our broader article on [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Quick & Easy Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in toilets. These harsh chemicals can damage the toilet's porcelain finish, corrode pipes, and pose serious health risks due to fumes or splashes. They are often ineffective on toilet clogs and can make a plumber's job more difficult and hazardous if they have to deal with standing chemicals.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like 'flushable' wipes (they often aren't), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper at once. Consider installing a low-flow toilet if your current one struggles with flushing efficiency, or ensure it's maintained properly.
