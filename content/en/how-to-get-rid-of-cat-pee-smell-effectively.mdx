Getting rid of cat pee smell requires quick action and the right cleaning agents to neutralize the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor. Start by blotting fresh accidents immediately and then applying an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down the urine at a molecular level for lasting relief.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Black light (optional, for locating old stains)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate All Affected Areas

Even if you only smell it in one spot, cat urine can spread or be present in multiple locations. Use your nose to sniff out all soiled areas. For older, dried stains, a black light in a darkened room can reveal hidden urine spots, making them glow, which is crucial for thorough cleaning.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine (Don't Rub)

For fresh accidents, put on gloves and immediately blot the area with several layers of paper towels or an old rag. Press firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into carpet fibers or upholstery, making it harder to remove.

### Step 3: Apply a Vinegar Solution

Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Generously spray the affected area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Vinegar is an excellent natural odor neutralizer and can help break down some of the urine components. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then blot dry.

### Step 4: Sprinkle with Baking Soda

Once the vinegar solution has been blotted, generously sprinkle baking soda over the damp area. Baking soda is a powerful odor absorber. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, to draw out and neutralize residual odors. The longer it sits, the more effective it will be.

### Step 5: Use an Enzymatic Cleaner

Vacuum up the baking soda. Then, apply a high-quality enzymatic pet odor cleaner according to the product instructions. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down uric acid crystals, which are the main cause of the persistent cat pee smell. For truly permanent removal of stubborn odors, an enzymatic cleaner is essential. For more detailed insights into effectively and permanently removing cat urine odor, see [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the enzymatic cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did, especially on carpets or upholstery. You may need to use a stiff brush to work it in.

### Step 6: Allow to Air Dry Completely

Do not rush the drying process. Allow the treated area to air dry completely, which can take several hours or even a day or two, depending on humidity and the saturation level. You can use fans or open windows to speed up drying. Re-cover the area with foil or an inverted laundry basket to prevent your cat from returning to the spot while it dries.

### Step 7: Re-evaluate and Re-treat if Necessary

Once the area is completely dry, do a smell test. If a faint odor remains, you may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. Old, deeply set stains might require multiple applications to fully eradicate the smell. Patience is key for complete odor elimination.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions in an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't stain or damage the material.
- Address the underlying cause of your cat's inappropriate urination with your vet to prevent future accidents.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the spot.
- Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently bond the odor-causing proteins to the fibers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to remove completely?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that are insoluble in water and bind tightly to surfaces. These crystals reactivated by moisture, releasing a strong odor even after initial cleaning. Enzymatic cleaners are needed to break them down.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, it's generally not recommended to use bleach on cat urine. Bleach can be harsh on fabrics, may not effectively neutralize the uric acid crystals, and can produce dangerous fumes when mixed with other substances. It's also dangerous for pets.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into smaller, odorless compounds, effectively eliminating the source of the smell rather than just masking it.

**What if the cat pee smell is in a mattress or carpet pad?**

For deep saturation in mattresses or carpet pads, the cleaning process is more challenging. You might need to saturate the area with enzymatic cleaner multiple times, blot heavily, and allow for extended drying. In severe cases, the affected padding or mattress may need to be replaced.

**How can I prevent future cat urine accidents?**

Prevention involves addressing the root cause, which can be medical or behavioral. Consult your vet to rule out urinary tract infections or other health issues. Ensure litter boxes are clean, accessible, and sufficient in number (one per cat plus one extra).

**Can the cat pee smell come back after I've cleaned it?**

Yes, if the uric acid crystals are not completely broken down, the smell can reappear, especially in humid conditions or if the area becomes damp. This is why thorough cleaning with an enzymatic cleaner and complete drying are crucial for permanent removal.

**Is white vinegar safe to use around pets?**

Yes, diluted white vinegar is generally safe for cleaning around pets. Its strong scent dissipates as it dries and is non-toxic. However, always ensure the area is completely dry before allowing pets back to prevent them from ingesting any residue.
