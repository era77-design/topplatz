These cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, making them essential for complete removal. Follow up with proper drying and ventilation to eliminate any remaining scent.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Clean water
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)
- UV black light (optional, for locating old stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Pinpoint the Urine Stain

Begin by finding all affected areas. Fresh stains are usually visible, but dried urine can be harder to spot. Use your nose to sniff out the odor, or for older, invisible stains, a UV black light can be incredibly helpful as it makes urine glow in the dark. Mark these spots for thorough treatment.

### Step 2: Absorb Excess Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is key. Put on rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth to blot the urine. Press down firmly to soak up as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. Replace saturated towels until no more moisture transfers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels for a minute or two to maximize absorption, especially on carpets.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with a Vinegar Solution

After blotting, mix a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Generously spray the affected area, ensuring it saturates the spot. Vinegar helps neutralize the ammonia smell in urine and acts as a mild disinfectant. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then blot dry again with clean towels.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test the vinegar solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration, especially on delicate fabrics.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Pet Odor Remover

This is the most crucial step for [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor). Enzymatic cleaners contain specialized bacteria that "eat" the uric acid crystals found in cat urine, which are responsible for the persistent odor. Follow the product instructions carefully, often requiring saturation of the area and a specific dwell time. Ensure the cleaner reaches deep into carpet padding or upholstery.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not use other cleaning products before or after the enzymatic cleaner, as they can interfere with its effectiveness.

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

After applying the enzymatic cleaner, it's vital to let the treated area air dry completely. This allows the enzymes ample time to break down all the odor-causing compounds. You can cover the area with a clean towel and weigh it down to continue absorbing moisture, or use a fan to speed up drying. Avoid walking on the area until it's fully dry.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional)

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any lingering odors. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, to maximize its odor-absorbing power.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very stubborn odors, you can leave the baking soda on for 24-48 hours before vacuuming.

### Step 7: Vacuum Away Residue

Finally, thoroughly vacuum the baking soda from the carpet or upholstery. This removes both the baking soda and any remaining odor particles. For hard surfaces, wipe away the baking soda with a damp cloth. Repeat the entire process if any cat pee smell persists after the area is completely dry.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act Fast: The quicker you address a fresh urine stain, the easier it is to remove the odor completely.
- Don't Scrub: Blotting is always better than scrubbing, which can spread the urine and damage fibers.
- Ventilate: Open windows and use fans to improve air circulation during and after cleaning, helping to dissipate odors.
- Prevent Re-soiling: Once clean, deter your cat from returning to the spot by placing tin foil, a plastic mat, or their food bowl there temporarily.
- Professional Help: For very old, large, or deeply saturated stains, especially in subflooring, professional cleaning or remediation might be necessary.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to prevent damage or discoloration.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves to protect your hands from urine and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Never mix ammonia-based cleaners with bleach, as this creates dangerous fumes. Cat urine already contains ammonia, so avoid using bleach directly on urine stains.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals, which don't dissolve easily in water and react with moisture in the air, reactivating the strong, unpleasant odor. These crystals are difficult to remove without specialized cleaners that break them down.

**Can I use bleach to get rid of cat pee smell?**

No, you should never use bleach on cat urine. Bleach can react with the ammonia in urine to create dangerous chloramine gas. Furthermore, the strong scent of bleach might even attract your cat to re-mark the area.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work for cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine, effectively eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**What if the smell is in the subfloor or wall?**

If urine has soaked deeply into subflooring or drywall, you may need to remove and replace the affected materials. For subfloors, sealing the cleaned wood with an odor-blocking primer after enzymatic treatment can also help prevent residual smells.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is clean, easily accessible, and your cat has enough boxes (one per cat plus one extra). Stress, medical issues (like UTIs), or changes in routine can also cause inappropriate urination, so consult a vet if the problem persists.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to go away after cleaning?**

With proper cleaning using an enzymatic cleaner, the smell should dissipate significantly as the area dries, typically within a few hours to a day. For very old or deep stains, it might require multiple treatments and complete drying to fully eliminate the odor.

**Can white vinegar alone permanently remove cat pee smell?**

White vinegar can help neutralize some of the ammonia smell and is a good temporary measure or pre-treatment. However, it doesn't break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, so it's usually not enough for permanent removal. An enzymatic cleaner is essential for that.
