To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by blotting up fresh urine thoroughly, then applying an enzymatic cleaner directly to the affected area. These specialized cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the pungent odor, effectively neutralizing the smell at its source. For a comprehensive approach, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- Spray bottle (optional, for vinegar/water mix)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Rubber gloves
- Old toothbrush or scrub brush (for carpets)
- UV black light (optional, for locating old stains)
- Plastic sheeting or foil (to cover treated areas)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source

Before you can eliminate the smell, you need to find all affected areas. Use your nose to pinpoint the strongest odor, or for hidden or older stains, a UV (black) light in a darkened room will make urine glow. Mark the edges of the affected area with tape to ensure you treat the full extent of the stain.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cat urine can soak deeper and wider than it appears on the surface, especially in carpets and upholstery. Ensure you identify the entire affected zone.

### Step 2: Blot, Don't Rub

For fresh urine, immediate action is crucial. Immediately blot up as much liquid as possible using thick layers of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth. Press down firmly, standing on the towels if necessary, and replace them until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into the material, making it harder to remove.

### Step 3: Apply Enzymatic Cleaner

Generously saturate the soiled area with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor cleaner. These cleaners contain specialized enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals, which are the main cause of the persistent, pungent smell. Follow the product instructions carefully regarding saturation and dwell time for optimal results. For more on achieving lasting odor removal, see our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did. For carpets, this means saturating the padding underneath if the urine has seeped through.

### Step 4: Allow Dwell Time & Air Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit for the recommended time specified on the product label, which can range from 10 minutes to several hours, or even overnight. Do not rinse the area during this time. To prevent pets from disturbing the treated area and to ensure slow, natural drying, cover it with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil.

### Step 5: Remove Residue and Re-Treat (If Needed)

Once the area is completely dry, vacuum up any remaining residue from the cleaner. Sniff the area closely to check for any lingering odors. If the smell persists, it indicates that some uric acid crystals remain, and you will need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. Old or deeply saturated stains often require multiple applications for complete odor elimination.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional)

For an added layer of odor absorption and freshness, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the dried, treated area. Baking soda is known for its excellent ability to absorb residual odors. Let it sit for several hours or overnight, then vacuum it up thoroughly. This step can help ensure a completely fresh scent.

> 💡 **Tip:** For hard, non-porous surfaces, a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle can be used after blotting, but always follow up with an enzymatic cleaner for the most effective odor breakdown.

### Step 7: Address the Air and Prevent Recurrence

Open windows to air out the room and consider using an air purifier to help eliminate airborne odor particles. It's also vital to understand why your cat might be urinating outside the litter box (e.g., litter box issues, stress, medical problems) to prevent future accidents. Consult a veterinarian if behavioral issues persist, as underlying health conditions can often be the cause.


## Tips & Tricks

- **Test Cleaners First**: Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the material (carpet, upholstery) to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage before applying it widely.
- **Avoid Ammonia-Based Cleaners**: Ammonia is a component of urine and can actually attract your cat back to the same spot to pee again, intensifying the problem. Stick to enzymatic cleaners designed for pet urine.
- **Patience is Key**: Eliminating cat pee smell, especially from old or deeply saturated stains, often requires patience and multiple treatments. Don't rush the drying or re-application process.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ **Do Not Mix Cleaning Products**: Never mix different cleaning chemicals, especially bleach with ammonia-based products or vinegar, as this can create dangerous fumes. Stick to one product at a time and rinse thoroughly between applications if switching solutions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so much stronger than other animal urine?**

Cat urine contains a high concentration of urea and uric acid crystals. These uric acid crystals are particularly difficult to break down and become more pungent over time as they react with moisture, causing the persistent, strong odor that is unique to cat urine.

**Can I use regular household cleaners to get rid of cat pee smell?**

Regular household cleaners like soap and water or general disinfectants may mask the smell temporarily but will not break down the uric acid crystals. Only enzymatic cleaners are truly effective at neutralizing the odor at its source by biologically digesting these crystals.

**Does white vinegar really help with cat pee smell?**

Yes, white vinegar is an acidic substance that can help neutralize the alkaline salts in cat urine, reducing some of the odor. However, it doesn't break down uric acid crystals like enzymatic cleaners do, so it's best used as a pre-treatment or in conjunction with enzymatic solutions for comprehensive odor removal.

**How can I find old, dried cat pee stains that I can't see or smell easily?**

The most effective way to locate old, dried cat pee stains is by using a UV (black) light in a darkened room. The uric acid crystals in the dried urine will fluoresce under the UV light, making them visible as glowing spots, even if they're invisible to the naked eye.

**Is it safe to use enzymatic cleaners around my pets?**

Most enzymatic pet odor cleaners are formulated to be safe for use around pets once dry, but always check the specific product label for safety instructions and precautions. It's generally best to keep pets away from wet, treated areas until they are completely dry to avoid ingestion or skin irritation.

**How long will the cat pee smell last if I don't treat it properly?**

If untreated or improperly cleaned, cat pee smell can last indefinitely. The uric acid crystals will continue to emit a strong odor, especially in humid conditions, making the smell a permanent and unpleasant fixture in your home until thoroughly neutralized by appropriate cleaning methods.

**What if the cat pee smell is in concrete or subflooring?**

For concrete or subflooring, the cleaning process is similar but may require more saturation and longer dwell times for enzymatic cleaners to penetrate deeply. In severe or persistent cases, after thorough cleaning, sealing the concrete with a specialized odor-blocking primer might be necessary, or even replacing affected sections of subflooring.
