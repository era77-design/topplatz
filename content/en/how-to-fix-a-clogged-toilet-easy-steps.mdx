Fixing a clogged toilet often requires just a few simple steps and common household tools. Start by assessing the water level and then try methods like plunging, using hot water and soap, or a homemade drain cleaner to clear the blockage.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange type recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (closet snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Bathroom and Assess the Clog

Before you begin, lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any spills. Put on rubber gloves for hygiene and protection. Observe the water level in the bowl; if it's very high, avoid flushing again to prevent an overflow, as this indicates a significant blockage.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal around the entire opening. Push down and pull up with force, maintaining the seal, for about 10-15 cycles. On the last pull, break the seal quickly to create maximum suction. Repeat this process a few times if necessary to dislodge the clog.

### Step 3: Try the Hot Water and Dish Soap Method

If a plunger isn't available or hasn't worked, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured from waist height to create some force. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, allowing the soap to lubricate the clog and the hot water to help break it down.

### Step 4: Utilize Baking Soda and Vinegar

For a natural alternative, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction helps to break apart organic matter and dislodge blockages. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for stubborn clogs, before attempting to flush.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Closet Snake)

If the previous methods fail, a toilet auger, also known as a closet snake, can clear deeper clogs. Carefully insert the end of the auger into the drain, turning the handle as you push it deeper until you feel resistance. Once you hit the obstruction, rotate the handle to break it up or hook it, then slowly pull the auger back out.

### Step 6: Flush and Verify the Fix

After attempting any of the above methods, carefully try to flush the toilet. If the water drains normally and the clog is gone, you've succeeded. If it still drains slowly or doesn't drain at all, you may need to repeat a method or consider calling a professional plumber for more severe blockages.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets; its unique shape creates a better seal for effective plunging.
- Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- If the water level is rising rapidly after a flush, do not flush again! This will prevent an overflow onto your bathroom floor.
- For persistent or recurring clogs, a professional plumber might be needed to diagnose underlying issues within your plumbing system.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and release dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as the extreme temperature can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when using a toilet auger to avoid scratching or damaging the ceramic surface of your toilet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can effectively fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of hot water and dish soap, or a baking soda and vinegar mixture. Pouring dish soap followed by hot (not boiling) water can help break down and lubricate the clog, while baking soda and vinegar create a fizzing reaction that dislodges blockages.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by using a plunger vigorously to create suction and push the blockage through. If that doesn't work, try the hot water and dish soap method to help break down the organic matter and lubricate its passage. For stubborn blockages, a toilet auger can be used to physically clear the obstruction; always wear rubber gloves for hygiene.

**What should I do if my toilet is still clogged after trying these methods?**

If your toilet remains clogged after trying plunging, hot water/soap, and baking soda/vinegar, it might indicate a more severe blockage deeper in the pipes. At this point, it's recommended to use a toilet auger if you haven't already, or consider calling a professional plumber to avoid potential damage or further complications. For more advanced tips, you can refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Expert Tips](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

No, it is strongly advised not to use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. These harsh chemicals can damage your toilet's porcelain, corrode pipes, harm your septic system, and release toxic fumes. They often fail to clear toilet clogs and can make the problem worse or more hazardous for a plumber to address.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

Prevent future toilet clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wet wipes (even those labeled 'flushable'), feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Educating household members on proper flushing habits and performing occasional maintenance can also help.

**How do I know if the clog is serious and I need a plumber?**

You might need a plumber if the toilet water doesn't drain at all, if multiple fixtures in your home (like sinks or showers) are also slow to drain, or if you notice sewage backing up into other drains. These signs suggest a main line clog or a more complex plumbing issue that requires professional intervention.
