A clogged toilet can be a frustrating problem, but often it's a simple fix you can do yourself. Before you panic, try these common methods to clear the blockage and get your toilet flushing properly again.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Bucket
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)
- Bleach or disinfectant spray

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Assess the Situation and Protect Your Bathroom

First, if the toilet bowl is full and threatening to overflow, do not flush it again. Immediately lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from any spills or splashes.

### Step 2: 2. Try the Plunger Method

Ensure you have a good quality flange plunger, which creates a better seal than a cup plunger. Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it covers the entire opening to create a vacuum. Push down firmly to establish a seal, then pull up sharply without breaking the seal. Repeat this vigorous pushing and pulling motion 10-20 times until you hear a gurgling sound or see the water begin to drain, indicating the clog is moving.

### Step 3: 3. Use Dish Soap and Hot Water (No Plunger Needed)

If a plunger isn't available or hasn't worked for a milder clog, this method can often be effective. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes to allow the soap to sink and lubricate the clog. After waiting, carefully pour a bucket of hot (but not boiling) water from waist height into the bowl, allowing the force and temperature to help break down and push the clog through. Wait a few minutes and observe if the water level drops.

### Step 4: 4. Employ a Toilet Auger or Snake for Stubborn Clogs

For more stubborn clogs that resist plunging or the soap/water method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is your next best tool. This specialized tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the end of the auger into the drain opening, crank the handle to extend the cable, and gently push until you feel resistance. Rotate the handle to break up or hook the clog, then slowly retract the auger.

### Step 5: 5. Flush and Verify

Once you believe the clog is cleared, give the toilet a test flush. If the water drains normally, the clog is gone. You might need to flush a couple of times to ensure everything is flowing freely and completely clear the pipe. If the water still rises or drains slowly, the clog might be deeper or require another attempt with your chosen method.

### Step 6: 6. Clean and Sanitize Your Bathroom

After successfully clearing the clog, it's crucial to clean and sanitize the area to maintain hygiene. Dispose of any soiled towels or newspaper responsibly. Use a disinfectant spray or bleach solution to thoroughly wipe down the toilet seat, rim, handle, and any surrounding surfaces that may have come into contact with toilet water. This ensures your bathroom is clean and fresh.


## Tips & Tricks

- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage your pipes, are often ineffective on toilet clogs, and pose safety and environmental risks.
- Keep a dedicated, high-quality flange plunger readily accessible in each bathroom. This ensures you're prepared for quick action when a clog occurs.
- Educate all household members on what can and cannot be flushed down the toilet. Only flush human waste and toilet paper to prevent future blockages.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as the extreme temperature can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not repeatedly flush an overflowing toilet; turn off the water supply at the tank or the shut-off valve behind the toilet if necessary to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves to protect yourself from germs and bacteria when dealing with clogged toilets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 15-20 minutes. Then, carefully pour a bucket of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl from waist height; the soap lubricates the clog, and the hot water helps break it down and push it through.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, the best first approach is usually a plunger, ensuring a good seal and plunging vigorously. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the organic matter. For very stubborn blockages, a toilet auger is effective for dislodging or hooking the material to pull it out or push it through.

**What if my toilet is clogged and overflowing?**

If your toilet is clogged and overflowing, immediately lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl. Do not flush again, as this will only worsen the overflow. Then, proceed with a plunger or the dish soap and hot water method to clear the clog, having towels ready to absorb any spilled water.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners for clogged toilets. They are often ineffective on toilet clogs, can damage your pipes, and pose significant health and environmental risks. Stick to mechanical methods like plunging or snaking, or the dish soap and hot water trick for safer and often more effective results.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all the common methods (plunging, dish soap/hot water, auger) and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged. Also, call a plumber if multiple toilets or drains in your home are backing up, as this could indicate a more serious main sewer line blockage requiring professional intervention.

**How can I prevent future toilet clogs?**

To prevent future toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like wet wipes (even if labeled 'flushable'), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Regularly clean your toilet and educate household members on proper flushing habits to maintain good flow.
