A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but most common clogs can be resolved with basic tools and techniques. Before calling a plumber, try these DIY solutions to get your toilet flushing properly again.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange or cup type)
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda (optional)
- White vinegar (optional)
- Toilet auger/snake (for stubborn clogs)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess and Prepare Your Workspace

First, put on rubber gloves to protect your hands. If the toilet bowl is overflowing or close to it, avoid flushing again. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and protect your floor. If the water level is very high, you might need to bail some out into a bucket before proceeding.

### Step 2: Plunge with Proper Technique

Position your plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down slowly to create a vacuum, then pull up sharply without breaking the seal. Repeat this vigorous plunging motion 10-15 times. The goal is to dislodge the clog with alternating pressure. After several plunges, pull the plunger away quickly to see if the water drains.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, squirt a generous amount of dish soap (about 1/4 cup) into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for a few minutes to lubricate the clog. Then, carefully pour about a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl from waist height. The heat and soap can help break down organic matter and allow the clog to slide through. Wait 20-30 minutes for it to work, then gently attempt a flush.

### Step 4: Use Baking Soda and Vinegar for Stubborn Clogs

For a more powerful natural solution, especially useful if you're without a plunger, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl. Follow it with two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz, creating a chemical reaction that can help break down tough clogs. Let it sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very stubborn blockages, before flushing.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger or Snake

If DIY methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is the next step. This specialized tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the auger's end into the drain, crank the handle to feed the cable, and gently push until you feel resistance. Rotate the handle to break up or hook the clog, then slowly pull the auger back out.

### Step 6: Test and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, carefully flush the toilet once to confirm. If the water drains freely, the clog is gone. If not, you may need to repeat a method or consider professional help. Thoroughly clean the toilet, plunger, and any spilled areas with disinfectant to maintain hygiene.


## Tips & Tricks

- Prevent clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products.
- Keep a good quality flange plunger readily accessible in every bathroom for quick clog resolution.
- Do not use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and pose a health risk if they splash back.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid repeated flushing when the toilet is clogged and the water level is rising, as this can cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Be cautious with harsh chemical drain cleaners; they are generally not recommended for toilets due to potential damage and safety hazards.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

You can fix a clogged toilet using a plunger, a combination of dish soap and hot water, or a baking soda and vinegar solution. For more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger is an effective tool to clear the blockage.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

To fix a clogged toilet without a plunger, try pouring about 1/4 cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a mix of one cup of baking soda and two cups of white vinegar can also help break down the clog.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

For a toilet clogged with poop, the best first step is usually a good quality plunger, as the force can break up the organic matter. If a plunger isn't available, the dish soap and hot water method is often very effective, as the soap helps lubricate and the hot water helps dissolve the waste.

**What is the best homemade solution for a clogged toilet?**

The best homemade solution for a clogged toilet typically involves a combination of dish soap and hot water. For a more reactive approach, a mixture of baking soda and white vinegar can create a fizzing action that helps dislodge and break down clogs, especially those composed of organic material.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods without success, if the toilet frequently clogs, or if you suspect a deeper issue in your plumbing system. Persistent clogs can indicate a problem beyond the immediate toilet drain, such as a main sewer line blockage.

**Can I use a regular drain snake in a toilet?**

It's generally not recommended to use a regular drain snake in a toilet. A standard drain snake can scratch the porcelain bowl and potentially damage the toilet's internal mechanisms. Instead, use a specialized toilet auger (or closet auger), which has a protective sleeve designed to prevent damage while clearing clogs.
