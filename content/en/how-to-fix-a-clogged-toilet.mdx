To fix a clogged toilet, start by using a plunger with a good seal. If that doesn't work, try a mixture of hot water and dish soap, or a chemical drain cleaner as a last resort. Always exercise caution and protect your surroundings.

## What You Need

- Flange plunger
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket
- Wire coat hanger (optional)
- Baking soda and white vinegar (optional)
- Toilet auger or drain snake (optional)
- Chemical drain cleaner (last resort)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Assess the Situation and Protect Your Space

Before you begin, stop flushing the toilet if the water level is rising to prevent overflow. Locate the water shut-off valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise to stop the water flow. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills.

### Step 2: 2. Grab Your Plunger and Plunge Effectively

For toilet clogs, a flange plunger (the one with an extended rubber lip) is most effective as it creates a better seal. Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a complete seal. Push and pull firmly but steadily for about 15-20 times, maintaining the seal, then quickly pull it away to break the suction. Repeat if necessary, adding a little water to the bowl if it's too low.

### Step 3: 3. Try the Hot Water and Dish Soap Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the hot water helps to break down the clog while the soap acts as a lubricant, allowing it to slide down the drain. After waiting, try flushing the toilet.

### Step 4: 4. Use Baking Soda and Vinegar (Optional)

For a natural approach, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction helps to break down organic matter in the clog. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for stubborn clogs. After the waiting period, flush the toilet to see if the clog has cleared.

### Step 5: 5. Snake the Drain with a Wire Hanger or Auger

If the above methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger) is designed to clear clogs without damaging the porcelain. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain, turning the handle to extend the cable and break up or retrieve the clog. If you don't have an auger, an unbent wire coat hanger can be used carefully, but be gentle to avoid scratching the bowl.

### Step 6: 6. Consider a Chemical Drain Cleaner (Last Resort)

As a final DIY option, you can use a chemical drain cleaner formulated for toilets. Follow the product instructions precisely, ensuring good ventilation and wearing protective gloves and eyewear. Pour the recommended amount into the bowl and allow it to work for the specified time before flushing. Only use this as a last resort due to potential harsh chemicals.

### Step 7: 7. Flush and Test

Once you believe the clog is cleared, turn the water supply valve back on. Flush the toilet to test if the water drains properly. If the water level still rises, it indicates a persistent clog that may require professional attention. If it drains well, your toilet is fixed!


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger, as its design creates a better seal for toilet drains.
- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- If the toilet water level starts to rise rapidly, immediately turn off the water supply valve behind the toilet to prevent an overflow.
- Wear rubber gloves throughout the process to maintain hygiene and protect your hands.
- Regularly clean your toilet bowl and pipes with a mild cleaner to prevent buildup.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix different chemical drain cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes or reactions.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water to unclog a toilet, as the extreme temperature can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Chemical drain cleaners can be corrosive; use them sparingly, with proper ventilation, and always follow manufacturer's safety instructions.
- ⚠️ If the clog persists after multiple attempts or if you suspect a deeper issue, it's best to call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can try pouring half a cup of dish soap followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl and letting it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a mixture of baking soda and vinegar can create a fizzing reaction to break down the clog. For physical obstructions, a straightened wire coat hanger or a toilet auger can be used.

**What should I do if my toilet is clogged with poop?**

If your toilet is clogged with poop, start by using a flange plunger with firm, consistent pushes. If plunging doesn't work, try the hot water and dish soap method, which can help break down organic matter and lubricate the drain. Baking soda and vinegar can also be effective. Always wear gloves and take precautions to prevent overflow.

**Can I use boiling water to unclog a toilet?**

No, you should never use boiling water to unclog a toilet. The sudden change in temperature can cause the porcelain bowl to crack, leading to significant damage and potential leaks. Always use hot, but not boiling, water for safety and effectiveness.

**How can I prevent toilet clogs in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like wet wipes (even if labeled 'flushable'), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider using less toilet paper per flush and ensuring your toilet has good flushing power.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a professional plumber if the clog persists after trying multiple DIY methods, if the toilet water is overflowing and you can't stop it, or if you suspect a deeper issue in your plumbing system beyond the toilet itself. Recurring clogs can also indicate a larger problem that a plumber can diagnose and fix.

**Are chemical drain cleaners safe to use in a toilet?**

Chemical drain cleaners should be used with extreme caution and only as a last resort. They can be corrosive, potentially damaging pipes or harming your skin and eyes if not handled properly. Always ensure good ventilation, wear protective gear, and strictly follow the product's instructions. Never mix different chemical cleaners.
