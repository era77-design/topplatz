To fix a clogged toilet, start by blocking the water supply and then use a plunger with firm, consistent thrusts. If a plunger isn't available, try a mix of hot water and dish soap, or a toilet auger for deeper blockages.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Flange plunger (recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water Flow

Immediately open the toilet tank lid and push down on the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl, preventing overflow. If the bowl is already full, reach behind the toilet and turn the shut-off valve clockwise to completely stop the water supply to the toilet. This crucial first step prevents a messy overflow while you work.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Lay old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to catch any splashes or spills. This will protect your flooring and make cleanup much easier. Put on your rubber gloves to maintain hygiene and protect your hands from dirty water.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position your flange plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it forms a tight seal around the opening. Push down gently to release any trapped air, then plunge vigorously with a strong, in-and-out motion for 15-20 seconds without breaking the seal. Repeat this process several times, if necessary, allowing the suction to dislodge the clog. For more detailed techniques and troubleshooting, consult our guide on [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Expert Tips](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

### Step 4: Try the Hot Water and Dish Soap Method

If a plunger isn't available or hasn't worked, pour about half a cup of dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water from a bucket, pouring it from waist height to add some force. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water helps break it down. After waiting, try to flush.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs that are deeper in the drain, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is a highly effective tool. Insert the auger's coiled cable into the drain opening, feeding it gently until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to extend the cable and break through or retrieve the obstruction. Be careful not to scratch the porcelain.

### Step 6: Flush and Re-assess

Once you believe the clog is clear, turn the water supply back on and flush the toilet. If the water drains freely, the clog is gone. If it still drains slowly or appears to struggle, repeat your chosen method or try an alternative. Avoid repeated flushing if the bowl looks like it will overflow again.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger (the one with an extended rubber lip) as it creates a better seal for toilets than a standard cup plunger.
- Never flush repeatedly if the toilet bowl is full; this will only cause an overflow.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Be mindful of how much paper is used at once.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do NOT use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and pose a severe health risk due to fumes and skin contact.
- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves to protect yourself from germs and dirty water.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow. Then, use a flange plunger with firm, consistent thrusts. If that doesn't work, try a mixture of hot water and dish soap, or for more stubborn blockages, a toilet auger is often effective.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try pouring a mixture of hot (not boiling) water and a generous squirt of dish soap into the bowl. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to help break down the clog. Alternatively, a toilet auger can effectively clear deeper blockages without needing a plunger.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

A toilet clogged with poop often responds well to the plunger method, as the plunging action can help break up the waste and move it through the drain. If a plunger isn't immediately effective, try the hot water and dish soap method to help soften and lubricate the clog. For very stubborn situations, a toilet auger is usually the most reliable tool.

**What if my toilet is still clogged after trying these methods?**

If your toilet remains clogged after trying plunging, the dish soap method, and a toilet auger, the blockage might be further down the main drain line. At this point, it's best to contact a professional plumber, as it could indicate a larger plumbing issue.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet?**

No, it is strongly advised not to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can be corrosive, damage your toilet's porcelain or pipes, and produce toxic fumes. They also rarely work on severe toilet clogs and can make the problem worse or more dangerous for a plumber to address.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

Prevent clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing sanitary products, wet wipes (even 'flushable' ones), paper towels, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider installing a wastebasket next to the toilet for non-flushable items.
