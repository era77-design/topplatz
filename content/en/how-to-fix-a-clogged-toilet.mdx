A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but often it can be fixed with simple tools and techniques. Before calling a plumber, try these practical steps to clear the blockage yourself. Most common clogs are easily resolved at home.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Flange plunger (recommended)
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- White vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

First, resist the urge to flush again if the water level is high; this will likely cause an overflow. Immediately locate and turn the water supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. Put on your rubber gloves to maintain hygiene and protect your hands.

### Step 2: Employ the Plunger Correctly

If you have a plunger, ensure it's a flange plunger, which is designed for toilets and creates a better seal. Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring it forms an airtight seal. Push down gently to release air, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion for 15-20 seconds without breaking the seal. Finally, pull the plunger up sharply to break the seal and see if the water drains. You may need to repeat this process several times.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water level in the bowl is too low to cover the plunger's head, add some hot water (not boiling) to create a better seal and increase pressure for plunging.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this by pouring a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl. The hot water helps break down the clog, and the dish soap acts as a lubricant, helping the clog slide down the pipes. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer for stubborn clogs, allowing gravity and the soap to do their work. After waiting, check if the water level has dropped, then try a gentle flush.

### Step 4: Use Baking Soda and Vinegar for Organic Clogs

For a natural alternative, especially effective on organic clogs, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl. Wait a few minutes, then slowly pour two cups of white vinegar into the bowl. The mixture will fizz and foam, which helps to break down the blockage. Let this sit for at least an hour, or preferably overnight, to allow the chemical reaction to work on the clog. After the waiting period, flush the toilet gently to see if the clog has cleared.

### Step 5: When to Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

If the previous methods fail, a toilet auger, also known as a closet auger, is your next best tool. This specialized snake is designed with a protective rubber sleeve to prevent scratching your toilet's porcelain. Insert the auger's end into the drain opening and crank the handle clockwise to extend the cable into the trap. Continue pushing and cranking until you feel resistance, indicating the clog. Rotate the auger to break up or hook the clog, then carefully pull it back out. Repeat if necessary.

### Step 6: Test and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, turn the water supply valve back on. Flush the toilet carefully, observing if the water drains normally. If it does, you've succeeded! Clean any tools used and disinfect the toilet area and floor. Dispose of soiled towels or newspaper appropriately. If the toilet still clogs or drains slowly, you may have a more complex issue requiring professional plumbing assistance.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves and lay down towels to protect yourself and your bathroom from splashes and spills.
- Never use boiling water, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners; they can damage your pipes and pose health risks.
- If your toilet seems like it's about to overflow, quickly reach behind the bowl and turn the water supply valve clockwise to shut off the water flow.
- For recurring clogs, consider having a plumber inspect your toilet and drain lines for underlying issues.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water in your toilet, as it can cause the porcelain to crack.
- ⚠️ Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners, which can damage plumbing and are hazardous if splashed.
- ⚠️ If the toilet is overflowing, immediately turn off the water supply valve located behind the toilet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can effectively fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using the dish soap and hot water method or the baking soda and vinegar method. For the dish soap method, pour half a cup of dish soap followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. For baking soda and vinegar, pour one cup of baking soda, then two cups of white vinegar, and let it sit for an hour or overnight.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

Clogged toilets with poop are typically organic clogs and can often be resolved with standard methods. Try plunging first, ensuring a good seal. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method is very effective at breaking down organic matter. As a last resort, a toilet auger can physically clear the blockage.

**What causes a toilet to clog?**

Toilets commonly clog due to flushing too much toilet paper at once, or flushing non-flushable items like wet wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or cotton balls. Sometimes, an older toilet with weaker flushing power or issues within the drain line itself can also contribute to frequent clogs.

**How do I know if my toilet is clogged or if it's a main drain issue?**

If only your toilet is clogged, it's likely an isolated toilet clog. However, if other drains in your house, such as sinks, showers, or bathtubs, are also draining slowly or backing up when you flush the toilet, this indicates a blockage in your main sewer line, which requires professional plumbing attention.

**Can I use a regular drain snake for a toilet?**

It's not recommended to use a regular drain snake for a toilet. A standard drain snake can scratch or damage the porcelain finish of your toilet bowl. Instead, use a specialized tool called a toilet auger (or closet auger), which has a protective rubber sleeve designed to prevent damage while effectively clearing clogs.

**How can I prevent toilet clogs?**

To prevent toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing excessive amounts of toilet paper at once, and never flush items like wet wipes (even if labeled 'flushable'), paper towels, cotton swabs, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products. Consider upgrading to a higher-efficiency toilet if yours is old and prone to weak flushes.
