To fix a clogged toilet, start by assessing the water level and preventing overflow, then use a plunger with proper technique. If plunging fails, try methods like dish soap and hot water or a toilet auger to clear the obstruction. Always wear gloves and avoid chemical drain cleaners for safety.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Plunger (flange style recommended)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)
- Bucket (optional, for bailing water)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water and Prepare the Area

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and close the flapper valve to stop water from entering the bowl, or turn off the water supply valve located behind the toilet at the wall. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from potential splashes or overflows. This crucial first step prevents the situation from worsening.

### Step 2: Assess the Clog and Water Level

Observe the water level in the toilet bowl. If it's very full and close to overflowing, you may need to bail out some water into a bucket to prevent spillage when you start plunging. If the water level is normal or slightly elevated, you can proceed directly to plunging.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

Place the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it forms a tight seal to create suction. Push down slowly to expel air, then plunge vigorously 15-20 times, maintaining the seal throughout the motion. On the last plunge, pull the plunger up sharply to break the suction and dislodge the clog. Repeat this process a few times if necessary.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap directly into the toilet bowl. Follow this with approximately a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap acts as a lubricant, and the hot water helps to break down organic matter and toilet paper, allowing the clog to slide through.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs that resist plunging and the soap/water method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your next tool. Carefully feed the end of the auger into the toilet drain, cranking the handle to extend the cable. Once you feel resistance, rotate the handle to break up or hook the obstruction, then slowly retract the auger.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, you've succeeded! If the water level rises again, repeat the process. After the toilet is flushing properly, clean your tools and disinfect the surrounding area, especially if there were any spills.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Clogs

To avoid future clogs, educate household members on what not to flush. Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper, including 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Regular, moderate flushing can also help prevent buildup.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves when dealing with clogged toilets for hygiene and safety.
- A flange-style plunger is specifically designed for toilets and provides a better seal than cup plungers.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes, porcelain, and pose a health risk.
- Be patient; some clogs take time to dissolve or dislodge, so multiple attempts might be necessary.
- Keep a dedicated bucket nearby to bail water from an overflowing bowl if you need to reduce the water level before plunging.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet repeatedly if it's already full and not draining, as this will cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water, as the sudden temperature change can crack the porcelain bowl of your toilet.
- ⚠️ Chemical drain cleaners can be corrosive and harmful to pipes, septic systems, and your skin/eyes. Always prioritize mechanical methods.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. The soap lubricates the clog, and the hot water helps break it down.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

A clogged toilet with poop can often be fixed with a plunger. Ensure a good seal and plunge vigorously to dislodge the waste. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down organic matter, making it easier to flush.

**What dissolves toilet paper clogs?**

Hot (not boiling) water and dish soap are effective for dissolving toilet paper clogs. The soap acts as a lubricant, and the hot water helps break down the paper fibers. For more stubborn paper clogs, a toilet auger can physically clear the obstruction.

**How long does it take for a toilet clog to dissolve?**

The time it takes for a toilet clog to dissolve varies depending on its severity and what caused it. A simple paper clog might dissolve within 20-30 minutes with the dish soap and hot water method, while more stubborn clogs might require multiple attempts or mechanical intervention.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all DIY methods (plunging, dish soap/hot water, auger) and the toilet remains clogged, or if multiple drains in your home are slow or backed up. This could indicate a more serious issue with your main sewer line that requires professional attention.

**What if my toilet is gurgling when I use other drains?**

If your toilet gurgles when you use other drains like a sink or shower, it's often a sign of a partial clog deeper in your main sewer line, not just in the toilet itself. This indicates a more widespread plumbing issue that might require professional assessment and clearing.
