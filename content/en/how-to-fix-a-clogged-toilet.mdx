To fix a clogged toilet, first stop flushing to prevent overflow. Most clogs can be resolved using a plunger, a mix of dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange type preferred)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)
- Wet/dry vacuum (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Assess the Situation

Immediately stop flushing the toilet if you notice it's not draining. Repeated flushing will only cause the bowl to overflow, creating a bigger mess. Observe the water level; if it's high, wait for it to recede slightly before attempting any fixes. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from potential splashes.

### Step 2: Prepare for Plunging

Put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Ensure your plunger is a 'flange' type, which has an extended rubber lip designed to create a better seal in a toilet's drain opening. Before plunging, make sure there's enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger's head, which helps create optimal suction. If the water level is too low, add some from the sink or a bucket.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down gently at first to expel any air, then plunge vigorously with a strong up-and-down motion for about 15-20 seconds. Maintain the seal throughout this process. After plunging, quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and check if the water drains. You may need to repeat this process several times.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap directly into the toilet bowl. The soap acts as a lubricant, helping the clog slide down. Follow this by pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl. Hot water helps to dissolve or soften certain types of clogs. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, allowing the soap and water to work on the blockage, then check if it drains.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger) is a specialized tool designed to clear blockages without damaging the porcelain. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain, turning the handle as you feed the cable further into the pipe. When you feel resistance, you've likely hit the clog. Rotate the auger to break up or hook the obstruction, then slowly pull it out.

### Step 6: Consider a Wet/Dry Vacuum

As a last resort before calling a professional, a wet/dry vacuum can effectively remove clogs. First, remove all water from the toilet bowl. Then, place the vacuum hose directly into the drain opening, creating as tight a seal as possible. Turn on the vacuum to suck out the clog. Be sure to use a wet/dry vacuum, as a regular household vacuum is not safe for liquids.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves and lay down old towels to protect your bathroom floor.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes and are ineffective against many clogs.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. This includes 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, and hygiene products.
- Perform regular maintenance flushes with hot water to help keep pipes clear.
- If your toilet frequently clogs, there might be a larger plumbing issue requiring professional attention.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can be dangerous, corrosive to pipes, and harmful to the environment.
- ⚠️ Do not continue to flush a clogged toilet, as this will lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when using a toilet auger to avoid scratching or damaging the toilet's porcelain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How can I fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the clog to loosen and slide down. If that doesn't work, a toilet auger is another effective plunger-free option.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet when there's poop in it?**

When there's poop in a clogged toilet, the methods remain largely the same, but it's crucial to wear rubber gloves and protect your floor. Start with a plunger, ensuring a good seal. If unsuccessful, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down organic matter. For stubborn clogs, a toilet auger is effective for clearing solid blockages. Always avoid flushing again until the clog is cleared.

**What household items can I use to unclog a toilet?**

Common household items for unclogging a toilet include dish soap and hot water. The soap acts as a lubricant, and the hot water helps to break down the clog. You can also create a makeshift snake with a straightened wire hanger, though this should be used with caution to avoid scratching the bowl.

**How long should I wait after trying the dish soap and hot water method?**

After pouring dish soap and hot water into a clogged toilet, you should wait at least 20-30 minutes. This allows sufficient time for the soap to lubricate the blockage and the hot water to soften it, making it easier for the clog to clear on its own. Sometimes, waiting an hour or even overnight can yield better results for very stubborn clogs.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you've tried all common methods (plunging, dish soap, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, if multiple toilets or drains in your home are backing up, it could indicate a larger issue in your main sewer line that requires expert diagnosis and repair.
