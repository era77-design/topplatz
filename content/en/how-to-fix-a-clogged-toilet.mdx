To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow by closing the flapper or turning off the water supply valve. Then, use a flange plunger with firm, downward thrusts followed by a strong pull to create suction. If plunging fails, try a dish soap and hot water solution or a toilet auger.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspapers
- Flange plunger (recommended)
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (snake)
- Eye protection (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Stop Water Flow

Before you begin, lay old towels or newspapers around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes. If the water level in the bowl is rising and threatening to overflow, immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down the flapper valve to stop water from entering the bowl. Alternatively, locate the small shut-off valve on the wall behind the toilet and turn it clockwise to stop the water supply completely.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always wear rubber gloves to maintain hygiene while dealing with toilet clogs.

### Step 2: Assess the Water Level

Observe the water level in the toilet bowl. If the bowl is nearly overflowing, use a small bucket or disposable cup to scoop out some of the excess water into a larger bucket. This prevents spills and gives you room to work without making a mess, especially before attempting to plunge.

> 💡 **Tip:** Never flush the toilet repeatedly if it’s not draining, as this will only cause the bowl to overflow.

### Step 3: Plunge Effectively

Position your plunger over the toilet drain opening, ensuring a complete seal. The best plungers for toilets are flange plungers, which have an extended rubber lip. Push down slowly to expel air, then plunge vigorously with 10-20 firm, rapid up-and-down thrusts, maintaining the seal. On the last thrust, pull the plunger up sharply to break the seal and create strong suction, hopefully dislodging the clog.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water drains, flush once to check if the clog is gone. If not, repeat the plunging process a few more times.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. The hot water helps break down the clog, and the dish soap acts as a lubricant, helping the clog slide down the drain. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer for stubborn clogs.

> 💡 **Tip:** Using boiling water can crack the porcelain bowl, so ensure the water is hot but not scalding.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For very stubborn clogs that resist both plunging and the dish soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is your next best tool. This specialized tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the auger's tip into the drain opening, gently push until you feel resistance, then crank the handle to extend the cable and break up or retrieve the clog.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always wear eye protection when using an auger, as debris can sometimes fly out.

### Step 6: Flush and Verify

Once you believe the clog is clear, carefully flush the toilet to check. If the water drains normally, flush a second time to ensure everything is flowing freely. If the toilet still seems sluggish or partially clogged, you may need to repeat one of the earlier steps or consider that the clog might be further down the main drain line.

> 💡 **Tip:** Listen for the sound of normal flushing and observe the water level to confirm success.

### Step 7: Clean Up

After successfully clearing the clog, clean and sanitize your plunger, auger, and any buckets or tools used. Thoroughly wipe down the toilet bowl, seat, and surrounding floor area with a disinfectant cleaner. Dispose of any soiled towels or newspapers properly to maintain a hygienic bathroom environment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Regular cleaning and maintenance can help prevent future clogs.


## Tips & Tricks

- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, and hygiene products are common culprits.
- Consider a regular maintenance routine, such as occasionally pouring a cup of white vinegar down the drain, to help break down minor buildup.
- Educate household members on what should and should not be flushed down the toilet.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and are dangerous if splashed on skin or eyes.
- ⚠️ Do not flush repeatedly if the toilet is not draining, as this will inevitably lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves and, ideally, eye protection when dealing with clogged toilets for hygiene and safety.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

To fix a clogged toilet, start by using a flange plunger with firm thrusts. If that doesn't work, try pouring dish soap followed by hot (not boiling) water into the bowl and letting it sit. For persistent clogs, a toilet auger is an effective tool.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can often fix a clogged toilet by pouring half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog. A toilet auger is another effective plunger-free option.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

Fixing a clogged toilet with poop involves the same methods as any other clog. Begin with effective plunging to create suction and dislodge the blockage. If plunging fails, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the organic matter, or a toilet auger can be used to push through or retrieve the clog.

**What should I do if my toilet is clogged and the water is rising?**

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down the flapper valve to stop water from entering the bowl. Alternatively, turn the shut-off valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water supply. Then, scoop out excess water before attempting to clear the clog.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar can create a fizzing reaction, they are generally less effective than dish soap and hot water or a plunger for severe toilet clogs. They might help with very minor clogs or to deodorize, but are not recommended as a primary solution for significant blockages.

**What if my toilet is still clogged after trying everything?**

If your toilet remains clogged after trying plunging, dish soap and hot water, and a toilet auger, the clog might be deeper in your main drain line. At this point, it's best to call a professional plumber, as they have specialized tools and expertise to diagnose and clear more complex drain issues.

**How can I prevent toilet clogs?**

Prevent toilet clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products. Educate everyone in your household about proper toilet usage to minimize future issues.
