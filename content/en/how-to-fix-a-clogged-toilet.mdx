A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but many clogs can be resolved quickly with a few simple tools and techniques. Before panicking, assess the situation and try these practical steps to get your toilet flushing properly again.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Bucket
- Toilet auger (snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

First, check the water level in the toilet bowl. If it's already full to the brim, do not flush again, as this will cause an overflow. Turn off the water supply valve located behind the toilet at the wall to prevent further water from entering the bowl. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any potential splashes or overflows and put on a pair of rubber gloves for hygiene.

### Step 2: Try the Plunger Method

A good quality plunger is your best friend for most toilet clogs. Ensure you have a flange plunger, which has an extended rubber flap designed to create a better seal in the toilet's drain opening. Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it forms a complete seal. Plunge with strong, consistent up-and-down motions for about 15-20 seconds without breaking the seal, then quickly pull the plunger up to break the suction. Repeat this process a few times.

### Step 3: Use Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work, try a combination of dish soap and hot water. Squirt about half a cup of liquid dish soap directly into the toilet bowl. The soap helps to lubricate the clog, making it easier to move. Follow this by pouring a bucket of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl from about waist height, which adds force and helps dissolve the clog. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the soap and water to work on breaking down the obstruction.

### Step 4: Attempt a Second Plunge (if needed)

After allowing the dish soap and hot water to sit, try plunging again. The combined action of the lubricant and the added force of the water may have loosened the clog enough for the plunger to dislodge it. Continue to plunge vigorously, maintaining a good seal, and check if the water level begins to recede. If it does, the clog is likely breaking up.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger, also known as a closet auger or toilet snake, is specifically designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Carefully insert the end of the auger into the drain opening, feeding the cable down while cranking the handle. Once you feel resistance, you've likely hit the clog. Rotate the auger to break apart or hook the obstruction, then slowly pull it back out.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Once the clog appears to be clear and the water level has gone down, turn the water supply valve back on. Flush the toilet once to confirm that the water drains properly. If the clog seems to be gone, give it another flush to ensure everything is flowing smoothly. Clean up any spills and dispose of used towels and gloves appropriately.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger specifically designed for toilets; cup plungers are less effective.
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners in toilets, as they can damage pipes and porcelain and are often ineffective for toilet clogs.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Be especially mindful of 'flushable' wipes, as they often don't break down easily.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never pour boiling water into your toilet, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not repeatedly flush a clogged toilet, as this will only cause the bowl to overflow.
- ⚠️ If none of these methods work, or if you suspect a deeper issue in your plumbing system, it's best to call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a bucket of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog, then check if the water drains. If not, a toilet auger is the next best option.

**What do I do if my toilet is clogged with poop?**

A toilet clogged with poop should be treated similarly to other clogs. First, do not flush again to prevent overflow. Use a good flange plunger with strong, consistent motions. If that doesn't work, try the dish soap and hot water method to help break down the organic matter. Always wear rubber gloves and lay down towels for hygiene.

**What's the best way to unclog a toilet quickly?**

The quickest and often most effective method is using a good quality flange plunger. Ensure you create a tight seal over the drain and apply strong, consistent plunges. If the clog is minor, this can resolve the issue in under a minute.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar create a fizzing reaction, they are generally not powerful enough to clear significant toilet clogs. They work best for minor drain maintenance or odor removal. For effective unclogging, a plunger, dish soap/hot water, or a toilet auger are much more reliable.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all the common methods (plunging, dish soap/hot water, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, contact a professional if multiple drains in your home are slow or clogged, which could indicate a deeper main sewer line issue.

**How can I prevent my toilet from getting clogged?**

To prevent clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing paper towels, 'flushable' wipes, feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, or dental floss. Also, ensure you're not using excessive amounts of toilet paper, especially with older or low-flow toilets.
