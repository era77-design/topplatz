Fixing a clogged toilet often requires just a few simple tools and techniques. Most clogs can be resolved quickly using a plunger, but alternative methods are available for tougher blockages or if you don't have a plunger handy.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (snake)
- Bucket

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, put on rubber gloves to protect your hands from germs and potential splashes. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any overflow or spills, especially if the water level is high. If the toilet bowl is completely full and close to overflowing, turn off the water supply valve usually located behind or near the base of the toilet to prevent further overflow.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Correctly

Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring it creates a complete seal. If your plunger is a flange type, make sure the flange is extended into the drain. Plunge with a series of strong, consistent pushes and pulls for about 15-20 seconds without breaking the seal. After several plunges, quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and see if the water drains. Repeat this process a few times if necessary.

### Step 3: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. The soap acts as a lubricant, helping the clog slide down. Let the soap sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to work its way down to the clog. Follow this by slowly pouring a bucket of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl, aiming for the drain. The hot water helps dissolve the clog and move it along.

### Step 4: Employ Baking Soda and Vinegar

For stubborn clogs, a natural chemical reaction can sometimes help. Pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz and bubble, which helps break down the clog. Allow this mixture to sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very tough clogs, before flushing to check if the clog has cleared. You may need to follow up with another hot water pour.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

If all other methods fail, a toilet auger, also known as a closet auger or toilet snake, is your next best tool. This specialized tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain, turning the handle to extend the cable until you feel resistance. Once you hit the clog, crank the handle to break it up or hook onto it, then slowly pull the auger back out.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet to confirm the water drains properly. If the water level rises too high, quickly turn off the water supply again to prevent overflow. Once the toilet is functioning normally, clean up any spilled water or debris with your old towels and disinfect the area thoroughly. Dispose of gloves and towels properly.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets, as it provides a better seal than a cup plunger.
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes and are often ineffective against toilet clogs.
- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ If the toilet water is close to overflowing, turn off the water supply valve immediately to prevent a flood.
- ⚠️ If you suspect a main sewer line clog (multiple drains are slow or clogged), call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then slowly add a bucket of hot (not boiling) water. The soap lubricates the clog, and the hot water helps dissolve it.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by using a plunger as described in the steps. If plunging doesn't work, try the dish soap and hot water method. The soap can help break down and lubricate the organic matter, making it easier for the clog to pass.

**What should I do if my toilet is overflowing?**

If your toilet is overflowing, immediately reach behind the toilet and turn the water supply valve clockwise until it stops. This will shut off the water flow and prevent further spillage. Then, proceed with unclogging methods.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It's generally not recommended to use harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can be corrosive to your pipes, harmful to septic systems, and often ineffective against severe toilet clogs. Natural alternatives like baking soda and vinegar are safer.

**When should I call a professional plumber?**

You should call a professional plumber if you've tried all the methods (plunging, soap/water, baking soda/vinegar, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, if multiple drains in your home are slow or clogged, it could indicate a main sewer line issue requiring expert attention.

**How can I prevent future toilet clogs?**

Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like wet wipes (even 'flushable' ones), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider advising household members on proper flushing habits.
