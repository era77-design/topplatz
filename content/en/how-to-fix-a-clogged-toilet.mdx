A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but often, it's a fixable DIY task that doesn't require a professional. Before calling a plumber, try these straightforward methods to clear the blockage and get your toilet flushing properly again.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket or small trash can
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing & Assess the Situation

The very first step is to resist the urge to flush again, as this can cause the toilet to overflow and create a bigger mess. If the water level is high, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl. Assess the water level and the nature of the clog if visible.

### Step 2: Protect Your Bathroom Floor

Before you begin, lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet. This will protect your floor from any splashes or overflows during the unclogging process. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene and protect your hands.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position a sturdy flange plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion for 15-20 seconds. Maintain the seal and listen for the suction working. Pull the plunger up sharply after the last plunge to break the seal and clear the clog. Repeat if necessary.

### Step 4: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water (No Plunger Needed)

If you don't have a plunger, or it's not working, squirt about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate the clog. Follow this by slowly pouring about a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl. The heat and soap can help break down the blockage. Wait 20-30 minutes for the clog to loosen, then try flushing. For more quick and easy solutions, see [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Quick & Easy DIY Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

### Step 5: Employ Baking Soda and Vinegar

For another plunger-free method, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction helps to break down organic matter in the clog. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very stubborn clogs. Afterwards, try flushing the toilet.

### Step 6: Utilize a Toilet Auger or Snake

If the above methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is designed to clear deeper clogs without damaging the toilet bowl. Insert the auger's end into the drain, crank the handle to extend the cable, and gently push and twist until you feel resistance. Continue turning to break apart or retrieve the clog. Pull the auger back out slowly, cleaning it as you go.

### Step 7: When to Call a Professional

If after trying these methods, your toilet remains clogged or frequently clogs, it might indicate a more serious issue in your plumbing system. Extensive clogs, recurring problems, or multiple fixtures backing up suggest it's time to contact a professional plumber. They have specialized tools and expertise to diagnose and fix complex plumbing issues.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use rubber gloves when dealing with clogged toilets for hygiene and protection.
- Never use harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage your pipes and are hazardous.
- If your toilet water level is too high, remove some water with a small bucket before attempting to plunge or add solutions to prevent overflow.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid flushing the toilet repeatedly if it's clogged, as this can cause it to overflow.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different chemical cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can effectively fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water, or by creating a fizzing reaction with baking soda and white vinegar. For more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger or snake can also be used.

**What's the best way to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by trying the plunger method. If that doesn't work, use the dish soap and hot water method to help break down the organic matter. For persistent clogs, a toilet auger is highly effective at clearing solid waste. Always wear gloves and ensure good ventilation.

**How do I know if my toilet clog is serious?**

A clog might be serious if the water level doesn't go down after trying several DIY methods, or if multiple drains in your home (like sinks or showers) are also backing up. These signs often indicate a blockage deeper in your main sewer line, requiring professional help.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners for clogged toilets. They can be corrosive, potentially damage your pipes, and often don't fully clear the clog. Furthermore, if the cleaner doesn't work, you'll have a bowl full of hazardous chemicals that are difficult to remove safely.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wet wipes (even 'flushable' ones), feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider using a wastebasket for non-flushable items.
