Fixing a clogged toilet usually involves simple DIY methods like plunging or using a dish soap and hot water solution. Most minor clogs can be resolved quickly without professional help, restoring your toilet's flush and preventing inconvenient backups.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Flange plunger
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Dish soap
- Old towels or newspaper
- Toilet auger (snake) - for tougher clogs
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Prepare the Area

If the toilet bowl is filling up, do not flush again, as this can cause an overflow. Immediately remove the tank lid and close the flapper valve to stop water from entering the bowl. Lay old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your bathroom floor from potential splashes or overflows.

### Step 2: Employ a Flange Plunger Effectively

Position your flange plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal around the opening. Plunge vigorously with a strong up-and-down motion for about 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal. The goal is to create suction and pressure to dislodge the clog. After plunging, quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and check if the water drains. You may need to repeat this process several times. For more detailed steps, refer to our [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps) guide.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use a flange plunger, which has a rubber flap that extends to create a better seal in the toilet's drain.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or isn't an option, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl, allowing the water and soap to sit for 20-30 minutes. The soap acts as a lubricant, and the hot water helps break down the clog, making it easier to flush away.

### Step 4: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For clogs that resist a plunger or soap, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your next best tool. Gently feed the auger's coiled cable into the drain opening, turning the handle clockwise to extend it. Continue feeding and turning until you feel resistance, indicating the clog. Work the auger back and forth to break up or hook the obstruction, then slowly retract it. This method is highly effective for deeper or more solid clogs.

### Step 5: Perform a Test Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, carefully flush the toilet to test the drainage. If the water flows freely, the clog is gone. Thoroughly clean your plunger, auger, and any splashed areas with a disinfectant. Dispose of any towels or newspaper used to protect the floor, and wash your hands thoroughly.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the toilet still seems sluggish after the first flush, give it a few minutes before trying another flush.

### Step 6: Know When to Call a Professional Plumber

If none of these DIY methods resolve the clog, or if you experience frequent clogs, it might indicate a more significant issue within your plumbing system. In such cases, it's best to contact a professional plumber. They have specialized equipment and expertise to diagnose and fix complex problems, ensuring your plumbing system functions correctly. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves when dealing with a clogged toilet for hygiene and protection.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners as they can damage pipes, harm your skin, and release toxic fumes.
- Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, hair, or feminine hygiene products.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet multiple times if it appears to be overflowing, as this will only worsen the situation.
- ⚠️ Avoid pouring boiling water into your toilet, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ If using a toilet auger, be gentle to avoid scratching or damaging the toilet's porcelain surface.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

You can fix a clogged toilet using a few common DIY methods: try plunging with a flange plunger, pouring dish soap followed by hot water into the bowl, or using a toilet auger for more stubborn obstructions. Always ensure the water level isn't too high before attempting to clear the clog.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

To fix a clogged toilet without a plunger, you can use the dish soap and hot water method. Pour half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate and the water to break down the clog.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by using a flange plunger with firm, consistent strokes to create suction and dislodge the organic matter. If plunging isn't effective, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the waste, making it easier to flush away.

**What should I do if my toilet is clogged and overflowing?**

If your toilet is clogged and overflowing, immediately remove the tank lid and close the flapper valve to stop the water flow. Use a bucket to carefully remove some water from the bowl to prevent further spillage, then proceed with plunging or other unclogging methods.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar can create a fizzing reaction, they are generally not powerful enough to clear significant toilet clogs. They are more effective for minor drain maintenance or deodorizing. For actual clogs, a plunger, dish soap, or an auger will be more effective.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your DIY efforts with a plunger, dish soap, or toilet auger fail to clear the clog. Also, if you experience frequent clogs, hear gurgling from other drains, or suspect a main sewer line issue, it's time to contact a professional.
