To fix a clogged toilet, first stop flushing to prevent overflow. Most clogs can be resolved using a plunger with proper technique, or by trying a combination of dish soap and hot water. For particularly stubborn blockages, a toilet auger can often clear the obstruction effectively.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Plunger (flange type recommended)
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Stop Flushing

Immediately stop flushing the toilet if it's not draining. Repeated flushing will only cause the bowl to overflow, creating a bigger mess on your bathroom floor. If the water level is high, carefully remove some water with a small cup and a bucket to prevent an overflow when you begin to address the clog.

### Step 2: Protect Your Bathroom Floor

Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. This is especially important if the clog is particularly stubborn or messy. Wearing rubber gloves is also highly recommended for hygiene and to protect your hands.

### Step 3: Plunge with Proper Technique

Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal around the opening. Plunge with firm, consistent pushes and pulls for about 15-20 seconds without breaking the seal. On the last pull, break the seal quickly to create a strong suction effect. Repeat this process several times if necessary until the water starts to drain.

### Step 4: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water (No Plunger Alternative)

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger available, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water can aid in breaking down organic matter, allowing it to slide through.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For clogs that resist plunging or the soap-and-water method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your next best tool. Gently insert the auger's coiled cable into the drain, cranking the handle to feed it through and either break up or retrieve the obstruction. Be careful to avoid scratching the toilet's porcelain bowl.

### Step 6: Test the Toilet and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet to confirm it drains normally. If it does, success! If not, you may need to repeat the process or consider professional help. After the clog is resolved, thoroughly clean your toilet and the surrounding area with disinfectant to ensure hygiene.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Clogs

To avoid future clogs, be mindful of what gets flushed down the toilet. Only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper, as these are common culprits for blockages.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger for toilets; its specific shape creates a better seal for effective plunging than a standard cup plunger.
- When using the dish soap method, ensure the water is hot but never boiling, as boiling water can potentially crack the toilet's porcelain.
- If you suspect a deeper clog beyond the toilet's trap, or if multiple drains in your home are backing up, it might indicate a main line issue requiring professional plumbing assistance.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, corrode toilet components, and release harmful fumes, posing a significant safety risk.
- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet repeatedly if it is already full or close to overflowing, as this will inevitably cause an overflow and a larger mess.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can try the dish soap and hot water method. Pour half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate the clog and help it slide down.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

If the clog involves human waste, the plunger method is usually the most effective first step. Ensure a good seal and plunge firmly and consistently. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down organic matter, making it easier to flush.

**Why does my toilet keep getting clogged?**

Frequent clogs often result from flushing non-flushable items like 'flushable' wipes, feminine hygiene products, or excessive toilet paper. It could also indicate an issue with your toilet's flushing mechanism or a deeper problem in your home's plumbing system.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all the common DIY methods (plunging, dish soap, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, if multiple toilets and drains in your home are backing up, it could signal a main sewer line issue that requires professional intervention.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar create a fizzing reaction, they are generally not powerful enough to clear significant toilet clogs. They are more effective for minor drain maintenance or deodorizing than for breaking up solid obstructions in a toilet.

**What should I avoid flushing to prevent clogs?**

To prevent clogs, never flush 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, hair, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Only human waste and toilet paper should ever go down the toilet.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

No, it is not safe or recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can damage the toilet's porcelain, corrode pipes, and release toxic fumes. They are rarely effective for toilet clogs and pose significant health and safety risks.
