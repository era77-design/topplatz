Fixing a clogged toilet often requires just a few simple tools and techniques. Most minor clogs can be resolved with a plunger or household items, restoring proper flushing function quickly. If water is overflowing, immediately turn off the water supply to prevent further mess.

## What You Need

- Plunger (flange-style)
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda (optional)
- White vinegar (optional)
- Toilet snake/auger (optional)
- Eye protection (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

First, check the water level in the toilet bowl. If it looks like it will overflow, immediately lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl, or turn the water supply valve off at the wall behind the toilet. Lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to catch any potential spills and put on rubber gloves for hygiene.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Effectively

Position a good quality flange-style toilet plunger directly over the drain hole, ensuring you create a tight seal. Push down gently at first to expel any air, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion, maintaining the seal throughout. After about 10-15 strong plunges, pull the plunger up sharply to break the seal and observe if the water drains. Repeat this process a few times if the clog persists.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't clear the clog, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let the soap sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to lubricate the clog. Next, carefully pour approximately one gallon of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl from waist height. The combination of the hot water and slippery soap can help break down and push the obstruction through the pipes. Wait 20-30 minutes before attempting to flush.

### Step 4: Employ Baking Soda and Vinegar

For more stubborn clogs, a natural chemical reaction can sometimes help. Pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz, and this effervescence can help dislodge softer clogs. Allow this concoction to work for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for best results, before attempting to flush the toilet.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Snake or Auger

If household remedies fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger) is a specialized tool designed to clear clogs without damaging the toilet's porcelain. Insert the auger's flexible end into the drain opening, crank the handle to feed the cable into the pipe, and push it through any resistance. Once you feel the clog, rotate the auger to break it up or hook onto it for retrieval.

### Step 6: Check the Vent Pipe (Advanced)

In rare cases, a clogged vent pipe on your roof can cause flushing problems that mimic a toilet clog. If all other methods have failed and you're comfortable and safe accessing your roof, you might inspect the vent pipe for obstructions like leaves or bird nests. This is a more advanced step and often requires professional assistance if you're unsure or uncomfortable.

### Step 7: When to Call a Professional Plumber

If you've diligently tried all the above methods and your toilet remains stubbornly clogged, or if you suspect a deeper issue within your home's plumbing system, it's time to call a licensed professional plumber. They possess specialized diagnostic tools and expertise to handle complex clogs, main line blockages, or other significant plumbing problems efficiently and safely.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a specific toilet plunger, which has a flange at the bottom, for the best seal and most effective plunging action.
- Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners in toilets, as they can damage pipes, harm the environment, and are often ineffective against common toilet clogs.
- To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Never flush 'flushable' wipes, feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, dental floss, or excessive amounts of paper.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water to clear a toilet clog, as the extreme temperature change can cause the porcelain bowl to crack, leading to costly damage and potential injury.
- ⚠️ Avoid mixing different chemical cleaners together, as this can produce dangerous fumes and reactions that are harmful to your health.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then slowly add a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. The soap lubricates the clog, and the hot water helps break it down and push it through.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

Fixing a clogged toilet with poop involves the same methods as other clogs. Start with a plunger for mechanical force. If that doesn't work, try the dish soap and hot water method, as the soap can help break down organic material and lubricate its passage, making it easier to clear.

**What is the fastest way to unclog a toilet?**

The fastest way to unclog a toilet is typically with a good-quality plunger. Ensure you create a tight seal over the drain and plunge vigorously for 10-15 cycles. This mechanical force often dislodges common obstructions quickly and effectively.

**Can a toilet unclog itself overnight?**

Yes, sometimes a minor clog, especially one involving organic material and toilet paper, can unclog itself overnight. The water in the bowl can slowly soften and break down the obstruction, allowing it to pass through the drain eventually. It's often worth letting it sit if the water level isn't too high.

**What do you do if your toilet won't flush at all?**

If your toilet won't flush at all, first check the flapper and chain inside the tank to ensure they are connected and moving correctly. If the tank fills but the bowl doesn't drain, it's a clog. If the tank doesn't fill with water, check the water supply valve behind the toilet and the fill valve mechanism inside the tank.

**Is it okay to leave a clogged toilet overnight?**

Yes, it's generally safe and sometimes beneficial to leave a clogged toilet overnight, especially if the water level isn't threatening to overflow. This allows the clog more time to soften or dissolve naturally. Just ensure no one attempts to flush it again, which could cause an overflow.

**How much dish soap do you put in a clogged toilet?**

For a clogged toilet, you typically use about half a cup of liquid dish soap. Pour it directly into the toilet bowl and allow it to sit for 10-15 minutes before adding hot water. This amount is usually sufficient to help lubricate and soften most common clogs.
