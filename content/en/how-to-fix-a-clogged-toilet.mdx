To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow to prevent any overflow. Then, use a flange plunger with firm, consistent thrusts to dislodge the blockage from the drain. For more stubborn clogs, a combination of hot water and dish soap or a toilet auger can often clear the obstruction successfully.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Flange plunger
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (snake)
- Baking soda (optional)
- White vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prevent Overflow and Prepare Your Area

Immediately stop the water flow to prevent the toilet bowl from overflowing. You can do this by lifting the tank lid and pushing down the flapper, or by turning the shut-off valve located on the wall behind the toilet. Lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and protect your floor.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always wear rubber gloves when dealing with clogged toilets for hygiene and safety.

### Step 2: Position Your Plunger Correctly

Ensure you have a good quality flange plunger, which has an extended rubber flap designed to create a tight seal in the toilet bowl drain. Place the plunger over the drain hole, making sure it completely covers the opening. You want to create an airtight seal for maximum effectiveness.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water level is too low for a good seal, add some water to the bowl to cover the plunger's head.

### Step 3: Plunge with Force and Consistency

With the plunger firmly sealed over the drain, push down and pull up with strong, swift motions. Focus on creating a vacuum and then releasing it to dislodge the clog. Repeat this plunging action 10-15 times, then quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and see if the water drains. If it doesn't, repeat the process.

> 💡 **Tip:** The first few plunges might be gentle to expel air, then increase the force.

### Step 4: Try the Hot Water and Soap Method

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the hot water helps dissolve the clog while the soap acts as a lubricant, making it easier for the blockage to slide down the pipe. After waiting, try flushing.

> 💡 **Tip:** For an extra boost, add one cup of baking soda followed by two cups of white vinegar after the dish soap, let it fizz for 30 minutes, then add hot water.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For clogs that resist plunging and the soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is often the solution. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain opening, feeding the cable down until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to push through or hook the obstruction, then slowly pull the auger back out, clearing the clog. For more detailed steps, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use a toilet-specific auger to avoid scratching the porcelain bowl.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once the clog appears to be cleared, flush the toilet to ensure the water drains properly. If it flushes normally, you've succeeded. Clean any spills with your old towels, disinfect the plunger, and wash your hands thoroughly. Dispose of any collected debris appropriately.

> 💡 **Tip:** Flush with caution initially, having a bucket ready in case of a slow drain.

### Step 7: Know When to Call a Professional

If you've tried all the above methods and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged, it might indicate a more serious issue deeper in your plumbing system. This could include a main sewer line blockage or a vent pipe problem. In such cases, it's best to call a professional plumber to diagnose and fix the issue.

> 💡 **Tip:** Persistent or recurring clogs often signal a larger plumbing problem that requires expert attention.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets, as it creates a better seal than a cup plunger.
- Never pour chemical drain cleaners into your toilet; they can damage pipes and are ineffective against most toilet clogs.
- Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, or feminine hygiene products.
- Ventilate the bathroom well while working to disperse any odors.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water to unclog a toilet, as it can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not repeatedly flush a clogged toilet, as this will inevitably lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves and eye protection to protect yourself from unsanitary water and splashes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a clogged toilet quickly?**

To quickly fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow to prevent overflow. Then, use a flange plunger with strong, consistent thrusts to dislodge the blockage. If this doesn't work immediately, try the hot water and dish soap method.

**How can I fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

To fix a clogged toilet without a plunger, try pouring about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of very hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to dissolve and lubricate the clog, then attempt to flush. You can also use a toilet auger.

**What should I do if my toilet is clogged with poop?**

If your toilet is clogged with poop, first try plunging vigorously with a flange plunger. If that doesn't work, the hot water and dish soap method can help break down the organic matter. For very stubborn clogs of this nature, a toilet auger is often the most effective tool to clear the blockage.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaner for a clogged toilet?**

No, it's not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They are often ineffective against toilet clogs, can damage your pipes, and leave hazardous chemicals sitting in your toilet bowl if the clog isn't cleared.

**Is it safe to use hot water to unclog a toilet?**

Yes, it is generally safe to use hot tap water to unclog a toilet, but never use boiling water. Boiling water can cause the porcelain to crack due to extreme temperature changes, leading to costly damage. Stick to very hot tap water.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all common DIY methods (plunging, soap and water, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Persistent or recurring clogs, or issues affecting multiple drains in your home, often indicate a deeper problem requiring professional attention.

**How do I prevent my toilet from getting clogged?**

Prevent toilet clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Regularly clean your toilet to maintain good flow.
