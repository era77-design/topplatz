Fixing a clogged toilet often involves using a plunger to create suction and dislodge the blockage. For stubborn clogs, you might need to try a drain snake or a mixture of hot water and dish soap. Always stop flushing to prevent overflow.

## What You Need

- Plunger (flange style)
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Drain snake/auger (optional)
- Eye protection (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prevent Overflow

Do not flush the toilet again if the water level is high; this will only cause it to overflow. Quickly lift the tank lid and push down on the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from potential splashes and spills.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always wear rubber gloves to maintain hygiene while working on a clogged toilet.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Correctly

Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring you create a tight seal. Push down gently to release any trapped air, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion for 10-20 seconds. Maintain the seal throughout the process to maximize suction and dislodge the blockage.

> 💡 **Tip:** A flange-style plunger creates a better seal for toilet drains than a standard cup-style plunger, making it more effective.

### Step 3: Try Hot Water and Dish Soap (No Plunger Method)

If a plunger isn't available or hasn't worked, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, pouring it from waist height to add some force. Let the mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water softens it. You can find more alternative methods in [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: DIY Solutions & Easy Steps](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Pouring the hot water too quickly or using boiling water can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.

### Step 4: Employ a Drain Snake or Auger for Deeper Clogs

For stubborn clogs that are deeper in the pipe, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or drain snake) is an effective tool. Insert the auger's coiled end into the drain, gently push until you meet resistance, then crank the handle to break up or retrieve the blockage. Be careful not to scratch the porcelain.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always use a toilet-specific auger, as a standard drain snake can damage your toilet bowl's finish.

### Step 5: Flush and Check for Flow

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally and the bowl refills correctly, the clog is resolved. If it still drains slowly or appears to be backing up, you may need to repeat the process or try a different method. For more easy steps, check out [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water level rises too high again, immediately stop the flush by pressing the flapper down in the tank.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Sanitize

After successfully clearing the clog, thoroughly clean the toilet bowl, seat, and surrounding floor area with a disinfectant cleaner. Properly dispose of any soiled towels or newspaper. Finally, wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water to ensure good hygiene.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using disposable gloves and wiping down all touched surfaces with a disinfectant spray.


## Tips & Tricks

- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners in toilets, as they can damage pipes, harm your toilet's porcelain, and are often ineffective on toilet clogs.
- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs. Items like wipes (even 'flushable' ones), paper towels, and hygiene products should go in the trash.
- If the toilet still won't clear after multiple attempts with various methods, it might indicate a more serious main line clog, which typically requires the expertise of a professional plumber.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water to unclog a toilet, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid excessive force when plunging or using a snake, as this can damage the toilet fixture or the plumbing pipes.
- ⚠️ If sewage is backing up into other drains in your home (e.g., shower or sink), contact a professional plumber immediately, as this indicates a main sewer line blockage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can try pouring about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to soften and lubricate the clog, then try flushing. The combination often helps dislodge minor blockages.

**What is the fastest way to unclog a toilet?**

The fastest and most common way to unclog a toilet is by using a good quality plunger. Ensure a tight seal over the drain and plunge vigorously for 10-20 seconds. This creates suction and pressure to quickly dislodge most clogs.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

If the toilet is clogged with poop, first try plunging. The plunger creates suction to dislodge the material and push it through. If plunging isn't effective, a mixture of hot water and dish soap can help break down the organic matter, making it easier to clear.

**Can a clogged toilet fix itself overnight?**

Sometimes, minor clogs, especially those caused by excessive toilet paper, can soften and dissolve over several hours, allowing the water to drain naturally. However, this is not guaranteed for all clogs, especially those caused by non-dissolvable items.

**What should you do if your toilet is clogged and overflowing?**

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down on the flapper to stop water from filling the bowl and prevent further overflow. Then, use towels to contain any existing overflow and begin addressing the clog with a plunger or other methods.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods without success, if the clog is recurring frequently, or if you suspect a deeper issue like a main sewer line blockage affecting multiple drains in your home.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in toilets. They can be corrosive, potentially damage your pipes and toilet's porcelain, and are often ineffective on severe toilet clogs, sometimes making the situation worse.
