A clogged toilet can be a frustrating problem, but many common clogs can be fixed with simple DIY methods. Before calling a plumber, try these straightforward steps to clear your toilet and restore its function. ## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (snake) (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Assess the Situation

Immediately stop flushing the toilet if it's not draining. Repeated flushing can cause the bowl to overflow, creating a bigger mess. Observe the water level; if it's rising, lift the tank lid and close the flapper valve to prevent further water from entering the bowl. This initial assessment helps prevent spills and prepares you for the next steps.

### Step 2: Protect Your Bathroom Floor

Before you begin, lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet. This will help absorb any splashes or overflows that might occur during the clearing process. Wearing rubber gloves is also highly recommended for hygiene and to protect your hands from dirty water.

### Step 3: Plunge with Purpose

Position your plunger over the toilet drain, ensuring a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up and push down vigorously for 15-20 seconds. Maintain the seal as much as possible. Repeat this process several times; the suction and pressure are often enough to dislodge most common clogs. For effective plunging techniques, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured from waist height to add some force. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water helps break it down, allowing it to slide through the pipes.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is an effective tool. Insert the auger's coiled wire into the drain, pushing it gently until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to extend the cable and rotate it, allowing the auger to break up or hook onto the clog. Pull the auger back slowly, retrieving the obstruction if possible.

### Step 6: Flush and Inspect

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, the clog is gone. If it still drains slowly or appears to be backing up, you may need to repeat a previous step or consider the clog might be further down the main drain line.

### Step 7: Clean Up Thoroughly

After successfully clearing the clog, it's important to clean and disinfect the toilet bowl, seat, and any surrounding areas that may have been splashed. Dispose of dirty rags and gloves properly. A clean bathroom helps prevent the spread of germs and makes the whole experience feel resolved.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger designed specifically for toilets; its shape provides a better seal.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet, as they can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and are dangerous if they splash back.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Be mindful of excessive toilet paper use.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid over-flushing a clogged toilet, as this can cause it to overflow.
- ⚠️ If using a toilet auger, be gentle to avoid scratching the porcelain of the toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ If the toilet remains clogged after multiple attempts, or if you suspect a main sewer line issue, it's best to call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do first if my toilet is clogged?**

The very first step is to stop flushing immediately to prevent an overflow. Then, assess the water level in the bowl and, if it's rising, close the flapper in the tank to stop more water from entering. Prepare your area with towels and gloves.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

Clogs involving waste material can often be cleared with a good plunger technique. Ensure a tight seal and plunge vigorously. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down organic matter, or a toilet auger can physically dislodge it.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your DIY attempts with a plunger, soap and water, or an auger are unsuccessful. Also, if multiple toilets or drains in your home are backing up, it suggests a larger issue in your main sewer line that requires professional attention.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet?**

No, it is strongly advised not to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can be corrosive, damage your pipes, harm septic systems, and pose a safety risk if they splash back. Stick to mechanical methods or natural solutions.

**What's the best type of plunger to use for a toilet?**

The best type of plunger for a toilet is a flange plunger. It has an extended rubber flap that creates a much better seal over the toilet drain opening compared to a standard cup plunger, allowing for more effective suction and pressure.

**How can I prevent toilet clogs in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like wet wipes (even if labeled 'flushable'), cotton balls, dental floss, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider regular maintenance flushes with hot water.
