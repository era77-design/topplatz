A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but many common clogs can be fixed with simple DIY methods. You can often resolve the issue using a plunger, or by employing household items like dish soap and hot water. Knowing these techniques can save you time and the cost of a plumber.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (optional)
- All-purpose cleaner and disinfectant

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Overflow & Protect the Area

If the water level in the toilet bowl is high and rising, do not flush again. Quickly lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl. Lay old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any potential spills and put on your rubber gloves.

### Step 2: Plunge Away the Clog

Position your plunger over the toilet drain, ensuring it forms a tight seal. Plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion for 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal. Break the seal to check if the water drains; if not, repeat the plunging process several times until the water starts to recede.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If a plunger isn't available or didn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with 1-2 gallons of hot (but not boiling) water. Let the mixture sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water's weight can help push it through.

### Step 4: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For persistent clogs that resist plunging or the soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) can be effective. Gently insert the auger head into the drain, turning the handle as you push it through the curves of the toilet trap. When you feel resistance, rotate and push to break up or retrieve the obstruction. Slowly pull the auger back out.

### Step 5: Flush and Confirm

Once you believe the clog is clear, carefully flush the toilet. If the water drains normally and the bowl refills correctly, the clog is gone. You might need to flush a second time to ensure all remnants of the blockage have passed through the pipes. If it clogs again, reassess and repeat the appropriate steps.

### Step 6: Clean Up Thoroughly

Remove and dispose of any used towels or newspaper. Clean the toilet bowl, seat, and surrounding floor area with an all-purpose cleaner and disinfectant. Ensure all tools, especially the plunger or auger, are cleaned before storage. Finally, wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water after removing your gloves.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger, which is designed to create a better seal for toilet drains compared to cup plungers.
- Avoid chemical drain cleaners for toilets; they are often ineffective on large clogs, can damage pipes, and pose safety risks.
- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- For persistent or recurring clogs, especially if multiple drains are affected, it's best to call a professional plumber.
- Educate household members on what can and cannot be flushed down the toilet.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not mix different chemical cleaners, as this can create dangerous and harmful fumes.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water, as the extreme temperature can crack the porcelain bowl of your toilet.
- ⚠️ Do not force a toilet auger, as aggressive use can scratch or damage the toilet bowl's ceramic surface.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by 1-2 gallons of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes before trying to flush.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

A clogged toilet with poop often responds well to a plunger; ensure a good seal and plunge vigorously. If a plunger isn't available, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the organic matter and lubricate the blockage for it to pass.

**What causes a toilet to clog?**

Toilets commonly clog due to flushing non-flushable items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, excessive amounts of toilet paper, or feminine hygiene products. Sometimes, a blockage deeper in the drain line or an issue with the toilet's internal mechanisms can also be the cause.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaner to fix a clogged toilet?**

It's generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners for clogged toilets. They can be ineffective on large clogs, potentially damage your pipes, and pose a significant safety risk if they splash back or create dangerous fumes.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your toilet remains stubbornly clogged after trying several DIY methods, if you suspect a deeper blockage in your main drain line, or if multiple toilets or drains in your home are backing up simultaneously, indicating a larger plumbing issue.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like cotton balls, dental floss, hair, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Regularly cleaning your toilet can also help prevent mineral buildup that might contribute to blockages.
