To fix a clogged toilet, first assess the severity of the clog and ensure the water level isn't rising to overflow. Often, a simple plunger can resolve the issue by creating suction to dislodge the blockage. For stubborn clogs, household solutions like dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger, can be effective in restoring normal function.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Plunger (flange-style)
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

Before you begin, put on rubber gloves and lay old towels around the base of the toilet to catch any spills. If the toilet bowl is full and looks like it might overflow, do not flush again. Instead, lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl, then manually remove some water with a small bucket or cup to prevent overflow.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always have a bucket nearby to remove excess water if the bowl is too full to plunge safely without overflowing.

### Step 2: Try the Plunger Method First

Position a flange-style plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Plunge with strong, consistent pushes and pulls for about 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal. The goal is to create a vacuum effect that dislodges the clog. After several plunges, quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and check if the water drains. You may need to repeat this process a few times.

> 💡 **Tip:** For best results, ensure there's enough water in the bowl to cover the rubber bell of the plunger, helping to create a stronger seal and better suction.

### Step 3: Use Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured slowly from waist height. The hot water helps to break down the clog, while the dish soap acts as a lubricant, allowing the blockage to slide through. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, or even a few hours for stubborn clogs, then try flushing.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid using boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain bowl or damage the wax seal at the toilet's base. Very hot tap water is sufficient.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For more stubborn clogs that are deeper in the drainpipe, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is a highly effective tool. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain opening, feeding the cable down while cranking the handle. The auger's tip will break up or hook onto the obstruction. Once you feel resistance, retract the auger, hopefully bringing the clog with it. For a detailed guide on using this tool, see [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy).

> 💡 **Tip:** Toilet augers are designed with a protective sleeve to prevent scratching the porcelain of your toilet bowl, making them a safe choice for mechanical clog removal.

### Step 5: Consider a Wet/Dry Vacuum (for Severe Clogs)

If other methods fail and you have a wet/dry vacuum, it can be used to suction out the clog. First, remove all water from the toilet bowl. Then, position the vacuum hose over the drain opening, creating as tight a seal as possible (you might need an old towel to help). Turn on the vacuum to suction out the clog. This method is particularly useful for objects that have been accidentally flushed.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure your vacuum is specifically a wet/dry model; attempting this with a standard dry-only vacuum can damage the appliance and create an electrical hazard.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, the clog is resolved. If it still drains slowly or appears to be backing up, you may need to repeat a method or try a different one. After the toilet is functional, thoroughly clean your tools and the surrounding area with disinfectant.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water level rises significantly after flushing, immediately stop the flush by pushing down the flapper in the tank to prevent an overflow.


## Tips & Tricks

- Prevent future clogs by only flushing toilet paper and human waste. Avoid flushing wipes (even 'flushable' ones), feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, or excessive amounts of toilet paper.
- Keep a small trash can next to the toilet for items that should not be flushed, making it easier for everyone in the household to dispose of them properly.
- For toilets that frequently clog, consider installing a low-flow toilet or having a plumber inspect the drain lines for underlying issues like tree root intrusion or pipe damage.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can be corrosive to pipes, harmful to septic systems, and dangerous if they splash back.
- ⚠️ Do not repeatedly flush a clogged toilet if the water level is high, as this will almost certainly lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ If hot water and dish soap don't work, avoid pouring more and more hot water. Excessive heat can damage your toilet's wax ring or even crack the porcelain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes before attempting to flush. For deeper clogs, a toilet auger is an effective tool.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by using a plunger vigorously to try and dislodge the organic matter. If plunging doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the waste, making it easier to flush. For stubborn clogs involving solid waste, a toilet auger is often the most effective tool to push or pull the blockage through the drain.

**What causes a toilet to clog?**

Toilets commonly clog due to flushing non-flushable items like 'flushable' wipes, feminine hygiene products, paper towels, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Other causes can include a partially blocked drain line, a weak flush mechanism, or issues with the toilet's internal trap.

**How do I know if my toilet is severely clogged?**

A severely clogged toilet will typically have water that doesn't drain at all, or drains extremely slowly, often rising to the brim of the bowl. If multiple drains in your home are slow or backing up, it suggests a main sewer line clog, which is more severe than a simple toilet blockage.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your toilet remains clogged after trying several DIY methods, especially if you've used a toilet auger without success. Also, if you experience frequent clogs, notice sewage odors, or if multiple drains in your home are backing up, it's a sign of a deeper plumbing issue requiring professional attention.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to fix a clogged toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar can create a fizzing reaction, they are generally not powerful enough to clear a significant toilet clog. They are more effective for minor drain maintenance or odor removal. For actual blockages, a plunger, dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger are more reliable solutions.
