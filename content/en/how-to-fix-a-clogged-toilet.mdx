To fix a clogged toilet, first turn off the water supply to prevent overflow. Next, use a flange plunger with firm, consistent pressure to dislodge the clog. If a plunger isn't available, a combination of dish soap and hot water can often help break down the blockage.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Flange plunger (toilet plunger)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet brush (for cleaning)
- Bucket (optional, for scooping water)
- Toilet auger or drain snake (for stubborn clogs)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before attempting any fix, locate the shut-off valve, usually on the wall behind or near the base of the toilet. Turn it clockwise to stop the water flow into the tank. This is crucial to prevent an overflow if your efforts to clear the clog cause the bowl to fill further.

### Step 2: Prepare the Area

Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Ensure the bathroom is well-ventilated, especially if dealing with unpleasant odors.

### Step 3: Plunge with Purpose

Position a flange plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction. Repeat this motion 10-15 times with firm, consistent pressure. The goal is to create a vacuum that dislodges the clog without splashing water out of the bowl.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap & Hot Water Method

If a plunger isn't available or hasn't worked, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes; the soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water helps break it down, allowing it to slide through the pipes.

### Step 5: Utilize a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For stubborn clogs, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is highly effective. Insert the auger's flexible cable into the drain, pushing it gently until you feel resistance. Rotate the handle to allow the tip to bore through or hook the clog. Once the clog is cleared or retrieved, slowly pull the auger back out.

### Step 6: Test and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, turn the water supply valve back on. Flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, the clog is gone. Clean the toilet bowl, plunger, and any splashed areas thoroughly with disinfectant. Dispose of any soiled towels or newspaper properly.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Clogs

Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Items like wet wipes (even 'flushable' ones), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, and dental floss are common culprits for clogs. Encourage family members to be mindful of what goes into the toilet.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger, which has an extended rubber flap designed for a better seal in toilet drains.
- Be patient; some clogs require multiple attempts with a plunger or more time for the soap and water method to work.
- If using a toilet auger, be gentle to avoid scratching the porcelain bowl.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never flush a second time if the toilet bowl water is rising, as this will almost certainly cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water in your toilet, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes, harm the environment, and pose a health risk if they splash back.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog, then try flushing.

**What's the best way to fix a clogged toilet that has poop in it?**

The best approach for a toilet clogged with poop is typically to use a flange plunger. Ensure a tight seal and plunge with firm, consistent strokes. If plunging doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the organic matter, making it easier to clear.

**What should I do if my toilet bowl water is rising and might overflow?**

Immediately turn off the water supply to the toilet. The shut-off valve is usually located on the wall behind or near the base of the toilet. Turning it clockwise will stop water from filling the tank and bowl, preventing an overflow onto your bathroom floor.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It's generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners for toilets. They can be corrosive, potentially damaging your pipes or the toilet's porcelain, and are often ineffective against severe clogs. They also pose a safety risk due to fumes and skin contact.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a professional plumber if your toilet remains clogged after trying multiple DIY methods, if you suspect the clog is deep within your plumbing system, or if multiple drains in your home (like sinks or showers) are also slow or clogged. This could indicate a larger issue in your main sewer line.

**How can I prevent my toilet from getting clogged again?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider a wastebasket for non-flushable items near the toilet.
