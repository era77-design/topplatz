To fix a clogged toilet, start by assessing the water level and preventing overflow. Then, use a plunger with proper technique, or try alternative methods like dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger for more stubborn blockages. Always wear gloves and protect your bathroom floor before beginning.

## What You Need

- Flange-style plunger
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (closet snake)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Flow and Protect Your Floor

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from filling the bowl, preventing an overflow. Turn the water supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water completely. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your bathroom floor from any splashes or spills.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always wear rubber gloves when dealing with clogged toilets for hygiene.

### Step 2: Plunge with Purpose and Proper Technique

Position a flange-style plunger directly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction. Repeat this push-and-pull motion vigorously for 10-20 times. If the water starts to drain, flush the toilet once to clear the remaining clog. For more detailed instructions on effective plunging, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: DIY Solutions & Easy Steps](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** A proper plunger seal is crucial for effective plunging; ensure no air escapes around the edges.

### Step 3: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water for Lighter Clogs

If you don't have a plunger or suspect a minor clog, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes to lubricate the clog. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling!) water into the bowl from waist height. The combination of soap and hot water often helps break down and dislodge the blockage.

> 💡 **Tip:** Boiling water can crack porcelain, so always use hot tap water instead.

### Step 4: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Blockages

For clogs that resist plunging or the soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet snake) is your next best tool. Gently insert the auger's coiled cable into the toilet drain, feeding it until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to rotate the cable, allowing the tip to bore through or grab the obstruction. Slowly pull the auger back out, hopefully bringing the clog with it.

> 💡 **Tip:** Be gentle when inserting the auger to avoid scratching the toilet bowl's porcelain.

### Step 5: Baking Soda and Vinegar for Freshening and Minor Clogs

For lighter organic clogs or as a follow-up to other methods, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction helps to break down materials. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight, before flushing. This method is particularly useful for freshening drains and dissolving minor blockages.

> 💡 **Tip:** This method is less effective for large, solid obstructions but great for maintenance.

### Step 6: Know When to Call a Professional Plumber

If you've tried all the DIY methods and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged, or if multiple fixtures in your home are backing up, it's time to call a professional plumber. A persistent or recurring clog could indicate a more serious issue in your main drain line that requires specialized equipment and expertise.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't delay calling a plumber if you suspect a main line issue, as it can lead to bigger problems.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets, as it provides a better seal than cup plungers.
- Never use harsh chemical drain cleaners in toilets, as they can damage pipes and are hazardous.
- Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- Regularly clean your toilet and check for any slow draining signs to catch issues early.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can be corrosive to pipes, dangerous to handle, and harmful to the environment.
- ⚠️ If water is overflowing, immediately turn off the toilet's water supply valve to prevent water damage to your bathroom.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then slowly add a gallon of hot (not boiling) water to help dislodge the blockage.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, first try plunging vigorously with a flange-style plunger to break up and dislodge the material. If that doesn't work, a toilet auger can effectively reach and clear the blockage by either pushing through or grabbing the obstruction.

**What should I do if my toilet is overflowing?**

If your toilet is overflowing, immediately lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl. Then, turn the water supply valve located behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water completely, preventing further overflow and damage.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in toilets. They can be very harsh, potentially damaging your toilet's porcelain or pipes, and are hazardous to your health if they splash back. Stick to mechanical methods or natural solutions like baking soda and vinegar.

**How long should I wait after using dish soap and hot water?**

After pouring dish soap and hot water into a clogged toilet, it's best to wait at least 15-20 minutes. This allows the soap to lubricate the blockage and the hot water to help break it down before attempting to flush.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried all common DIY methods (plunging, auger, soap/water) and the toilet remains clogged, or if you notice multiple drains in your home are backing up. This could indicate a more serious issue in your main sewer line that requires professional attention.
