To fix a clogged toilet, start by assessing the severity of the blockage and gathering basic tools like a plunger. Most minor clogs can be resolved with simple DIY methods, preventing the need for a plumber. Always avoid flushing repeatedly once a clog is evident to prevent overflowing.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Plunger (flange type recommended)
- Liquid dish soap or shampoo
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Area and Assess the Clog

Before attempting to fix a clogged toilet, stop flushing immediately if the water level is rising to prevent an overflow. Lay down old towels or newspapers around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and protect your floor. Put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene and protect your hands from any contact with wastewater.

### Step 2: Plunge with Purpose

Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal to create maximum suction. Plunge vigorously with a strong, downward push, then pull up sharply to create a vacuum effect. Repeat this motion 10-15 times, maintaining the seal, then lift the plunger to see if the water drains. A flange-style plunger is most effective for toilets as it creates a better seal around the drain opening. For more general tips on clearing blockages, check out our [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Quick & Easy DIY Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

### Step 3: Try the Soap and Hot Water Trick

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap (or shampoo) into the toilet bowl. The soap helps lubricate the clog, making it easier to move. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl, allowing it to sit for 20-30 minutes. The combination can help break down and push the clog through the pipes.

### Step 4: Consider Baking Soda and Vinegar

For a natural alternative, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction created by these two ingredients can help loosen softer clogs and organic matter. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very stubborn blockages, before attempting to flush.

### Step 5: Tackle Stubborn Clogs with a Toilet Auger

If the previous methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your next best tool. Gently insert the auger's coiled wire into the drain, pushing it until you feel resistance, which indicates the clog. Rotate the handle to break up or hook the clog, then carefully pull the auger back out. For a comprehensive look at DIY solutions, refer to our [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy-guide).

### Step 6: Clear the Drain and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet once to confirm the water drains normally. If successful, thoroughly clean the area around the toilet, disposing of any soiled towels or newspaper. If the clog persists, you may need to repeat a previous step or consider professional help for more complex issues.


## Tips & Tricks

- Never use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet, as they can damage pipes or the porcelain and pose a significant safety risk.
- Prevent future clogs by only flushing toilet paper and human waste. Avoid flushing wipes (even 'flushable' ones), paper towels, or feminine hygiene products.
- Keep a flange-style plunger readily accessible in every bathroom for quick resolution of minor clogs.
- For persistent or recurring clogs, it might indicate a larger issue with your plumbing system, such as a main drain blockage, and professional help may be needed.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and release dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl. Always use hot tap water instead.
- ⚠️ If water levels remain high or rise after attempts to clear the clog, do not flush again to prevent overflowing and potential water damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water, or by creating a natural reaction with baking soda and vinegar. These methods help lubricate and break down organic clogs, allowing them to pass through the drain.

**What do you do if your toilet is clogged with poop?**

If your toilet is clogged with poop, first try using a plunger vigorously to break up and push the blockage through. If that doesn't work, a mix of dish soap and hot water can help lubricate and dissolve the waste. For more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger can effectively clear the obstruction.

**What is the best homemade solution for a clogged toilet?**

The best homemade solution for a clogged toilet typically involves pouring about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a cup of baking soda followed by two cups of white vinegar can create a fizzing action to loosen clogs.

**How long should I wait after pouring hot water and soap into a clogged toilet?**

After pouring hot water and dish soap into a clogged toilet, you should wait at least 20 to 30 minutes. This allows the soap to lubricate the clog and the hot water to help break it down. For very stubborn clogs, you might even let it sit for an hour or more before attempting to flush or plunge again.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber for a clogged toilet if you've tried multiple DIY methods without success, or if the clog is a recurring issue. Persistent clogs or multiple slow drains in your home can indicate a deeper problem in your main sewer line, which requires professional attention.

**Can I use boiling water to unclog a toilet?**

No, you should never use boiling water to unclog a toilet. The extreme temperature difference can cause the porcelain bowl to crack, leading to costly damage. Always use hot tap water instead, as it's warm enough to help break down clogs without posing a risk to your toilet's integrity.
