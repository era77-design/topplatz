To fix a clogged toilet, first assess the water level and avoid flushing again if it's high. Start by using a flange plunger with a good seal to dislodge the obstruction. If plunging isn't effective, hot water and dish soap can often help break down organic clogs.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Flange plunger
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger (snake) (optional)
- Baking soda and white vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess and Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, check the water level in the toilet bowl. If it's very high, do not flush again to prevent overflow, as this will only worsen the situation. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and drips. Put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene and protect your hands during the process.

### Step 2: Use a Flange Plunger Effectively

Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it forms a tight seal around the opening. Push down firmly to create suction, then pull up sharply to break the seal, repeating this vigorous motion 10-15 times. The goal is to create pressure changes that dislodge the clog. If the water starts to drain, flush carefully to see if the clog is cleared. For more detailed guidance, see [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Tips](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy).

### Step 3: Try Hot Water and Dish Soap

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of dish soap directly into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured from waist height to add some force. The hot water helps to soften the clog, while the dish soap acts as a lubricant, helping the blockage slide down the drain. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer for stubborn clogs, then try flushing carefully.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For persistent clogs that are deeper in the pipe or caused by non-flushable items, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger) is an effective tool. Insert the auger's coiled cable into the drain opening, feeding it gently until you feel resistance. Rotate the handle to allow the cable to bore through or hook the clog. Once you feel the obstruction break up or hook it, carefully retract the auger.

### Step 5: Consider Baking Soda and Vinegar for Minor Clogs

As an alternative for minor organic clogs, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction created by this mixture can help break down softer blockages. Let the mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or ideally overnight, before attempting to flush. This is a gentler, eco-friendly option compared to harsh chemical cleaners.

### Step 6: Test the Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet carefully. If the water drains freely, the problem is solved. If not, you may need to repeat a previous step or consider professional help. After successful unclogging, clean your tools and the surrounding area thoroughly with disinfectant to maintain hygiene.

### Step 7: Know When to Call a Professional Plumber

If you've tried all the DIY methods and the toilet remains clogged, or if you suspect a blockage deeper in your plumbing system, it's time to contact a professional plumber. Persistent or recurring clogs can indicate a more serious issue, such as a main sewer line problem, that requires expert diagnosis and specialized equipment.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger specifically designed for toilets, as they create a better seal than standard cup plungers for toilet drains.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes, harm your septic system or municipal water treatment, and pose safety risks.
- To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, dental floss, feminine hygiene products, or other non-biodegradable items.
- Keep a bucket nearby when attempting to unclog a toilet, especially if the water level is high, to quickly catch any potential overflow.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never flush the toilet repeatedly if the water level is rising, as this will almost certainly cause an overflow onto your bathroom floor.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water to unclog a toilet, as the extreme temperature difference can crack the porcelain bowl, leading to costly repairs.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can be corrosive to your plumbing, dangerous to handle, and harmful to the environment. They can also make a plumber's job more hazardous.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try pouring a mixture of hot (not boiling) water and about half a cup of dish soap into the bowl. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog. For deeper or more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger can be used effectively.

**What is the best way to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

For a toilet clogged with poop, the most effective first step is usually a flange plunger. The strong suction and pressure it creates can help break up and push the organic material through the drain. If plunging doesn't work, hot water and dish soap can further help dissolve the waste.

**What household items can you use to unclog a toilet?**

Common household items for unclogging a toilet include dish soap, hot water (not boiling), baking soda, and white vinegar. These can help break down organic clogs and lubricate the drain. A sturdy wire coat hanger, carefully straightened, can also be used for very shallow clogs, but an auger is safer for the porcelain.

**How long does it take for a clogged toilet to go down by itself?**

Minor organic clogs might eventually dissolve and go down on their own within a few hours, especially if hot water and dish soap are added to aid the process. However, more substantial clogs or those caused by non-flushable items will likely not clear by themselves and require manual intervention.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your DIY efforts with a plunger, hot water, dish soap, or an auger fail to clear the clog after several attempts. Also, if you experience frequent clogs, if multiple fixtures in your home are backed up, or if you suspect a main sewer line issue, professional help is necessary.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

Yes, baking soda and vinegar can be used for minor clogs. Pour one cup of baking soda into the bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction can help loosen and break down organic matter. Allow it to sit for at least 30 minutes, or ideally overnight, before attempting to flush.
