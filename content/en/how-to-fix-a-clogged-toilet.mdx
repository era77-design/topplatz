To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow to prevent overflow, then use a plunger with firm, consistent strokes. If plunging fails, try a mixture of dish soap and hot water to help break down the blockage.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (from tap, not boiling)
- Toilet snake or auger (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water Flow and Prepare

Before you begin, immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down on the flapper to stop water from filling the bowl if it's overflowing. If the bowl is already full and threatening to overflow, turn the small valve located on the wall behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water supply. Lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and protect your floor. Put on your rubber gloves to maintain hygiene.

### Step 2: Use a Plunger Correctly

Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Make sure the plunger cup is completely submerged in water to create effective suction. Push down firmly and then pull up sharply, repeating this motion 10-15 times without breaking the seal. The goal is to create pressure that dislodges the clog. After several plunges, break the seal and check if the water drains. You may need to repeat this process a few times.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, squirt about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. The soap acts as a lubricant, helping the clog slide down. Let the soap sit for 5-10 minutes. Then, slowly pour a gallon of hot (not boiling) tap water into the bowl from waist height to add force. Wait 20-30 minutes for the combination of soap and hot water to soften and break down the clog. The water should gradually start to drain.

### Step 4: Consider Baking Soda and Vinegar for Minor Clogs

For lighter clogs, you can try a natural alternative. Pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz, which can help break down softer blockages. Let this sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for best results. After the waiting period, flush the toilet to see if the clog has cleared. If not, you may need to try another method.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger or Snake for Stubborn Clogs

If basic methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is designed to clear deeper clogs without damaging the porcelain. Insert the coated end of the auger into the toilet drain. Crank the handle clockwise to feed the cable into the trap, pushing past the blockage. Once you feel resistance, rotate and push gently until the clog is broken up or hooked. Slowly pull the auger back out, removing any material it has caught. Flush the toilet to confirm the clog is cleared.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once the clog appears to be cleared, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, the problem is solved. If the water level rises too high, quickly shut off the water supply valve again to prevent overflow. Clean up any spills using your towels and disinfect the area, including your plunger and auger. Always ensure proper hygiene after dealing with a clogged toilet.

### Step 7: Know When to Call a Professional Plumber

If you've tried multiple methods and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged, or if you suspect a deeper issue within your plumbing system (like multiple drains backing up), it's time to call a professional plumber. Persistent clogs can indicate more serious problems that require specialized tools and expertise. Don't force the issue, as you could cause damage to your pipes or toilet.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves and have old towels ready to protect yourself and your bathroom from splashes and spills.
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners, as they can be corrosive, harmful to your pipes, and dangerous to handle.
- Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Keep wipes, paper towels, cotton swabs, and feminine hygiene products out of the toilet.
- Invest in a good quality flange plunger; they are specifically designed for toilets and create a much better seal than standard cup plungers.
- Consider regularly flushing your toilet with a gallon of hot water (not boiling) to help prevent grease and residue buildup.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water to clear a toilet clog, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not use harsh chemical drain cleaners. They can damage pipes, are dangerous if splashed, and can release toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If you notice multiple drains in your home are slow or clogged, this could indicate a main sewer line issue, which requires professional attention.
- ⚠️ Avoid flushing repeatedly if the toilet is not draining, as this will likely lead to an overflow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

You can fix a clogged toilet by first using a plunger with strong, consistent thrusts. If that doesn't work, try pouring dish soap and hot water into the bowl and letting it sit to break down the clog. For more stubborn blockages, a toilet auger or snake might be necessary.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

To fix a clogged toilet without a plunger, start by adding about half a cup of liquid dish soap to the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) tap water. Allow this mixture to sit for 20-30 minutes, giving the soap time to lubricate the clog. Alternatively, you can use a combination of baking soda and vinegar.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, the best first step is usually a plunger. Ensure a good seal and plunge vigorously to dislodge the material. If plunging isn't effective, try the dish soap and hot water method; the soap can help break down and lubricate the organic matter, allowing it to pass through the pipes.

**What causes a toilet to clog?**

Toilets typically clog when too much toilet paper is flushed at once, or when non-flushable items like wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or excessive waste are put down the drain. Sometimes, a deeper issue like tree roots in the sewer line or a faulty flapper can also cause clogs.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods (plunging, soap/water, auger) and the clog persists. Also, if you experience frequent clogs, hear gurgling from other drains, or notice sewage backing up into other fixtures, it indicates a more serious plumbing issue requiring professional expertise.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaner to fix a clogged toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet. These products can be corrosive, potentially damaging your toilet's porcelain and plumbing pipes. They are also dangerous to handle, can produce harmful fumes, and may not effectively clear the clog, leaving hazardous chemicals in standing water.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper (in moderation). Avoid flushing 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, or any other non-biodegradable items. Educate household members about proper toilet use, especially children, to maintain clear drains.
