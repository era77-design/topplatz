A clogged toilet can be a frustrating and messy problem, but often you can fix it yourself with a few simple tools and techniques. Before panicking, assess the situation and gather your supplies. Most clogs respond well to common household methods.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)
- Wire coat hanger (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing & Assess the Situation

Immediately stop flushing the toilet if it's not draining, as this can cause it to overflow and create a bigger mess. Observe the water level in the bowl; if it's high, avoid adding more liquid. Place old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from any potential splashes or overflow.

### Step 2: Prepare for Plunging

Put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Ensure there's enough water in the toilet bowl to cover the plunger's rubber cup. If the water level is too low, add some from a bucket. If it's too high and close to overflowing, carefully remove some water into a bucket to prevent spillage during plunging.

### Step 3: Plunge with Proper Technique

Place the plunger firmly over the drain hole, creating a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction. Repeat this push-and-pull motion 10-20 times, maintaining the seal. Listen for the sound of the clog clearing and observe if the water begins to drain. For more detailed steps on various solutions, you can also refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

### Step 4: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, squirt about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate the clog. Follow this by slowly pouring about a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl. The hot water helps break down the clog, and the soap helps it slide through. Wait 20-30 minutes for the combination to work, then check if the water level drops.

### Step 5: Use a Wire Hanger or Toilet Brush for Shallow Clogs

For clogs that seem to be close to the drain opening, you can carefully try to dislodge them. Unbend a wire coat hanger, leaving a small hook at one end. Gently push the hooked end into the drain, twisting and prodding to break up or pull out the obstruction. Alternatively, you can use a sturdy toilet brush to push and pull at the clog, being careful not to scratch the porcelain.

### Step 6: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake) for Stubborn Clogs

If the above methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is your next best option. This tool is designed to navigate the toilet's curves without scratching the porcelain. Insert the auger's end into the drain, crank the handle to extend the cable, and push it through the clog. Once you feel resistance, crank and push until the clog is broken up or retrieved.

### Step 7: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, carefully flush the toilet to ensure it drains properly. If the water drains, flush a second time. Thoroughly clean your plunger, auger, and any other tools used. Disinfect the toilet bowl, seat, and surrounding floor area to maintain hygiene.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger for toilets; its design creates a better seal than a cup plunger.
- Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- For persistent or recurring clogs, it might indicate a deeper plumbing issue, and it's best to call a professional plumber.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes and porcelain, and pose a health risk.
- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet repeatedly if it's clogged, as this can cause an overflow and potential water damage.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water, as extreme temperatures can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Squirt about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then slowly pour in a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Wait 20-30 minutes for the clog to dissolve or loosen.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

When a toilet is clogged with poop, the plunger is usually the most effective tool. The organic matter often responds well to the suction and pressure. If a plunger isn't available, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down the waste, making it easier to flush.

**What household items can I use to unclog a toilet?**

Common household items like dish soap, hot water (not boiling), and a wire coat hanger can be effective. You can also use a sturdy toilet brush to push and pull at a shallow clog. For tougher clogs, a toilet auger is a specialized but widely available tool.

**How long should I wait after trying a method before flushing again?**

After trying methods like dish soap and hot water, it's best to wait at least 20-30 minutes to give the solution time to work. For plunging, you can flush immediately after you feel the clog has cleared. If a method doesn't work after a reasonable waiting period, move on to the next solution.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods (plunger, soap/water, auger) and the toilet remains clogged. Also, if you have multiple drains backing up, or if the toilet frequently clogs, it indicates a more serious plumbing issue that requires professional attention.

**Can I use bleach to unclog a toilet?**

No, it is not recommended to use bleach to unclog a toilet. Bleach is not effective at breaking down clogs and can be dangerous if mixed with other cleaning agents or if it splashes onto your skin or eyes. Stick to safer and more effective methods like plungers, dish soap, or augers.
