To fix a clogged toilet, start by assessing the situation to prevent overflow and gather necessary tools. Often, a plunger can quickly resolve minor clogs, but more stubborn blockages may require alternative methods like dish soap, hot water, or a toilet auger. For a comprehensive guide on various solutions, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Prepare the Area

If the toilet bowl is full or appears to be overflowing, immediately stop flushing, as this will only worsen the situation. It's often helpful to lift the tank lid and close the flapper valve if water is still running into the bowl. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from potential splashes or spills, and put on your rubber gloves for hygiene.

### Step 2: Attempt to Plunge the Clog Effectively

Position your plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it forms a tight seal around the opening. Plunge vigorously with a firm push-pull motion for approximately 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal as much as possible. This creates hydraulic pressure that can dislodge the clog. If the water level begins to drop, the clog is likely moving. Remove the plunger and flush once to confirm the clog has cleared.

### Step 3: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water

If you don't have a plunger or plunging isn't working, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Allow it to sit for 10-15 minutes, giving the soap time to lubricate the blockage. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl. The hot water helps dissolve the clog, while the soap aids its passage. Wait another 20-30 minutes, then attempt to flush.

### Step 4: Create a Baking Soda and Vinegar Solution

For a natural and effective alternative, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl. Wait a few minutes for it to settle, then slowly add two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz and bubble, creating a reaction that can help break down organic matter causing the clog. Let this solution sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very stubborn clogs, before attempting to flush.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake) for Stubborn Clogs

If previous methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is your best tool for tough blockages. Carefully insert the auger's coiled end into the toilet drain opening, feeding it slowly until you encounter resistance. Rotate the handle to allow the auger to break up or hook onto the clog. Once you feel the clog give way, carefully retract the auger and flush to clear the drain.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Test the Flush

After successfully clearing the clog, flush the toilet once or twice to ensure the drain is completely clear and water flows freely without backing up. Thoroughly clean all tools used with disinfectant and store them appropriately. Wipe down any splashes around the toilet with a disinfectant cleaner to maintain a hygienic bathroom environment. You've successfully tackled the clogged toilet!


## Tips & Tricks

- Always keep a dedicated toilet plunger with a flange (the extended rubber lip) readily accessible, as it creates a more effective seal than basic cup plungers.
- Prevent future clogs by avoiding flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Even 'flushable' wipes can cause significant problems.
- Regularly clean your toilet to prevent mineral buildup and ensure the trap and drain remain clear.
- If you have a persistent clogging issue, consider checking your toilet's flushing mechanism or consulting a plumber, as it could indicate a deeper problem in your plumbing system.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water to clear a clog, as the extreme temperature can cause the porcelain of your toilet bowl to crack.
- ⚠️ Do not flush a second time if the toilet bowl is already full or looks like it will overflow, as this will result in water spilling onto your bathroom floor.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, are dangerous if splashed, and harmful to the environment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

To fix a clogged toilet, start by using a plunger with a good seal. If that doesn't work, try pouring a mix of dish soap and hot water, or baking soda and vinegar, into the bowl. For very stubborn clogs, a toilet auger is usually effective in breaking up or retrieving the obstruction.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by pouring about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, waiting 10-15 minutes, then slowly adding a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Another method involves combining one cup of baking soda with two cups of white vinegar, letting it sit, then flushing.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, first try a plunger, ensuring a strong seal and vigorous plunging motions. If that's not effective, pour a generous amount of dish soap into the bowl, wait 10-15 minutes, then add hot (not boiling) water to help lubricate and break down the waste. For persistent issues, a toilet auger can directly address the blockage.

**What causes a toilet to clog easily?**

Toilets often clog easily due to flushing non-biodegradable items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, or feminine hygiene products. Excessive amounts of toilet paper, older low-flow toilets with weaker flushing power, or existing blockages further down the drain line can also contribute to frequent clogging issues.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods without success, if your toilet frequently clogs, or if multiple drains in your home are backing up. These symptoms could indicate a more serious blockage in your main sewer line that requires professional tools and expertise.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

No, it is generally not safe or recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can be highly corrosive, potentially damaging your toilet's porcelain and plumbing pipes. Furthermore, the fumes can be hazardous, and the chemicals are harmful to the environment.
