Fixing a clogged toilet often requires just a few simple tools and techniques. Most minor clogs can be resolved with household items, saving you the cost of a plumber. Follow these straightforward steps to restore your toilet's flush.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Toilet plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Dish soap or shampoo
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (closet auger)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation & Prevent Overflow

First, check if the water level in the toilet bowl is rising. If it is, immediately lift the tank lid and push down on the flapper valve at the bottom of the tank to stop water from entering the bowl. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any potential spills and protect your flooring.

### Step 2: Try the Plunger Method First

A toilet plunger is often the most effective and quickest solution for minor clogs. Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring you create a tight seal. Plunge vigorously 10-20 times, maintaining the seal, then pull up sharply to create suction. Repeat this process a few times if necessary.

### Step 3: Use Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap (or shampoo) into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (but not boiling) water. The soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water aids in breaking it down. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes before attempting to flush again.

### Step 4: Apply Baking Soda and Vinegar

For more stubborn clogs, a natural chemical reaction can sometimes help. Add one cup of baking soda to the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction can help dislodge blockages by breaking down organic matter. Allow this mixture to sit for at least an hour, or even overnight, before flushing.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Closet Auger)

If other methods fail, a toilet auger, also known as a closet auger, is designed to clear clogs without damaging the toilet's porcelain. Extend the cable into the drain opening, crank the handle to push the cable through the clog, and then slowly retract it, hopefully pulling the obstruction with it. For more detailed instructions on using this tool, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Tips](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-diy).

### Step 6: Consider a Wet/Dry Shop Vacuum

If you own a wet/dry shop vacuum, it can be a powerful tool for removing clogs. Ensure the vacuum is set to 'wet' mode. Carefully remove all water from the toilet bowl, then create a tight seal around the drain opening with the vacuum hose before turning it on. The strong suction can often pull out the clog.

### Step 7: When to Call a Professional Plumber

If none of these DIY methods successfully clear the clog, or if you experience frequent clogs and slow drains across multiple fixtures, it's time to contact a professional plumber. Persistent issues can indicate a deeper problem within your main drain line or sewer system that requires specialized equipment and expertise.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger, which has an extended rubber flap, for the best seal in a toilet bowl.
- Never use harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes, porcelain, and are often ineffective against severe clogs.
- Prevent future clogs by only flushing human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing wipes (even 'flushable' ones), dental floss, cotton balls, or feminine hygiene products.
- If you have low-flow toilets, try flushing twice if needed, especially after heavy use, to ensure all waste clears the trap.
- Regularly clean your toilet and ensure the flapper in the tank is sealing properly to maintain good flushing power.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix different chemical cleaners, as this can create dangerous and harmful fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water to unclog a toilet, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain bowl or damage the wax seal at the base.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves throughout the unclogging process to protect yourself from germs and dirty water.
- ⚠️ If the toilet water is actively overflowing, immediately turn off the water supply valve located behind the toilet to prevent water damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water, or baking soda and vinegar, allowing them time to work on the clog. For more stubborn blockages, a toilet auger (closet auger) is an effective tool that doesn't require plunging.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

A clogged toilet with poop can often be cleared using the plunger method. If plunging doesn't work, try pouring a generous amount of dish soap and hot water into the bowl to help break down and lubricate the waste, then let it sit for 20-30 minutes before attempting to flush.

**What do you do if your toilet is clogged and the water is rising?**

Immediately lift the lid of the toilet tank and push down on the flapper valve at the bottom of the tank. This will stop more water from entering the bowl and prevent an overflow. Once the water flow is stopped, you can proceed with unclogging methods.

**Can I use boiling water to unclog a toilet?**

No, you should never use boiling water to unclog a toilet. The intense heat can cause the porcelain of your toilet bowl to crack or damage the internal wax seal, leading to costly leaks. Always use hot tap water instead.

**How long should I wait after using dish soap and hot water to unclog a toilet?**

It's best to wait at least 20-30 minutes after pouring dish soap and hot water into the toilet bowl. This allows sufficient time for the soap to lubricate the clog and the hot water to help soften it, making it easier to clear.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried several DIY methods without success, or if the clog is recurring. Persistent clogs or blockages affecting multiple drains in your home could indicate a more serious issue in your main sewer line that requires professional intervention.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in toilets. They can be very harsh, potentially damaging your toilet's porcelain finish or the pipes, and are often ineffective against solid clogs. Safer, gentler DIY methods are preferred.
