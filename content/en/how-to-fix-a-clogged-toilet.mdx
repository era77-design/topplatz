To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow to prevent overflow, then attempt to clear the obstruction using a plunger. If a plunger isn't available or effective, household remedies like dish soap and hot water or a baking soda and vinegar mixture can often resolve the issue.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (closet snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water Flow Immediately

As soon as you notice the toilet isn't flushing and the water level is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper back into place to stop more water from entering the bowl. Alternatively, locate the shut-off valve at the base of the toilet, usually behind or to the side, and turn it clockwise until the water stops flowing. This prevents an overflowing mess on your bathroom floor.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Ensure good ventilation in the bathroom, as you'll be dealing with potentially unpleasant odors and stagnant water.

### Step 3: Plunge the Clog (If You Have One)

Position your plunger directly over the drain hole, ensuring a tight seal. Push down firmly to create suction, then pull up sharply. Repeat this plunging motion 10-20 times with consistent force, maintaining the seal. Often, the suction and pressure changes will dislodge the obstruction, allowing water to drain.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If you don't have a plunger or it didn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate the clog. Then, carefully pour a bucket of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl from waist height to add force. Wait another 20-30 minutes for the combination to soften and push the clog through.

### Step 5: Use Baking Soda and Vinegar for Stubborn Clogs

For a more powerful natural solution, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz and bubble, creating a chemical reaction that can help break down organic matter. Let this sit for at least 30 minutes, or ideally overnight, before attempting to flush.

### Step 6: Employ a Toilet Auger (Closet Snake)

If simpler methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet snake) is designed to clear deeper clogs without damaging the porcelain. Insert the end of the auger into the drain opening, crank the handle to feed the cable, and push until you feel resistance. Rotate and push to break up or hook the clog, then carefully retract the auger.

### Step 7: Test the Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, slowly turn the water shut-off valve back on. Flush the toilet carefully, observing if the water drains normally. If it does, you've succeeded! Clean up any spills, disinfect the area, and properly dispose of any materials used, including the contents of the auger if used.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger specifically designed for toilets; cup plungers are less effective.
- Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes and are often ineffective against toilet clogs.
- To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Never flush wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never pour boiling water into your toilet, as the extreme heat can crack the porcelain bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid overfilling the toilet bowl with water or cleaning solutions, as this can lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ If you're using a toilet auger, be gentle to avoid scratching the toilet bowl's surface. Do not force it if it meets significant resistance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet?**

To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water supply to prevent overflow. Then, try plunging with a flange plunger. If that doesn't work, use a mixture of dish soap and hot water, or baking soda and vinegar, to help break down the clog. For stubborn blockages, a toilet auger is an effective tool.

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by pouring half a cup of dish soap into the bowl, letting it sit for 10-15 minutes, then adding a bucket of hot (not boiling) water. Alternatively, a mixture of one cup of baking soda followed by two cups of white vinegar can create a reaction to break down the clog after sitting for at least 30 minutes.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by stopping the water to prevent overflow. Use a plunger with a good seal, pushing and pulling firmly to dislodge the waste. If a plunger isn't available or effective, dish soap and hot water can help break down the organic material, or a toilet auger can manually clear the blockage.

**What should I do if my toilet is still clogged after trying these methods?**

If your toilet remains clogged after trying plunging, dish soap, baking soda/vinegar, and a toilet auger, it's likely a more severe obstruction deeper in your plumbing system. At this point, it's best to call a professional plumber who has specialized tools and expertise to diagnose and resolve the issue safely.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use harsh chemical drain cleaners for toilet clogs. They can be ineffective against many toilet obstructions, potentially damage your pipes and the toilet's internal components, and pose a health risk due to fumes or splashes. Natural alternatives are safer and often more effective for common toilet clogs.

**How can I prevent my toilet from getting clogged in the future?**

To prevent future toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing anything else, including 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Consider using less toilet paper per flush and ensuring your toilet has adequate flushing power.

**Is it safe to leave a clogged toilet overnight?**

Yes, it is generally safe to leave a clogged toilet overnight, especially if you've already stopped the water supply to prevent overflow. In fact, some methods like the baking soda and vinegar mixture work best when given several hours to sit and break down the clog. Just ensure the area is secure and free from potential spills.
