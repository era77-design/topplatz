To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow to prevent overflow. You can then use a plunger with proper technique, or try alternative methods like dish soap and hot water, or a toilet snake for more stubborn blockages.

## What You Need

- Flange-style plunger
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)
- Baking soda and white vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess and Prepare for the Clog

Before you do anything, check if the toilet bowl is filling up. If it is, immediately lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl, or turn the water supply valve off behind the toilet. Lay old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from any potential splashes or overflows.

### Step 2: Master the Plunger Technique

Ensure you have a flange-style plunger, which creates a better seal than a cup plunger. Position the plunger over the drain hole, ensuring it completely covers the opening. Push down gently to release air, then plunge vigorously with an up-and-down motion, maintaining the seal. After about 10-15 plunges, pull the plunger up quickly to break the seal and check if the water drains.

### Step 3: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water (No Plunger Method)

If you don't have a plunger or it didn't work, this method can often clear minor clogs. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap directly into the toilet bowl. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes, allowing it to lubricate the clog. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of hot (not boiling) water into the bowl, waiting another 15-20 minutes for the combination to work its magic.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake) for Stubborn Clogs

For more persistent blockages, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is highly effective. Carefully insert the end of the auger into the toilet drain, feeding the cable down until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to extend the cable and break up or retrieve the obstruction. Once the clog is cleared, slowly retract the auger.

### Step 5: Baking Soda and Vinegar Alternative

For an eco-friendly approach, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The fizzing reaction helps to break down organic matter in the clog. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for tough clogs, before attempting to flush.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet. If the water drains normally, you've succeeded! If not, repeat the chosen method or try another. After the clog is gone, thoroughly clean your toilet and any surrounding areas that may have been splashed, using disinfectant.

### Step 7: When to Call a Professional

If you've tried multiple methods and the toilet remains stubbornly clogged, or if you experience frequent clogs, it might indicate a deeper issue in your plumbing system. Don't hesitate to call a licensed plumber. They have specialized tools and expertise to diagnose and resolve complex drainage problems efficiently and safely.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger for toilets, as it creates a better seal than a cup plunger.
- Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- For persistent clogs, ensure you are not dealing with a main sewer line blockage, which would affect other drains in your home.
- Keep a bucket nearby when working on a clogged toilet to easily remove excess water if needed.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet. They can damage pipes, harm your porcelain, and release toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not repeatedly flush a clogged toilet, as this will only cause it to overflow.
- ⚠️ Avoid using boiling water, as extreme heat can crack the porcelain bowl or melt PVC pipes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then slowly add a gallon of hot (not boiling) water and wait another 15-20 minutes for it to work.

**What do you do if your toilet is clogged with poop?**

If your toilet is clogged with poop, first try plunging with a flange-style plunger. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can help break down organic matter. For stubborn blockages, a toilet auger is effective at clearing solid obstructions.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet that won't flush?**

If your toilet is clogged and won't flush, prevent overflow by stopping the water flow. Then, try plunging vigorously with a flange plunger. If that fails, use dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger to physically clear the blockage, allowing the water to drain.

**Can a clogged toilet fix itself?**

Minor clogs, especially those caused by soft organic material, can sometimes dissolve or break down on their own over time with the help of water. However, for most clogs, especially those involving solid objects or significant waste, manual intervention is usually required.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods without success, if the toilet frequently clogs, or if multiple drains in your home are slow or backed up, indicating a main sewer line issue.

**Can I use boiling water to unclog a toilet?**

No, you should never use boiling water to unclog a toilet. The extreme heat can cause the porcelain bowl to crack or damage the wax seal at the base of the toilet, leading to leaks and costly repairs. Use hot tap water instead.
