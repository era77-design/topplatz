Fixing a clogged toilet often involves using a plunger to create suction and dislodge the obstruction. For minor clogs or when a plunger isn't available, household items like dish soap and hot water can be surprisingly effective. Stubborn blockages may require a toilet auger to clear the drainpipe.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or rags
- Flange plunger (toilet plunger)
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (from tap, not boiling)
- Toilet auger (optional, for stubborn clogs)
- All-purpose cleaner and disinfectant

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

First, check the water level in the toilet bowl. If it's very high and close to overflowing, immediately lift the tank lid and push down the flapper to stop more water from entering, or turn the water supply valve off at the wall behind the toilet. Lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and put on your rubber gloves before proceeding.

### Step 2: Plunge with Purpose

Position your plunger over the drain opening, ensuring you create a complete seal around the hole. The best plungers for toilets have a flange that extends into the drain for a better seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction, repeating this motion vigorously for about 10-20 seconds. Break the seal occasionally to see if the water level drops; if it does, the clog is likely clearing.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this by slowly pouring a bucket of hot (not boiling) tap water into the bowl, allowing the water to dissolve the soap and the clog. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer if the clog is particularly stubborn, as the soap helps lubricate the blockage, making it easier to pass.

### Step 4: Use a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For clogs that resist both plunging and the dish soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a snake) is your next best option. Carefully insert the auger's coiled cable into the toilet drain, feeding it gently until you feel resistance. Crank the handle to extend the cable and break up the clog, or hook it to pull it out. Be careful not to scratch the porcelain bowl as you work the auger.

### Step 5: Flush and Confirm

Once you believe the clog is clear, flush the toilet. If you turned off the water supply, remember to turn it back on first. Flush cautiously, watching to ensure the water level drops normally and the toilet flushes without backing up. You may need to flush a couple of times to fully clear any remaining debris in the pipes.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Sanitize

After successfully clearing the clog, it's important to clean and sanitize the area. Dispose of any dirty towels or rags, and thoroughly clean your plunger and auger. Use an all-purpose cleaner or disinfectant on the toilet bowl, seat, and any surfaces that may have come into contact with toilet water, ensuring good hygiene.


## Tips & Tricks

- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- Keep a dedicated toilet plunger with a flange in each bathroom for quick access.
- If you have a slow-draining toilet, an occasional pour of dish soap and hot water can help prevent future blockages.
- Consider adding a wastebasket to your bathroom for non-flushable items like wipes, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage your pipes, are harmful to the environment, and can produce dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Never use boiling water to clear a clog, as the sudden temperature change can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Avoid repeated flushing when the toilet bowl is full; this will only cause an overflow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a bucket of hot (not boiling) tap water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. The soap lubricates the clog, helping it to slide down the drain.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, first try plunging with a proper flange plunger to create strong suction and dislodge the organic matter. If plunging isn't effective, follow up with the dish soap and hot water method to help break down and lubricate the clog. For very stubborn blockages, a toilet auger can effectively clear the obstruction.

**What causes a toilet to clog easily?**

Toilets often clog easily due to flushing non-flushable items like wet wipes, paper towels, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products. Using excessive amounts of toilet paper or having an older, low-flush toilet can also contribute to frequent clogs.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a professional plumber if your toilet remains clogged after trying all the common methods (plunger, soap/water, auger), or if you experience frequent clogs. Persistent issues could indicate a more serious problem like a blockage deep in the main drain line or a failing septic system.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaner to fix a clogged toilet?**

No, it is strongly advised not to use chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet. These harsh chemicals can damage your pipes, especially older ones, and pose significant health risks due to toxic fumes and potential skin burns. They are also generally ineffective for solid toilet clogs.

**Is it safe to use hot water to unclog a toilet?**

Yes, it is generally safe to use hot tap water to unclog a toilet, as long as it's not boiling. Boiling water can cause the porcelain of your toilet bowl to crack due to extreme temperature changes. Warm or hot tap water, combined with dish soap, is sufficient and safe for breaking down most clogs.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss, or feminine hygiene products. Using less toilet paper per flush and ensuring your toilet has adequate flushing power can also help.
