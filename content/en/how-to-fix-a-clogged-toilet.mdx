To fix a clogged toilet, first prevent overflow by shutting off the water supply valve behind the toilet. Next, attempt to dislodge the blockage using a plunger with firm, consistent strokes. For more stubborn clogs, a combination of dish soap and hot water or a toilet auger can often be effective.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Plunger (flange-style recommended)
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger or snake (optional)
- Bucket (optional, for scooping water)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water Flow

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from continuously filling the bowl. Locate the water supply valve, usually on the wall behind or beneath the toilet, and turn it clockwise until the water flow completely stops. This prevents the bowl from overflowing while you work.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Put on rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Lay old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet on the floor. This will protect your flooring from any splashes or spills that may occur during the unclogging process, making cleanup much easier afterwards.

### Step 3: Plunge with Purpose

Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring you create a tight seal. Push down slowly to expel air, then pull up sharply to create suction, repeating this motion 10-15 times. The goal is to create a vacuum effect that dislodges the clog. If the water starts to drain, remove the plunger and flush once to check if the clog is gone.

### Step 4: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water

If plunging doesn't work, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. The soap acts as a lubricant, and the hot water helps break down the clog. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer, to allow the mixture to work its way through the blockage.

### Step 5: Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

For very stubborn clogs, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is highly effective. Insert the auger's flexible cable into the drain opening, feeding it slowly until you meet resistance. Crank the handle to extend the cable and break through the clog. Once cleared, carefully retract the auger and flush.

### Step 6: Flush and Test

Once you believe the clog is clear, turn the water supply valve back on. Flush the toilet to confirm that the water drains properly and the bowl refills without issue. If the toilet still seems sluggish or partially clogged, you may need to repeat one of the earlier steps or consider professional help.

### Step 7: Clean Up Thoroughly

After successfully unclogging the toilet, thoroughly clean and disinfect your plunger, auger, and any other tools used. Dispose of soiled towels or newspaper responsibly. Wipe down the toilet and surrounding area with a disinfectant cleaner to ensure a hygienic environment.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange-style plunger; its extended rubber flap creates a better seal for toilet drains compared to cup plungers.
- Never flush anything other than human waste and toilet paper to prevent future clogs.
- If you have a persistent clog that resists all your efforts, or if multiple drains in your home are backing up, it's time to call a professional plumber.
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes, harm the environment, and pose a safety risk.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.
- ⚠️ Do not flush the toilet repeatedly if it is not draining, as this will almost certainly cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is turned off before attempting to fix a severe clog to prevent potential flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using a toilet auger to avoid scratching the porcelain of the toilet bowl.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a toilet auger (snake) can effectively clear more stubborn blockages.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, first ensure the water supply is off to prevent overflow. Then, use a flange-style plunger to create a strong seal and plunge vigorously to break up and dislodge the waste. If plunging isn't effective, try the dish soap and hot water method, as the soap can help lubricate and break down organic matter.

**What causes a toilet to clog?**

Toilets commonly clog due to flushing too much toilet paper at once, or flushing non-flushable items like wipes (even 'flushable' ones), feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, paper towels, or small toys. Sometimes, a weak flush or issues with the toilet's internal mechanisms can also contribute to clogging.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods and the clog persists, or if the toilet clogs frequently. Also, if multiple drains in your home (like sinks or showers) are backing up, or you hear gurgling noises from other drains when flushing, it indicates a deeper issue in your plumbing system that requires professional attention.

**Can I use bleach or chemical drain cleaner to unclog a toilet?**

It is generally not recommended to use bleach or chemical drain cleaners for clogged toilets. Bleach is not effective at breaking down clogs and can create dangerous fumes when mixed with other cleaning agents. Chemical drain cleaners can damage your pipes, especially older ones, and may sit in the bowl if the clog doesn't clear, posing a safety hazard.

**How long does it take for a toilet clog to clear itself?**

Most toilet clogs, especially those caused by non-dissolving items, will not clear themselves. If the clog is solely due to an excessive amount of toilet paper, it might eventually dissolve and clear over several hours, but intervention is usually required for effective and timely resolution. Waiting too long can sometimes make the problem worse.

**What is the best way to prevent a toilet from clogging?**

The best way to prevent a toilet from clogging is to only flush human waste and toilet paper in moderation. Avoid flushing paper towels, wet wipes, dental floss, cotton swabs, feminine hygiene products, or any other non-biodegradable items. Educate everyone in the household about proper toilet usage to minimize future issues.
