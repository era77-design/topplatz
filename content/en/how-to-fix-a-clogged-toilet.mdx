To fix a clogged toilet, first stop the water flow by checking the flapper or turning the water valve off. Then, you can use a plunger, a mixture of dish soap and hot water, or a toilet auger to clear the obstruction.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Plunger (flange plunger recommended)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot (not boiling) water
- Baking soda
- White vinegar
- Toilet auger (or snake)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Water Flow

If the toilet bowl looks like it's about to overflow, quickly remove the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl. Alternatively, locate the shut-off valve on the wall behind the toilet and turn it clockwise until the water stops running. This prevents an overflow while you work.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Lay old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Having a bucket nearby is also helpful for bailing out excess water if needed.

### Step 3: Use a Plunger Effectively

Ensure there's enough water in the bowl to cover the rubber cup of the plunger. Position the plunger over the drain hole, creating a tight seal. Plunge with firm, steady pushes and pulls for about 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal. After several plunges, quickly pull the plunger up to break the seal and check if the water drains.

### Step 4: Try Dish Soap and Hot Water

If a plunger isn't available or didn't work, pour about a quarter cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of very hot (not boiling) water into the bowl from waist height. The soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water helps break it down. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, then try flushing.

### Step 5: Apply Baking Soda and Vinegar

For stubborn clogs, a natural chemical reaction can sometimes help. Pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl, followed by two cups of white vinegar. The mixture will fizz, which helps to loosen the clog. Let it sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for tough clogs, before attempting to flush.

### Step 6: Use a Toilet Auger (Snake)

If the previous methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or toilet snake) is the next step. Insert the flexible cable into the drain opening, feeding it slowly until you meet resistance. Rotate the handle to work the auger head through the clog, breaking it up or hooking onto it. Once the clog is cleared, retract the auger and flush the toilet.

### Step 7: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is clear, turn the water shut-off valve back on (counter-clockwise) if you turned it off. Flush the toilet to confirm the water drains properly. If it does, clean up any spills and disinfect the toilet and surrounding area thoroughly.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger for toilets, as its design creates a better seal.
- Never flush anything other than toilet paper and human waste to prevent future clogs.
- Regularly clean your toilet bowl and check for any potential issues to prevent severe clogs.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet; they can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and are dangerous if they splash back.
- ⚠️ Avoid repeatedly flushing a clogged toilet if the water level is rising, as this will lead to an overflow.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves and eye protection when dealing with a clogged toilet to protect yourself from germs and splashing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour a quarter cup of dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water, and let it sit for 20-30 minutes. Alternatively, a mixture of baking soda and vinegar can also help break down the clog.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, first try using a plunger with firm, steady pushes and pulls to dislodge the waste. If that doesn't work, a mixture of hot water and dish soap can help break down the organic matter. For more stubborn clogs, a toilet auger is effective at clearing solid blockages.

**What household items can unclog a toilet?**

Several household items can help unclog a toilet. Dish soap, combined with hot water, creates a slippery mixture that can help move the clog. Baking soda and white vinegar, when mixed, create a fizzing reaction that can break down blockages.

**How long should I wait after pouring soap and hot water?**

After pouring dish soap and hot water into a clogged toilet, you should wait at least 20-30 minutes. This allows the soap to lubricate the clog and the hot water to soften it, making it easier for the clog to pass through the pipes.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple methods like plunging, dish soap, baking soda and vinegar, and even a toilet auger, but the clog remains. Persistent or recurring clogs, or if multiple drains in your home are slow or backed up, could indicate a larger issue in your main sewer line that requires professional attention.
