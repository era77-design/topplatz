Fixing a clogged toilet often requires simple tools and techniques. Most minor clogs can be resolved using a plunger, hot water, or a drain snake, restoring proper function without a professional.

## What You Need

- Rubber plunger (flange or cup type)
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)
- Baking soda and white vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation & Prepare

First, do not flush the toilet again if the water level is high; this prevents an overflow. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your bathroom floor from any potential spills. Put on rubber gloves for hygiene and to protect your hands from toilet water.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the water level is very high and an overflow seems imminent, quickly locate and turn off the water supply valve behind the toilet to stop the tank from refilling.

### Step 2: Plunge with Vigor

Use a high-quality plunger, preferably a flange plunger designed specifically for toilets. Insert the plunger into the toilet bowl, ensuring it forms a tight seal around the drain hole. Push down firmly and pull up sharply multiple times, maintaining the seal, to create strong suction and pressure. Repeat this vigorous motion for about 15-20 seconds to dislodge the clog. For more detailed guidance on effective plunging, refer to [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy DIY Solutions & Guide](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure there's enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger's cup for maximum suction. If not, add some water from a bucket.

### Step 3: Try the Hot Water and Dish Soap Method

If plunging doesn't clear the clog, pour about a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, pouring it slowly from waist height to add some force. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes, allowing the soap to lubricate the clog and the hot water to help break down organic matter.

> 💡 **Tip:** This method is particularly effective for clogs caused by toilet paper or other organic waste, as the soap acts as a lubricant.

### Step 4: Use Baking Soda and Vinegar for Stubborn Clogs

For a more powerful natural solution, pour one cup of baking soda into the toilet bowl. Immediately follow this with two cups of white vinegar. The chemical reaction will create fizzing action, which can help break down stubborn organic material. Let this mixture sit for at least 30 minutes, or even overnight for very difficult clogs, before attempting to flush.

> 💡 **Tip:** This combination is a safer alternative to harsh chemical drain cleaners, which can damage pipes and septic systems.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

If the above methods fail, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger) is your next best tool. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain opening, feeding the cable while gently cranking the handle. Once you feel resistance, rotate and push the auger to break up or hook the clog. Slowly retract the auger, pulling out any debris if possible.

> 💡 **Tip:** Toilet augers are specifically designed with a protective sleeve to navigate the toilet's trap without scratching the porcelain.

### Step 6: Flush and Observe

After attempting any of the methods, gently try to flush the toilet. If the water level drops and the toilet flushes normally, the clog is clear. If it still drains slowly or doesn't clear, you may need to repeat a step or consider that the clog is deeper in your plumbing system. If the problem persists, it may be time to call a professional plumber.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the toilet still seems sluggish, a second, gentle flush after a few minutes might help clear any remaining residue.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves and lay down old towels to protect your bathroom floor from potential splashes and overflows.
- Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners in your toilet, as they can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and pose safety risks.
- For persistent or recurring clogs, it's often a sign of a deeper issue in your plumbing system, and consulting a professional plumber is recommended.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never flush the toilet repeatedly when it's clogged, as this can cause the bowl to overflow and create a messy situation. Turn off the water supply immediately if an overflow is imminent.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as extreme temperature changes can crack the porcelain of your toilet bowl.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water, or by creating a baking soda and vinegar mixture. These methods help to lubricate and break down organic clogs. For tougher clogs, a toilet auger can be used.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop, start by using a plunger vigorously to create suction and dislodge the clog. If that doesn't work, try pouring dish soap followed by hot (not boiling) water into the bowl to help break down the organic matter. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes before attempting to flush.

**What can I use if I don't have a plunger for a clogged toilet?**

If you don't have a plunger, you can try pouring a generous amount of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a bucket of hot (not boiling) water. Let this sit for at least 20 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate the clog and the water to soften it, then attempt to flush.

**How long does it take for a clogged toilet to unclog itself?**

Most minor clogs, especially those caused by toilet paper, may eventually dissolve and clear on their own within a few hours. However, it's usually faster and more effective to actively address the clog with methods like plunging or using dish soap and hot water.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your toilet remains clogged after trying several DIY methods, if water is backing up into other drains, or if you suspect a deeper issue in your main sewer line. Persistent or recurring clogs also warrant professional attention.

**Can Coca-Cola unclog a toilet?**

While some anecdotal evidence suggests Coca-Cola can help with minor clogs due to its phosphoric acid, its effectiveness is generally limited compared to dedicated drain cleaners or DIY methods. It's not a recommended primary solution for most clogs and can be quite expensive for the volume needed.

**Is it okay to leave a clogged toilet overnight?**

It's generally safe to leave a clogged toilet overnight, especially if the water level is low and there's no risk of overflow. This can sometimes allow soft clogs to dissolve naturally. However, if the bowl is full, it's best to address it to prevent accidental spills.
