A clogged toilet can be a frustrating household problem, but many blockages can be resolved with simple DIY methods. Before calling a plumber, try these effective techniques using common household items to get your toilet flushing properly again.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Flange plunger
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- Dish soap
- Hot water (not boiling)
- Toilet snake or auger (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Flushing and Assess the Situation

Immediately stop flushing the toilet if the water level is rising to prevent overflow. Lift the tank lid and close the flapper valve if water is close to overflowing the bowl. Assess the water level and the nature of the clog to determine the best approach.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Lay down old towels or rags around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes and spills. Put on a pair of rubber gloves to maintain hygiene. Have a bucket ready in case you need to remove some water from the bowl manually.

### Step 3: Use a Flange Plunger Effectively

A flange plunger, with its extended rubber lip, creates a better seal in the toilet's drain opening. Insert the plunger into the toilet bowl, ensuring the flange completely covers the drain. Push down firmly to create a vacuum, then pull up sharply several times without breaking the seal. Repeat this vigorous plunging action for about 15-20 seconds, then pull the plunger up quickly to break the seal and see if the water drains.

### Step 4: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If a plunger isn't available or didn't work, this method can often loosen clogs. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this by slowly pouring a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water into the bowl. The hot water helps to soften the clog, while the dish soap acts as a lubricant, helping the blockage slide down. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes, or even longer, before trying to flush.

### Step 5: Employ a Toilet Snake or Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For blockages that resist plunging and the dish soap method, a toilet snake (also known as a closet auger) is often the solution. Gently feed the auger's cable into the toilet drain, pushing it until you feel resistance. Rotate the handle to allow the auger to bore through or hook onto the clog. Once you've broken up or retrieved the obstruction, slowly pull the auger back out of the toilet.

### Step 6: Flush and Clean Up

Once you believe the clog is cleared, give the toilet a test flush. If the water drains normally, you've succeeded. If not, you may need to repeat one of the methods or consider the clog might be further down the drainpipe. Carefully clean all your tools and the surrounding area with disinfectant.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger designed for toilets, as cup plungers are less effective.
- Be patient. Some clogs require multiple attempts with plunging or soaking.
- To prevent future clogs, avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper.
- Consider a monthly maintenance flush with hot water and dish soap to keep pipes clear.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never flush the toilet if the water level is already high, as this will cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water, as it can crack the porcelain bowl or damage PVC pipes.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can be corrosive to pipes and dangerous if splashed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

If you don't have a plunger, try the dish soap and hot water method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of very hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to allow the soap to lubricate and the hot water to break down the clog, then attempt to flush.

**What's the best way to fix a clogged toilet with poop in it?**

A clogged toilet with poop is a common issue and can be resolved using the same methods. Start with a flange plunger, ensuring a good seal. If that doesn't work, the dish soap and hot water method can often help break down the organic matter. Always wear rubber gloves and protect your floor during the process.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried multiple DIY methods (plunging, dish soap, auger) without success, or if the clog is a recurring problem. Additionally, if you notice multiple drains in your home are slow or clogged, it could indicate a more serious main line issue requiring professional attention.

**What causes toilets to clog frequently?**

Frequent clogs are often caused by flushing non-flushable items like wipes, cotton balls, paper towels, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Older, low-flow toilets can also be more prone to clogging due to weaker flushing power. Sometimes, issues like tree roots in the main sewer line can also lead to recurring blockages.

**Can I use bleach or chemical drain cleaners to fix a clogged toilet?**

It's generally not recommended to use bleach or harsh chemical drain cleaners for a clogged toilet. These chemicals can be corrosive to your plumbing, dangerous if splashed, and often ineffective on tough clogs. They can also create toxic fumes when mixed with other cleaning agents and harm the environment.

**How can I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing 'flushable' wipes, feminine hygiene products, cotton swabs, or excessive toilet paper. Consider a monthly maintenance routine of pouring hot water and dish soap down the drain to help keep pipes clear, and ensure you have a good quality, strong-flushing toilet.
