To fix a clogged toilet, first assess the water level and stop it from overflowing. Then, try simple methods like hot water and dish soap. For more stubborn clogs, a flange plunger or a toilet auger can often clear the blockage effectively, restoring your toilet's function.

## What You Need

- Flange plunger
- Dish soap
- Hot water (from tap, not boiling)
- Rubber gloves
- Old towels or newspaper
- Small bucket (optional)
- Toilet auger/snake (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop the Overflow and Prepare

Immediately lift the toilet tank lid and push down on the flapper to stop water from entering the bowl. If the water level is high, use a small bucket to carefully remove some water to prevent overflow while you work. Lay down old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to catch any spills and wear rubber gloves for hygiene.

### Step 2: Try Hot Water and Dish Soap

Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured from waist height to add some force. Let this mixture sit for 20-30 minutes. The soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water can help break down organic matter. After waiting, attempt to flush and observe if the clog has cleared.

### Step 3: Use a Flange Plunger Effectively

Ensure you are using a flange plunger, which has an extended rubber flap designed to create a tight seal in the toilet's drain. Position the plunger firmly over the drain hole, ensuring a complete seal. Plunge with strong, consistent up-and-down motions for 15-20 seconds, maintaining the seal. On the last pull, break the seal quickly to create a vacuum effect. Repeat this process several times if needed.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger (Snake)

If plunging doesn't work, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your next best tool. This tool is designed to clear deeper clogs without scratching the porcelain. Carefully insert the auger's end into the drain, crank the handle to feed the cable, and rotate it until you feel resistance. Push and pull the auger to break up or retrieve the clog, then slowly retract it.

### Step 5: Flush and Confirm the Clear Drain

Once you believe the clog is cleared, flush the toilet to ensure it drains properly. If the water level still rises too high or drains slowly, repeat the previous steps. You may need to try plunging again after using the auger to clear any remaining debris.

### Step 6: Clean Up Thoroughly

After the toilet is clear and flushing correctly, thoroughly clean the toilet bowl, plunger, and any splashed areas. Use a disinfectant cleaner to sanitize all surfaces that came into contact with toilet water. Dispose of any used towels or newspaper appropriately.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Clogs

To prevent future clogs, educate household members on what not to flush down the toilet. Only human waste and toilet paper should ever be flushed. Avoid items like 'flushable' wipes (even if labeled as such), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper at once.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a flange plunger for toilets, as its design creates a superior seal compared to a standard cup plunger, making it more effective for toilet clogs.
- Never use chemical drain cleaners in your toilet; they can damage pipes, harm your septic system, and are dangerous if splashed or inhaled.
- To prevent clogs, educate household members on what not to flush, including wipes (even those marketed as 'flushable'), paper towels, feminine hygiene products, and dental floss.
- If you're using a toilet auger, be gentle and avoid forcing it, as excessive force can scratch the porcelain bowl or damage pipes.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use boiling water in your toilet, as the extreme temperature difference can cause the porcelain bowl to crack or shatter.
- ⚠️ Avoid chemical drain cleaners, as they are corrosive, can damage your plumbing system, and release harmful fumes that are dangerous to inhale.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when dealing with a clogged toilet for hygiene and protection against bacteria.
- ⚠️ If water is overflowing, immediately turn off the water supply valve usually located behind or near the base of the toilet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using the hot water and dish soap method. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of very hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to lubricate and break down the clog before flushing.

**How to fix a clogged toilet with poop?**

A clogged toilet with poop can often be cleared using the hot water and dish soap method, or by effectively using a flange plunger. Ensure you have adequate ventilation and wear rubber gloves for hygiene while working. If these methods fail, a toilet auger can help remove the blockage.

**What causes a toilet to clog easily?**

Toilets often clog easily due to flushing non-flushable items like 'flushable' wipes, paper towels, feminine hygiene products, or dental floss. Using excessive amounts of toilet paper at once is also a very common cause of blockages.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if you've tried various DIY methods like plunging and snaking, and the clog persists or recurs frequently. Additionally, if multiple toilets or drains in your home are backing up, it could indicate a larger main line issue requiring professional help.

**Can I use chemical drain cleaner for a clogged toilet?**

No, it is strongly advised not to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can be highly corrosive, damage your toilet's internal components and plumbing pipes, and pose significant safety risks due to dangerous fumes and potential splashes.

**What's the best type of plunger for a toilet?**

The best type of plunger for a toilet is a flange plunger. It features an extended rubber flap that creates a tight, effective seal over the toilet's drain opening, making it much more efficient at dislodging clogs than a standard cup-style plunger.

**How do I prevent my toilet from clogging in the future?**

To prevent future clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing any other items, including so-called 'flushable' wipes, cotton balls, or hygiene products. Also, be mindful of using less toilet paper per flush and periodically pouring hot water and dish soap down the drain as a preventative measure.
