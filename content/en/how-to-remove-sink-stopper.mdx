Removing a sink stopper is a common task for cleaning, unclogging, or replacement. The process varies slightly depending on the type of stopper, but generally involves disconnecting a pivot rod or simply unscrewing the stopper.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or needle-nose)
- Screwdriver (Phillips or flathead)
- Bucket or small container
- Old towel or rag
- Flashlight (optional)
- Cleaning brush or old toothbrush
- Gloves (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Before you begin, determine the type of sink stopper you have. Common types include pop-up stoppers (controlled by a lift rod behind the faucet), lift-and-turn stoppers (you twist and lift), push-pull stoppers (you press down to close, pull up to open), and simple drop-in stoppers. The removal method differs based on the mechanism.

### Step 2: 2. Prepare Your Workspace

Clear out any items from under the sink to give yourself ample room to work. Place an old towel or rag on the cabinet floor and position a bucket or small container directly under the drain pipe. This will catch any water or debris that might escape when you loosen connections.

### Step 3: 3. Disconnect the Pivot Rod (for Pop-Up Stoppers)

For pop-up stoppers, look under the sink for the horizontal pivot rod extending from the drain pipe to the clevis strap, which connects to the vertical lift rod. Using pliers, loosen the retaining nut or clip that holds the pivot rod in place. Carefully pull the pivot rod out of the drain assembly. Be prepared for a small amount of water to drain into your bucket.

### Step 4: 4. Remove the Stopper Itself

Once the pivot rod is disconnected (for pop-up stoppers), you can simply lift the stopper straight out of the drain opening from above. For lift-and-turn or push-pull stoppers, grasp the top of the stopper and unscrew it counter-clockwise until it detaches from the drain. Drop-in stoppers simply lift out.

### Step 5: 5. Clean the Stopper and Drain Opening

With the stopper removed, thoroughly clean it and the inside of the drain opening. Use a brush or old toothbrush to remove accumulated hair, soap scum, and grime. If you find any mold buildup, clean it carefully; general principles for cleaning can be similar to how you might tackle [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) in other areas, though focus on plumbing-safe cleaners here.

### Step 6: 6. Reassemble the Stopper (if applicable)

Once cleaned, reinsert the stopper into the drain. For pop-up stoppers, guide the pivot rod back into the drain assembly and through the hole in the stopper, then re-tighten the retaining nut or clip. For screw-in stoppers, simply screw them back into place clockwise. Ensure all connections are snug but not overtightened to prevent damage.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or small container under the sink when working on plumbing connections to catch any drips.
- Take a photo of the drain assembly before disassembling it, which can be a helpful reference during reassembly.
- Use a flashlight for better visibility under the sink, especially in dimly lit cabinets.
- Regularly removing and cleaning your sink stopper can prevent major clogs and unpleasant odors.
- If your stopper is stuck due to old caulk or thread sealant, you might need to carefully scrape it away. For stubborn residue, principles for [How to Remove Adhesive Residue Easily & Safely](/en/how-to-remove-adhesive-residue) can offer guidance on gentle removal.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not over-tighten nuts or screws when reassembling, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to drop small parts, such as clips or screws, down the drain.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can corrode plumbing parts and pose safety risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my sink stopper not holding water?**

This is often due to a buildup of hair and grime around the stopper's seal or an issue with the pivot rod not engaging correctly. Removing and cleaning the stopper, or adjusting the pivot rod, usually resolves this.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice slow drainage or unpleasant odors. Regular cleaning prevents significant buildup.

**Can I replace a broken sink stopper myself?**

Yes, replacing a broken sink stopper is typically a straightforward DIY task. You'll need to measure the diameter of your drain opening and sometimes the length of the stopper to get a compatible replacement.

**What if the pivot rod is stuck and won't come out?**

If the pivot rod is stuck, it might be corroded or have excessive buildup. Gently try to wiggle it while pulling. Applying a penetrating lubricant (like WD-40) and letting it sit for a few minutes can sometimes help loosen it.

**How do I remove a stopper without access to under the sink?**

For lift-and-turn, push-pull, or drop-in stoppers, you don't need under-sink access – they can be removed from above. Pop-up stoppers, however, require access to disconnect the pivot rod from underneath.

**What's the difference between a pop-up and a push-pull stopper?**

A pop-up stopper is operated by a lift rod behind the faucet, which connects to a pivot rod under the sink. A push-pull stopper is operated by pressing down on the stopper itself to close it and pulling up to open it, with no external rod.

**Can I use a universal sink stopper?**

Universal sink stoppers are available and can work for many standard drains, especially drop-in or push-pull types. However, for pop-up drains, you typically need a stopper designed to work with the pivot rod mechanism.
