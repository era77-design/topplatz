Removing a sink stopper typically involves detaching the pivot rod beneath the sink, which connects to the stopper inside the drain. For most pop-up stoppers, you'll need to loosen a nut on the drainpipe to free the rod, then lift the stopper out. Some newer stopper designs simply lift out or unscrew from the drain opening without needing to go under the sink.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or channel-lock)
- Small bucket or container
- Towel or rag
- Flashlight (optional)
- Wire brush or old toothbrush
- Gloves (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Clear out any items from the cabinet directly under your sink to provide easy access to the drainpipe. Place a small bucket or container directly under the pivot rod connection to catch any residual water that might leak out when you loosen the parts. Lay down a towel to protect the cabinet floor from spills.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a flashlight to better illuminate the area under the sink, especially if it's dark or cramped.

### Step 2: Locate the Pivot Rod Assembly

Look at the drainpipe directly under your sink. You should see a horizontal rod, known as the pivot rod, extending from the side of the drain assembly into the main drainpipe. This rod is usually held in place by a large nut or a retaining clip. This mechanism controls your sink stopper's up and down movement.

### Step 3: Detach the Pivot Rod

Use pliers to carefully loosen the large nut (called the pivot nut or coupling nut) that secures the pivot rod to the drainpipe. Turn the nut counter-clockwise until it's loose enough to pull the pivot rod out of the drain assembly. Be prepared for a small amount of water to drip out when the rod is removed, which is why the bucket is important.

### Step 4: Remove the Sink Stopper

Once the pivot rod is completely detached and pulled out of the drain, you can go back to the top of the sink. Simply grasp the sink stopper from the drain opening and lift it straight out. It should come out easily now that the pivot rod is no longer holding it in place.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain

This is an excellent opportunity to clean accumulated hair, soap scum, and grime from the stopper and the inside of the drain opening. Use a wire brush, an old toothbrush, or a rag to scrub these parts thoroughly. A clean stopper and drain will improve water flow and prevent future clogs. If you notice any mold buildup in the drain area, now is a good time to address it safely. For more extensive mold issues on surrounding surfaces, consult resources like [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls).

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn grime, a mixture of baking soda and vinegar can be effective for cleaning the stopper and drain opening.

### Step 6: Reinstall the Stopper (Optional)

If you only removed the stopper for cleaning or to retrieve an item, you can reinstall it by reversing the removal process. Insert the stopper back into the drain, ensuring the hole on its side (where the pivot rod goes) faces the back of the sink. Carefully reinsert the pivot rod through the drainpipe and the stopper's hole, then tighten the pivot nut until snug, but do not overtighten.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks

Once everything is reassembled, turn on the faucet and fill the sink with some water. Then, release the stopper and let the water drain. Carefully inspect the pivot rod connection under the sink for any drips or leaks. If you see water, gently tighten the pivot nut a little more until the leak stops.


## Tips & Tricks

- For stoppers that simply lift out or unscrew from the drain opening (common in newer sinks), no tools are needed, and you won't have to go under the sink.
- Wear gloves to maintain hygiene, especially when dealing with grime and hair from the drain.
- Keep track of all small parts, like the pivot rod and nut, to ensure proper reassembly.
- Avoid overtightening plastic nuts to prevent cracking or damaging the drainpipe assembly.
- Regularly clean your sink stopper and drain to prevent future clogs and unpleasant odors.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use harsh chemical drain cleaners as they can damage your pipes and pose health risks. Mechanical removal and cleaning are safer.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to drop any small parts, tools, or retrieved items down the open drain while the stopper is removed.
- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply to the sink if you anticipate working on the plumbing for an extended period, though it's usually not necessary for a simple stopper removal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if my sink stopper doesn't have a pivot rod?**

Some modern sink stoppers are designed to simply lift straight out of the drain or unscrew from the top. If there's no visible pivot rod under the sink, try twisting the stopper counter-clockwise or pulling it straight up to remove it.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every 1-3 months, especially in bathroom sinks where hair and soap scum tend to accumulate quickly. Regular cleaning prevents clogs and keeps your sink draining efficiently.

**Can I remove a kitchen sink stopper the same way?**

Kitchen sink stoppers often differ from bathroom stoppers. Many kitchen stoppers are either a simple drop-in type or have a disposal flange. If it's a pop-up type connected to a lever, the process will be similar, but always check your specific model.

**What if the pivot nut is corroded or stuck?**

If the pivot nut is corroded and difficult to loosen, apply a penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again with pliers. If it's severely corroded, you might need to gently tap the pliers with a hammer or use a specialized plumbing wrench, but be careful not to damage the pipes.

**How do I prevent future clogs after removing the stopper?**

To prevent future clogs, consider using a drain protector or hair catcher in your sink. Regularly flush your drain with hot water, and avoid pouring grease or coffee grounds down the kitchen sink.

**Is it okay to use chemical drain cleaners?**

It's generally not recommended to use chemical drain cleaners as they can be harsh on your pipes, especially older ones, and are environmentally unfriendly. Manual removal of blockages and regular cleaning are safer and more effective methods.

**What if I drop something small down the drain while the stopper is out?**

If you drop a small item down the drain, you can often retrieve it by removing the P-trap directly under the sink. This U-shaped pipe is designed to catch items and prevent sewer gases from entering your home. Place a bucket underneath before loosening the nuts on either side of the P-trap to empty its contents.
