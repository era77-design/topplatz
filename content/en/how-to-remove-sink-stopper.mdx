To remove a sink stopper, first identify if it's a pop-up or a lift-rod style. For most stoppers, you'll typically need to disconnect the pivot rod underneath the sink, then pull the stopper straight up from the drain opening. This process allows for cleaning or replacement.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or channel-lock)
- Bucket or small container
- Towels
- Gloves (optional)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Stopper Type

Before you begin, determine whether your sink has a pop-up stopper (controlled by a lift rod behind the faucet) or a simple drop-in stopper. Most bathroom sinks use a lift-rod pop-up mechanism, while kitchen sinks often have a basket strainer or a separate plunger-style stopper. Understanding the type will guide your removal process.

### Step 2: Clear the Area Under the Sink

Empty the cabinet directly beneath the sink to give yourself clear access to the drain assembly. Place a bucket or an old towel directly under the P-trap and pivot rod connection to catch any residual water that might leak out during the removal process. This prevents messes and makes cleanup easier.

### Step 3: Locate and Disconnect the Pivot Rod

For a lift-rod pop-up stopper, look for a horizontal pivot rod protruding from the drainpipe under the sink. This rod connects to the vertical lift rod and controls the stopper. Use pliers to loosen the retaining nut or spring clip that holds the pivot rod in place. Carefully pull the pivot rod out of the drain assembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you encounter any signs of mold or mildew in the cabinet while working, it's a good time to address it. [How to Remove Mold From Walls Safely & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) provides helpful guidance for cleaning.

### Step 4: Remove the Stopper from Above

Once the pivot rod is disconnected, return to the top of the sink. Grasp the sink stopper and pull it straight up and out of the drain opening. It should come out easily now that its connection to the pivot rod is released. If it feels stuck due to grime, gently twist it while pulling.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain Area

With the stopper removed, thoroughly clean off any accumulated hair, soap scum, or debris from the stopper itself and the inside of the drain opening. An old toothbrush or small brush works well for this. This is often the primary reason for slow draining and should resolve many common issues.

### Step 6: Reinstall the Stopper and Test

Carefully lower the clean stopper back into the drain. From under the sink, reinsert the pivot rod through the hole in the drainpipe and through the loop on the stopper's shaft. Secure the retaining nut or spring clip, ensuring it's snug but not overtightened. Test the stopper by lifting and lowering the lift rod to ensure it operates smoothly and seals properly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Make sure the pivot rod is fully seated and the retaining nut is tight enough to prevent leaks but not so tight that it strips the threads or prevents the stopper from moving freely.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or towel ready under the sink to catch any drips when working on plumbing.
- Wear gloves to protect your hands from grime and sharp edges.
- If your stopper is badly corroded or damaged, consider replacing it with a new one; they are relatively inexpensive and widely available.
- Regularly removing and cleaning your sink stopper can prevent serious clogs and maintain good drainage.
- For persistent clogs after cleaning the stopper, you might need to clean the P-trap or use a drain snake.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or fittings, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to leaks.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when pulling on the pivot rod or stopper to avoid bending or breaking parts.
- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply if you plan to disconnect any major drain components beyond the pivot rod, though this is usually not necessary for just the stopper.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my sink stopper not holding water?**

A sink stopper typically fails to hold water due to a worn-out rubber gasket or a misalignment of the stopper within the drain. Inspect the gasket for damage and ensure the stopper is properly seated and sealing against the drain opening when closed.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice the sink draining slowly. Regular cleaning prevents the buildup of hair and soap scum, which are common causes of clogs and odors.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without going under the sink?**

Some simple drop-in stoppers or basket strainers in kitchen sinks can be removed without going under the sink. However, most bathroom pop-up stoppers require you to disconnect the pivot rod from underneath to fully remove them.

**What if the pivot rod is stuck or corroded?**

If the pivot rod is stuck, try applying a penetrating oil (like WD-40) and letting it sit for a few minutes before attempting to loosen it with pliers. For severe corrosion, you might need to replace the entire drain assembly.

**My sink is still draining slowly after cleaning the stopper. What's next?**

If cleaning the stopper doesn't resolve slow drainage, the clog is likely further down the drainpipe, possibly in the P-trap or beyond. You may need to clean the P-trap or use a drain snake to clear the obstruction.

**Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaners?**

While chemical drain cleaners can sometimes clear clogs, they are often harsh and can damage pipes over time, especially older ones. They can also pose safety risks. It's generally safer and more effective to use mechanical methods like snaking or disassembling the P-trap.

**Can I replace just the stopper, or do I need a whole new drain assembly?**

In many cases, you can purchase and replace just the stopper itself, especially if the old one is corroded or the gasket is worn. However, if the entire pivot rod mechanism or the drain body is damaged, you might need to replace the complete drain assembly.
