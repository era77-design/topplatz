Removing a sink stopper typically involves detaching the pivot rod assembly beneath the sink. This process allows you to lift the stopper out for cleaning, unclogging, or replacement, restoring proper drainage to your basin.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or adjustable)
- Bucket or small bowl
- Old towel or rags
- Flashlight (optional)
- Cleaning brush (e.g., old toothbrush)
- Gloves (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, clear any items from under the sink to give yourself ample room to work. Place a bucket or a small bowl directly beneath the drain pipe where the pivot rod enters, as a small amount of water may drip out. Lay down an old towel or rags to catch any splashes and keep the area dry and clean.

> 💡 **Tip:** Good lighting is crucial. Use a flashlight or headlamp to illuminate the cramped space under the sink for better visibility.

### Step 2: Locate the Pivot Rod

Look directly under the sink basin for the main drain pipe. You will identify a horizontal metal rod, known as the pivot rod, entering the side of this pipe. This rod is connected to the stopper mechanism inside the sink and is responsible for moving the stopper up and down.

> 💡 **Tip:** The pivot rod is usually held in place by a large threaded nut or a clip, which you'll need to manipulate next.

### Step 3: Loosen the Retaining Nut

Using your pliers, carefully grip the retaining nut that secures the pivot rod to the drain pipe. Turn the nut counter-clockwise to loosen it. As you loosen the nut, be prepared for a small amount of water to escape from the drain pipe, which your bucket should catch.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the nut is stuck or corroded, apply gentle, steady pressure. Avoid excessive force, which could damage the plumbing fixtures or strip the threads.

### Step 4: Remove the Pivot Rod

Once the retaining nut is sufficiently loose, gently pull the pivot rod straight out from the drain pipe. Ensure your bucket remains in position to catch any additional water that may drain from the pipe as the rod is removed. Keep the retaining nut and any washers nearby in a safe place so they are not lost.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a quick photo of the assembly before completely removing the rod; this can be a helpful reference during reinstallation.

### Step 5: Lift Out the Stopper

With the pivot rod no longer holding it in place, you can now easily access the stopper from the top of the sink. Simply reach into the drain opening and lift the stopper straight up and out of the basin. It should come out without much resistance.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the stopper feels stuck, it might be due to accumulated hair or debris. A gentle wiggle and pull should free it.

### Step 6: Clean the Stopper and Drain

With the stopper removed, this is the perfect opportunity for a thorough cleaning. Use a cleaning brush or an old toothbrush to scrub away accumulated hair, soap scum, and debris from both the stopper and the inside of the drain opening. This step is crucial for clearing clogs and eliminating foul odors. If you notice any signs of mold in damp areas around the drain, consider consulting resources like [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) for guidance on safe cleaning practices.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wearing gloves is recommended for hygiene, especially when dealing with built-up grime and hair.

### Step 7: Reinstall the Stopper

To reinstall, reverse the steps. Insert the stopper back into the drain from the top, ensuring its loop aligns with the pivot rod's entry point. From underneath, carefully push the pivot rod back into its hole in the drain pipe, making sure it engages with the stopper's lower loop. Hand-tighten the retaining nut, then use pliers to secure it firmly but without overtightening.

> 💡 **Tip:** After reinstallation, run water into the sink and observe for any leaks around the pivot rod connection. If you notice drips, tighten the retaining nut slightly more until the leak stops.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket and towel ready to catch water when working under the sink to prevent messes and water damage.
- Take a photo of the pivot rod assembly before disassembling it; this visual guide can be invaluable during reinstallation.
- Regularly cleaning your sink stopper every few months can prevent major clogs, maintain good drainage, and reduce odors.
- If the pivot rod assembly or stopper is heavily corroded or damaged, consider replacing the entire drain assembly for optimal performance.
- When tightening nuts, ensure they are snug to prevent leaks, but avoid excessive force that could strip threads or crack pipes.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use excessive force when loosening or tightening nuts, as this can damage plumbing fixtures, strip threads, or crack pipes.
- ⚠️ Be mindful of potentially sharp edges or corroded parts under the sink, and consider wearing gloves for protection.
- ⚠️ Ensure all connections are watertight after reinstallation. Leaks, even minor ones, can lead to significant water damage over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What tools do I need to remove a sink stopper?**

Typically, you will need a pair of pliers (slip-joint or adjustable are best), a bucket or small bowl to catch water, and an old towel or rags. A flashlight can also be very helpful to improve visibility in the confined space under the sink.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without going under the sink?**

Most modern sink stoppers, especially pop-up types, are connected to a pivot rod assembly located underneath the sink. While some older or simpler stoppers might just lift out, for the common pop-up stopper, you'll need to access and disconnect the pivot rod from below the basin.

**What if my sink stopper is stuck?**

If your sink stopper is stuck after disconnecting the pivot rod, it's most likely due to a build-up of hair, soap scum, and other debris. Gently wiggle and pull the stopper to free it from the drain opening. A cleaning brush can help dislodge stubborn blockages.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice signs of slow drainage or unpleasant odors coming from the sink. Regular maintenance helps prevent major clogs and keeps your sink functioning properly.

**Is it possible to break something while removing the sink stopper?**

While unlikely if you follow the instructions carefully, using excessive force can potentially damage plumbing fixtures, strip threads, or crack pipes. Always apply gentle, steady pressure and ensure you are using the correct tools for the task to minimize risk.

**What if my sink still drains slowly after cleaning the stopper?**

If your sink continues to drain slowly even after you've thoroughly cleaned the stopper and the visible drain opening, the clog is likely located further down the drainpipe. In such cases, you might need to use a drain snake, a chemical drain cleaner, or consider calling a professional plumber.
