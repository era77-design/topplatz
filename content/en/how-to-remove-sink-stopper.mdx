Removing a sink stopper typically involves disconnecting a pivot rod found under the sink. This process allows you to access the drain for cleaning clogs or to replace a damaged stopper. It's a straightforward DIY task that can improve drainage and hygiene.

## What You Need

- Bucket or small container
- Pliers (slip-joint or tongue-and-groove)
- Adjustable wrench (optional, for stubborn nuts)
- Old towel or rag
- Gloves (optional)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Cleaning brush or old toothbrush
- All-purpose cleaner or vinegar solution

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area Under the Sink

Before you begin, clear out any items from the cabinet directly beneath your sink to provide ample working space. Place an old towel on the cabinet floor and position a small bucket or container underneath the drain pipe. This will catch any water that might drip out when components are disconnected, keeping your cabinet dry.

### Step 2: Locate the Pivot Rod and Clevis Strap

Look directly under the sink, behind the drain pipe. You'll see a horizontal metal rod, known as the pivot rod, that connects to a vertical strap with several holes (the clevis strap). This strap is usually secured to the drain's lift rod (the knob you pull up and down on top of the sink) with a spring clip or setscrew.

### Step 3: Disconnect the Pivot Rod

Identify the nut holding the pivot rod into the drain body. Place your bucket directly under this connection. Using pliers or an adjustable wrench, carefully loosen and remove this nut. Once the nut is off, gently pull the pivot rod straight out from the drain assembly. Be prepared for a small amount of water to escape from the opening.

### Step 4: Remove the Sink Stopper

With the pivot rod disconnected, the stopper inside the sink basin is now free. Simply reach into the sink drain and lift the stopper straight up and out. Some stoppers might require a slight wiggle or twist to clear any remaining debris, but it should come out without resistance once the pivot rod is fully detached.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain

Inspect the removed stopper and the visible parts of the drain for hair, soap scum, and other debris that can cause clogs and odors. Use a cleaning brush or an old toothbrush with an all-purpose cleaner or a vinegar solution to thoroughly scrub the stopper. This is also a good opportunity to check under the sink; if you notice any dark spots or musty smells, you might have [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) to deal with in your cabinet.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Stopper (or Install New One)

Once the stopper and drain are clean, carefully insert the stopper back into the drain opening. Ensure it sits correctly. From underneath, reinsert the pivot rod through the hole in the drain body, making sure it passes through the loop or hole in the bottom of the stopper. Tighten the pivot rod nut securely with your pliers or wrench, being careful not to overtighten.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks and Functionality

Turn on the faucet and fill the sink with some water. Pull the lift rod up and down to ensure the stopper operates smoothly, opening and closing properly. While the sink is filling and draining, carefully check under the sink for any leaks around the pivot rod nut. If you notice any drips, tighten the nut slightly more until the leak stops.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take a photo of the assembly before you start disassembling it. This can be a great reference for reassembly.
- If the pivot rod nut is very stubborn, a penetrating oil can help loosen it, but be careful not to get it on plastic parts.
- Regularly removing and cleaning your sink stopper can prevent significant clogs and foul odors in the future.
- Keep all small parts, like spring clips and nuts, in a small container so they don't get lost.
- If the stopper doesn't seal well after reassembly, check that the pivot rod is properly seated in the stopper's loop.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the water supply to the sink if you are concerned about accidental leaks, though it's usually not necessary for just removing the stopper.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when tightening nuts to avoid stripping threads or cracking plastic components.
- ⚠️ Wear gloves if you are sensitive to cleaning agents or if the drain contents are particularly unpleasant.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes and are often unnecessary if you regularly clean your stopper.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my sink stopper not holding water?**

Your sink stopper might not be holding water because it's either not properly seated, or there's an accumulation of hair and debris preventing a tight seal. The pivot rod underneath the sink might also be misaligned or loose, affecting the stopper's movement.

**How often should I remove and clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to remove and clean your sink stopper every few months, especially if you notice slow drainage or unpleasant odors. Regular cleaning prevents buildup of hair, soap scum, and grime that can lead to clogs.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without tools?**

While some older or simpler stoppers might just lift out, most modern pop-up stoppers require tools like pliers to disconnect the pivot rod underneath the sink. Attempting to force it out without disconnecting the rod can damage the drain assembly.

**What if I can't reach the pivot rod under the sink?**

If space is tight, try using a flashlight to get a better view and smaller tools like long-nose pliers or a basin wrench. Sometimes, temporarily removing items from the cabinet or even the cabinet drawer can provide better access.

**My sink stopper is broken; can I replace just the stopper?**

Yes, in many cases, you can replace just the stopper itself, or the entire pop-up assembly if needed. Take the old stopper to a hardware store to ensure you purchase a compatible replacement that fits your drain size and pivot rod connection.

**Will removing the stopper help with a clogged drain?**

Removing the stopper often helps significantly with minor clogs, as much of the blockage (hair, soap scum) accumulates directly around the stopper and the top part of the drain. Once removed, you can easily clean out visible debris.

**What's the difference between a pop-up stopper and a push-pull stopper?**

A pop-up stopper is controlled by a lift rod behind the faucet, which operates a pivot rod to move the stopper. A push-pull stopper (or "toe-touch" stopper) is operated by pressing down on the stopper itself to close or open it, and it typically doesn't have an external linkage.
