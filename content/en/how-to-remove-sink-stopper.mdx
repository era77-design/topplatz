To remove a sink stopper, especially a common pop-up type, you typically need to access the pivot rod mechanism located under the sink. By loosening a retaining nut and pulling out the pivot rod, the stopper can then be lifted directly out of the drain opening from above.

## What You Need

- Bucket or small container
- Pliers (slip-joint or tongue-and-groove)
- Rags or towels
- Flashlight (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush (for cleaning)
- Mild cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, clear out any items from under the sink cabinet to give yourself ample room to work. Place a bucket or small container directly under the drain pipe to catch any residual water that might leak out when components are disconnected. Lay down a few old rags or towels to protect the cabinet floor from spills.

### Step 2: Identify Your Stopper Type

Most sink stoppers are either a 'pop-up' style, controlled by a lift rod behind the faucet, or a 'push-pull' or 'lift-and-turn' style, which you operate directly from the drain opening. This guide primarily focuses on the common pop-up stopper, which has a mechanism connected to a horizontal pivot rod under the sink. For push-pull or lift-and-turn types, you might simply unscrew or pull the stopper directly from the drain.

### Step 3: Locate the Pivot Rod Under the Sink

For a pop-up stopper, look directly under the sink basin for the drain pipe. You will see a horizontal metal rod, called the pivot rod, protruding into the drain pipe. This rod is usually held in place by a large retaining nut that screws into the drain assembly. A vertical strap (clevis strap) connects the pivot rod to the lift rod coming from the faucet.

### Step 4: Disconnect the Pivot Rod

Using your pliers, carefully loosen the large retaining nut that secures the pivot rod to the drain pipe. Once loose, unscrew it completely by hand and slide it off the rod. Gently pull the pivot rod straight out of the drain pipe; be prepared for a small amount of water to drip into your bucket. Disconnect the pivot rod from the clevis strap if it's still attached.

### Step 5: Remove the Stopper from Above

With the pivot rod completely removed from the drain pipe, the stopper inside the sink is now free. Go back to the top of the sink and simply lift the stopper straight up and out of the drain opening. It should come out easily without any resistance.

### Step 6: Clean and Inspect the Stopper and Drain

Now that the stopper is removed, thoroughly clean it of any hair, soap scum, or debris using an old brush and mild cleaner. This is also an excellent opportunity to clean the inside of the drain opening and check for any blockages or buildup. If you notice persistent moisture issues or unusual discoloration in the cabinet or around the drain, it might be worth investigating for other issues, such as mold. ### Step 7: Reinstall the Stopper (Reverse Process)

To reinstall, drop the clean stopper back into the drain opening. From under the sink, reinsert the pivot rod through the hole in the drain pipe, making sure it engages the loop or hole at the bottom of the stopper. Hand-tighten the retaining nut back onto the drain pipe, then use pliers for a final snug turn. Reconnect the clevis strap to the pivot rod and test the stopper's operation.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket ready under the drain to catch any water when disconnecting the pivot rod.
- Take a photo of the assembly under the sink before you start dismantling it; this can be helpful for reassembly.
- If the retaining nut is stubborn, a penetrating oil applied a few minutes beforehand can help loosen it. Just be careful not to get it on plastic components.
- When cleaning, an old toothbrush is excellent for reaching tight spots on the stopper and inside the drain opening.
- After reassembly, fill the sink with water and then drain it, checking carefully under the sink for any leaks around the retaining nut.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not overtighten plastic retaining nuts as they can crack.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using pliers not to scratch chrome finishes on visible parts of the drain or faucet.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners immediately before or after working on the drain assembly, as they can be corrosive and harmful if they come into contact with skin or eyes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my sink stopper not holding water?**

A sink stopper that won't hold water often indicates that the rubber gasket or seal on the stopper is worn out or missing. Alternatively, the pivot rod might not be engaging the stopper correctly, or the retaining nut under the sink could be loose, allowing water to escape.

**How do I remove a stopper without access under the sink?**

If you cannot access under the sink (e.g., for a push-pull or lift-and-turn stopper), try twisting the stopper counter-clockwise to see if it unscrews. Some simply pull straight up. For pop-up stoppers, under-sink access is almost always required.

**What if the pivot rod is stuck?**

If the pivot rod is stuck, it might be corroded or have accumulated mineral deposits. Try gently wiggling it while pulling. Applying a penetrating lubricant around the rod and letting it sit for a few minutes can also help loosen it.

**Can I replace just the stopper, or do I need a whole new drain assembly?**

Often, you can replace just the stopper itself if it's damaged or discolored, especially for universal sizes. However, if the entire drain body, pivot rod, or retaining nut is corroded or broken, replacing the whole drain assembly might be a better long-term solution.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice slow drainage or unpleasant odors. Regular cleaning prevents the buildup of hair, soap scum, and grime that can lead to clogs and hygiene issues.

**My stopper has a push-button mechanism. How do I remove that?**

Push-button (or 'click-clack') stoppers are typically removed by unscrewing the top cap counter-clockwise. Once the cap is off, the entire mechanism often lifts straight out of the drain opening, allowing for cleaning or replacement.

**What tools do I need for a basic sink stopper removal?**

For most common pop-up sink stoppers, you'll primarily need slip-joint or tongue-and-groove pliers to loosen the retaining nut under the sink. A bucket or small container is essential for catching water, and a flashlight can be helpful for visibility.
