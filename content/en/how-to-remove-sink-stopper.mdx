To remove a sink stopper, you typically start by identifying its type: a pop-up stopper, a lift-and-turn, or a push-pull stopper. Most require detaching a clevis or pivot rod assembly located under the sink, or simply unscrewing the stopper itself from the drain opening. This process is usually straightforward and can be done with basic tools.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or tongue-and-groove recommended)
- Screwdriver (flathead or Phillips, depending on fasteners)
- Flashlight (optional, for under-sink visibility)
- Bucket or small container
- Towels or rags
- Gloves (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush
- All-purpose cleaner or soap

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, ensure you have adequate lighting, especially if working under the sink. Clear out any items from the cabinet directly beneath the sink to give yourself room to maneuver. Place a bucket or small container and some old towels directly under the drain pipe to catch any drips or water that might escape when you disconnect parts. This preparation helps keep your area clean and prevents potential water damage.

### Step 2: Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Sink stoppers come in several common varieties. A 'pop-up' stopper is usually connected to a lever behind the faucet and has a pivot rod assembly under the sink. A 'lift-and-turn' stopper has a small knob on top that you twist and lift. A 'push-pull' stopper simply pushes down to close and pulls up to open. Knowing your stopper type will guide your removal method.

### Step 3: Access the Pivot Rod (for Pop-Up Stoppers)

For pop-up stoppers, you'll need to work under the sink. Locate the horizontal pivot rod that extends into the drain pipe. It's usually held in place by a large nut, called a retaining nut or clevis screw, which you can loosen by hand or with pliers. Once loosened, gently pull the pivot rod straight out of the drain pipe. Be ready for a small amount of water to drain out as you remove the rod.

### Step 4: Remove the Stopper from the Drain

With the pivot rod removed (for pop-up stoppers), the stopper should now be free to lift straight out of the drain opening from above the sink. For lift-and-turn stoppers, simply twist the knob counter-clockwise to unscrew the stopper from its threaded post. Push-pull stoppers usually just pull straight up, but some might have a small screw on top that needs to be loosened first. Examine the top of the stopper for any visible fasteners.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain Opening

Once the stopper is removed, you'll likely find hair, soap scum, and other debris accumulated around it and inside the drain opening. Use an old toothbrush or small brush to scrub the stopper clean with soap and water. For the drain opening, carefully remove any visible obstructions. If you notice significant mold buildup around the drain or on the stopper itself, ensure you clean it thoroughly, similar to how you would approach [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) in other areas of your home.

### Step 6: Reinstall the Stopper (If Desired)

If you're reinstalling the stopper, reverse the removal process. For pop-up stoppers, drop the stopper back into the drain, ensuring the hole on the side faces the back of the sink. Reinsert the pivot rod through the hole and tighten the retaining nut. For lift-and-turn or push-pull stoppers, simply screw or push them back into place. Test the stopper's function to ensure it seals properly and moves freely.


## Tips & Tricks

- Wear gloves to protect your hands from grime and cleaning agents.
- Take a photo of the under-sink assembly before you start disassembly; it can be a helpful reference for reassembly.
- If the retaining nut under the sink is very tight, a little penetrating oil can help loosen it, but be careful not to get it on plastic components.
- Regularly removing and cleaning your sink stopper can prevent slow drains and unpleasant odors.
- Consider replacing old, corroded stoppers with new ones, especially if they are difficult to clean or no longer seal effectively.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws when reinstalling, as this can damage plumbing fixtures.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using tools under the sink to avoid bending or cracking pipes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why won't my sink stopper come out?**

It's likely still connected to its mechanism. For pop-up stoppers, you must first disconnect the pivot rod assembly under the sink. For other types, check for a small screw on the top or ensure you're twisting it in the correct direction to unscrew it.

**How do I clean a sink stopper after removing it?**

Once removed, use an old toothbrush or small brush with warm water and dish soap to scrub away hair, soap scum, and grime. For stubborn buildup, a mild all-purpose cleaner can be effective. Rinse thoroughly before reinstalling.

**What are the common types of sink stoppers?**

The most common types are pop-up stoppers (connected to a lever behind the faucet), lift-and-turn stoppers (with a small knob you twist and lift), and push-pull stoppers (which you push down to close and pull up to open).

**Can I remove a sink stopper without any tools?**

Some stoppers, like basic push-pull or lift-and-turn types, can often be removed by hand simply by twisting or pulling. However, pop-up stoppers typically require pliers to loosen the retaining nut under the sink before they can be removed.

**How often should I remove my sink stopper for cleaning?**

It's a good practice to remove and clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice the drain slowing down or detect an odor. Regular cleaning prevents major blockages and keeps your drain functioning smoothly.

**What if the pivot rod under the sink is stuck?**

If the pivot rod is stuck, try applying a small amount of penetrating oil or WD-40 to the retaining nut and let it sit for a few minutes. Use slip-joint pliers to gently try and loosen the nut. Avoid excessive force to prevent damaging the plumbing.

**Can I replace my old sink stopper with a new one?**

Yes, you can often replace an old sink stopper. Ensure you measure the diameter of your drain opening and the length of the old stopper to get a compatible replacement. Many universal stoppers are available that fit various drain sizes.
