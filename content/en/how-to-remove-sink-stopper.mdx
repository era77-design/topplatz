To remove a sink stopper, first identify its type. For most pop-up stoppers, you'll need to locate and detach the pivot rod underneath the sink, then simply lift the stopper out. Other stopper types may unscrew or pull straight out without tools.

## What You Need

- Pliers or adjustable wrench
- Bucket or old towel
- Gloves (optional)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Old toothbrush or cleaning brush
- Paper towels or rags
- Mild degreaser or cleaner

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, clear out any items from the cabinet directly under your sink to give yourself ample room to work. Place a bucket or an old towel directly beneath the drainpipe assembly. This will catch any water that might drip when you detach components, keeping your cabinet dry and clean.

### Step 2: Locate the Pivot Rod (for Pop-Up Stoppers)

For the most common pop-up sink stoppers, you'll need to work from underneath the sink. Look directly below the drain opening for a horizontal rod, known as the pivot rod, entering the side of the drainpipe. This rod is usually secured by a large, threaded nut that holds it in place.

### Step 3: Detach the Pivot Rod

Using pliers or an adjustable wrench, carefully loosen and unscrew the nut that secures the pivot rod. Once the nut is loose, gently pull the pivot rod straight out from the drainpipe. Be prepared for a small amount of water to drip out when the rod is removed, which is normal. Keep the nut and rod nearby for reassembly.

### Step 4: Remove the Stopper

Now that the pivot rod is detached, the stopper above the sink is free. From the top of the sink, simply reach into the drain opening and lift the stopper straight out. It should come out easily without any resistance. For other stopper types (like push-pull or lift-and-turn), they might simply unscrew from the top or pull straight out without needing to access the pivot rod.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain Area

This is a crucial step for maintaining good drainage and hygiene. Use an old toothbrush or a cleaning brush and a mild degreaser or cleaner to thoroughly remove accumulated hair, soap scum, and grime from the stopper and the upper drain pipe. If you notice any mold growth around the drain opening, address it promptly. For more extensive mold issues, consult resources like [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls).

### Step 6: Reinstall the Stopper

Carefully lower the clean stopper back into the drain opening from above the sink. From underneath, guide the pivot rod back into its hole in the drainpipe, ensuring it engages with the loop or hole at the bottom of the stopper. This connection allows the stopper to move up and down. Secure the pivot rod by tightening its nut firmly with pliers or a wrench, being careful not to overtighten.

### Step 7: Test for Operation and Leaks

Run water into the sink and operate the stopper several times to ensure it seals properly when closed and opens freely. While the water is running and the stopper is in use, carefully check under the sink for any leaks around the pivot rod nut. If you notice any drips, tighten the nut slightly more until the leak stops.


## Tips & Tricks

- For push-pull or lift-and-turn stoppers, often you can simply unscrew the top knob or pull the stopper straight out without needing to go under the sink.
- If your stopper is stuck due to excessive grime, try twisting it gently while pulling upwards after detaching the pivot rod.
- Regularly cleaning your sink stopper every few months can prevent clogs, slow drainage, and unpleasant odors.
- If you experience persistent small leaks around the pivot rod nut after reinstallation, applying a small amount of plumber's tape to the threads before tightening can help create a better seal.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always place a bucket or towel under the drain area to catch water when working on plumbing components.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes, stoppers, and potentially harm your skin or eyes.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to drop small parts or tools down the drain while working, as they can cause further clogs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why won't my sink stopper come out?**

For most pop-up stoppers, it's held in place by a pivot rod underneath the sink. You'll need to locate and disconnect this rod from the drain pipe to free the stopper. Other types might be screwed in or simply stuck with grime.

**How do I remove a stopper without a pivot rod?**

Many modern stoppers, such as some push-pull, drop-in, or lift-and-turn types, are designed to simply lift out or unscrew from the top without needing to access anything underneath the sink. Try twisting and pulling gently upwards.

**What tools do I need to remove a sink stopper?**

For common pop-up stoppers, you'll typically need pliers or an adjustable wrench to loosen the pivot rod nut. A bucket or towel is also highly recommended to catch any drips, and an old toothbrush for cleaning.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to remove and clean your sink stopper every few months, or whenever you notice slow drainage, standing water, or unpleasant odors coming from the drain. Regular cleaning prevents buildup.

**Can I replace my sink stopper with a different type?**

Yes, many universal replacement stoppers are available at hardware stores. Ensure the new stopper is compatible with your drain opening size and preferred operation, such as a simple drop-in or a push-pop style.

**What if the pivot rod nut is too tight or corroded?**

If the nut is stuck, try applying a penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes. Use good quality pliers or a wrench for better grip. In cases of severe corrosion, you might need to replace the entire drain assembly, including the pivot rod.

**What if my sink stopper is still leaking after reinstallation?**

First, ensure the pivot rod nut is tightened securely but not overtightened. If it still leaks, try adding a small amount of plumber's tape to the threads of the pivot rod before reinserting it, or consider replacing the rubber washer inside the pivot rod nut if it's old or damaged.
