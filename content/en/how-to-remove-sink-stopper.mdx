To remove a sink stopper, typically a pop-up type, you'll need to disconnect the pivot rod assembly located under the sink. This usually involves loosening a nut, detaching the clip, and then pulling the stopper straight up from the drain opening. This process allows for cleaning, unclogging, or replacing the stopper.

## What You Need

- Bucket or small container
- Pliers (slip-joint or channel-lock)
- Rag or old towel
- Flashlight (optional)
- Rubber gloves (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Clear any items from around and under the sink. Place a bucket or small container directly under the drain pipe where the stopper mechanism is located to catch any drips or dropped parts. Have a rag or old towel nearby for spills and cleanup.

### Step 2: Locate the Pivot Rod Assembly

Under the sink, look for the drainpipe that extends from the sink basin. You should see a horizontal rod, known as the pivot rod, entering the side of this pipe. This rod is connected to the sink stopper inside the drain via a clevis strap and a spring clip.

### Step 3: Disconnect the Pivot Rod

First, squeeze the spring clip to detach the clevis strap from the pivot rod. This allows the rod to move freely. Next, locate the large retaining nut where the pivot rod enters the drainpipe. Use your pliers to carefully loosen this nut, then gently pull the pivot rod straight out of the drainpipe.

### Step 4: Remove the Stopper

With the pivot rod completely disconnected and removed from the drainpipe, you can now access the stopper from the sink basin. Grasp the sink stopper by its top knob or edge and simply pull it straight up and out of the drain opening. It should lift out easily without resistance.

### Step 5: Clean the Stopper and Drain

Use an old toothbrush or small brush, along with soap and water, to thoroughly clean the stopper. Remove any accumulated hair, soap scum, grime, or other debris. You can also clean inside the drain opening as far as you can reach with your brush to remove any visible buildup.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Stopper

If you're reinstalling the same stopper, insert it back into the drain opening. From underneath the sink, carefully reinsert the pivot rod into the drainpipe, ensuring its end catches the hole in the stopper. Tighten the retaining nut firmly by hand, then use pliers for a final snug turn. Reattach the clevis strap to the pivot rod using the spring clip. Test the stopper's function to ensure it opens and closes correctly and check for any leaks.


## Tips & Tricks

- Place a towel in the sink basin before starting to prevent small parts from falling down the drain.
- Take a photo of the pivot rod assembly before disassembling it; this can be a helpful reference for reassembly.
- If the stopper is stuck, gently wiggle it while pulling upwards after the pivot rod is disconnected.
- Regularly cleaning your sink stopper can prevent significant hair and soap scum buildup, keeping your drain clear.
- Consider replacing the rubber gaskets around the pivot rod if you notice any leaks after reassembly.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Be careful not to overtighten plastic retaining nuts, as they can crack easily.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves if you are uncomfortable handling potentially unsanitary drain gunk.
- ⚠️ Do not use excessive force when disconnecting or reassembling parts to avoid damaging your plumbing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why would I need to remove my sink stopper?**

Removing your sink stopper is typically done for cleaning purposes, to clear minor clogs caused by hair or debris, or to replace a damaged or broken stopper. It helps maintain good drainage and hygiene in your sink.

**What if my sink stopper doesn't have a visible pivot rod underneath?**

Some stoppers are push-pull, lift-and-turn, or simply drop-in types. For these, you usually just unscrew the top knob, twist and lift, or pull the stopper directly from the drain opening without needing to go under the sink.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every month or two, especially in high-use sinks like bathrooms. This prevents the buildup of hair, soap scum, and other debris that can cause slow drains and odors.

**What tools are essential for removing a sink stopper?**

You'll primarily need a bucket to catch drips and pliers (like slip-joint or channel-lock) to loosen the retaining nut. A rag is useful for cleanup, and a flashlight can be helpful for better visibility under the sink.

**I've cleaned the stopper, but my sink still drains slowly. What should I do next?**

If the drain is still slow after cleaning the stopper, the clog might be further down the pipe. You could try a drain snake, a natural drain cleaner, or consider calling a plumber for a more stubborn blockage.

**Can I replace just the sink stopper, or do I need to replace the entire drain assembly?**

In most cases, you can replace just the stopper itself, especially if it's a standard pop-up type. Ensure you purchase a replacement stopper that is compatible with your existing drain assembly's size and pivot rod mechanism.

**Are there any warnings I should be aware of when removing a sink stopper?**

Be careful not to overtighten plastic retaining nuts during reassembly, as they can crack. Also, wear gloves if you prefer, as the gunk in drains can be unsanitary. Avoid using harsh chemicals that could damage your pipes.
