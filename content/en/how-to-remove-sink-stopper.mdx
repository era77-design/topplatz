Removing a sink stopper is a common task for cleaning drains, retrieving dropped items, or performing repairs. Most sink stoppers, including pop-up, lift-and-turn, and push-pull types, can be removed with basic tools or even by hand. This process typically involves disconnecting the pivot rod mechanism beneath the sink or unscrewing the stopper itself.

## What You Need

- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Screwdriver (Phillips or flathead)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Bucket or old towel
- Gloves (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush
- Cleaning solution

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Before you begin, determine what kind of sink stopper you have. Pop-up stoppers are connected to a lever behind the faucet, while lift-and-turn stoppers have a knob you twist to lock them open or closed. Push-pull (or toe-touch) stoppers simply press down to close and press again to open. Understanding your stopper type will guide the removal process.

### Step 2: Clear the Area Under the Sink

Place a bucket or an old towel directly under the sink's drain pipe where the stopper mechanism is located. This will catch any drips or water that might spill during the removal process. Ensure you have enough light, using a flashlight if necessary, to clearly see the connections.

### Step 3: For Pop-Up Stoppers: Disconnect the Pivot Rod

Locate the horizontal pivot rod that extends from the drain pipe to the vertical lift rod. You'll usually find a retaining nut or clip holding this rod in place. Loosen the nut with an adjustable wrench or pliers, then carefully pull the pivot rod out of the drain pipe. Be prepared for a small amount of water to escape.

> 💡 **Tip:** Some pivot rods have a spring clip instead of a nut; simply squeeze and slide it off.

### Step 4: Remove the Pop-Up Stopper

Once the pivot rod is disconnected, you can simply lift the pop-up stopper straight out from the drain opening in the sink basin. It might require a gentle wiggle to free it from any grime or hair. If it feels stuck, try rotating it slightly while pulling up.

### Step 5: For Lift-and-Turn or Push-Pull Stoppers: Unscrew the Stopper

These types of stoppers are typically threaded into the drain assembly. For lift-and-turn stoppers, grasp the knob and turn counter-clockwise to unscrew and remove it. Push-pull stoppers often have a cap that unscrews, revealing a screw underneath; use a screwdriver to remove this screw, then lift the stopper out.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the stopper is very tight, a rubber grip pad can help you get a better hold.

### Step 6: Clean the Stopper and Drain Opening

With the stopper removed, thoroughly clean it and the inside of the drain opening using an old toothbrush or small brush and a cleaning solution. This is an excellent opportunity to remove accumulated hair, soap scum, and grime that can cause slow drains. If you notice any mold, this would be a good time to address it, though for general mold removal, you might find tips in [How to Remove Mold from Walls: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls).

### Step 7: Reinstall the Sink Stopper

After cleaning, reinstall the stopper by reversing the removal steps. For pop-up stoppers, insert it into the drain, then reinsert the pivot rod through the hole in the stopper's shaft and secure it with the nut or clip. For threaded stoppers, simply screw them back into place. Test the stopper's function and check for any leaks.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a towel or bucket handy to catch any water or debris that might come out from under the sink.
- Take a photo of the under-sink assembly before you start, especially for pop-up stoppers, to help remember how to reassemble it.
- Regularly remove and clean your sink stopper to prevent clogs and maintain good drainage.
- If you encounter excessive resistance or can't remove a specific part, consult your sink's manual or a plumbing professional to avoid damaging the fixture.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply to the sink if you anticipate working on the drain for an extended period or if you are unsure about disconnecting pipes.
- ⚠️ Avoid using excessive force when loosening nuts or pulling out rods, as this can damage plumbing components.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when dealing with old, corroded pipes or fittings, as they can break easily.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why would I need to remove my sink stopper?**

You might need to remove your sink stopper to clean accumulated hair and grime that causes slow drainage, retrieve dropped items like jewelry, or perform repairs on the stopper mechanism itself. It's a key step in routine drain maintenance.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without tools?**

Some sink stoppers, particularly older pop-up models or simple drop-in stoppers, can often be removed by hand. However, many modern stoppers, especially pop-up types connected to a lever, will require basic tools like an adjustable wrench or pliers to disconnect the pivot rod.

**What's the difference between a pop-up and a lift-and-turn stopper?**

A pop-up stopper is controlled by a lift rod located behind the faucet, which operates a pivot rod under the sink to raise or lower the stopper. A lift-and-turn stopper, on the other hand, is operated by twisting a knob on the stopper itself to lock it in the open or closed position.

**My sink stopper is stuck. What should I do?**

If your sink stopper is stuck, it's often due to accumulated hair, soap scum, or corrosion. Try wiggling it gently while pulling up. For pop-up stoppers, ensure the pivot rod is fully disconnected. You might need to apply a penetrating lubricant if corrosion is severe, but always be careful not to damage plastic parts.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every 1-3 months, especially in bathroom sinks where hair and toothpaste accumulate quickly. Regular cleaning prevents clogs, eliminates odors, and keeps your drain functioning smoothly.

**What if I can't reconnect the pivot rod correctly?**

Reconnecting the pivot rod can be tricky. Ensure the stopper is positioned correctly in the drain and the pivot rod passes through the hole in the stopper's shaft. Adjust the retaining nut or clip to allow the stopper to fully close and open. If it's still not working, double-check the alignment and try adjusting the height of the lift rod behind the faucet.

**Can removing the stopper help with a slow drain?**

Yes, absolutely. Removing the stopper allows you to access and clean out the hair, soap scum, and other debris that often accumulate around the stopper and in the top part of the drain. This is a common cause of slow drains and can significantly improve water flow.
