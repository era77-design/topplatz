Removing a sink stopper is a common task for cleaning, unclogging, or replacement. Most bathroom sink stoppers are connected to a pivot rod assembly under the sink, while kitchen stoppers often lift out directly. This guide focuses on the most common types and how to remove them safely.

## What You Need

- Pliers (slip-joint or channel-lock recommended)
- Bucket or small container
- Towel/Rag
- Gloves (optional)
- Screwdriver (flathead or Phillips, if needed for clip)
- Cleaning brush/old toothbrush
- Plumber's grease (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Clear out any items from under the sink cabinet to give yourself ample room to work comfortably. Place a small bucket or container directly under the drainpipe where the stopper assembly is located; this will catch any residual water that might drip out when you disconnect components. Lay down a towel or rag to protect the cabinet floor from spills.

### Step 2: Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Most bathroom sinks feature a 'pop-up' stopper connected to a lever behind the faucet, which operates a pivot rod underneath the sink. Kitchen sinks often have a simple drop-in basket stopper or a push-pull type. This guide primarily focuses on the common pop-up stopper, which requires access from underneath the sink; simple drop-in stoppers usually just lift out.

### Step 3: Locate the Pivot Rod Assembly (Pop-Up Stoppers)

Crouch down and look directly under your sink basin. You should see the drainpipe extending downwards from the sink. About halfway down this pipe, you'll notice a horizontal rod (the pivot rod) extending into the side of the pipe, held in place by a large nut or a spring clip. This rod is what connects to and operates your stopper.

### Step 4: Disconnect the Pivot Rod

Carefully hold the nut that secures the pivot rod to the drainpipe. If it's a nut, use slip-joint pliers or channel-locks to loosen it by turning counter-clockwise. If it's a spring clip, you might need a flathead screwdriver to pry it off. Once the nut is loose or the clip is removed, gently pull the pivot rod straight out from the drainpipe. Be prepared for a small amount of water to drip out, which your bucket will catch.

### Step 5: Remove the Sink Stopper

With the pivot rod disconnected, go back to the top of the sink basin. The stopper should now be free to move without resistance from the lever. Simply lift the stopper straight up and out of the drain opening. It might be covered in accumulated hair, grime, or soap scum, which is often the reason for its removal.

### Step 6: Clean the Stopper and Drain

Thoroughly clean the removed stopper, removing all hair, soap scum, and debris using an old toothbrush or small brush and hot soapy water. While the stopper is out, take the opportunity to clean inside the drain opening as well, as significant grime can accumulate there. Inspect the area under the sink for any signs of leaks or moisture, which can sometimes lead to mold growth. If you notice any, addressing them promptly is important, and you can find guidance on [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls).

### Step 7: Reinstall the Stopper (Optional)

If you're reinstalling the same stopper, simply drop it back into the drain opening, ensuring the hole or slot on the stopper aligns with where the pivot rod will re-enter. From underneath, carefully reinsert the pivot rod through the drainpipe opening and through the stopper's hole. Tighten the nut or reattach the spring clip, ensuring it's snug but not overtightened, which could damage the pipe. Test the stopper's operation with the lever.


## Tips & Tricks

- **Lubricate:** A little plumber's grease on the pivot rod can help it move smoothly and prevent future corrosion and sticking.
- **Check Gaskets:** When reinstalling, ensure any rubber gaskets on the pivot rod nut are in good condition to prevent leaks. Replace them if they appear cracked or worn.
- **Regular Cleaning:** To prevent future clogs and odors, periodically remove and clean your sink stopper, especially in bathrooms where hair accumulates quickly.
- **Test for Leaks:** After reinstallation, fill the sink with water and then drain it, carefully checking under the sink for any drips around the pivot rod nut.
- **Different Stopper Types:** For kitchen sinks with basket strainers or simple drop-in stoppers, removal is usually as simple as lifting them out by hand.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ **Do not overtighten:** When re-securing the pivot rod nut, tighten it firmly but do not overtighten, as this can crack plastic pipes or strip threads.
- ⚠️ **Avoid harsh chemicals:** When cleaning, avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners if possible, as they can damage pipes and are hazardous. Stick to manual cleaning with brushes and soapy water.
- ⚠️ **Wear gloves:** Sink stoppers can be very dirty and covered in bacteria, so wearing gloves is advisable for hygiene and protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my sink stopper not closing properly?**

This is often due to debris like hair and soap scum blocking its movement, or the pivot rod being bent or misaligned. Removing and thoroughly cleaning the stopper and inspecting the pivot rod usually resolves the issue.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without tools?**

Simple drop-in or push-pull stoppers can often be removed by hand. However, most pop-up stoppers require tools like pliers to disconnect the pivot rod assembly underneath the sink for full removal.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, especially in high-use bathrooms where hair and debris tend to accumulate quickly. Regular cleaning prevents clogs, improves drainage, and keeps your drain smelling fresh.

**What if the pivot rod nut is stuck?**

If the nut is seized, you can try applying a penetrating oil (like WD-40) and letting it sit for a while before trying again with pliers or a pipe wrench. Be careful not to apply excessive force, which could damage the drainpipe.

**My sink stopper is broken; can I replace just the stopper?**

Yes, you can often purchase universal replacement stoppers that fit most standard drain sizes. Make sure to match the type (e.g., pop-up, lift-and-turn) and measure your drain opening for the best fit.

**How do I know if my sink stopper is a pop-up type?**

A pop-up stopper is typically controlled by a small lever or knob located on the back of your faucet. Pushing or pulling this lever will make the stopper go up or down in the drain opening.

**What if water still drains slowly after cleaning the stopper?**

If slow drainage persists after cleaning the stopper, the clog is likely further down the drainpipe beyond the stopper assembly. You may need to use a drain snake or call a plumber to clear the obstruction effectively.
