To remove a sink stopper, identify its type (pop-up, lift-and-turn, or push-pull) and then disconnect it from the linkage under the sink, if applicable, or simply unscrew and lift it out. This process is generally straightforward and helps with cleaning or repairs.

## What You Need

- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Screwdriver (Phillips or flathead, depending on stopper type)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Bucket or towel
- Gloves (optional)
- Cleaning brush or old toothbrush

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Before you begin, determine whether you have a pop-up stopper (connected to a rod behind the faucet), a lift-and-turn stopper (screws down and lifts to open), or a push-pull stopper (presses down to close, pulls up to open). Knowing the type will guide your removal process. Most bathroom sinks have pop-up stoppers.

### Step 2: Clear the Area Under the Sink

Place a bucket or an old towel directly under the drain pipe assembly beneath your sink. This will catch any drips or small parts that might fall during the removal process. Ensure you have enough light, using a flashlight if needed, to clearly see the connections.

### Step 3: Disconnect the Pop-Up Stopper Rod (If Applicable)

For pop-up stoppers, locate the horizontal pivot rod that connects to the stopper from the drain pipe. This rod typically has a retaining nut. Loosen this nut by hand or with an adjustable wrench, then pull the rod out. The stopper should now be free to be lifted from above the sink.

### Step 4: Remove Lift-and-Turn or Push-Pull Stoppers

If you have a lift-and-turn stopper, simply grasp the knob or top of the stopper and turn it counter-clockwise to unscrew it from the drain. For push-pull stoppers, they often just pull straight up and out. Some push-pull types might have a small screw or clip underneath the cap that needs to be loosened first.

### Step 5: Lift the Stopper Out of the Drain

Once disconnected or unscrewed, gently lift the stopper straight up and out of the sink drain opening. Be careful as there might be hair, grime, or standing water around the stopper and inside the drain. Have a paper towel or rag ready for immediate cleanup.

### Step 6: Clean the Stopper and Drain Area

With the stopper removed, thoroughly clean it using a brush and warm soapy water. This is also an excellent opportunity to clean the inside of the drain opening, where hair and soap scum often accumulate. If you notice any signs of mold around the drain or on the stopper, you can address it carefully. For persistent mold issues in the surrounding area, refer to our guide on [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls).

### Step 7: Reinstall the Stopper (Optional)

If you plan to reuse the stopper, reverse the steps for reinstallation. For pop-up stoppers, insert the stopper, then reinsert the pivot rod, ensuring it catches the stopper's loop, and tighten the retaining nut. For other types, simply screw or push them back into place. Test for proper function and ensure no leaks are present under the sink.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take a photo of the under-sink assembly before you start, especially for pop-up stoppers, to help with reassembly.
- Keep all small parts, like screws or nuts, in a small container so they don't get lost.
- If the stopper is heavily corroded or damaged, consider replacing it rather than just cleaning.
- Regularly cleaning your sink stopper can prevent clogs and unpleasant odors.
- Use a flashlight to get a better view of the connections under the sink.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply to the sink if you plan to disconnect any P-trap or drain pipes, to prevent accidental leaks.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when loosening or tightening nuts and screws to avoid stripping them or damaging plastic components.
- ⚠️ Wear gloves if you are concerned about touching grime or mold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why would I need to remove my sink stopper?**

Removing your sink stopper is often necessary for thorough cleaning, especially to clear hair and soap scum that can cause slow drains or odors. It's also required if you need to replace the stopper or access the drain for repairs.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without tools?**

Some simple lift-and-turn or push-pull stoppers can be removed by hand, especially if they just unscrew or pull straight out. However, pop-up stoppers typically require an adjustable wrench or pliers to loosen the pivot rod nut under the sink.

**What's the difference between a pop-up and a lift-and-turn stopper?**

A pop-up stopper is controlled by a rod located behind the faucet, which operates a linkage under the sink. A lift-and-turn stopper, on the other hand, is operated directly by twisting and lifting the stopper itself to seal or open the drain.

**My sink stopper is stuck. What should I do?**

If your stopper is stuck, try applying a small amount of penetrating oil to any visible threads or pivot points. For pop-up stoppers, check for hair or debris binding the pivot rod under the sink and clean it. Avoid forcing it, as this can cause damage.

**How do I clean the gunk off my sink stopper?**

After removing the stopper, use a stiff brush (like an old toothbrush) and warm soapy water to scrub away hair, soap scum, and grime. For stubborn buildup, a mixture of vinegar and baking soda or a commercial drain cleaner can be used, ensuring proper ventilation.

**Is it safe to put a new stopper in an old drain?**

Yes, it is generally safe to put a new stopper in an old drain, provided the new stopper is the correct size and type for your sink. Ensure the drain opening is clean and free of debris for a proper seal.

**Can a sink stopper be a source of bad smells?**

Absolutely. Hair, soap scum, and other organic matter can build up around and under the sink stopper, decomposing and causing foul odors. Regular removal and cleaning of the stopper and the immediate drain area can significantly reduce these smells.
