Removing a sink stopper is a straightforward task often needed for cleaning clogs or making repairs. For most common pop-up stoppers, this involves detaching a pivot rod located beneath the sink, allowing the stopper to be pulled free from above. The process typically takes only a few minutes with basic tools.

## What You Need

- Bucket or small container
- Pliers (slip-joint or tongue-and-groove)
- Adjustable wrench (optional, for stubborn nuts)
- Rag or old towel
- Flashlight (optional)
- Gloves (optional)
- Pipe cleaner or small brush

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, clear out any items from the cabinet directly beneath your sink. Place a bucket or a small container directly under the drain pipe assembly where the stopper's pivot rod enters. This will catch any residual water that might leak out during the removal process, preventing a mess. Have a rag or old towel handy for quick cleanups.

### Step 2: Identify Your Stopper Type

Most common sink stoppers are 'pop-up' types, connected to a horizontal pivot rod under the sink. Others include 'lift-and-turn' or 'push-pull' stoppers, which often simply unscrew or pull out from the drain opening without needing access underneath. This guide primarily focuses on the pop-up type, which requires accessing the pivot rod.

### Step 3: Locate the Pivot Rod Nut

Underneath the sink, look at the drain pipe. You'll see a horizontal rod, typically made of metal, extending into the side of the drain pipe. This is the pivot rod, and it's held in place by a large nut, often made of plastic or brass. This nut secures the rod and usually has a gasket to prevent leaks.

### Step 4: Remove the Pivot Rod

Using pliers or an adjustable wrench, carefully loosen the pivot rod nut. Once loose, pull the nut and the pivot rod straight out from the drain pipe. Be prepared for a small amount of dirty water to drip out into your bucket as you remove the rod. Keep the nut and rod together to avoid losing any parts.

### Step 5: Remove the Stopper

With the pivot rod completely removed from the drain pipe, you can now access the stopper from above the sink. Simply reach into the sink drain opening and lift the stopper straight up and out. It should come out easily without any resistance, as its connection to the pivot rod has been disengaged.

### Step 6: Clean and Inspect the Components

Now that the stopper is out, thoroughly clean the stopper itself, the inside of the drain opening, and the pivot rod opening. Use a brush or pipe cleaner to remove accumulated hair, soap scum, and grime, which often cause slow drainage. If you notice any mold buildup around the drain area, consider addressing it promptly to maintain hygiene. [How to Remove Mold from Walls: A Complete DIY Guide](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) can provide general guidance on mold removal, though for a sink, a diluted bleach solution or vinegar is often effective.

### Step 7: Reinstall and Test

Once everything is clean, reinsert the stopper into the drain opening from above. Make sure the hole in the bottom of the stopper aligns with where the pivot rod will re-enter. From underneath, carefully push the pivot rod back into its hole, ensuring it engages with the stopper's mechanism. Hand-tighten the pivot rod nut, then give it a quarter-turn with pliers to secure it without over-tightening. Run water into the sink and check for any leaks around the pivot rod nut.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a flashlight handy when working under the sink to ensure clear visibility of the pipes and connections.
- Do not overtighten the pivot rod nut; this can strip the threads or crack plastic components, leading to leaks.
- Regularly remove and clean your sink stopper to prevent excessive buildup of hair and soap scum, which causes clogs.
- If your stopper is old or damaged, consider replacing the entire stopper assembly for better performance and a fresh look.
- For stubborn clogs, after removing the stopper, you can use a drain snake or a chemical drain cleaner (follow product instructions carefully).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when handling dirty stoppers and drain components, as they can harbor bacteria and grime.
- ⚠️ Be prepared for some dirty water to spill from the drain pipe when removing the pivot rod; place your bucket directly underneath.
- ⚠️ Avoid using excessive force when loosening or tightening nuts to prevent damaging pipes or stripping threads.
- ⚠️ If you use chemical drain cleaners, ensure proper ventilation and follow all safety instructions on the product label.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why won't my sink stopper come out after removing the pivot rod?**

If the stopper doesn't lift out easily, it might be stuck due to corrosion or heavy grime buildup around its edges. Try gently wiggling it or rotating it while pulling upwards. Ensure the pivot rod is completely disengaged and not snagging on anything inside the drain.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every 1-3 months, or whenever you notice the sink draining slower than usual. Regular cleaning prevents the accumulation of hair, soap scum, and other debris that can lead to clogs and unpleasant odors.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without going under the sink?**

Yes, some types of stoppers, like simple drop-in stoppers, 'push-pull' stoppers, or 'lift-and-turn' stoppers, can often be removed by simply unscrewing them or pulling them straight out from above the drain, without needing to access the pivot rod underneath.

**What if my sink stopper is broken or missing parts?**

If your stopper is broken, corroded, or missing essential parts like the pivot rod or a gasket, it's usually best to replace the entire stopper assembly. These are readily available at hardware stores and are relatively inexpensive, often coming with all necessary components.

**What's the difference between a pop-up stopper and a lift-and-turn stopper?**

A pop-up stopper is operated by a lever behind the faucet, which connects to a pivot rod under the sink. A lift-and-turn stopper is operated by twisting and lifting the stopper itself directly from the drain opening, without an external lever or pivot rod.

**How do I prevent future clogs after removing the stopper?**

To prevent future clogs, consider using a hair catcher or drain screen in your sink. Regularly flush your drain with hot water, and perform periodic cleanings of the stopper and drain opening to remove accumulating debris before it becomes a problem.

**Do all sink stoppers have a pivot rod?**

No, not all sink stoppers use a pivot rod. While common for bathroom sinks, kitchen sinks often have basket strainers that simply drop in. Other types like 'push-pull' or 'lift-and-turn' stoppers operate differently and may not have a pivot rod mechanism.
