Removing a sink stopper typically involves identifying its type, then either twisting it out from above the drain or disconnecting a pivot rod located underneath the sink. This process allows for cleaning the stopper and clearing minor clogs.

## What You Need

- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Screwdriver (Phillips or flathead)
- Bucket or old towel
- Gloves (optional)
- Old toothbrush or small brush
- All-purpose cleaner or mild soap
- Flashlight

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Sink Stopper Type

Sink stoppers come in several common designs. A "pop-up" stopper usually has a lift rod behind the faucet that you pull up or push down. "Lift-and-turn" stoppers have a knob on top that you twist to lock or unlock, then lift. "Push-pull" stoppers simply push down to seal and pull up to open. Knowing your type will guide the removal process.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you're unsure, try gently twisting or pulling the stopper first to see how it operates.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Workspace

Before you begin, clear any items from under the sink to give yourself ample room to work. Place a bucket or an old towel directly beneath the drainpipe connections to catch any residual water or drips. Ensure you have good lighting, especially if you need to access components under the sink.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wearing gloves can help maintain hygiene, especially if you anticipate encountering grime.

### Step 3: For Pop-Up Stoppers: Locate the Pivot Rod

If you have a pop-up stopper, you'll need to access the pivot rod assembly under the sink. This is usually a horizontal rod extending from the drain pipe, connected to the lift rod by a strap. The pivot rod passes through the drain pipe and connects to the bottom of the stopper itself.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a flashlight to clearly see the components and connections under your sink.

### Step 4: Disconnect the Pivot Rod (Pop-Up Stoppers Only)

Find the nut that secures the pivot rod to the drain pipe. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to loosen and remove this nut. Carefully pull the pivot rod out from the drain pipe. Be prepared for a small amount of water to drip out when the rod is removed.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all nuts, washers, and the pivot rod in a safe place so they don't get lost during the process.

### Step 5: Remove the Stopper

Once the pivot rod is disconnected (for pop-up stoppers), or if you have a lift-and-turn or push-pull stopper, you can now remove the stopper from above the drain. For pop-up types, simply lift it straight out. For lift-and-turn or push-pull, twist counter-clockwise (if it has threads) and lift it out.

> 💡 **Tip:** Some stoppers might be a bit stiff due to accumulated grime, gently wiggle them as you pull.

### Step 6: Clean the Stopper and Drain Area

This is an excellent opportunity to clean the stopper and the inside of the drain opening. Use an old toothbrush or small brush with all-purpose cleaner or mild soap to scrub away hair, soap scum, and grime. This can significantly improve drainage and prevent odors. If you notice persistent mold buildup in the drain area, consider a more thorough cleaning. [How to Remove Mold from Walls Safely & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-mold-from-walls) can provide general guidance on safely tackling mold.

> 💡 **Tip:** Regularly cleaning your sink stopper can prevent future clogs and keep your drain smelling fresh.

### Step 7: Reinstall the Stopper and Test

After cleaning, carefully reinsert the stopper into the drain. For pop-up stoppers, reinsert the pivot rod through the drain pipe opening and into the hole at the bottom of the stopper, then tighten the securing nut. Reconnect the lift rod strap if applicable. Test the stopper's operation to ensure it seals and opens correctly, and check for any leaks under the sink.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not overtighten the pivot rod nut, as this can damage the drain pipe or the nut itself. Snug is sufficient.


## Tips & Tricks

- If your stopper is stuck due to excessive hair or debris, try pouring a mixture of baking soda and vinegar down the drain, followed by hot water, to loosen it before removal.
- For persistent clogs after removing the stopper, a drain snake or a plumbing auger can be used to reach deeper into the pipe.
- Consider replacing an old, corroded, or damaged stopper with a new one for better sealing and appearance.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply to the sink if you plan on disconnecting any major plumbing pipes, though this is usually not necessary for stopper removal.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes and are hazardous to handle.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to drop any small parts down the drain during the process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why would I need to remove my sink stopper?**

You might need to remove your sink stopper to clean it thoroughly, clear a clog caused by hair and debris, or replace a damaged or corroded stopper with a new one. Regular removal and cleaning can also prevent future drainage issues.

**Can I remove a sink stopper without going under the sink?**

Yes, many types of stoppers, such as lift-and-turn or push-pull models, can be removed by simply twisting and lifting them from above the drain. However, pop-up stoppers typically require you to disconnect a pivot rod from underneath the sink.

**What if my stopper is stuck and won't come out?**

If your stopper is stuck, it's likely due to accumulated hair, soap scum, or corrosion. Try gently wiggling it while pulling. For pop-up stoppers, ensure the pivot rod is fully disconnected. You can also try pouring hot water or a natural drain cleaner (like baking soda and vinegar) to loosen debris.

**How often should I clean my sink stopper?**

It's a good practice to clean your sink stopper every few months, or more frequently if you notice slow drainage or unpleasant odors. Regular cleaning helps prevent buildup of hair, soap scum, and grime, keeping your drain free-flowing.

**What tools do I need to remove a pop-up sink stopper?**

To remove a pop-up sink stopper, you'll typically need an adjustable wrench or pliers to loosen the nut securing the pivot rod under the sink. A flashlight is also helpful for visibility, and a bucket or towel for any drips.

**Can I replace my old sink stopper with a different type?**

Yes, it's often possible to replace your old sink stopper with a different type, such as converting a pop-up to a simple drop-in stopper. Ensure the new stopper is compatible with your drain opening's size and style for a proper fit and seal.

**What if I lose a part of the stopper assembly?**

If you lose a small part like a nut or washer, check the bucket or towel you placed under the sink. If it's gone, you might be able to find replacement parts at a local hardware store or plumbing supply shop. Take the remaining parts with you to ensure compatibility.
