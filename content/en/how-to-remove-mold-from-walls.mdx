To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. For small areas, a solution of white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution can be highly effective. Always address the underlying moisture source to prevent mold from returning.

## What You Need

- Safety glasses
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub pad
- Microfiber cloths or old towels
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Bucket
- Fan (for ventilation)
- Drop cloths or plastic sheeting

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, open windows and use fans to ensure good ventilation in the room. Lay down drop cloths or plastic sheeting to protect the floor and furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Put on your safety glasses, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from airborne spores and chemicals.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Identify the Source

Carefully examine the moldy area to determine its size and the type of surface affected. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to consult a professional. Identifying and fixing the moisture source, such as a leak or condensation issue, is crucial for preventing recurrence. For specific guidance on more severe cases, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most mold on walls, especially on painted surfaces, a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is effective and safer than bleach. For non-porous surfaces like tile, you can use a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water). Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the wall.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes, giving it time to penetrate and kill the mold. Use a stiff brush or scrub pad to gently scrub away the mold from the wall surface. Avoid scrubbing too aggressively on painted walls to prevent damaging the paint.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp microfiber cloth to remove any remaining mold spores and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use clean, dry towels and run a fan or dehumidifier in the room until the wall is entirely dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Place all used cloths, scrub pads, and drop cloths into a plastic bag immediately after cleaning. Seal the bag tightly and dispose of it in an outdoor trash can. This prevents any lingering mold spores from spreading to other areas of your home.

### Step 7: Implement Preventive Measures

To prevent future mold growth, ensure good ventilation in your home, especially in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Use exhaust fans and consider a dehumidifier if you live in a perpetually damp climate. Promptly fix any leaks and regularly inspect areas prone to moisture for early signs of mold.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear protective gear, including gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 mask, to prevent exposure to mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia, as it can create toxic fumes.
- Ensure the cleaned area is completely dry within 24-48 hours to prevent mold from regrowing.
- Address the source of moisture immediately to prevent future mold issues.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean large areas of mold (over 10 square feet) yourself; this indicates a larger issue that requires professional remediation.
- ⚠️ If you suspect the mold is black mold, or if anyone in your household has respiratory issues, consult a professional mold remediator.
- ⚠️ Avoid scrubbing dry mold, as this can release a large number of spores into the air, potentially spreading the problem and causing health risks.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when working with bleach; ensure the area is well-ventilated and avoid skin and eye contact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle. Spray the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or cloth. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to properly access and treat the affected area. This often indicates a significant moisture problem, such as a hidden leak, and may require professional mold remediation. It's crucial to identify and fix the moisture source before attempting any removal.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold grows on walls when there's an accumulation of moisture, combined with an organic food source (like drywall or paint) and a lack of ventilation. Common causes include high humidity, condensation, water leaks from pipes or the roof, and poor air circulation in a room.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), or if you suspect it's hidden behind walls. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively remove extensive mold and prevent its return.

**What's the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

For most mold on walls, especially on porous surfaces like drywall, undiluted white vinegar is an excellent, natural, and effective cleaner. For non-porous surfaces like tile, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can also be used. Always ensure proper ventilation when cleaning.

**Is it safe to clean mold myself?**

Cleaning small areas of mold (less than 10 square feet) is generally safe for homeowners, provided you wear appropriate personal protective equipment like gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 mask. However, if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or suspect black mold, it's safer to hire a professional.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, control humidity levels in your home by using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and a dehumidifier in damp areas. Promptly fix any leaks, ensure good air circulation, and keep surfaces dry. Regularly inspect for signs of moisture or new mold growth.
