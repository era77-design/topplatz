To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold's source and ensuring proper ventilation. Use a suitable cleaning solution like white vinegar or a bleach solution, apply it to the affected area, scrub gently, and allow it to dry completely. Always prioritize safety by wearing appropriate protective gear.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or coveralls
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or old toothbrush
- Microfiber cloths or sponges
- Bucket
- White distilled vinegar
- OR Household bleach (sodium hypochlorite)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional, for covering furniture)

## Steps

### Step 1: Gear Up and Ventilate the Area

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. If possible, use a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Put on your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Wear old clothes that you don't mind getting stained or disposing of afterward.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the moldy area is near furniture, move it or cover it with plastic sheeting to protect it from spores and cleaning agents.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold's Source and Type

Before cleaning, it's crucial to find and fix the source of moisture that caused the mold to grow. This could be a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Addressing the moisture problem is key to preventing mold from returning. While most surface mold can be cleaned, if you suspect [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) or large infestations, proceed with extreme caution.

> 💡 **Tip:** Common moisture sources include leaky roofs, plumbing issues, damp basements, and poor ventilation in bathrooms or kitchens.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, you have two main options. For a natural approach, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white distilled vinegar. For tougher mold, mix 1 cup of household bleach with 1 gallon of water in a bucket. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub the Mold

Carefully spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or an old toothbrush, gently scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections to avoid spreading spores.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid scrubbing too aggressively, especially on painted walls, as this can damage the paint or drywall surface.

### Step 5: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

Once the mold is removed, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residual cleaning solution and mold spores. It's essential that the wall dries completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process, which can take several hours.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry before considering any repainting or re-sealing, as trapped moisture will encourage mold to return.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Prevent Future Growth

Carefully dispose of all contaminated materials like cloths, sponges, and gloves in a sealed plastic bag. Wash any reusable items (like your scrub brush) thoroughly with hot, soapy water. To prevent future mold, maintain good ventilation, control indoor humidity levels (ideally below 60%), and promptly repair any leaks or water damage. Consider mold-resistant paint for high-humidity areas.

> 💡 **Tip:** Regularly inspect areas prone to mold growth, such as bathrooms and basements, to catch any new mold early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear proper protective gear, including an N95 mask, goggles, and gloves, to avoid inhaling spores or skin contact.
- For small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), DIY removal is generally safe. For larger infestations, consult a professional.
- Never paint over mold without first cleaning and treating the affected area, as this will only hide the problem and not solve it.
- Improve air circulation by using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and ensure adequate ventilation in basements and attics.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can produce toxic fumes that are extremely dangerous.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have a compromised immune system, respiratory conditions, or severe mold allergies; hire a professional instead.
- ⚠️ For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet), or if mold is due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), always consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a solution of either undiluted white distilled vinegar or a bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water). After scrubbing, wipe with clean water and ensure the wall dries completely to prevent damage to the paint and surface.

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way to remove mold from walls involves identifying and fixing the moisture source, then cleaning with a mild fungicide like white vinegar or a bleach solution. Always wear protective gear, ensure good ventilation, and thoroughly dry the area afterward to prevent recurrence.

**Can you remove mold from inside walls yourself?**

Removing mold from inside walls is generally not recommended for DIY. Mold inside walls often indicates a significant moisture problem and can be extensive. It's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist who can safely open the wall, remove the mold, and address the underlying issue.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the moldy area is larger than 10 square feet, if mold growth is due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), if you suspect mold is hidden inside walls, or if you or your family members experience health issues related to mold exposure.

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls again?**

Prevent mold by controlling indoor humidity levels (below 60%), ensuring proper ventilation in high-moisture areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and promptly fixing any leaks or water damage. Consider using mold-resistant paint in damp environments and regularly cleaning surfaces.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls myself?**

For small, superficial mold patches (under 10 square feet), it can be safe to remove mold yourself, provided you wear appropriate personal protective equipment like an N95 mask, gloves, and goggles. Always ensure good ventilation and never mix cleaning chemicals.
