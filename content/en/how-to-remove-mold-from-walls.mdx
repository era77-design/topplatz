To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the moisture source and addressing it. Then, prepare the area with proper ventilation and protective gear. For most surfaces, a mixture of water and bleach or vinegar can effectively kill and clean the mold, followed by thorough drying.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Old clothes/protective clothing
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- Bucket
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- White vinegar (alternative to bleach)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify and Fix the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there's moisture. Before you even think about cleaning, find out what's causing the dampness. This could be a leaky pipe, a humid room, or condensation. Repair any leaks and ensure good ventilation to prevent the mold from returning.

### Step 2: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Open windows and doors to ensure good ventilation. Turn off any HVAC systems to prevent mold spores from spreading throughout your home. Lay down plastic sheeting to protect the floor beneath the affected area. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask or respirator to protect yourself from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like tiles or sealed concrete, a solution of 1 part bleach to 10 parts water is effective. For painted walls or porous surfaces, undiluted white vinegar is a safer and often equally effective alternative. Fill a spray bottle with your chosen solution. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring complete saturation. If using bleach, allow it to sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold spores. For vinegar, allow it to sit for at least an hour for maximum effectiveness before proceeding.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away Mold

Using a stiff brush or a scrub sponge, scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections to avoid spreading spores to unaffected areas. Once the mold is loosened, wipe the area clean with a damp cloth or paper towels. Repeat the application and scrubbing process if necessary for stubborn stains.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, lightly mist the cleaned area with clean water to rinse away any residue from the cleaning solution and dead mold spores. Immediately dry the wall thoroughly using clean cloths, paper towels, or a fan. Ensuring the area is completely dry is crucial to prevent immediate regrowth and further issues.

### Step 7: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully place all used cloths, paper towels, and any plastic sheeting into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of this bag outside of your home to prevent reintroducing mold spores into your indoor environment. Clean your protective gear thoroughly before storing it for future use.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Good ventilation is absolutely critical during and after mold removal to disperse spores and fumes.
- Never paint over mold without first cleaning and treating the affected area; this will only hide the problem temporarily and allow it to continue growing.
- Promptly address any underlying moisture issues, such as leaks or high humidity, to prevent future mold growth.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or other household cleaners, as this can produce dangerous, toxic gases.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask or respirator.
- ⚠️ Avoid using bleach on porous materials like drywall or wood, as it may not effectively kill mold at its roots and can damage the material.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet), or if you suspect mold behind walls or within HVAC systems, it is best to call a professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

Start by fixing the moisture source, then ventilate the area and wear protective gear. Use a solution of bleach and water for non-porous surfaces, or undiluted white vinegar for painted or porous walls, then scrub and dry thoroughly.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, undiluted white vinegar is often the best and safest option. Spray it on, let it sit for an hour, then scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge and rinse with clean water before drying completely.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

To permanently kill mold on walls, you must not only clean it but also completely eliminate the source of moisture that allowed it to grow in the first place. Without addressing the moisture, mold will inevitably return.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is often a job for professionals due to the potential for extensive damage and the risk of widespread spore dispersal.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is behind walls or in your HVAC system, or if you have health concerns related to mold exposure.

**What prevents mold from growing back on walls?**

Preventing mold regrowth involves controlling moisture. Ensure good ventilation, fix leaks promptly, use dehumidifiers in humid areas, and keep surfaces clean and dry. Applying mold-inhibiting paint can also help.
