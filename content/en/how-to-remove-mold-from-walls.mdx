Removing mold from walls involves identifying and fixing the moisture source, then thoroughly cleaning the affected area using appropriate protective gear and solutions like vinegar or bleach. Prompt action is key to prevent its spread and maintain healthy indoor air quality.

## What You Need

- Rubber or nitrile gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 or P100 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Drop cloth or plastic sheeting
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- White vinegar (undiluted) OR household bleach (diluted)
- Warm water
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure good ventilation by opening windows and doors, and use a fan to direct air outside. Lay down a drop cloth to protect the floor. Crucially, wear protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 or P100 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the area if possible by closing interior doors to prevent spores from spreading to other rooms.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives on moisture. Before cleaning, find and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Failing to address the moisture will lead to the mold returning quickly after cleaning.

> 💡 **Tip:** Common sources include leaky roofs, plumbing issues, or poor ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold, white vinegar is effective and less harsh than bleach. Pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. If using bleach, mix 1 cup of household bleach with 1 gallon of water in a bucket. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as it creates toxic fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Generously spray the moldy area with your chosen solution. For vinegar, let it sit for at least an hour to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold. If using the bleach solution, let it sit for about 10-15 minutes. This dwell time is crucial for the solution to effectively kill the mold spores.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the entire affected area is saturated with the cleaning solution for best results.

### Step 5: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge, vigorously scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. As you scrub, dispose of any removed mold and dirty cloths into a sealed bag immediately to prevent spores from spreading.

> 💡 **Tip:** For persistent stains, you may need to repeat the application and scrubbing process.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry the Area Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the area clean with a damp cloth (using plain water) to remove any residue from the cleaning solution and dead mold spores. Then, it's critical to dry the wall completely. Use old towels, a fan, or even a dehumidifier to ensure no moisture remains, which could encourage new mold growth.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry before considering any repainting or sealing.

### Step 7: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully collect all used cloths, sponges, drop cloths, and any other contaminated materials. Place them in a heavy-duty plastic bag, seal it tightly, and dispose of it immediately in an outdoor trash receptacle. This prevents mold spores from lingering in your home.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always address the root cause of the moisture to prevent mold from returning.
- Never paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first, as this will only hide the problem and not solve it.
- Consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas like basements or bathrooms to maintain optimal humidity levels (ideally below 60%).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or any other household cleaners, as this can produce highly toxic and dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ For mold covering an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) or hidden mold inside walls, it is strongly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (gloves, goggles, N95 mask) to avoid direct contact with mold and inhaling spores, which can cause respiratory issues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

When removing mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of white vinegar or a mild bleach solution (1/4 cup bleach per gallon of water) applied with a soft sponge or cloth is usually effective. Test in an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't strip the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a more complex issue, often indicating a significant moisture problem that has penetrated the wall structure. This typically requires opening up the wall to properly clean, dry, and potentially replace affected materials. If the mold is extensive, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

To permanently get rid of mold on walls, you must first eliminate the moisture source that allows it to grow. After cleaning the visible mold with a fungicide like vinegar or bleach, ensure the area remains dry and well-ventilated to prevent its return. Without addressing the moisture, mold will almost certainly reappear.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's black mold, if you have health concerns, or if the mold is hidden inside walls or HVAC systems. Professionals have the expertise and equipment for safe and thorough remediation of extensive or hazardous mold.

**Can I just paint over mold?**

No, you should never just paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; it will only hide it temporarily. The mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, potentially causing further damage to your wall and releasing spores into the air. Always clean and dry the area thoroughly before painting.

**How do I prevent mold from returning after removal?**

To prevent mold from returning, consistently control moisture levels in your home. Fix leaks promptly, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and consider a dehumidifier in humid areas to keep relative humidity below 60%. Regular cleaning and inspection of damp areas also help.
