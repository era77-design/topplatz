Removing mold from walls typically involves cleaning the affected surface with a diluted bleach solution or vinegar, ensuring proper ventilation, and drying the area thoroughly to prevent recurrence. Always wear protective gear and address the moisture source to stop mold growth.

## What You Need

- Safety glasses
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Two buckets
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting or old tarps

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before tackling mold, ensure your safety and protect your surroundings. Open windows and doors to ventilate the space thoroughly. Lay down old sheets or plastic tarps to protect floors and furniture from spores and cleaning solutions. Don your safety glasses, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to prevent inhaling spores and protect your skin from irritants.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives on moisture, so cleaning it without addressing the source will lead to its return. Inspect the area for leaks from pipes, roofs, or windows, and check for condensation issues or poor ventilation. Repair any leaks promptly and improve air circulation to prevent future growth. This step is critical for long-term mold prevention.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like most painted walls, you have effective options. White vinegar, used undiluted, is a natural and potent mold killer. Alternatively, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup household bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but it's crucial never to mix bleach with other cleaners like ammonia, as it creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

If using vinegar, pour it undiluted into a spray bottle and saturate the moldy area. If using bleach, mix it in one of your buckets and apply with a sponge or cloth. Allow the chosen solution to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes. This dwell time allows the solution to penetrate and effectively kill the mold spores.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff brush or a scrub sponge, vigorously scrub the treated area to remove the dead mold. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. After scrubbing, use a clean, damp microfiber cloth to wipe the area clean, rinsing the cloth frequently in a second bucket of clean water to avoid spreading spores.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

Proper drying is absolutely essential to prevent immediate mold regrowth. Use clean, dry microfiber cloths to absorb as much moisture as possible from the cleaned wall surface. Then, utilize fans or a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is completely dry, which can take several hours. Do not skip this crucial step.

### Step 7: Dispose of Contaminated Materials and Monitor

Carefully bag and dispose of any cloths, brushes, or tarps used during the cleaning process to prevent spreading spores. Keep a close eye on the cleaned area for several days or weeks to confirm the mold hasn't returned. Continue to maintain good ventilation and promptly address any new moisture sources to keep your walls mold-free.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the paint.
- Improve ventilation in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans or opening windows during and after moisture-producing activities.
- Do not paint over mold; it will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the new paint.
- For porous surfaces like drywall that are heavily affected, replacement is often more effective than cleaning.
- Consider using a dehumidifier in basements or other naturally damp areas to keep humidity levels below 60%.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach and ammonia-based cleaners, as this creates highly toxic and dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including an N95 mask, safety glasses, and rubber gloves, to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's generally recommended to consult with a professional mold remediation specialist. Learn more about [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first fix any moisture issues. Then, clean the affected area with a solution of white vinegar or diluted bleach, scrub the mold away, and ensure the wall is completely dry. Always wear protective gear during the process.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls involves gently scrubbing the affected area with a mild cleaning solution. A solution of equal parts water and white vinegar, or a very diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to a gallon of water), is effective. Always test in an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

No single solution kills mold permanently if the moisture source remains. The key to permanent removal is to eliminate the moisture that allows mold to grow, combined with thorough cleaning using solutions like white vinegar or bleach, and ensuring the area stays dry.

**How do you get rid of mold on walls naturally?**

White vinegar is an excellent natural option. Spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the moldy area, let it sit for an hour, then scrub and wipe clean. Tea tree oil solution (1 teaspoon per cup of water) can also be used, but it's more expensive and has a strong scent.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is behind walls or in HVAC systems, or if anyone in your household has severe allergies or respiratory issues. Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise for safe remediation.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often necessitates professional help, especially if the mold growth is extensive. Addressing the hidden moisture source is paramount to prevent recurrence.
