To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. Use a solution of distilled white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution to scrub the affected area, then rinse and dry thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old rags
- Distilled white vinegar (undiluted)
- OR Household bleach (1 part bleach to 10 parts water)
- Clean water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Place a fan near the affected area to direct airflow outwards. Don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or coming into direct contact with the mold and cleaning solutions. Remove any furniture or objects near the wall to prevent cross-contamination.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cover nearby furniture and flooring with plastic sheeting or old towels to protect them from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For porous surfaces like drywall, distilled white vinegar is often preferred as it can penetrate deeper than bleach to kill mold roots. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. If using bleach, mix 1 cup of household bleach with 1 gallon of water in a separate spray bottle. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia, as this creates dangerous toxic fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** For more severe mold issues, especially if you suspect black mold, consult our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for specific precautions and methods.

### Step 3: Apply the Solution to Mold

Generously spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy areas of the wall. Ensure the mold is thoroughly saturated. For vinegar, allow it to sit for at least an hour to effectively kill the mold. If using a bleach solution, let it sit for 10-15 minutes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't discolor or damage your wall finish.

### Step 4: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a durable sponge, vigorously scrub the mold off the wall. Apply firm pressure to remove all visible traces of mold. For stubborn spots, reapply the cleaning solution and let it sit for a few more minutes before scrubbing again. Be thorough, but avoid damaging the wall surface.

> 💡 **Tip:** Dispose of any sponges or brushes used for mold removal immediately after use to prevent spreading spores.

### Step 5: Rinse and Wipe Down the Wall

Once all visible mold is gone, dampen a clean microfiber cloth with plain water and wipe down the treated area to remove any residue from the cleaning solution and loosened mold spores. Ensure you wipe thoroughly to leave the surface clean. Avoid over-saturating the wall with water, especially if it's drywall.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a fresh cloth for rinsing to avoid reapplying mold spores or cleaning solution residue.

### Step 6: Dry the Area Completely

Thorough drying is crucial to prevent mold from returning. Use dry cloths to blot the wall as much as possible. Then, use a fan, dehumidifier, or open windows to ensure the area dries completely within 24-48 hours. Any lingering moisture can create a perfect environment for mold regrowth.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a moisture meter to ensure the wall is completely dry before repainting or covering the area.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place. This might involve fixing leaks, improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms or kitchens, or using a dehumidifier. Regularly clean and inspect areas prone to moisture. Maintaining low humidity levels (ideally below 60%) is key to long-term mold prevention.

> 💡 **Tip:** Apply mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to dampness.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear an N95 respirator mask, gloves, and eye protection when dealing with mold.
- Ensure adequate ventilation during and after cleaning to disperse spores and fumes.
- Never mix bleach with other cleaners like ammonia or vinegar, as this creates toxic gases.
- For areas larger than 10 square feet or suspected black mold, consider hiring a professional mold remediation service.
- Identify and fix the source of moisture immediately to prevent mold from returning.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not mix bleach and vinegar, as it produces highly toxic chlorine gas.
- ⚠️ Avoid touching mold directly with bare skin; always wear gloves.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air.
- ⚠️ For extensive mold growth (larger than a 3x3 foot area) or mold resulting from contaminated water (e.g., sewage), professional remediation is recommended.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold typically grows on walls due to excess moisture. Common causes include high humidity, water leaks from pipes or the roof, condensation, poor ventilation, and flooding. It thrives in dark, damp environments with organic material to feed on, like paint or drywall.

**Is it dangerous to remove mold from walls yourself?**

Removing small amounts of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself is generally safe with proper precautions. However, mold spores can cause respiratory issues, allergic reactions, or other health problems. Always wear protective gear (mask, gloves, goggles) and ensure good ventilation to minimize risks.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a soft brush or sponge dampened with a solution of white vinegar or a mild bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water). Avoid abrasive scrubbing that could damage the paint. Rinse lightly with clean water and dry thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is more complex and often requires professional help. If you suspect mold behind drywall (e.g., from a hidden leak), you may need to open up the wall to properly assess and remediate it. This typically indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be addressed by an expert to prevent further spread and structural damage.

**Can I use bleach on all types of walls?**

While bleach is effective on non-porous surfaces, it's not ideal for all walls, especially porous ones like drywall, where it might not penetrate deeply enough to kill mold roots. It can also discolor or damage certain finishes. Distilled white vinegar is often a safer and more effective alternative for many wall types, particularly painted ones.

**How can I prevent mold from coming back on my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier if necessary. Regularly clean and inspect areas prone to condensation.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation service if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold behind walls or in HVAC systems, if you have health concerns (allergies, asthma), or if the mold resulted from contaminated water (e.g., sewage backup). Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive mold.
