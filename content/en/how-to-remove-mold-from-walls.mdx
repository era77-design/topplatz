To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold type and ensuring proper ventilation. For surface mold, a simple solution of bleach or vinegar can often be effective, followed by thorough drying to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar (undiluted)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Buckets
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Mild detergent (optional)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans to direct air outdoors. Wear your protective gear: rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and eye protection. Move any furniture away from the wall and, if desired, cover nearby items with plastic sheeting to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent mold spores from spreading through your home's ventilation.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Area

Determine if the mold is superficial or if it indicates a deeper issue. Small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) can often be handled with DIY methods. If the mold covers a large area, appears furry, or if you suspect it's growing behind the drywall, it's best to consult a professional. For information on more serious infestations, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a 'before' photo for reference and to monitor if the mold returns.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For general mold removal, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is an effective natural option, particularly for painted walls. For more stubborn mold on non-porous surfaces, you can use a diluted bleach solution (1 cup household bleach mixed with 1 gallon of water). Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this creates dangerous toxic fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Spray your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Let the solution sit for about 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold. Then, using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid scrubbing too aggressively on painted walls to prevent removing paint along with the mold.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the treated area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining mold spores and cleaning solution residue. It is crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent mold from returning. Use clean, dry towels, and consider using a fan or dehumidifier to accelerate the drying process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry before considering any repainting or re-covering, which can take several hours.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

The most important step to prevent recurrence is to address the underlying moisture source. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier if humidity levels are consistently high. Regular cleaning and inspection can also help catch new growth early.

> 💡 **Tip:** Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally 30-50%) to discourage mold growth.

### Step 7: Dispose of Materials Safely

Once you've finished cleaning, immediately place all used cloths, sponges, and any other disposable cleaning materials into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of the bag in an outdoor trash can to prevent mold spores from lingering inside your home. Thoroughly clean your reusable tools with hot, soapy water.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wash your old clothes that were worn during cleaning separately in hot water to kill any lingering spores.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first, especially on painted surfaces, to check for discoloration or damage.
- Never paint over mold without cleaning it first; the mold will continue to grow underneath the paint and cause further damage.
- For recurring mold problems or extensive infestations, consulting a professional mold remediation service is highly recommended.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can produce dangerous, toxic gases.
- ⚠️ Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator, to prevent exposure to mold spores.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or you suspect it's behind drywall, it's best to call a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ If you experience allergic reactions or respiratory issues while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek medical advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentler solution like undiluted white vinegar or a mild detergent mixed with water. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint. Scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge, then rinse with a clean, damp cloth and dry the wall thoroughly.

**What is the best household solution for removing mold from walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective for killing mold and is a natural disinfectant. For more stubborn mold on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can be used. Always ensure good ventilation and never mix bleach with other cleaners.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's part of a larger structural issue, or if you experience allergic reactions or health issues from the mold exposure. Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive mold.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a significant undertaking. This usually indicates a serious moisture problem that needs to be resolved. It's often best to consult a professional mold remediation service to ensure all mold is removed and the moisture source is properly addressed.

**Can mold grow back after cleaning?**

Yes, mold can grow back if the underlying moisture problem is not resolved. While thorough cleaning removes existing mold, preventing recurrence relies on identifying and fixing the source of dampness, improving ventilation, and controlling humidity levels in the affected area.

**Is it safe to clean mold myself?**

For small patches of surface mold, it is generally safe to clean yourself with proper precautions, including wearing protective gear (gloves, mask, eye protection) and ensuring good ventilation. However, if you are sensitive to mold, have respiratory issues, or the mold is extensive, it's safer to hire a professional.
