To remove mold from walls, first identify the mold type and ensure proper ventilation. Use a mild detergent or specialized mold cleaner, applying it to the affected area before gently scrubbing away the mold. Always wear protective gear and address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Ventilation fan (optional)
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old rags
- Mild detergent (e.g., dish soap) OR White vinegar OR Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Water
- Drop cloths or old towels

## Steps

### Step 1: Gear Up and Ventilate

Put on your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask. Open windows and doors to ensure good air circulation, and use a fan to direct airflow outdoors. This protects you from inhaling mold spores and harmful fumes from cleaning solutions, ensuring a safer working environment.

### Step 2: Prepare the Affected Area

Lay down drop cloths or old towels on the floor beneath the moldy wall to catch any drips or debris. If the mold is dry and flaky, gently brush off any loose spores into a trash bag before wetting the area. This helps prevent spores from spreading once wet and makes the cleaning process more contained.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For light mold, a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle or a mixture of mild dish soap and water works well. For tougher mold, hydrogen peroxide (3% solution) can be effective. Avoid using bleach on porous walls like drywall, as it often doesn't kill the mold roots and can damage the surface.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Generously spray or apply your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring full coverage. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes. This gives the active ingredients time to penetrate the mold and loosen its grip on the wall surface, making it easier to remove.

### Step 5: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff brush or a scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall. Be thorough but avoid aggressive scrubbing, especially on painted walls, to prevent damage to the paint or drywall. For stubborn spots, reapply the solution and let it sit longer before scrubbing again.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp microfiber cloth to remove any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate mold regrowth. You can use a clean, dry towel or a fan to speed up the drying process and ensure no moisture is left behind.

### Step 7: Address the Source of Moisture and Prevent Recurrence

Mold grows where there's moisture. Identify and fix any leaks, reduce humidity with a dehumidifier, or improve ventilation to prevent future mold issues. Regularly inspect areas prone to moisture to catch any new growth early. For significant black mold, professional remediation may be necessary. [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) offers further guidance.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't discolor or damage your wall's finish.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or suspected toxic black mold, consider professional help.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and running a dehumidifier to prevent future mold growth.
- Act quickly when you spot mold; the sooner you address it, the easier it is to remove and prevent its spread.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it may not kill the roots and can damage the material.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), especially an N95 mask, to avoid inhaling mold spores which can be harmful to your respiratory system.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or is suspected to be toxic black mold, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Never attempt to clean mold if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or a compromised immune system, as exposure can worsen your condition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

When removing mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. Opt for mild solutions like diluted dish soap or white vinegar, and use a soft scrub brush or sponge. Always test the cleaner on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't strip the paint or cause discoloration.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is more complex, as it often requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This usually indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be addressed immediately. It's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediator for this type of issue, as improper removal can spread spores and cause further damage.

**What is the best homemade solution to remove mold from walls?**

White vinegar is a highly effective and natural solution for removing most types of mold from walls. Spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for an hour, then scrub it away. Hydrogen peroxide (3%) is another good option, applied similarly and allowed to sit before scrubbing.

**Can mold on walls make you sick?**

Yes, mold exposure can cause a range of health problems, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Symptoms can include respiratory issues, skin irritation, headaches, and fatigue. It's important to remove mold promptly and safely to protect your health.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

The key to preventing mold recurrence is controlling moisture. Fix leaks immediately, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use a dehumidifier in humid areas, and keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly clean and inspect walls, especially in damp environments, to catch any new growth early.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, if you have health concerns that could be exacerbated by mold exposure, or if the mold is inside walls or HVAC systems. Professionals have the right equipment and expertise for safe and thorough remediation.
