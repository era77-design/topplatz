To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the source of moisture and ensuring proper ventilation. For small areas, a simple solution of diluted bleach or vinegar can be effective, followed by scrubbing and thorough drying. Always wear protective gear to prevent exposure to mold spores.

## What You Need

- Protective gear (gloves, N95 mask, eye protection)
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Water
- Bucket
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Optional: Mild dish soap
- Optional: Bleach (for non-porous surfaces)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the affected area is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. To prevent mold spores from spreading, close off the room from the rest of your home if possible. Always wear protective gear, including gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection, to safeguard yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold growth is always a sign of excess moisture. Before cleaning, it's crucial to find and fix the source of the moisture, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Failing to address the underlying moisture problem will lead to the mold returning quickly after cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most mold types on non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 part bleach to 10 parts water works effectively. Alternatively, white vinegar is a natural and effective mold killer; use it undiluted in a spray bottle. For painted walls or more delicate surfaces, a solution of mild dish soap and water can be used.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall surface. For painted walls, be careful not to scrub too vigorously to avoid damaging the paint.

### Step 5: Wipe and Rinse the Area

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp microfiber cloth to remove any residue and remaining mold spores. If you used a bleach solution, a light rinse with clean water might be beneficial. Ensure you don't over-wet the wall, especially if it's drywall.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

Thorough drying is essential to prevent immediate re-growth. Use fans, a dehumidifier, or open windows to circulate air and dry the wall completely. Do not paint or seal the wall until it is absolutely dry to the touch, which might take several hours or even a day.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For persistent mold odors after cleaning, an open container of baking soda can help absorb lingering smells.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's advisable to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For more information on dealing with black mold, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the surface first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually reappear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first wear protective gear and ventilate the area. Then, apply a cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (1:10 with water) to the affected surface. Scrub the mold away with a brush, wipe clean, and ensure the wall dries completely to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, use a gentler approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap is often effective, or use undiluted white vinegar. Spray the solution, let it sit briefly, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, and wipe dry. Always test on an inconspicuous spot first.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall cavity, which is a more complex and potentially hazardous task. If you suspect mold behind your walls due to persistent musty odors or visible signs on the exterior, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. They can safely assess, contain, and remove hidden mold without spreading spores.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

No cleaning solution can permanently kill mold if the underlying moisture problem isn't resolved. While bleach and vinegar kill mold spores on surfaces, preventing regrowth depends on fixing leaks, controlling humidity, and ensuring proper ventilation. Addressing the source of moisture is the only permanent solution.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is hidden behind walls or in HVAC systems, or if anyone in your household has severe mold allergies or respiratory issues. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely contain and remove large or hidden mold infestations.

**Can I paint over mold?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow underneath the paint, eventually resurfacing and potentially causing more damage. Always thoroughly clean, treat, and dry the moldy area completely before applying any primer or paint.
