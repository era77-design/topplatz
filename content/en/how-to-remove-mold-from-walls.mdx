To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. For most surface mold, a simple solution of diluted bleach or white vinegar can be applied, scrubbed, and then allowed to air dry thoroughly.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Eye protection (goggles)
- N95 respirator mask
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or sponges
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan (for ventilation)
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct air outdoors. Put on your rubber gloves, eye protection, and N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. If the mold patch is small, you might consider isolating the area with plastic sheeting to prevent spores from spreading.

### Step 2: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For porous surfaces like drywall, white vinegar is often preferred as it can penetrate and kill mold more effectively than bleach, which primarily sanitizes the surface. For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a solution of 1 part bleach to 10 parts water can be used. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this creates dangerous fumes. For more information on tackling different types of mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Apply the Solution

If using vinegar, pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. If using bleach, mix 1 cup of bleach with 1 gallon of water in a bucket. Liberally spray or apply your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is fully saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold.

### Step 4: Scrub the Mold Away

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a scrub sponge, gently but firmly scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections, making sure to remove all visible mold. Be careful not to scrub too vigorously on painted surfaces, as this could damage the paint.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe away any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the area completely to prevent mold from returning. You can use a dry cloth, a fan, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process. Ensure the wall is bone dry before proceeding.

### Step 6: Dispose of Materials and Ventilate

Carefully place all used cloths, sponges, and brushes into a sealed plastic bag and dispose of them immediately to prevent mold spores from spreading. Continue to ventilate the room for several hours after cleaning to ensure any lingering fumes dissipate and the area remains dry.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always address the source of moisture causing the mold growth; otherwise, it will likely return.
- Test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or if you suspect mold is hidden behind walls, consider consulting a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with other cleaning products, especially ammonia, as it can create highly toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator mask, to avoid inhaling mold spores or cleaning solution fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not simply paint over mold. It will not kill the mold and the problem will eventually resurface, potentially worse than before.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. Apply a solution of white vinegar or diluted bleach, let it sit, then scrub the mold away with a brush. Rinse the area with a clean, damp cloth and dry thoroughly to prevent regrowth.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is often effective and less harsh than bleach. Spray the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, rinse, and dry completely.

**What is the best solution for removing mold from walls?**

For most surface mold on walls, white vinegar is highly effective as it penetrates porous surfaces and kills mold spores. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) can also be used. Always choose the solution appropriate for your wall material.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the moldy areas, which can be a complex and potentially hazardous task. It's often best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for mold inside walls, as it may indicate a larger, hidden moisture problem that needs expert assessment and repair.

**Can I just paint over mold?**

No, you should never just paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; it will only hide the problem temporarily. The mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, potentially causing further damage to your walls and negatively impacting indoor air quality. Always remove the mold completely and address the moisture source before repainting.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, it's crucial to control moisture levels. Fix any leaks promptly, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, ensure proper ventilation, and use a dehumidifier in damp areas. Keep indoor humidity below 60% and clean up spills or condensation immediately.
