Removing mold from walls involves identifying the source, preparing the area, and using appropriate cleaning solutions. For small, superficial areas, a simple bleach or vinegar solution can be effective, but always prioritize safety and proper ventilation to prevent health risks.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- Clean cloths or old towels
- White vinegar OR household bleach
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Remove any furniture, rugs, or other items from the immediate area to prevent cross-contamination. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: 2. Identify the Mold Type and Source

Before tackling the mold, try to determine what kind it is and, more importantly, what's causing it. Most common wall molds are superficial and appear as fuzzy, discolored patches. However, if you suspect [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold), or if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, professional assessment is recommended. Addressing the underlying moisture issue, such as a leaky pipe or high humidity, is crucial to prevent its return.

### Step 3: 3. Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a solution of one part bleach to three parts water is highly effective. Alternatively, for a more natural approach or if you prefer to avoid bleach, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle works well. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the wall surface.

### Step 4: 4. Apply and Scrub the Mold

If using bleach, carefully pour the mixture into a spray bottle. If using vinegar, fill the bottle with undiluted white vinegar. Spray the moldy area thoroughly, ensuring it's completely saturated. Let the solution sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold spores. Then, use a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge to vigorously scrub away the mold from the wall surface.

### Step 5: 5. Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any remaining cleaning solution and dead mold particles. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate re-growth. You can use clean, dry towels, a fan, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process. Ensure the wall is bone dry before proceeding.

### Step 6: 6. Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is clean and dry, focus on preventing mold from returning. Address the source of moisture immediately, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Improve ventilation in the room, especially in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Regular cleaning and monitoring can help catch new mold growth early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot on the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with other cleaning products, especially ammonia, as it can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- For persistent odors after cleaning, an open container of baking soda placed in the room can help absorb lingering smells.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.
- Small areas of mold (less than 10 square feet) can often be handled by homeowners, but larger infestations or recurring mold may require professional help.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including an N95 mask, safety goggles, and rubber gloves, to protect against mold spores and harsh cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure the area is well-ventilated during and after cleaning to disperse fumes and mold spores.
- ⚠️ Mold can pose health risks, especially to individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. If you experience respiratory issues or other symptoms, consult a healthcare professional.
- ⚠️ If mold is extensive, covers a large area, or keeps returning despite your efforts, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first test your chosen cleaning solution (either a diluted bleach solution or undiluted white vinegar) on an inconspicuous spot. Apply the solution, let it sit, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse with water and dry thoroughly to prevent moisture from lingering.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to expose the moldy area. This is often an indicator of a significant moisture problem, such as a pipe leak. For hidden mold, it's usually best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist who can safely identify the source, remove the mold, and repair the wall structure.

**Is bleach or vinegar better for mold removal on walls?**

Both bleach and vinegar can be effective for mold removal on non-porous surfaces. Bleach is a strong disinfectant that kills mold spores but may not penetrate porous materials. Vinegar is a milder, natural alternative that is effective at killing many types of mold and is safe for most surfaces, though it may require more scrubbing. Choose based on your preference and wall material.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and ensure good ventilation throughout your home. A dehumidifier can help in humid areas, and keeping indoor humidity below 60% is generally recommended. Regularly inspect walls for signs of condensation or dampness.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect hidden mold behind walls, or if you have health concerns related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely contain, remove, and prevent the return of extensive mold infestations.

**What are the health risks of mold exposure?**

Exposure to mold can cause a variety of health problems, especially for sensitive individuals. Symptoms can include allergic reactions like sneezing, runny nose, red eyes, and skin rash. Mold can also trigger asthma attacks in people with asthma and may cause irritation of the eyes, skin, nose, throat, and lungs in others. Prolonged exposure can lead to more severe respiratory issues.
