To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear. Then, clean the affected area with a diluted bleach solution or vinegar, scrub gently, and allow it to dry completely to prevent regrowth.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety glasses
- N95 face mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or sponge
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Bleach (for non-porous surfaces, optional)
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- Fan (for ventilation)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct air outdoors. Wear protective gear including rubber gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 face mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or coming into contact with cleaning solutions. Cover nearby furniture with plastic sheeting if needed to protect it from spores or splashes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent spores from spreading to other areas of your home.

### Step 2: Identify the Type and Extent of Mold

Examine the mold to determine its size and location. For small patches (under 10 square feet), DIY removal is generally safe. If you suspect you're dealing with black mold, or if the mold covers a large area, it's best to consult a professional. For more detailed guidance on identifying and handling specific types, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Determine the source of moisture causing the mold, as addressing this is crucial for long-term prevention.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most mold types on walls, white vinegar is an effective and natural option. Pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. For non-porous surfaces like tiles or painted walls, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia. Test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the surface.

> 💡 **Tip:** Vinegar is excellent for porous surfaces like drywall as it can penetrate and kill mold at the root.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Generously spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes. This dwell time is crucial for the solution to penetrate and effectively kill the mold spores. Ensure the entire affected area is saturated.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid scrubbing dry mold, as this can release spores into the air. Always apply solution first.

### Step 5: Scrub and Clean the Wall

Using a stiff-bristled brush or sponge, gently scrub the moldy area. Apply moderate pressure to lift the mold from the surface. For painted walls, be careful not to scrub too vigorously to avoid damaging the paint. Once the mold is removed, wipe the area with a clean, damp cloth to rinse off any residue.

> 💡 **Tip:** Dispose of cloths, sponges, and brushes used for cleaning mold, or thoroughly disinfect them.

### Step 6: Dry the Area Thoroughly

After cleaning, it is absolutely critical to dry the wall completely. Use a clean, dry cloth or paper towels to absorb as much moisture as possible. Continue to use a fan and keep windows open to promote air circulation. Any lingering moisture can lead to mold regrowth, so ensure the area is bone dry before proceeding.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a dehumidifier in the room to help speed up the drying process and reduce overall humidity.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is dry, take steps to prevent mold from returning. Address the underlying moisture source, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider applying mold-inhibiting paint in susceptible locations. Regular cleaning and monitoring can help catch new growth early.

> 💡 **Tip:** Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally 30-50%) using ventilation or a dehumidifier.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure no discoloration or damage occurs.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold odors, try placing bowls of activated charcoal or baking soda in the room to absorb smells.
- Inspect areas prone to moisture (e.g., behind furniture, near windows) regularly for early signs of mold growth.
- Improve air circulation by moving furniture away from walls and opening closet doors in damp rooms.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or a compromised immune system. Seek professional help instead.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if it appears after water damage that hasn't been properly dried, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Never use harsh chemicals like ammonia or strong detergents on mold, as they can irritate your respiratory system and may not effectively kill the mold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure the area is well-ventilated and wear protective gear. Use a spray bottle to apply white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) to the mold. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint, and wipe clean. Ensure the wall dries completely afterwards.

**What is the best solution to remove mold from walls?**

For most wall surfaces, white vinegar is highly effective at killing mold spores and preventing regrowth. For non-porous surfaces like tiles or sealed painted walls, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can also be used. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area first.

**Can mold on walls be dangerous?**

Yes, mold on walls can pose health risks, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Exposure to mold spores can cause respiratory issues, skin irritation, and other allergic reactions. It's important to remove mold safely and address the underlying moisture issue to protect your health.

**How do you prevent mold from growing on walls?**

Preventing mold involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and use a dehumidifier in humid climates to keep indoor humidity below 60%. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, and ensure good airflow around furniture and behind walls.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is typically a job for professionals. If mold is growing within wall cavities, it indicates a significant moisture problem that requires opening up the wall to properly identify the source, clean the affected materials, and dry the structure. DIY attempts without proper tools and expertise can worsen the problem and spread spores.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation service if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's black mold, if it returns repeatedly, or if you suspect it's deep within walls or HVAC systems. Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely and effectively remove extensive mold and address its root cause.
