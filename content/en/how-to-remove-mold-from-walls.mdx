Removing mold from walls requires careful attention to safety and proper cleaning techniques. For surface mold, solutions like diluted bleach or vinegar can be effective, but always ensure good ventilation and protective gear. Addressing the underlying moisture source is crucial to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Old clothes or disposable coveralls
- Drop cloths or old towels
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub pad
- Microfiber cloths or sponges
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Lay down drop cloths or old towels to protect flooring and furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Put on your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and N95 mask to protect your eyes, skin, and respiratory system from mold spores and chemical fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or appears to be black mold, consider consulting a professional or refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for more detailed guidance.

### Step 2: Isolate the Moldy Area

To prevent mold spores from spreading to other parts of your home, close off the affected room if possible. You can use plastic sheeting and tape to seal doorways. If using a fan, position it to blow air *out* of an open window, not into other rooms of your house, to exhaust spores outdoors.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, a 1:1 mixture of white vinegar and water in a spray bottle is effective and less harsh than bleach. For non-porous surfaces like tiles or painted walls, you can use a solution of 1 cup household bleach per gallon of water. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes. This dwell time allows the cleaner to penetrate and kill the mold before you start scrubbing. Avoid scrubbing dry mold, as this can release more spores into the air.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Clean

Using a stiff brush or scrub pad, gently scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. Once the mold is removed, wipe the area clean with a damp microfiber cloth or sponge, rinsing it frequently in a separate bucket of clean water. Avoid over-saturating the wall, especially if it's drywall.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After cleaning, it's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate mold regrowth. Use clean, dry cloths to absorb as much moisture as possible. Then, use a fan or dehumidifier to thoroughly dry the area. Ensure good air circulation for at least 24-48 hours, or until the wall is completely dry to the touch.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Identify and fix the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place, such as a leaky pipe or excessive humidity. Improve ventilation in the area and consider using mold-resistant paint in high-humidity rooms. Regularly inspect and clean areas prone to mold to catch any new growth early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia or vinegar, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- Address the underlying moisture source immediately; otherwise, the mold will likely return.
- Improve ventilation in bathrooms, kitchens, and basements by using exhaust fans or opening windows.
- Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier in damp environments.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can produce deadly chlorine gas.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including an N95 mask, safety goggles, and rubber gloves, to protect against mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. Spray the mold with a solution of diluted white vinegar or bleach, let it sit, then scrub and wipe clean. Thoroughly dry the area afterwards to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water is often the safest choice to avoid damaging the paint. Spray it on, let it dwell, gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, and then wipe clean. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a more complex task that often indicates a significant moisture problem and may require professional help. It typically involves opening up the wall to expose and properly remediate the mold, as well as fixing the moisture source.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

No single product can kill mold on walls permanently without addressing the underlying cause. The key to permanent removal is not just cleaning the mold, but also eliminating the moisture source that allows it to grow and thrive, and maintaining dry conditions.

**Can I use bleach to clean mold on walls?**

Bleach can be used to clean mold on non-porous wall surfaces, diluted at 1 cup of bleach per gallon of water. However, it's less effective on porous surfaces and should always be used in a well-ventilated area with proper safety gear. Never mix it with other cleaners.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, fix any water leaks promptly, ensure adequate ventilation in damp areas, and use a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly clean and inspect areas prone to moisture for early detection.

**What should I do if the mold keeps coming back on my walls?**

If mold repeatedly returns to your walls after cleaning, it's a strong indication that the underlying moisture problem has not been fully resolved. You should thoroughly investigate for hidden leaks, condensation issues, or poor ventilation, and consider consulting a professional for an inspection.
