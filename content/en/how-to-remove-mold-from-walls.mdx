To remove mold from walls, always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation. For small, superficial mold, solutions like distilled white vinegar or a mild detergent can be highly effective when applied, scrubbed, and thoroughly dried to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety glasses
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or disposable coveralls
- Plastic sheeting or tarp
- Scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- Distilled white vinegar
- Mild dish soap or detergent
- Clean cloths or towels
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Empty spray bottle

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, move furniture and other items away from the affected wall. Lay down plastic sheeting or a tarp to protect the floor and surrounding areas from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on your rubber gloves, safety glasses, and N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning agents. Consider wearing old clothes or disposable coveralls that can be washed or discarded afterward.

### Step 2: Identify and Isolate the Mold

Carefully assess the extent of the mold growth. For small areas (less than 10 square feet), DIY removal is generally safe. If the mold covers a larger area, appears fuzzy or black, or you suspect it's deep within the wall, it's best to consult a professional. Avoid touching the mold directly to prevent further spread of spores. If you suspect [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold), follow specific safety protocols.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most types of mold on walls, distilled white vinegar is an excellent, non-toxic choice. Pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. For painted walls or less severe mold, a solution of warm water mixed with a few drops of mild dish soap or detergent can also be effective. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it may not kill the mold at the root and its fumes can be harmful.

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution generously onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. This dwell time helps to kill the mold spores. After waiting, use a stiff scrub brush or a rough sponge to scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections, applying gentle but firm pressure.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Once you've scrubbed away the visible mold, wipe down the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining residue and spores. Be careful not to spread mold to unaffected areas. The most crucial step is to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use clean, dry towels, and consider using a fan or dehumidifier to accelerate the drying process. Ensure the wall is bone dry before proceeding to any further steps.

### Step 6: Address the Moisture Source

Mold will return if the underlying moisture problem isn't fixed. Identify and resolve the source of dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, high humidity, or poor ventilation. Repair leaks promptly, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier in humid climates. Controlling moisture is key to long-term mold prevention.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

After cleaning and drying, consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer to the wall before repainting, especially in areas prone to moisture. Regularly inspect walls for any signs of new mold growth, particularly in damp areas. Maintain good air circulation throughout your home and keep indoor humidity levels below 60% to create an environment less hospitable to mold.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always address the source of moisture first; otherwise, mold will return.
- Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the surface first.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and ensuring furniture isn't pressed directly against walls.
- Consider using a dehumidifier in damp basements or humid climates to keep indoor humidity levels low.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach and ammonia, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask when dealing with mold.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, is suspected to be toxic (like black mold), or returns repeatedly, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Avoid touching mold directly with bare skin, as it can cause irritation or allergic reactions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls?**

For small areas, prepare the space with protective gear and ventilation. Apply a solution of distilled white vinegar or mild detergent, scrub the mold away, then rinse and thoroughly dry the wall. Addressing the moisture source is crucial for long-term prevention.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap is often effective. Spray, let sit, gently scrub with a soft sponge, then rinse and dry completely. Avoid abrasive cleaners or harsh scrubbing.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. If you suspect mold behind drywall due to a musty smell or visible mold on the exterior wall that keeps returning, it likely indicates a larger, hidden problem. This usually involves opening up the wall, which is best left to mold remediation specialists.

**What is the best way to clean small areas of mold from walls?**

The best way to clean small areas of mold is to spray with undiluted white vinegar, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub with a brush or sponge. Rinse with a clean, damp cloth and dry the area thoroughly with towels and a fan to prevent re-growth.

**Is it safe to clean mold yourself?**

Cleaning small, superficial mold areas (less than 10 square feet) yourself is generally safe if you wear proper PPE (gloves, mask, eye protection) and ensure good ventilation. However, for large infestations, recurring mold, or if you have health concerns, it's safer to hire a professional.

**Can I paint over mold on walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint layer and eventually resurface, potentially causing structural damage or health issues. Always clean, treat, and thoroughly dry the moldy area before applying any primer or paint.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, identify and fix the underlying moisture source, whether it's a leak or high humidity. Improve ventilation in damp areas, use exhaust fans, and consider a dehumidifier. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, especially in bathrooms and kitchens.
