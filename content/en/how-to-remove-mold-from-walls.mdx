To remove mold from walls, first identify and fix the source of moisture. Then, don protective gear and clean the affected area with a bleach solution or vinegar, scrubbing gently before rinsing and drying thoroughly to prevent re-growth.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Old towels or rags
- White vinegar (undiluted)
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, clear the area around the moldy wall of any furniture or items that could be damaged or contaminated. Open windows and doors to ensure good ventilation. Put on your rubber gloves, N95 mask or respirator, and eye protection to safeguard yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold cannot grow without moisture, so it's crucial to find and fix the source of the dampness. This could be a leaky pipe, a poorly sealed window, condensation, or high humidity. Addressing the moisture problem is essential to prevent mold from returning after cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For general mold on most wall types, including painted walls, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is an effective and natural option. For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup (240 ml) household bleach mixed with 1 gallon (3.8 liters) of water can be used. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Let it Sit

Generously spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area. Ensure the entire affected surface is saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes; this gives it time to penetrate and kill the mold spores effectively.

### Step 5: Scrub and Rinse the Wall

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a sponge, gently scrub the moldy area to remove the visible mold. Be careful not to scrub too vigorously, especially on painted surfaces, to avoid damaging the paint. After scrubbing, wipe the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any residue, then follow with a dry cloth.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

Proper drying is critical to prevent mold from returning. Use old towels or rags to thoroughly dry the cleaned area. You can also use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process. Ensure the wall is completely dry before repainting or replacing any items.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

To keep mold from returning, maintain indoor humidity levels between 30-50% using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens with exhaust fans. Promptly fix any leaks and address condensation issues. Regular cleaning of high-moisture areas can also help prevent recurrence.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to check for discoloration or damage.
- For severe or widespread mold (larger than 10 square feet), or if you suspect mold is behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using fans, and avoiding overcrowding furniture against walls, especially in damp rooms.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection when cleaning mold.
- ⚠️ If mold covers a large area (>10 sq ft) or returns quickly, it indicates a more significant moisture problem that may require professional assessment.
- ⚠️ If you have health conditions that could be exacerbated by mold exposure, consider having a professional handle the removal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, a solution of undiluted white vinegar or a mild dish soap and water mixture is often safer than bleach, which can discolor paint. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the finish or color.

**What is the best homemade solution for removing mold from walls?**

A common and effective homemade solution is undiluted white vinegar, which can be sprayed directly onto the mold. For tougher spots on non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup (240 ml) bleach to 1 gallon (3.8 liters) of water can be used, but always test it first.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is behind walls, or if you experience health issues related to mold exposure. For guidance on specific types of mold, you can also refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls?**

Prevent mold by controlling indoor humidity levels (ideally between 30-50%), ensuring good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, fixing any leaks immediately, and regularly cleaning areas prone to moisture. Good air circulation also helps.

**Is it possible to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often necessitates professional help, especially if the mold is extensive, structural integrity is compromised, or you cannot identify the source of moisture.

**How long does it take for mold to grow back after cleaning?**

If the underlying moisture problem is not resolved, mold can begin to regrow within 24-48 hours after cleaning. It's crucial to address the source of moisture and ensure the area is thoroughly dried to prevent recurrence and ensure long-term mold prevention.
