Removing small patches of mold from walls is a manageable DIY task that typically involves cleaning with a bleach solution or vinegar. Proper ventilation, protective gear, and addressing the moisture source are crucial for safety and preventing recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or apron
- Plastic sheeting or old towels
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Buckets
- Clean cloths or rags
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- White vinegar
- Borax (optional)
- Mild dish soap (optional)
- Fan
- Dehumidifier (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Lay down plastic sheeting or old towels on the floor beneath the affected wall to catch any drips and prevent mold spores from spreading. Don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask are essential to protect your skin, eyes, and lungs from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there is moisture. Before cleaning, try to identify and fix the source of the dampness, such as a leaky pipe, roof, or excessive humidity. If you don't address the underlying moisture issue, the mold is likely to return shortly after cleaning, making your efforts temporary.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, tiles, or sealed concrete, a solution of 1 cup household bleach mixed with 1 gallon of water is effective. Alternatively, a solution of 1 cup borax in 1 gallon of water can be used, which also helps inhibit future growth. For more porous surfaces or a bleach-free option, undiluted white vinegar can be sprayed directly onto the mold. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this creates dangerous fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Generously spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, vigorously scrub the mold away from the wall. For stubborn stains, you might need to reapply the solution and scrub again.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the area with a clean cloth dampened with plain water to remove any cleaning solution residue and dead mold spores. It is critical to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use clean, dry cloths, open windows, run a fan, or even a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is bone dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials Safely

Once you're finished, seal all contaminated materials, including cloths, sponges, and plastic sheeting, in a heavy-duty trash bag. Dispose of this bag immediately in an outdoor trash receptacle. Clean any buckets or tools used with soap and water, wearing your gloves, before storing them away.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

To keep mold from returning, maintain low indoor humidity levels (ideally below 60%) using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans. Promptly repair any leaks, and consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to moisture.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For persistent musty odors after cleaning, an activated charcoal air purifier or baking soda can help absorb lingering smells.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows regularly, especially after showering or cooking, and ensure furniture isn't pushed directly against exterior walls.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes that are extremely dangerous to inhale.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (N95 mask, gloves, goggles) when cleaning mold to prevent exposure to spores and chemicals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first protect yourself with an N95 mask, gloves, and goggles, and ensure good ventilation. Then, apply a cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (for non-porous surfaces), scrub the mold away, rinse with clean water, and thoroughly dry the area to prevent recurrence.

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot to ensure it won't damage the paint. A diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) or a borax solution is effective for non-porous painted surfaces. For milder mold, undiluted white vinegar can also be used; spray it on, let it sit, then scrub and dry thoroughly.

**What's the best homemade solution for mold removal?**

For most surface mold, undiluted white vinegar is a highly effective and natural homemade option. For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup household bleach to 1 gallon of water can be used. Borax dissolved in water (1 cup borax per gallon of water) is another good alternative that helps inhibit future mold growth.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold growth inside walls, if you experience health issues related to mold exposure, or if you find [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold). Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise to safely contain and remove extensive mold.

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls again?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens with exhaust fans, and consider using a dehumidifier in humid areas to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Cleaning surfaces regularly with a mild disinfectant can also help.

**Can mold grow inside walls, and how do you address it?**

Yes, mold can grow inside walls, often indicated by a musty smell, peeling paint, or bulging drywall, even without visible surface mold. Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall, which is a job best left to professionals due to the potential for widespread contamination, health risks, and the need for proper remediation techniques.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

Removing small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) can be safe if proper precautions are taken, including wearing an N95 mask, rubber gloves, and safety goggles, and ensuring good ventilation. However, for extensive mold, black mold, or if you have respiratory issues, it's safer to hire a professional to ensure complete and safe removal.
