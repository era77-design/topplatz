To remove mold from walls, start by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation. For small, superficial mold, you can typically use a solution of white vinegar or borax and water, scrubbing the affected area thoroughly before drying completely.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 mask or respirator
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Borax
- Dish soap (mild)
- Clean cloths or sponges
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Trash bags

## Steps

### Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on your rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 mask or respirator to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Keep children and pets away from the work area during the cleaning process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Good ventilation is key to dispersing mold spores and fumes from cleaning agents. If possible, turn off your HVAC system to prevent spores from spreading.

### Step 2: Prepare the Area

Remove any furniture, rugs, or items from the immediate vicinity to prevent contamination. If the mold is on a painted wall, test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint. Cover the floor with old sheets or plastic sheeting for easier cleanup.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a fan facing outwards from a window to create negative pressure, drawing air and spores out of the room.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, white vinegar is an excellent natural mold killer. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray it directly onto the mold. Alternatively, mix 1 cup of borax with 1 gallon of hot water and a squirt of dish soap; this solution also effectively kills mold and inhibits future growth. Avoid mixing different cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** For tougher mold stains, you can let the vinegar or borax solution sit on the mold for 10-15 minutes before scrubbing.

### Step 4: Scrub and Clean the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a clean cloth, thoroughly scrub the moldy area. Apply firm pressure to penetrate the mold growth and remove it from the wall surface. For painted walls, be gentle to avoid damaging the finish. Continue scrubbing until all visible mold is gone, being careful not to spread spores to other areas.

> 💡 **Tip:** Dip your scrub brush in a fresh solution periodically to avoid reintroducing mold spores to the wall.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use clean, dry towels, and run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours until the wall is bone dry.

> 💡 **Tip:** Mold thrives in damp conditions, so ensuring the wall is completely dry is the most critical step in preventing recurrence.

### Step 6: Address the Source of Moisture

Mold grows where there is moisture. After cleaning, identify and fix the source of the moisture problem, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Repair any leaks promptly and consider using a dehumidifier in damp rooms. Proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens can also significantly reduce moisture buildup.

> 💡 **Tip:** Small leaks can often go unnoticed until mold appears. Regularly inspect pipes, windows, and roofs for any signs of water intrusion.

### Step 7: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Once you've finished cleaning, immediately place all used cloths, sponges, and any protective coverings into a sealed plastic trash bag. Dispose of the bag outdoors to prevent mold spores from spreading within your home. Wash your gloves and scrub brush thoroughly with hot, soapy water, or discard them if heavily contaminated.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider washing any clothes you wore during the cleaning process in hot water to remove any lingering mold spores.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first, especially on painted walls, to ensure they don't cause discoloration or damage.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and avoiding overcrowding spaces, which can help prevent mold growth.
- Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier, especially in basements or bathrooms, to create an environment less conducive to mold.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can produce toxic and potentially lethal fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's 'black mold' (Stachybotrys chartarum) or hidden behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For serious cases, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems should avoid direct contact with mold and consider having a professional handle the removal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls safely and effectively?**

To safely remove mold from walls, wear protective gear like gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask. Use a solution of undiluted white vinegar or a borax and water mixture, spray it on the mold, scrub thoroughly, then rinse and dry the area completely to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

When removing mold from painted walls, always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it won't damage the paint. Gentle solutions like diluted white vinegar (50/50 with water) or a mild borax solution are often effective without harming the finish. Scrub gently with a soft brush or cloth.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is usually beyond a DIY project, as it indicates a significant moisture problem and potentially extensive growth. This often requires opening up the wall, which can release many spores. It's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for mold inside walls.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While cleaning solutions like white vinegar or borax kill existing mold, permanent prevention relies on eliminating the moisture source. Fix leaks, improve ventilation, and control humidity levels to stop mold from returning. Without addressing the moisture, mold will likely reappear.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

Removing small patches of mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself can be safe if you take proper precautions. Always wear protective gear (gloves, goggles, N95 mask) and ensure good ventilation. For larger infestations, or if you have health concerns, it's safer to hire a professional.

**What causes mold on walls?**

Mold on walls is primarily caused by excessive moisture. This can stem from leaky pipes, roof leaks, condensation due to poor ventilation, high indoor humidity, or flooding. Mold spores are always present in the air, but they only grow when they find a damp surface.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is hidden behind walls or in HVAC systems, if you have health issues aggravated by mold, or if the mold returns repeatedly after cleaning. Professionals have the equipment and expertise for safe and thorough remediation.
