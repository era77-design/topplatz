To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the type and extent of the growth. For small areas, a simple solution of diluted bleach or vinegar can be effective, followed by scrubbing and thorough drying. Always wear protective gear to ensure safety and prevent spore inhalation during the cleaning process.

## What You Need

- N95 respirator mask
- Rubber gloves
- Safety glasses
- Stiff scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- Buckets
- Old rags or towels
- Plastic sheeting
- Diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon water) OR undiluted white vinegar
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Gear Up and Ventilate

Before you begin, prioritize your safety. Put on protective gear including an N95 respirator mask, rubber gloves, and safety glasses to prevent inhaling spores and skin contact with mold or cleaning solutions. Open windows and use fans to ensure good ventilation in the room, which helps to disperse fumes and prevent the spread of airborne mold spores.

### Step 2: Prepare the Area

Remove any furniture, rugs, or items from the immediate vicinity of the moldy wall to prevent contamination. Cover any nearby surfaces that cannot be moved with plastic sheeting to protect them from stray mold spores and cleaning solutions. This containment helps to isolate the mold and makes cleanup more manageable.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) is highly effective at killing mold. As an alternative, or if you prefer not to use bleach, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle works well as a natural mold killer. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the wall surface.

> 💡 **Tip:** For extensive black mold infestations or if the mold covers a large area, it's often best to consult a professional. You can find more detailed guidance on severe cases in our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall, ensuring the entire affected surface is saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff scrub brush or sponge, firmly scrub the mold away. Avoid overly aggressive scrubbing that could damage the wall's finish or spread spores further.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Once the mold is visibly removed, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to rinse off any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. It is crucial to dry the wall surface completely and quickly to prevent new mold growth. Use old towels, a fan, or a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is bone dry before proceeding.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully gather all used rags, sponges, brushes, and protective gear. Place them immediately into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of this bag outside your home to prevent any remaining mold spores from re-contaminating your indoor environment and to ensure they do not spread.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Address the source of moisture immediately. Mold will return if the underlying issue (e.g., a leak, high humidity) is not resolved.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans or opening windows.
- For large mold infestations (over 10 square feet) or if you suspect mold is deeply embedded behind walls, consider hiring a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear proper personal protective equipment (N95 mask, gloves, eye protection) to avoid exposure to mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If you have health concerns related to mold exposure or if the mold area is extensive, consult a professional for safe removal.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually reappear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first protect yourself with an N95 mask, gloves, and eye protection, and ensure good ventilation. Apply a diluted bleach solution or undiluted white vinegar to the mold, scrub it away with a stiff brush, then rinse the area and dry it thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls requires care to avoid damaging the paint. Use a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) or undiluted white vinegar. Test the solution on an inconspicuous area first, then gently scrub the mold with a soft brush or sponge, rinse, and dry completely.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

No single solution can permanently kill mold on walls if the moisture source persists. While bleach and vinegar effectively kill surface mold, preventing its return requires addressing the underlying moisture problem, such as fixing leaks or reducing humidity levels.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

For small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), it is generally safe to remove yourself with proper protective gear and good ventilation. However, for larger infestations, suspected black mold, or mold inside walls, it's safer and recommended to consult a professional.

**How do I prevent mold from coming back on walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence on walls is primarily about moisture control. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure adequate ventilation in damp areas, use dehumidifiers if humidity is consistently high, and keep surfaces clean and dry. Regular cleaning can also help.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, if you have health issues aggravated by mold, or if the mold is hidden behind walls or in your HVAC system.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. It often involves opening up the wall, identifying and fixing the moisture source, and then safely removing the moldy building materials. Attempting this yourself without proper containment can spread spores and exacerbate the problem.
