Removing mold from walls typically involves identifying the source of moisture, preparing the area with protective gear, and scrubbing the affected surface with appropriate cleaning solutions like vinegar, hydrogen peroxide, or borax. Ensure proper ventilation and thorough drying to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Eye protection
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Borax
- Baking soda
- Mild dish soap
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Old rags or towels
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Mold and Prepare the Area

Before you begin, determine the extent of the mold growth. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if it appears to be black mold, it's often best to consult a professional. For smaller areas, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Remove any furniture, rugs, or items near the affected wall to prevent cross-contamination.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you suspect black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for more detailed safety precautions.

### Step 2: Protect Yourself and Contain the Area

Always wear protective gear to prevent exposure to mold spores, which can cause respiratory issues and skin irritation. Don an N95 respirator mask, rubber gloves, and eye protection. You might also consider wearing old clothes that can be washed immediately or disposed of afterward. If possible, seal off the affected room from the rest of your home using plastic sheeting and tape to contain spores.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

Several effective, non-toxic solutions can be used to kill and remove mold. White vinegar is a popular choice, used undiluted in a spray bottle. Hydrogen peroxide (3%) is another option, applied directly and left to sit for 10-15 minutes. For tougher mold, a solution of 1 cup borax dissolved in 1 gallon of hot water, or a mixture of baking soda and water to form a paste, can be very effective. Avoid mixing different cleaning solutions together, especially bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Generously spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Let the solution sit for 5-15 minutes, depending on its strength and the severity of the mold, to allow it to penetrate and kill the spores. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, scrub the mold away from the wall surface. Apply firm but gentle pressure to avoid damaging the wall.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe down the treated area, removing any residual mold and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use clean, dry towels, and consider pointing a fan directly at the wall or using a dehumidifier in the room for several hours. Ensure the wall is bone dry before proceeding or repainting.

### Step 6: Address the Source of Moisture

Mold will inevitably return if the underlying moisture problem isn't resolved. Identify and fix any leaks, condensation issues, or high humidity levels that contributed to the mold growth. This might involve repairing plumbing, improving ventilation in bathrooms or kitchens, or using a dehumidifier in damp basements. Without addressing the moisture source, your efforts to remove mold will only be temporary.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For porous surfaces like drywall, mold can penetrate deeply, making complete removal difficult without replacing the affected section.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and keeping interior doors open to prevent moisture buildup.
- Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally between 30-50%) using a dehumidifier, especially in humid climates or damp areas.
- Clean small mold spots immediately upon discovery to prevent them from spreading and becoming a larger problem.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If you have allergies or asthma, be extra cautious and consider having someone else clean the mold, or wear appropriate respiratory protection.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually reappear.
- ⚠️ If mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet) or appears after water damage from sewage or contaminated sources, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows the same general steps. Use a mild cleaning solution like white vinegar or a diluted borax solution, as harsh chemicals can strip paint. Gently scrub the area with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint, then rinse and dry thoroughly.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a more complex task that often requires professional intervention. It typically involves opening up the wall to access the moldy studs and insulation, then cleaning, treating, and replacing affected materials. Addressing the moisture source that allowed mold to grow inside the wall is crucial for prevention.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold thrives in damp, dark, and poorly ventilated environments. Common causes include water leaks from pipes or roofs, high indoor humidity levels (e.g., from showering or cooking without ventilation), condensation, and inadequate drying after a flood or spill.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold growth behind walls or in HVAC systems, if you have health concerns related to mold exposure, or if the mold resulted from contaminated water (like sewage).

**Is mold on walls dangerous?**

Exposure to mold can cause various health issues, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Symptoms can include respiratory problems, skin irritation, eye irritation, and nasal stuffiness. Certain types of mold, like black mold, can be more hazardous.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

While bleach can kill mold on non-porous surfaces, it's less effective on porous surfaces like drywall, as it often only bleaches the surface without killing the roots. It can also be corrosive and emit strong fumes. Vinegar, hydrogen peroxide, and borax are often safer and more effective alternatives for walls.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Prevent mold by controlling moisture: fix leaks promptly, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, ensure proper ventilation, and maintain indoor humidity below 60% with a dehumidifier if necessary. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, especially in areas prone to dampness.
