To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. For small areas, a solution of white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide can effectively kill and clean surface mold. Always address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes/coveralls
- Plastic sheeting
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Soft brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Bucket of clean water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Lay down plastic sheeting to protect the floor and furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Don your protective gear, including rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask, to prevent inhaling spores or skin contact.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the area if possible by closing doors and sealing vents to prevent mold spores from spreading to other parts of your home.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives in damp conditions, so identifying and fixing the moisture problem is crucial for long-term prevention. This could be a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Repair any leaks and ensure proper ventilation in humid areas like bathrooms or kitchens before attempting to clean the mold.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a dehumidifier in basements or other consistently damp areas to keep humidity levels below 50%.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces and painted walls, white vinegar is an effective and natural mold killer. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray directly onto the moldy area. Alternatively, a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution can also be used, applied similarly. For severe cases on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but test it on an inconspicuous area first.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid mixing bleach with vinegar or ammonia, as this creates dangerous toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply and Let it Sit

Once you've applied your chosen cleaning solution, allow it to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes. This gives the solution time to penetrate and kill the mold spores. For tougher stains, you might extend this waiting period slightly, ensuring the area remains saturated.

> 💡 **Tip:** For deeply embedded mold on porous surfaces, multiple applications might be necessary.

### Step 5: Scrub the Mold Away

Using a soft brush or scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold off the wall. Start from the top of the affected area and work your way down. Be thorough but avoid aggressive scrubbing that could damage the wall surface or spread spores unnecessarily. Dispose of any heavily soiled sponges or cloths.

> 💡 **Tip:** For delicate painted surfaces, use a very soft cloth or sponge to avoid stripping paint.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the area with a clean, damp microfiber cloth to remove any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. Ensure no moisture is left behind, as this can encourage mold regrowth. Use a dry cloth to blot the area, then use fans or open windows to thoroughly air dry the wall for several hours.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a dehumidifier to speed up drying and prevent future moisture buildup.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is completely dry, take steps to prevent mold from returning. This includes maintaining good ventilation, addressing any leaks promptly, and using mold-resistant paint in high-humidity areas. Regularly inspect areas prone to mold for any early signs of recurrence, allowing for quick intervention.

> 💡 **Tip:** Applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting can add an extra layer of protection against future mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure they don't damage or discolor the surface.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or suspected black mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. Learn more about [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- Improve air circulation by opening windows, using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and avoiding clutter against walls.
- Clean and dry spills or condensation immediately to prevent moisture from lingering and promoting mold growth.
- Consider installing humidity monitors in damp areas to help maintain optimal indoor humidity levels (typically 30-50%).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia or vinegar, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and get fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.
- ⚠️ Do not use abrasive tools or harsh chemicals on delicate wall surfaces like wallpaper, as this can cause irreversible damage.
- ⚠️ Be aware that mold can cause allergic reactions, asthma attacks, or other health problems in sensitive individuals. Always wear appropriate PPE.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls without damaging the paint?**

For painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like white vinegar or a mild dish soap solution. Spray it on the mold, let it sit for a few minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a clean, damp cloth and dry thoroughly to avoid stripping the paint or leaving water marks.

**What is the best homemade solution for removing mold from walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on walls. Simply spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the moldy area, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub and rinse. Hydrogen peroxide (3%) is another excellent non-toxic option.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation specialist if the moldy area is larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect black mold, or if the mold is due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage). Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive mold and prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is usually best handled by professionals. This indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be fully addressed to prevent structural damage and widespread mold growth. Attempting to clean it yourself without proper containment can spread spores.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

Bleach can be used on non-porous surfaces for mold removal, but it's not always the best choice for porous materials or painted walls, as it can discolor them or not penetrate deeply enough to kill the mold roots. If used, dilute 1 cup of bleach in 1 gallon of water, ensure extreme ventilation, and wear full protective gear.

**How long does it take for mold to grow on walls?**

Mold can begin to grow on walls within 24 to 48 hours if moisture is present and conditions are favorable. It thrives in damp, humid, and poorly ventilated environments, quickly colonizing surfaces once spores land and find a food source.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Prevent mold by controlling humidity levels (below 50%), ensuring good ventilation, promptly fixing any leaks or water damage, and regularly cleaning high-humidity areas. Using mold-resistant paint or primer can also offer additional protection.
