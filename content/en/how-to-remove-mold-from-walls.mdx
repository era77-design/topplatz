To remove mold from walls, first identify the source of moisture and ensure proper ventilation. Then, prepare the area with protective gear, clean the mold with appropriate solutions like white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide, and thoroughly dry the surface to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Plastic sheeting or tarp
- Scrub brush or stiff-bristled brush
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Clean cloths or sponges
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Bucket
- Mild dish soap (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Mold and Area

Before you begin, determine the size and type of mold growth. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's deeply embedded, or if you suspect it's black mold, professional help is highly recommended. Ensure the affected area can be well-ventilated before proceeding with cleaning.

### Step 2: Gather Protective Gear

Safety is paramount when dealing with mold spores and cleaning solutions. Don appropriate personal protective equipment, including an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling spores, rubber gloves to protect your skin, and safety goggles to shield your eyes. Wear old clothes that can be washed immediately or discarded after cleaning.

### Step 3: Prepare the Work Area

Isolate the moldy area to prevent spores from spreading to other parts of your home. Close doors and windows, and cover any nearby vents. Lay down plastic sheeting or a tarp on the floor directly beneath the mold to catch any falling mold particles or cleaning solution drips.

### Step 4: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most common molds, white vinegar is an effective and natural option. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and apply it directly onto the moldy surface. For tougher mold stains, a solution of 3% hydrogen peroxide can be used; spray it directly without diluting. Never mix different cleaning agents, especially bleach with ammonia, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 5: Scrub and Clean the Mold

Allow your chosen cleaning solution to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes to penetrate the growth. Then, use a stiff-bristled brush or scrub brush to vigorously scrub the mold away from the wall. You may need to reapply the solution and scrub multiple times for stubborn patches. Use a separate bucket of water with a little dish soap for rinsing your brush if needed.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining mold spores or cleaning solution residue. The most critical step to prevent mold recurrence is to ensure the wall is completely dry. Use a fan, open windows for cross-ventilation, or a dehumidifier to dry the area thoroughly and quickly.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the underlying moisture issue that led to the mold growth. This might involve fixing leaky pipes, sealing cracks, or improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting the wall to add an extra layer of protection against future mold.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage, especially on painted surfaces.
- For persistent mold or if you suspect [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold), consider consulting a professional mold remediation specialist for a thorough assessment.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas by using exhaust fans during and after showering or cooking, and consider running a dehumidifier in humid environments to keep moisture levels low.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have respiratory issues, severe allergies, or a compromised immune system without consulting a healthcare professional or hiring a specialist.
- ⚠️ If mold growth covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly a 3x3 foot section) or is deeply embedded within porous materials, it is safer and more effective to hire a professional mold remediation service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows similar steps to general wall mold removal. Use a gentle cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar or a mild detergent mixed with water. Scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint, and always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to check for discoloration.

**What is the best homemade solution to remove mold from walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective and generally safe for most wall surfaces. Spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub it away. Hydrogen peroxide (3%) is another excellent, non-toxic option for tackling tougher mold stains.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically indicates a significant hidden moisture problem and usually requires opening up the wall to access and properly treat the affected area. This type of mold removal is often best handled by a professional mold remediation specialist to ensure thorough removal and prevent further structural damage or health risks.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

While bleach can kill mold on non-porous surfaces, it's generally not recommended for porous walls because it often doesn't penetrate deeply enough to kill mold roots. It can also discolor surfaces, damage materials, and release harsh fumes. White vinegar or hydrogen peroxide are often safer and more effective alternatives for wall surfaces.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

The key to preventing mold recurrence is to control moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas by using exhaust fans or opening windows, and consider using a dehumidifier if your home has high humidity. Always ensure surfaces are thoroughly dried after cleaning or any water exposure.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, if you have health concerns or respiratory issues, or if the mold returns repeatedly despite your cleaning efforts. Mold found inside walls or after a flood also typically warrants professional assessment and remediation.
