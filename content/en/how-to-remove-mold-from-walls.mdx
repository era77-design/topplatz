To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold type and ensuring proper ventilation. Use a mild cleaning solution like white vinegar or a bleach solution, scrubbing the affected area thoroughly. Always wear protective gear and address the moisture source to prevent future growth.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Water
- Bucket
- Old towels or rags
- Fan or dehumidifier (optional)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Protective sheeting or plastic tarps

## Steps

### Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet) or you have health concerns, it's best to consult a professional.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the contaminated area by closing doors and covering vents to prevent spores from spreading to other parts of your home.

### Step 2: Prepare the Affected Area

Remove any furniture, rugs, or items from the immediate vicinity of the moldy wall. If items cannot be moved, cover them with plastic sheeting to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions. Lay down old towels or plastic tarps on the floor beneath the mold to catch any drips or debris.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, especially painted ones, a solution of white vinegar is effective and less harsh than bleach. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. For non-porous surfaces or more stubborn mold, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water) can be used. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint or surface. For detailed advice on specific types of mold, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray the chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, saturating it thoroughly. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a scrub brush or sponge, gently but firmly scrub the mold away from the wall. Avoid aggressive scrubbing that could damage the paint or plaster.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Once the mold is removed, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any residue from the cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process, ensuring no moisture remains. This step is critical for long-term mold prevention.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

After cleaning, identify and fix the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Maintain good ventilation in your home and consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Regularly inspect walls for any signs of returning moisture or mold to catch issues early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Never paint over mold without cleaning and drying the area first; it will only grow back.
- For stubborn mold stains, a hydrogen peroxide solution (3%) can be used as an alternative to vinegar or bleach.
- Always discard used sponges, rags, and cleaning materials in a sealed plastic bag immediately after cleaning to prevent spores from spreading.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.
- Improve air circulation by moving furniture away from walls and opening windows regularly.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as it can create highly toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean large areas of mold (exceeding 10 square feet) yourself; contact a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ If you suspect mold is growing inside walls or behind wallpaper, do not attempt to remove it without professional assessment, as disturbing it can release a high concentration of spores.
- ⚠️ If anyone in your household experiences respiratory issues or allergic reactions during or after mold removal, seek medical advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle. Spray the affected area, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a damp cloth and ensure the wall dries completely to protect the paint finish.

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way involves prioritizing safety with protective gear and good ventilation. For surface mold, white vinegar is highly effective. For more severe cases on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can be used. Always address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's recurrent, if you suspect it's growing inside walls, or if anyone in your home experiences persistent health issues that might be linked to mold exposure. Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise for safe remediation.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a complex and potentially hazardous task that usually requires professional intervention. It involves opening up the walls to identify the extent of the mold, removing contaminated materials, and addressing the moisture source. Attempting this yourself can spread spores and worsen the problem.

**Can I just paint over mold on walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, eventually reappearing and potentially causing more significant damage. Always clean and thoroughly dry the moldy area before applying any primer or paint.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, identify and fix the moisture source, such as leaks or condensation issues. Improve ventilation in your home, especially in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier if necessary. Regular cleaning and inspection can also help catch early signs of growth.
