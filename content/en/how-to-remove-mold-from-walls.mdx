Removing mold from walls typically involves cleaning the affected area with a suitable solution, ensuring proper ventilation, and wearing protective gear. It's crucial to address the underlying moisture source to prevent its return. For small, superficial mold growth, household cleaners can be effective.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 mask or respirator
- Old clothes
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- Mild dish soap
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Lay down old sheets or plastic sheeting to protect the floor from drips and mold spores. Don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, an N95 mask or respirator, and old clothes that can be washed immediately after or disposed of. This protects you from direct contact with mold and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there's moisture. Before cleaning, find and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Failing to address the moisture will only lead to the mold returning. This step is critical for long-term mold prevention.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup bleach per gallon of water can be used, but test in an inconspicuous area first. For a natural alternative, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is effective for many types of mold. Hydrogen peroxide (3%) can also be sprayed directly and left to sit for 10 minutes before scrubbing. For severe black mold, consider professional help or consult [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes (vinegar and peroxide need a bit longer to work). Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, scrub the mold away from the wall. Apply firm but gentle pressure to avoid damaging the wall surface.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the treated area, removing any residual cleaning solution and dead mold spores. Do not oversaturate the wall with water. Once rinsed, use dry cloths or paper towels to thoroughly dry the wall. You can also use a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process and prevent new mold growth.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Prevent Recurrence

Carefully gather all contaminated cloths, sponges, and sheeting. Dispose of them in a sealed bag or wash reusable items in hot water immediately. Keep the cleaned area dry and well-ventilated going forward. Consider using mold-inhibiting paint in susceptible areas or maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or mold suspected behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- Improve ventilation in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans during and after showering or cooking.
- Regularly inspect areas prone to moisture, such as basements, bathrooms, and around windows, for early signs of mold.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear protective gear, including gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 mask, to protect against mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure adequate ventilation when working with cleaning solutions, especially bleach, to avoid inhaling fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold on your own if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or a compromised immune system. Consult a professional.
- ⚠️ Avoid scrubbing mold aggressively on dry surfaces, as this can release a large number of spores into the air.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like white vinegar or a mild dish soap solution. Spray the solution, let it sit for a few minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse and dry thoroughly, ensuring the area is well-ventilated.

**What is the best homemade solution to remove mold from walls?**

For a homemade solution, white vinegar is highly effective. Spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for at least an hour, then scrub and rinse. Another option is a solution of 3% hydrogen peroxide, sprayed directly and left for 10-15 minutes before scrubbing.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is behind walls or in your HVAC system, or if you have health concerns like allergies or respiratory issues. Professionals have the specialized equipment and expertise to safely handle large infestations.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a job best left to professionals. It indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be fully addressed to prevent recurrence and ensure all mold is eliminated safely.

**Can mold grow back after I clean it?**

Yes, mold can grow back if the underlying moisture problem is not resolved. Simply cleaning the visible mold without addressing the source of dampness will likely lead to its return. Ensure the area remains dry and well-ventilated to prevent regrowth.

**Is it safe to clean mold with bleach?**

Bleach can be effective for killing mold on non-porous surfaces like tiles or glass, but it's less effective on porous surfaces like drywall where mold roots can penetrate. Always dilute bleach with water (1 cup per gallon), ensure excellent ventilation, and never mix it with other cleaners to avoid toxic fumes.
