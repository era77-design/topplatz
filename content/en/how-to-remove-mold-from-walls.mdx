To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. Then, clean the affected area with a mold-killing solution like white vinegar or a bleach solution, scrub gently, and allow the surface to dry completely to prevent regrowth.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Clean buckets
- Scrub brush or stiff-bristled brush
- Old rags or sponges
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on your rubber gloves, N95 respirator mask, and safety goggles to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. If the mold patch is large, consider covering nearby furniture with plastic sheeting to prevent spore spread.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Assess the Damage

Determine if the mold is superficial or if it indicates a deeper issue. Small surface mold growth (less than 10 square feet) can often be handled with DIY methods. If you suspect extensive mold growth behind walls or have encountered what appears to be black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) or consider contacting a professional.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For a natural and effective solution, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. For tougher mold on non-porous surfaces, you can mix 1 cup of bleach with 1 gallon of water in a bucket. Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous fumes.

### Step 4: Apply Solution and Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Let the solution sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a scrub brush or stiff-bristled brush, gently scrub the mold away. For painted walls, scrub carefully to avoid damaging the paint.

### Step 5: Wipe Clean and Dry Thoroughly

Once the mold is removed, wipe the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any residue. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a clean, dry cloth, open windows, run a fan, or employ a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is bone dry. Moisture is mold's best friend, so this step is critical.

### Step 6: Address the Moisture Source

Cleaning the mold is only a temporary fix if the underlying moisture problem isn't resolved. Identify and repair any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, or use a dehumidifier to maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Preventing moisture is key to long-term mold control.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For porous surfaces like drywall, avoid oversaturating the area, as too much moisture can cause further damage and encourage mold growth within the wall.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer to painted walls after cleaning and before repainting to offer an extra layer of protection.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows regularly or using exhaust fans, especially after showering or cooking.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaners, as it can produce toxic chlorine gas.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (N95 mask, gloves, goggles) to prevent inhaling mold spores or coming into contact with cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's part of a larger infestation behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning it first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the new paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way to remove mold from walls involves wearing protective gear, ensuring good ventilation, and cleaning with a solution like undiluted white vinegar or a bleach solution (for non-porous surfaces). After scrubbing, thoroughly dry the area to prevent future growth.

**How do you remove mold from painted walls without damaging the paint?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach. A solution of undiluted white vinegar is often effective and less harsh than bleach. Apply it with a soft cloth or sponge and gently scrub, then rinse and dry quickly to minimize exposure to moisture.

**What should I do if I suspect mold is growing inside walls?**

If you suspect mold is growing inside walls due to a musty odor or visible signs on the exterior, it indicates a significant moisture problem. This usually requires opening up the wall to properly assess, clean, and repair the damage. It's often best to consult a professional for this type of remediation.

**Can white vinegar effectively kill mold on walls?**

Yes, white vinegar is an excellent natural disinfectant and mold killer. Its acidic nature can kill up to 82% of mold species. Spray it directly onto the mold, let it sit for an hour, then scrub and wipe clean. It's safe for most surfaces and doesn't produce harsh fumes.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect hidden mold behind walls, or if anyone in your household has severe allergies or respiratory issues that could be aggravated by mold exposure.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold return involves controlling moisture. Fix leaks promptly, improve ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier if necessary. Good air circulation also helps.
