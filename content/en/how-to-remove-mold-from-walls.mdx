Removing mold from walls requires careful attention to safety and effective cleaning techniques. For minor mold issues, you can often tackle the problem yourself using common household products. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring proper ventilation during the process.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Old rags or towels
- Bucket
- White vinegar (undiluted) OR Household bleach
- Water (if using bleach)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional, for protecting floor/furniture)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Mold and Prepare the Area

Before you begin, determine the extent of the mold growth. If it covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet) or if you suspect it's behind walls, consider contacting a professional. For smaller areas, ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using fans. Cover nearby furniture and flooring with plastic sheeting to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Protect Yourself with PPE

Mold spores can be irritating and harmful to inhale. Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and safety goggles. This will help prevent skin contact, inhalation of spores, and eye irritation during the cleaning process.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup household bleach per gallon of water is effective. For painted walls or more sensitive surfaces, undiluted white vinegar is a gentler yet effective alternative. Pour your chosen solution into a clean spray bottle. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaning products, as this can create toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Let it Sit

Lightly spray the moldy area with your chosen cleaning solution, ensuring it's saturated but not dripping excessively. For bleach solutions, let it sit for about 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold. If using vinegar, let it sit for at least an hour before scrubbing.

### Step 5: Scrub and Clean the Mold Away

Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, firmly scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections, making sure to remove all visible traces of mold. For painted walls, use a softer sponge to avoid damaging the paint finish. Dispose of any rags or sponges used in a sealed bag immediately after cleaning.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining cleaning solution or mold residue. It is crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the area is thoroughly dry within 24-48 hours.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is clean and dry, address the underlying cause of the mold. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier to maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regular cleaning and moisture control are key to keeping mold from returning.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure they don't damage or discolor your wall surface.
- For small, surface mold, a simple solution of dish soap and water can sometimes be effective, followed by thorough drying.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to dampness.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and avoiding overcrowding furniture against walls.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, N95 respirator, and eye protection throughout the cleaning process.
- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaning products, as this can create highly toxic and dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (about 3x3 feet), if you suspect it's behind walls, or if you have health concerns, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning and addressing the moisture source, as the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls safely and effectively?**

To safely remove mold from walls, wear protective gear like gloves, a respirator, and goggles. Use a solution of bleach and water for non-porous surfaces or undiluted white vinegar for painted walls. Scrub the mold away, rinse, and ensure the area dries completely to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls without damaging the paint?**

For painted walls, use a gentler approach with undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle. Spray the mold, let it sit for at least an hour, then gently scrub with a soft sponge. Rinse with a clean, damp cloth and ensure the wall dries thoroughly to protect the paint finish.

**How do you get mold out of drywall?**

For surface mold on drywall, follow the steps for painted walls using vinegar. However, if the mold has penetrated deep into the drywall, or if you suspect mold growth inside walls, removal typically requires cutting out and replacing the affected sections. This often indicates a significant moisture issue that may require professional assessment.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

The best cleaner depends on the wall surface. For non-porous walls, a bleach and water solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) is highly effective. For painted or more porous surfaces, undiluted white vinegar is a safer and effective alternative that kills most mold types without harsh chemicals.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold growth inside walls, or if you have health concerns. Professionals have the specialized equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive or hidden mold. For guidance on more severe cases, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity below 60%. Ensure good air circulation and periodically inspect walls for any signs of dampness.

**Is it safe to remove black mold yourself?**

While small patches of black mold on non-porous surfaces can often be handled with caution, it's generally recommended to consult a professional for larger areas or if you have health concerns. Black mold can be more hazardous. Always wear full PPE, ensure excellent ventilation, and consider professional help for extensive growth.
