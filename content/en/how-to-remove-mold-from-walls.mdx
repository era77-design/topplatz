Removing mold from walls is a common household task that can be tackled with the right approach and safety precautions. Small areas of surface mold can often be cleaned with household solutions, but larger infestations may require professional help. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Old clothes
- Plastic sheeting or tarps
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or old towels
- White distilled vinegar
- Baking soda
- Borax (optional)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces only)
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Safety First: Gear Up and Ventilate

Before you begin, don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores. Open windows and doors to ensure good cross-ventilation in the area. If possible, use a fan to direct airflow outdoors, but avoid blowing air directly onto the mold as this can spread spores.

### Step 2: 2. Identify the Source and Contain the Area

Mold grows where there's moisture, so it's crucial to identify and fix the underlying water problem, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Once the source is addressed, isolate the work area by closing doors and covering doorways with plastic sheeting to prevent spores from spreading to other rooms. Lay down plastic sheeting or tarps on the floor to catch any mold debris or cleaning solutions.

### Step 3: 3. Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, a simple white vinegar solution is effective and non-toxic. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white distilled vinegar. For tougher stains or non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup bleach per gallon of water can be used, but never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia. Borax (1 cup per gallon of water) is another effective, less harsh option that inhibits mold growth.

### Step 4: 4. Apply and Scrub Away the Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge, vigorously scrub the mold away from the wall. For stubborn areas, reapply the solution and scrub again.

### Step 5: 5. Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any remaining mold residue or cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate re-growth. Use clean, dry towels, and run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours until the wall is bone dry.

### Step 6: 6. Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Place all used cloths, sponges, and any plastic sheeting in a heavy-duty trash bag. Seal the bag tightly and dispose of it immediately to prevent mold spores from spreading. Clean your gloves and scrub brush thoroughly, or dispose of them if heavily contaminated.

### Step 7: 7. Monitor and Prevent Future Growth

After cleaning, regularly inspect the area for any signs of mold recurrence. Maintain good ventilation and control humidity levels in your home, especially in bathrooms, kitchens, and basements. Consider using mold-inhibiting paint in areas prone to moisture to help prevent future issues.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the paint or surface.
- For porous surfaces like drywall, bleach can be less effective as it doesn't penetrate deeply enough to kill mold roots. Vinegar or borax are often better choices.
- If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or if you suspect it's black mold, consider calling a professional mold remediation service. For more specific guidance on severe infestations, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not use harsh scrubbing pads on painted walls, as they can remove paint along with the mold.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure the area is well-ventilated and wear protective gear. Spray the mold with undiluted white distilled vinegar or a mild borax solution (1 cup borax per gallon of water). Let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly with a fan.

**What's the best way to remove mold from drywall?**

For mold on drywall, avoid oversaturating the surface as drywall can be easily damaged by excessive moisture. Use a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar or a borax solution. Lightly mist the area, allow it to sit, then gently scrub with a soft brush. Ensure the area is dried completely and promptly to prevent further damage and re-growth.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically indicates a significant moisture problem and often requires professional intervention. This usually involves opening up the wall to expose the mold, addressing the moisture source, and then cleaning or replacing affected materials. Attempting to clean mold inside walls without proper containment can spread spores and worsen the problem.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, or if you have mold growing inside walls or in HVAC systems. Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely contain and remove large infestations and address hidden moisture issues.

**Is black mold more dangerous, and how do I remove it?**

While all mold should be treated with caution due to potential health effects, certain types like Stachybotrys chartarum (often referred to as 'black mold') can produce mycotoxins. If you suspect you have black mold, it's highly recommended to consult a professional for safe removal, especially if the area is large. For more detailed information, please see our article on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls in the future?**

Preventing mold involves controlling moisture and humidity. Fix any leaks immediately, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and use exhaust fans. Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier if necessary. Regularly clean and dry surfaces prone to moisture, and ensure proper drainage around your home's foundation.

**What if the mold keeps coming back after cleaning?**

If mold repeatedly returns after cleaning, it's a strong indicator that the underlying moisture source has not been fully addressed. Re-evaluate the area for hidden leaks, excessive condensation, or persistent humidity issues. You may need to consult a plumber, HVAC specialist, or mold remediation professional to identify and permanently fix the root cause.
