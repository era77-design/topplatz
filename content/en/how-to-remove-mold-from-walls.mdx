To effectively remove mold from walls, prioritize safety by ensuring good ventilation and wearing protective gear. For most surface mold, a simple solution of white vinegar or diluted bleach can be applied, scrubbed, and then thoroughly dried to prevent its return.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Safety glasses
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar or household bleach
- Water
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- Old towels or rags
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Protect yourself by wearing rubber gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 mask or respirator to avoid inhaling mold spores or cleaning fumes. Move furniture away from the wall and cover any nearby items with plastic sheeting to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold Source and Type

Determine the cause of the mold growth, such as a leaky pipe or high humidity, and address it to prevent recurrence. While most surface mold can be cleaned, some types, like black mold, require extra caution. If you suspect black mold or have extensive growth, it's often best to consult a professional. For more detailed information on specific mold types, refer to resources like [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces and painted walls, you can use either white vinegar or diluted bleach. For vinegar, pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. For bleach, mix one cup of household bleach with one gallon of water in a bucket. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub Away Mold

Spray your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sponge, vigorously scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any remaining solution and mold residue. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use old towels to blot the area dry, then use a fan or dehumidifier to ensure no moisture remains.

### Step 6: Dispose of Materials and Prevent Recurrence

Place all used rags, sponges, and protective gear that cannot be cleaned into a sealed plastic bag and dispose of them immediately. To prevent future mold growth, maintain good ventilation in your home, especially in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Address any leaks promptly and consider using a dehumidifier in damp environments.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the paint or surface.
- For stubborn mold stains, a paste made from baking soda and water can be applied, allowed to dry, and then scrubbed off.
- Improve air circulation by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and ensuring furniture isn't pushed directly against exterior walls.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaning products, as this creates dangerous toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including an N95 mask, safety glasses, and rubber gloves, to protect against mold spores and harsh chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning it first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the new paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls without damaging the paint?**

To remove mold from painted walls safely, use a solution of undiluted white vinegar or a mild detergent mixed with water. Apply it gently with a soft sponge or cloth, scrub lightly, and rinse quickly. Vinegar is generally less harsh on paint than bleach, but always test in an inconspicuous area first.

**What is the most effective solution for removing mold from walls?**

For most surface mold on non-porous walls, white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) are highly effective. Vinegar kills about 82% of mold species and is a natural alternative, while bleach is a powerful disinfectant for surface mold.

**Can mold grow inside walls, and how do I address it?**

Yes, mold can grow inside walls, often due to hidden leaks or excessive humidity. If you suspect mold inside walls (e.g., musty smell, visible warping), you'll likely need to open the wall to inspect and clean. This often requires professional help to ensure all mold is removed and the moisture source is addressed.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold growth covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you have a severe mold allergy or respiratory issues, or if you suspect the presence of toxic black mold. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively remediate large or hazardous infestations.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls after cleaning?**

To prevent mold recurrence, identify and fix the source of moisture, such as leaks or condensation. Improve ventilation by using exhaust fans and opening windows, and consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%.

**Is it safe to clean mold myself, or are there risks involved?**

Cleaning small areas of mold yourself is generally safe if you take proper precautions, including wearing an N95 mask, gloves, and safety glasses, and ensuring good ventilation. However, for large areas, black mold, or if you have health concerns, it's safer to consult a professional due to potential health risks from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
