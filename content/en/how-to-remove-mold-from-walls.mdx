Removing mold from walls is a common household task that can be managed safely with the right tools and techniques. For small surface areas, simple household solutions like vinegar or bleach can be effective, but always prioritize safety.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean rags or microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- White distilled vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Heavy-duty trash bags

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and eye protection to prevent contact with mold spores. Change into old clothes that you don't mind getting dirty or potentially stained by cleaning solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you have allergies or respiratory issues, consider having someone else perform the cleaning or consult a professional.

### Step 2: 2. Prepare the Affected Area

Remove any furniture, rugs, or other items from the immediate vicinity of the mold. If items cannot be moved, cover them with plastic sheeting to protect them from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Lay down old towels or plastic sheeting on the floor directly beneath the moldy area to catch any drips or debris.

### Step 3: 3. Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, white distilled vinegar is an excellent, non-toxic choice. Pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle. For more stubborn mold on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but never mix bleach with vinegar or other cleaners as this can create toxic fumes. For information on more severe infestations, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: 4. Apply the Solution and Scrub

Generously spray the moldy area with your chosen solution, making sure to saturate the mold. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, gently scrub the mold away. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning, being careful not to spread spores to unaffected areas.

### Step 5: 5. Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Once the mold is removed, lightly dampen a clean rag with plain water and wipe down the cleaned area to remove any residue from the cleaning solution. The most crucial step to prevent mold recurrence is thorough drying. Use clean, dry rags to absorb as much moisture as possible, then use a fan or dehumidifier to completely dry the wall for several hours.

### Step 6: 6. Dispose of Materials and Prevent Recurrence

Place all used rags, sponges, and protective gear (if disposable) into a heavy-duty trash bag, seal it tightly, and dispose of it outdoors immediately. To prevent future mold growth, address any sources of moisture, such as leaks or high humidity. Ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using mold-inhibiting paint in damp areas.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always tackle mold in small sections to prevent cross-contamination and to manage the task more effectively.
- If you're dealing with a large mold infestation (more than 10 square feet) or suspect hidden mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using dehumidifiers, especially in basements and bathrooms, to inhibit mold growth.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can produce dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Avoid dry brushing mold, as this can release a high concentration of spores into the air, potentially spreading the problem and causing respiratory irritation.
- ⚠️ If mold returns quickly after cleaning, it indicates a persistent moisture problem that needs to be identified and fixed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold thrives in damp, humid environments. Common causes include water leaks, condensation from poor ventilation, high indoor humidity levels, and areas where moisture gets trapped, such as behind furniture against an exterior wall.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

When removing mold from painted walls, it's best to use a gentler approach to avoid damaging the paint. White distilled vinegar is generally safe for most painted surfaces. Apply it, let it sit, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, followed by rinsing and thorough drying.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls yourself?**

For small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), it is generally safe to remove yourself with proper safety precautions. However, for larger infestations, recurring mold, or if you have health concerns, it's advisable to consult a professional.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

White distilled vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on most surfaces and is a safer, natural alternative to bleach. For non-porous surfaces with stubborn mold, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can be used, but always ensure proper ventilation and never mix with other cleaners.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. If you suspect mold behind drywall due to a musty smell, water stains, or visible mold on the exterior wall, it likely indicates a significant moisture issue that needs to be addressed by a remediation specialist who can safely open walls and fix the source of the dampness.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure adequate ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier if needed. Regularly inspect walls for signs of moisture.
