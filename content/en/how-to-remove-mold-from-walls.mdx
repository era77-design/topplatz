Removing mold from walls involves identifying the type and extent of the growth, preparing the area safely, and applying an appropriate cleaning solution. With the right tools and precautions, you can effectively eliminate surface mold and prevent its return.

## What You Need

- Safety glasses
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Borax (optional)
- Dish soap (optional)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before tackling any mold, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Put on safety glasses, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Remove any furniture or objects near the wall to prevent contamination and provide clear access.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent spores from spreading throughout your home.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Assess Severity

Observe the mold's appearance. Small, superficial mold patches (often green, black, or white) on surfaces are usually manageable. If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), appears fuzzy, or you suspect it's deeply embedded or is black mold, it might require professional remediation. For guidance on more severe cases, refer to resources like [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Never attempt to dry-brush mold, as this releases spores into the air.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most common surface mold, a solution of distilled white vinegar is highly effective. You can also use a mixture of one cup borax dissolved in one gallon of hot water, or a diluted dish soap solution for very light growth. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it often doesn't penetrate to kill the roots and can damage the material.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Pour your chosen solution into a spray bottle. Liberally spray the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. For vinegar or borax solutions, let it sit for at least 15-30 minutes to allow the solution to penetrate and kill the mold spores and roots. This dwell time is crucial for effective removal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the area remains wet with the solution during the dwell time for maximum effectiveness.

### Step 5: Scrub and Remove the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a scrub sponge, firmly scrub the moldy surface. Work in small sections, applying consistent pressure to lift the mold from the wall. If the mold is stubborn, reapply the solution and let it sit for a few more minutes before scrubbing again. Continue until all visible mold is gone.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always scrub away from yourself to avoid splashing mold spores or cleaning solution onto your face.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp microfiber cloth to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a fan or open windows to expedite drying. A dehumidifier can also help remove excess moisture from the air.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not rinse with too much water, especially on drywall, as excessive moisture can cause further damage and mold growth.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the source of moisture that led to the mold growth, whether it's a leaky pipe, high humidity, or condensation. Ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Regularly clean and inspect walls, especially in high-moisture zones, to catch any new growth early.

> 💡 **Tip:** Apply a mold-resistant primer and paint to walls in areas prone to dampness for added protection.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear protective gear, including an N95 mask, safety glasses, and rubber gloves.
- Ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using fans during and after cleaning.
- Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar, as this can create toxic fumes.
- Address the underlying moisture problem to prevent mold from returning.
- For extensive mold growth or if you suspect mold is inside walls, consult a professional.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it can damage the material and may not effectively kill mold roots.
- ⚠️ Avoid disturbing large areas of mold, especially black mold, without proper protective gear and containment.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently clean with a solution of white vinegar or a mild dish soap and water. Test the solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't strip the paint. Scrub with a soft brush or sponge, then rinse and dry thoroughly.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often indicates a significant moisture issue. It's generally recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for thorough and safe removal.

**What is the best homemade solution to kill mold on walls?**

Distilled white vinegar is an excellent natural mold killer. Spray it undiluted onto the mold, let it sit for at least 15-30 minutes, then scrub and wipe clean. Borax mixed with water is another effective and non-toxic option.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls myself?**

For small, superficial patches of mold (less than 10 square feet) and if you are not sensitive to mold, it is generally safe to remove it yourself with proper protective gear and ventilation. For larger areas, suspected black mold, or if you have health concerns, it's best to call a professional.

**How can I prevent mold from growing back on my walls?**

To prevent mold recurrence, identify and fix the moisture source, whether it's a leak or high humidity. Ensure good ventilation in damp areas, use a dehumidifier, and clean surfaces regularly. Consider using mold-inhibiting primers and paints in prone areas.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

While bleach can kill surface mold, it's generally not recommended for porous surfaces like drywall because it doesn't penetrate to kill the mold roots and can damage the material. It also releases harsh fumes. Vinegar or borax solutions are often safer and more effective alternatives.

**How long does it take for mold to grow on walls?**

Mold can begin to grow on walls within 24-48 hours if moisture is present and conditions are favorable. It thrives in damp, dark environments, so addressing moisture promptly is crucial to prevent its rapid spread.
