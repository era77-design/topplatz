Removing mold from walls typically involves cleaning the affected area with a mold-killing solution, scrubbing, and ensuring the surface is thoroughly dried. Always wear protective gear to prevent exposure to mold spores. Addressing the moisture source is crucial to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or scrub brush
- Microfiber cloths or sponges
- Two buckets (one for solution, one for rinse water)
- White vinegar (undiluted) OR Borax (1 cup per gallon of hot water)
- Clean water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional, to protect floor)
- Trash bags

## Steps

### Step 1: Gather Your Gear and Prepare the Area

Before you begin, don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Open windows and doors to ensure good ventilation in the room. If desired, lay down plastic sheeting to protect your floor or nearby furniture from drips.

### Step 2: Identify and Isolate the Mold

Carefully assess the extent of the mold growth. This guide is for small, superficial patches of mold. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's black mold, or if you suspect it's growing inside your walls, it's best to consult a professional for safe and effective removal.

> 💡 **Tip:** For extensive black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most wall surfaces, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is an effective and natural mold killer. Alternatively, you can mix 1 cup of borax with 1 gallon of hot water in a bucket, stirring until the borax is fully dissolved. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces or mixing it with other cleaners.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution to the Mold

Spray your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring the entire affected surface is thoroughly saturated. If using the borax solution, you can apply it with a sponge or cloth. Allow the solution to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold spores.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a dedicated scrub brush, gently scrub the mold away from the wall. For painted walls, scrub gently to avoid damaging the paint. Wipe the area clean with a damp microfiber cloth, rinsing the cloth frequently in a separate bucket of clean water to avoid spreading spores.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Once the mold is removed, lightly mist the cleaned area with clean water to rinse off any remaining cleaning solution. Immediately dry the wall completely using clean, dry cloths. Use a fan or dehumidifier to ensure the wall is bone-dry, as any residual moisture can quickly lead to mold regrowth.

### Step 7: Address the Moisture Source

The most critical step in preventing future mold is to eliminate its water source. Fix any leaks immediately, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Without addressing the moisture, mold will likely return.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix different cleaning solutions, especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and running a dehumidifier in humid environments.
- For persistent mold odors after cleaning, place bowls of activated charcoal or baking soda in the room to absorb lingering smells.
- Dispose of all cleaning materials, including cloths and brushes, in sealed trash bags immediately after use to prevent mold spore spread.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (N95 mask, goggles, gloves) when dealing with mold to prevent inhalation of spores and skin irritation.
- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or vinegar, as this creates highly toxic and dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet), or if you suspect it's black mold or growing inside walls, it's recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly removing it first. Painting over mold will not kill it and it will eventually grow through the new paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like diluted white vinegar or a borax solution. Test it on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint. Scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge, then rinse and thoroughly dry the wall.

**What is the best homemade solution for removing mold from walls?**

Undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is a highly effective and natural mold killer for most wall surfaces. Another excellent option is a solution of 1 cup of borax dissolved in 1 gallon of hot water. These options are safer than bleach for many household applications.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, or if you find mold growing inside walls. Professionals have the specialized equipment and expertise to safely and effectively handle extensive or hidden mold infestations.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence hinges on controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure good ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and use dehumidifiers to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Always thoroughly dry any wet surfaces immediately.

**Can I remove mold from inside walls myself?**

Removing mold from inside walls is generally not a DIY task and is best left to professionals. It often requires opening up the wall, identifying the moisture source, and safely removing or replacing affected materials, which can expose you to high concentrations of mold spores.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls myself?**

For small, superficial patches of mold, it is generally safe to remove yourself with proper protective gear and good ventilation. However, for large infestations, black mold, or mold inside walls, professional intervention is strongly recommended to ensure safety and effective remediation.
