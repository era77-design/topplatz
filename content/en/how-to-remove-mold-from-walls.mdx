To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear like gloves and a mask. Clean the affected area with a solution of white vinegar and water or a mild detergent, scrubbing gently until the mold is gone. Finish by thoroughly drying the area to prevent future growth and address any underlying moisture issues.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar (undiluted)
- Microfiber cloths or soft-bristle brush
- Bucket
- Warm water
- Mild dish soap (optional)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Dry towels

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, open windows and doors to ensure good ventilation in the room. Put on your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask or respirator to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Clear the immediate area around the moldy wall of any furniture or decor.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Its Source

Observe the extent and type of mold. Small, superficial patches are generally safe to tackle yourself. If you suspect you're dealing with black mold, or if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), it's best to consult a professional. Identifying and fixing the moisture source, such as a leak or condensation, is crucial for preventing recurrence. For specific guidance on more stubborn types, you may find additional tips in [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most wall mold, a solution of undiluted white vinegar is effective and safe. Fill a spray bottle with white vinegar. For very mild cases or painted walls, you can use a solution of warm water mixed with a few drops of mild dish soap. Avoid using bleach, especially on painted surfaces, as it can damage paint and release strong fumes without effectively killing mold roots.

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray the chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Then, use a microfiber cloth or a soft-bristle brush to gently scrub the mold away. Avoid aggressive scrubbing on painted walls to prevent removing paint.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Treated Area

Once the mold is removed, dampen a clean microfiber cloth with plain water and wipe down the treated area to remove any cleaning solution residue. It's crucial to dry the wall thoroughly to prevent mold from returning. Use dry towels, run a fan, or employ a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is completely dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials Safely

Place all used cloths, sponges, and brushes in a sealed plastic bag immediately after cleaning. Dispose of the bag in an outdoor trash can to prevent the spread of mold spores indoors. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water after removing your gloves and mask.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Address the underlying moisture problem (e.g., leaky pipes, condensation) to prevent mold from returning.
- Maintain good air circulation in your home, especially in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens, to deter mold growth.
- Consider using mold-inhibiting primers or paints in areas prone to dampness.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Avoid scrubbing dry mold, as it can release a significant amount of spores into the air, potentially worsening respiratory issues.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if it quickly returns after cleaning, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask to avoid inhaling mold spores or skin contact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, opt for gentle cleaning solutions like white vinegar or a mild dish soap and water mixture. Avoid harsh chemicals or abrasive scrubbing, which can damage the paint. Always test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is generally not a DIY task. Mold within walls indicates a significant, often hidden, moisture problem that could compromise structural integrity. It's highly recommended to contact a professional mold remediation service to safely identify the source, remove the mold, and repair any damage.

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way to remove mold from walls depends on the severity and type of mold. For small, superficial patches, a solution of undiluted white vinegar is often effective and safe. Always ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear during the cleaning process.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

While bleach can kill surface mold, it's not always the most effective or safest option for walls, especially painted ones. Bleach can damage paint, and its fumes can be irritating. White vinegar is often preferred as it penetrates porous surfaces better and is less harsh.

**How do I prevent mold from growing on walls again?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves addressing the underlying moisture source. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in humid areas, use a dehumidifier, and ensure good airflow. Keeping indoor humidity levels below 60% can significantly deter mold growth.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal from walls?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), if it's recurrent, if you suspect it's inside walls, or if anyone in your household experiences persistent health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and thoroughly remove extensive mold.
