Removing mold from walls involves identifying the source of moisture, preparing the area for cleaning, and using appropriate solutions like diluted bleach or vinegar. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation during the process.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- White distilled vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Clean cloths/rags
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Drop cloths or plastic sheeting

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows or using fans. Lay down drop cloths or plastic sheeting to protect the floor and furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect your eyes, skin, and respiratory system from mold and chemical fumes.

### Step 2: Identify and Fix the Moisture Source

Mold cannot grow without moisture. It's crucial to find and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, a humid room, or condensation. Simply cleaning the mold without addressing the underlying moisture issue will only lead to its recurrence.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a hygrometer to monitor indoor humidity levels, aiming for below 60%.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, a solution of white distilled vinegar works effectively. You can also use a mixture of 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water for non-porous surfaces, but avoid this on painted or colored walls as it can cause discoloration. Always test your chosen solution in an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the wall finish.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Using a spray bottle, generously apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes to penetrate and kill the mold spores. Then, using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall surface.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residue from the cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's vital to dry the wall completely to prevent new mold growth. Use fans, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the area is bone dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully gather all used cloths, sponges, drop cloths, and any other materials that came into contact with the mold. Place them in a sealed plastic bag and dispose of them immediately outside your home. Clean your protective gear thoroughly before storing it away.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to prevent damage or discoloration.
- For large mold infestations (exceeding 10 square feet) or if you suspect black mold, it's safer to consult a professional mold remediation service. See [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for more information.
- Improve ventilation in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans or opening windows to reduce moisture buildup.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create highly toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first, as the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.
- ⚠️ If you experience persistent respiratory issues, headaches, or other health problems while dealing with mold, consult a medical professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, start by fixing any moisture source. Then, apply a cleaning solution like white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (for non-porous surfaces) to the affected area. Scrub the mold away, rinse, and ensure the wall is thoroughly dried to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

When removing mold from painted walls, opt for gentler solutions like white distilled vinegar, as bleach can strip paint or cause discoloration. Apply the vinegar, let it sit, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with clean water and dry completely to protect the paint finish.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a more complex and potentially hazardous task. It's generally recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for mold growing inside walls, as it can indicate a significant moisture issue and widespread growth.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

White distilled vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on most wall surfaces, including painted ones, and is a safer alternative to harsh chemicals. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can also be used, but always test it first.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, address the root cause of moisture, such as leaks or high humidity. Ensure good ventilation, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider a dehumidifier in damp areas. Regularly clean and dry surfaces that are prone to moisture.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the moldy area is larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is behind walls or in HVAC systems, or if you or family members experience severe health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment for safe and thorough remediation.
