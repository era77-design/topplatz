To remove mold from walls, prioritize safety by ensuring good ventilation and wearing protective gear like gloves and a mask. For visible surface mold, clean the area with a diluted solution of white vinegar or bleach, scrubbing gently. After cleaning, thoroughly rinse and dry the wall to prevent mold from returning.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- White vinegar (undiluted or diluted 1:1 with water)
- Bleach (optional, diluted 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water)
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Protective sheeting (optional, for floor/furniture)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Protect yourself by wearing rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling mold spores. If possible, remove furniture from the immediate area or cover it with plastic sheeting to prevent contamination.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off any HVAC systems to prevent spreading spores throughout your home.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold Type and Extent

Determine if the mold is superficial or if it indicates a deeper issue. Small patches of surface mold, often green, black, or white, can typically be handled yourself. However, if you suspect extensive mold growth behind walls, or if you encounter [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) in large quantities, it's best to consult a professional.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, professional assessment is recommended.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

White vinegar is a natural, non-toxic mold killer for porous and non-porous surfaces. Use it undiluted in a spray bottle or diluted 1:1 with water. For more stubborn mold or non-porous surfaces, a bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water) can be effective. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the wall surface.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring full coverage. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes. This dwell time is crucial for the solution to penetrate and kill the mold spores effectively, making subsequent scrubbing easier and more efficient.

> 💡 **Tip:** For vinegar, you can let it sit for up to an hour for maximum effectiveness.

### Step 5: Scrub and Clean the Wall

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, gently but firmly scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections, applying consistent pressure until the visible mold is removed. Be careful not to damage the wall material, especially if it's painted or drywall.

> 💡 **Tip:** For painted walls, use a softer brush or sponge to avoid stripping paint.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining mold spores and cleaning solution residue. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate mold regrowth. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process, ensuring no moisture is left behind.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry before considering repainting or sealing.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

The best way to keep mold from returning is to address the underlying moisture source. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regular cleaning and inspection can also help catch new growth early.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to dampness.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always address the moisture source that caused the mold in the first place to prevent recurrence.
- Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask, even for small patches of mold.
- For large areas of mold (over 10 square feet) or if you suspect mold within wall cavities, consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas by using exhaust fans or opening windows, especially after showering or cooking.
- Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier if necessary.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, as this creates highly toxic and potentially fatal chlorine gas.
- ⚠️ Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE) to protect your skin, eyes, and respiratory system from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If you suspect mold is growing inside walls or if you find extensive black mold, do not attempt to clean it yourself. This often requires professional assessment and remediation to ensure safe and complete removal.
- ⚠️ Avoid excessively wetting drywall, as it can be easily damaged and create a breeding ground for more mold if not dried quickly and thoroughly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. Gently scrub the mold with a soft brush or sponge using a diluted white vinegar solution (1:1 with water) or a mild bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water). Test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to avoid paint damage, then rinse and dry thoroughly.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. This usually indicates a significant moisture problem and may involve opening up the wall to properly clean, treat, and dry the affected area. Attempting to clean mold within walls without proper equipment and expertise can spread spores and worsen the problem.

**What causes mold on walls?**

Mold on walls is primarily caused by excess moisture and high humidity. This can result from leaky pipes, roof leaks, condensation, poor ventilation, or water intrusion from outside. Mold spores are always present in the air, but they only grow and multiply when they find a damp surface with an organic food source.

**Is it safe to clean mold yourself?**

Small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) can often be safely cleaned by homeowners using proper protective equipment and cleaning solutions. However, for large infestations, persistent mold, or suspected black mold, it's safer and more effective to hire a professional mold remediation specialist.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is growing behind walls or in HVAC systems, if you have health concerns related to mold exposure, or if the mold returns repeatedly after cleaning. Professionals have the tools and expertise for safe and thorough remediation.

**How can I prevent mold from growing on walls?**

Prevent mold by controlling moisture. Fix leaks immediately, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and keep indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier if needed. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, and consider using mold-resistant paint in damp areas.

**What's the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

For most surface mold, white vinegar is an excellent and natural choice, effective on both porous and non-porous surfaces. For non-porous surfaces or stubborn mold, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can be used. Always ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear regardless of the cleaner used.
