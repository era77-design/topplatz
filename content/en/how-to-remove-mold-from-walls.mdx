To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. Use a household cleaning solution like white vinegar or borax, apply it to the affected area, and gently scrub the mold away before thoroughly drying the surface.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- White vinegar, borax, or hydrogen peroxide
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Wear protective gear including rubber gloves, an N95 mask or respirator, and eye protection to prevent contact with mold spores. If possible, isolate the area by closing doors and covering nearby items with plastic sheeting to prevent spore spread.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent mold spores from circulating throughout your home during cleaning.

### Step 2: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, white vinegar is an effective natural mold killer. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. Alternatively, you can mix 1 cup of borax with 1 gallon of hot water, or use a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution directly from its bottle. Avoid mixing different cleaning agents, especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar, as this can create dangerous fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the paint.

### Step 3: Apply the Solution to Mold

Generously spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes. This gives the active ingredients time to penetrate the mold and kill the spores, making it easier to scrub away.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn mold, you may need to reapply the solution and let it soak a bit longer.

### Step 4: Scrub and Remove the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, gently but firmly scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. Be careful not to scrub too aggressively, especially on painted walls, to avoid damaging the finish. Dispose of any heavily contaminated cloths or sponges immediately.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very small, light patches of surface mold, a damp cloth with a mild detergent might suffice before moving to stronger solutions.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent mold from returning. Use a clean, dry cloth, open windows, and run a fan or dehumidifier until the wall is bone dry.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is completely dry within 24-48 hours to prevent new mold growth. A dehumidifier can significantly speed up this process.

### Step 6: Address the Source of Moisture

Removing the mold is only a temporary fix if you don't address the underlying moisture problem. Inspect the area for leaks, condensation issues, or poor ventilation. Repair any leaks, improve air circulation, and consider using moisture absorbers or a dehumidifier to keep humidity levels down. This step is critical for long-term mold prevention.

> 💡 **Tip:** Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally 30-50%) to discourage mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) when dealing with mold to protect your respiratory system, skin, and eyes.
- For small, superficial mold growth, white vinegar or borax are often effective and safer alternatives to bleach, which can be harsh on surfaces and fumes.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans or opening windows during and after moisture-producing activities.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, especially vinegar, as this can create highly toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For more information, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning and addressing the moisture issue first, as the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.
- ⚠️ If you experience health symptoms like persistent coughing, sneezing, or difficulty breathing while dealing with mold, stop immediately and seek medical advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure the paint is durable enough to withstand scrubbing. Use a gentle cleaning solution like diluted white vinegar or a borax solution. Apply, let it sit, gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, and then rinse and dry thoroughly to avoid damaging the paint finish.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more extensive remediation project that often indicates a significant moisture problem. Due to potential structural damage and widespread spore contamination, it's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for this type of issue.

**What kills mold on walls effectively?**

Several common household solutions effectively kill mold on walls. White vinegar, used undiluted, is a popular choice. A solution of borax (1 cup per gallon of water) or 3% hydrogen peroxide also works well. For very stubborn mold on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but with extreme caution and proper ventilation.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches of superficial mold yourself, provided you take proper safety precautions like wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation. However, if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), appears to be black mold, or if you have underlying health conditions, it's safer to hire a professional.

**How do I prevent mold from coming back on my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, it's crucial to control moisture. Identify and fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using fans or a dehumidifier. Ensure surfaces are always dried quickly after getting wet, especially in bathrooms and kitchens.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), if you suspect it's black mold, or if you have a persistent mold problem that keeps returning. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and thoroughly address extensive or hidden mold issues, especially those within wall cavities.
