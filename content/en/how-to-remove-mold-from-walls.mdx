Removing mold from walls is crucial for maintaining a healthy home environment and preventing structural damage. With the right supplies and careful technique, you can effectively tackle most surface mold issues yourself. This guide will walk you through the process, ensuring a safe and thorough clean.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety glasses
- N95 mask
- Old clothes
- White vinegar (undiluted)
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or old sponge
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Bucket
- Dish soap (optional)
- Borax (optional)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)
- Sealed plastic bags

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows or using a fan. Put on protective gear including rubber gloves, safety glasses, and a N95 mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or cleaning fumes. Remove any furniture or items from the affected area to create a clear workspace and minimize contamination.

### Step 2: Identify and Isolate the Mold

Determine the extent of the mold growth. For small, surface-level mold patches (less than 10 square feet), DIY removal is generally safe. If the mold covers a large area, appears furry, or has penetrated deeply into the wall, it's best to consult a professional. Isolate the area with plastic sheeting if possible to prevent spores from spreading.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you suspect you're dealing with black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for specific precautions.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold, a solution of undiluted white vinegar is effective. Fill a spray bottle with white vinegar and spray it directly onto the mold. Alternatively, you can use a solution of 1 cup borax dissolved in 1 gallon of hot water, or a mixture of dish soap and water for very mild cases. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it may not kill the mold's roots and can damage the material.

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Allow your chosen cleaning solution to sit on the mold for at least 15-30 minutes to penetrate and kill the spores. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or an old sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections, being careful not to spread spores to other areas. For painted walls, scrub gently to avoid damaging the paint.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe away any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use old towels to absorb moisture, and then run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours until the wall is bone dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Place all used sponges, cloths, and protective gear (if disposable) into a sealed plastic bag immediately after cleaning. Dispose of the bag in an outdoor trash can to prevent mold spores from re-entering your home. Thoroughly clean and disinfect any reusable tools you used with hot, soapy water.

### Step 7: Address the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there is moisture. Identify and fix the source of the dampness that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Repair any leaks promptly and consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas to maintain humidity levels below 60%.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans during and after showering or cooking.
- For extensive mold growth, or if you have underlying health conditions, consider hiring a professional mold remediation service.
- Never paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the affected area first, as the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.
- Keep indoor humidity levels between 30-50% to discourage mold growth.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask to avoid direct contact with mold and inhaling spores.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet) or if you suspect it's growing behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Address the underlying moisture problem that caused the mold; otherwise, it will likely return.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls requires a gentle approach to avoid damaging the finish. Use a mild cleaning solution like white vinegar or a mix of dish soap and water, applied with a soft cloth or sponge. Scrub gently, rinse thoroughly with clean water, and ensure the wall dries completely to prevent recurrence.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls, especially if it's extensive or behind drywall, often requires professional intervention. This usually involves opening up the wall to access and remediate the mold, as well as fixing the underlying moisture problem. Attempting this yourself without proper equipment and experience can spread spores and pose health risks.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

To kill mold on walls permanently, you must not only remove the visible mold but also eliminate its moisture source. Cleaning solutions like vinegar or hydrogen peroxide can kill mold, but ensuring the area remains dry and addressing issues like leaks or high humidity are essential for long-term prevention.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, or if you experience health symptoms related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely contain, remove, and prevent mold recurrence.

**Can I just paint over mold?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow underneath the paint, potentially damaging the wall further and releasing spores. Always clean and thoroughly dry the moldy surface first before applying any primer or paint.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Ensure good ventilation in your home, especially in bathrooms and kitchens. Fix any leaks promptly, use a dehumidifier in damp areas to keep humidity below 60%, and clean up spills or condensation immediately.
