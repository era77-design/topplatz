Removing mold from walls is a manageable task for small infestations, but it requires proper safety precautions and the right cleaning agents. Addressing mold quickly prevents it from spreading and helps maintain a healthy indoor environment. Always wear protective gear and ensure good ventilation during the cleaning process.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Buckets
- Clean rags or towels
- White vinegar
- Borax
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Drop cloths or plastic sheeting

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Lay down drop cloths or plastic sheeting to protect the floor and furniture from cleaning solutions and mold spores. Crucially, wear your protective gear: rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and safety goggles to prevent contact with mold spores and cleaning chemicals. This step is vital for your health and safety.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent mold spores from circulating throughout your home during cleaning.

### Step 2: Identify the Scope and Type of Mold

Assess the size of the mold growth. For areas larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect black mold, it's generally recommended to consult a professional. For smaller, surface-level mold, proceed with caution. Understanding the extent helps determine if this is a DIY job or requires expert intervention. If you encounter significant black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Never scrape or brush dry mold, as this can release spores into the air. Lightly dampen the area if you need to before cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water can be effective, but avoid using it on painted walls as it can strip paint. A safer and often equally effective option for most surfaces, including painted walls, is undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle. For a non-toxic alternative, mix 1 cup borax with 1 gallon of hot water until dissolved. Always prepare your solution in a well-ventilated area.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply Solution and Scrub Away Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution generously onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes, which gives it time to penetrate and kill the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sponge, gently scrub the mold away. Apply consistent pressure but avoid scrubbing so hard that you damage the wall surface.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn mold stains, you might need to reapply the solution and let it sit for a longer period before scrubbing again.

### Step 5: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

Once the mold is removed, use a clean rag dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residual cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use clean, dry towels, and run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours to ensure the wall is bone-dry. Moisture is mold's best friend.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a hairdryer on a cool setting for smaller areas to speed up the drying process.

### Step 6: Dispose of Materials and Prevent Future Growth

Carefully place all used rags, sponges, and drop cloths into a sealed plastic bag and dispose of them immediately outside your home. Clean your scrub brush thoroughly or discard it. To prevent future mold, address the source of moisture, improve ventilation, and consider using mold-inhibiting paint in damp areas. Regularly inspect walls for any new signs of moisture or mold.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider investing in a hygrometer to monitor indoor humidity levels, aiming to keep them below 60%.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always ensure adequate ventilation when cleaning mold to disperse spores and fumes from cleaning agents.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- For porous materials like drywall that have extensive mold, removal and replacement might be necessary.
- Address the root cause of the moisture problem (e.g., leaky pipes, condensation) to prevent mold from returning.
- If you have allergies or asthma, consider having someone else clean the mold or wear specialized respiratory protection.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never attempt to clean mold larger than 10 square feet yourself; this requires professional remediation.
- ⚠️ If you suspect black mold, or experience health symptoms like persistent coughing, wheezing, or skin irritation, consult a professional immediately.
- ⚠️ Wear an N95 respirator mask, rubber gloves, and safety goggles to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning it first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls without damaging the paint?**

For painted walls, opt for a gentler cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar or a borax solution instead of bleach. Spray the solution, let it sit, and gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid stripping the paint. Always test on an inconspicuous area first to ensure no discoloration occurs.

**What should I do if I suspect mold is growing inside walls?**

If you suspect mold inside walls due to a musty smell, water stains, or visible mold on the exterior wall surface, it's best to call a professional mold remediator. Mold inside walls often indicates a significant moisture issue that requires specialized equipment to locate and address, and DIY attempts can spread spores or miss the source.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold thrives in damp, dark environments. Common causes include high indoor humidity, water leaks from pipes or the roof, condensation on cold surfaces, and insufficient ventilation. Addressing these moisture sources is key to preventing mold growth.

**What is the best household cleaner for removing mold from walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on most surfaces, including painted walls, and is a natural, non-toxic option. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can also be used. Borax mixed with hot water is another excellent non-toxic alternative.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and keep indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier if necessary. Regularly clean and dry areas prone to dampness.

**Is it safe to clean mold on walls myself?**

For small areas (less than 10 square feet) of surface mold, it is generally safe to clean yourself with proper precautions, including wearing an N95 mask, gloves, and eye protection. For larger infestations, extensive black mold, or if you have allergies/respiratory issues, it's safer to hire a professional.
