To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear. Then, clean the affected area with a solution of bleach and water or vinegar, scrubbing gently. Rinse and dry thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Bucket
- Clean cloths or towels
- Household bleach (3-5% sodium hypochlorite)
- White vinegar
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional, for protection)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Lay down plastic sheeting or old towels to protect flooring and furniture. Crucially, don protective gear: safety goggles to shield your eyes, rubber gloves to protect your hands, and an N95 mask to prevent inhaling mold spores.

> 💡 **Tip:** If possible, turn off any HVAC systems to prevent mold spores from spreading through your home.

### Step 2: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, you have two primary options. A bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) is highly effective at killing mold. Alternatively, white vinegar (undiluted) is a natural, less harsh option that also kills many mold species and is often preferred for painted surfaces to avoid discoloration.

### Step 3: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Transfer your chosen solution into a spray bottle. Generously spray the moldy area, ensuring the entire affected surface is saturated. If using bleach, let the solution sit for 5-10 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold. For vinegar, allow it to sit for at least an hour.

### Step 4: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a durable sponge, scrub the moldy areas with firm, circular motions. You should see the mold beginning to lift and disappear. Be thorough but gentle, especially on painted walls, to avoid damaging the surface beneath. If the mold is stubborn, reapply the solution and scrub again.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. It's vital to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate mold regrowth. Use a dry cloth, open windows, or a fan to speed up the drying process. Ensure the area is bone dry before proceeding.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Addressing the source of moisture is key to preventing mold from returning. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier if humidity levels are consistently high. Regular cleaning and maintaining dry conditions will help keep your walls mold-free.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- For large areas of mold (more than 10 square feet) or if you suspect mold is growing behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Do not mix bleach with products containing ammonia, acids, or other chemicals, as this can produce hazardous gases.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, skin irritation, or other adverse reactions while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. If symptoms persist, consult a medical professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best way to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is often preferred as it's less likely to strip paint or cause discoloration than bleach. Spray the vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for an hour, then scrub with a soft brush or sponge and rinse thoroughly. Always test on an inconspicuous area first.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is more complex and often requires professional help, especially if the mold is extensive. It typically involves opening up the wall to expose the affected areas, removing contaminated insulation or drywall, and then cleaning and treating the studs. Addressing the moisture source is critical to prevent recurrence.

**Is bleach or vinegar better for killing mold on walls?**

Both bleach and vinegar are effective at killing mold on non-porous surfaces. Bleach is a strong disinfectant that kills mold spores, while vinegar is a natural fungicide that can kill about 82% of mold species and is generally safer for painted surfaces. Choose based on the wall material and your preference for harshness.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation service if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is growing behind walls or in HVAC systems, or if you or family members have health issues exacerbated by mold exposure. Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive mold.

**Can I just paint over mold on walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it or stop its growth; the mold will eventually grow through the new paint. It's crucial to completely remove the mold and address the moisture source before applying any paint to ensure a lasting and healthy solution.

**What if the mold is black mold?**

If you suspect the mold is black mold (Stachybotrys chartarum), it's important to approach with extreme caution. While often sensationalized, black mold can produce mycotoxins that may cause health issues. For guidance on handling it, refer to specific advice on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold). For extensive black mold, professional remediation is strongly recommended due to potential health risks.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, control moisture levels by fixing leaks promptly, improving ventilation in humid areas with fans or open windows, and using a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity below 60%. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, especially in bathrooms and kitchens, to inhibit mold growth.
