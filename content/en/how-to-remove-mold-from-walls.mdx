To remove mold from walls, start by identifying and fixing the source of moisture. For small, surface mold, a solution of bleach and water (for non-porous surfaces) or white vinegar (for most surfaces) can be effective. Always wear protective gear and ensure good ventilation during the cleaning process to prevent exposure to mold spores.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Eye protection
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or scrub sponge
- Two buckets
- Clean cloths or old towels
- White vinegar or household bleach
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to blow air outwards. Turn off your HVAC system to prevent mold spores from spreading. Don protective gear including rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and eye protection to safeguard against mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify and Fix the Moisture Source

Mold cannot grow without moisture. Before cleaning, locate and repair any leaks, condensation issues, or other sources of dampness that are contributing to the mold growth. If the moisture source isn't addressed, the mold will likely return, making your cleaning efforts temporary.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like tiles or sealed painted walls, a solution of 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water can be used. For porous surfaces or a more natural option, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is effective. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this creates toxic fumes. For specific guidance on severe or black mold, consider reviewing [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Then, use a stiff-bristled brush or scrub sponge to vigorously scrub the mold from the wall surface, working from top to bottom.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process, ensuring the area is bone dry within 24-48 hours.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Maintain low indoor humidity, ideally between 30-50%, using dehumidifiers in damp areas like basements and bathrooms. Ensure proper ventilation, especially in moisture-prone rooms such as kitchens and bathrooms. Promptly repair any new leaks and clean up spills immediately to prevent new mold colonies from forming.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ensure the room is extremely well-ventilated while cleaning, opening windows and using fans to circulate air.
- Never paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the affected area first, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the paint.
- Consider wearing old clothes that you can wash immediately or dispose of after cleaning to avoid spreading mold spores throughout your home.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous and potentially lethal toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ For areas larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet), or if you suspect mold growth behind walls, it is highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ If you experience symptoms like persistent coughing, wheezing, or skin irritation while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air.
- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on porous materials like drywall or wood unless it's a very diluted solution and you've tested it carefully, as it can damage the material and doesn't effectively penetrate to kill mold roots.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first identify and fix the moisture source, then clean the affected area with a solution of white vinegar or diluted bleach. Scrub thoroughly, rinse, and dry the wall completely to prevent recurrence. Always wear appropriate protective gear during the process.

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of undiluted white vinegar sprayed onto the mold and allowed to sit before scrubbing with a soft brush or sponge is effective. Rinse lightly with a damp cloth and dry quickly to prevent new moisture issues.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

There's no single 'permanent' killer; complete removal and prevention of moisture are key. White vinegar effectively kills mold spores and roots on many surfaces. Bleach also kills mold on non-porous surfaces. The most important step is fixing the underlying moisture problem that allowed the mold to grow.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls yourself?**

Removing small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) is generally safe for DIY, provided you wear proper personal protective equipment (PPE) like gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection. For larger or hidden infestations, or if you have respiratory issues, it's safer to hire professionals.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a more complex task. This often warrants professional mold remediation, as it can involve structural damage, hidden widespread growth, and specialized containment procedures.

**Can mold grow back after cleaning?**

Yes, mold can grow back if the underlying moisture problem is not fully resolved. After cleaning, it's crucial to eliminate all sources of dampness, maintain low indoor humidity, and ensure good ventilation to prevent its return and maintain a healthy environment.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the moldy area is larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold behind walls, or if you have health concerns related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment for safe and thorough remediation.
