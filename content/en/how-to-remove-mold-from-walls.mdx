Removing mold from walls involves identifying the source, preparing the area safely, and using appropriate cleaning solutions like vinegar or bleach. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation to prevent health issues and recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old rags
- Bucket
- White vinegar (undiluted) OR household bleach (diluted)
- Warm water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Drop cloth or old towels

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area Safely

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Lay down a drop cloth or old towels to protect the floor. Wear rubber gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or coming into contact with cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there is moisture. Before cleaning, locate and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. If the moisture source isn't resolved, the mold will likely return quickly after cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, white vinegar is an effective and less harsh option. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. Alternatively, for more stubborn mold or non-porous surfaces, you can use a solution of 1 cup household bleach mixed with 1 gallon of water. Never mix bleach with other cleaners like ammonia, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution to the Mold

Generously spray the chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. For vinegar, let it sit for at least an hour to penetrate and kill the mold. If using a bleach solution, let it sit for about 10-15 minutes. This dwell time is crucial for the solution to effectively kill the mold spores.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, thoroughly scrub the mold from the wall. Apply firm pressure to remove all visible traces. Once scrubbed, wipe down the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining mold spores or cleaning solution residue.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After cleaning, it's essential to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the area is aired out and dry. Avoid leaving any moisture behind, as this creates an ideal environment for mold regrowth.

### Step 7: Monitor and Take Preventive Measures

Regularly inspect the area for any signs of mold recurrence. To prevent future growth, maintain good ventilation, control indoor humidity levels (ideally below 50%), and promptly address any leaks or water damage. Consider applying mold-resistant primer and paint if the area is prone to dampness.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint or finish.
- For particularly stubborn mold, a second application and scrubbing might be necessary.
- Never paint over mold; it will not kill the mold and it will likely grow through the new paint.
- Ensure proper ventilation during and after cleaning to disperse fumes and aid drying.
- Consider using mold-inhibiting paint in areas prone to dampness, like bathrooms.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous and toxic gases.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator mask, to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's generally recommended to consult with a professional mold remediation specialist. For information on black mold, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ If you experience health issues like respiratory problems, dizziness, or headaches while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls requires a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. Start with white vinegar or a mild detergent solution, testing it on an inconspicuous spot first. Avoid harsh scrubbing or abrasive cleaners that could strip the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a more complex and potentially hazardous task, often indicating a significant moisture problem. This usually requires opening up the wall to properly clean, dry, and repair the affected area, and it's often best handled by a professional mold remediation service.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While cleaning solutions like vinegar or bleach kill existing mold, the only way to permanently prevent mold on walls is to eliminate its moisture source. This includes fixing leaks, reducing humidity, and ensuring proper ventilation.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you wear appropriate protective gear, ensure good ventilation, and follow safety guidelines. For larger areas, extensive growth, or if you have health concerns, professional help is recommended.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's growing behind walls or in HVAC systems, if you suspect it's a hazardous type like black mold, or if anyone in your household has mold-related health issues.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

Yes, diluted bleach (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be effective for killing mold on non-porous surfaces like painted walls. However, vinegar is often preferred for porous surfaces and as a less toxic alternative, and it is generally more effective at killing mold roots.

**How do I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, consistently control moisture levels in your home by fixing leaks promptly, using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, running a dehumidifier in damp areas, and ensuring adequate ventilation, especially in humid climates.
