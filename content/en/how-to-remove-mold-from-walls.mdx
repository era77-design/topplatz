To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold type and ensuring proper ventilation. For surface mold, use a solution of white vinegar or a mild detergent and water, scrubbing gently. Always address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- N95 respirator mask
- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- Stiff scrub brush or coarse sponge
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Mild detergent
- Clean cloths
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on protective gear: gloves, goggles, and a N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling mold spores. Clear the area around the moldy spot of furniture or cover it with plastic sheeting to prevent spore spread.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the area if possible by closing doors to adjacent rooms and covering air vents.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Its Source

Before cleaning, try to determine what type of mold you're dealing with and, more importantly, the source of moisture causing it. Common household molds appear fuzzy, slimy, or powdery and can be black, green, white, or orange. Addressing the underlying moisture problem (e.g., a leaky pipe or high humidity) is crucial to prevent recurrence. For more aggressive mold types, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, a simple white vinegar solution is effective. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray directly onto the mold. For more stubborn mold or painted walls, a solution of 1/4 cup bleach per gallon of water (never mix with ammonia!) can be used, but test in an inconspicuous area first. A mild detergent mixed with warm water also works for light mold.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Liberally apply your chosen cleaning solution to the moldy area, allowing it to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff brush or a coarse sponge, scrub the affected wall surface vigorously to remove the mold. Be thorough, but avoid damaging the wall material.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining mold spores and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use fans, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the area is arid.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the mold is removed and the wall is dry, take steps to prevent its return. Ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens. Fix any leaks promptly, use a dehumidifier in damp areas, and consider mold-resistant paint for high-humidity rooms. Controlling indoor humidity levels, ideally below 60%, is key.

### Step 7: When to Call a Professional

If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet), if it returns quickly, or if you suspect it's deeply embedded within the wall structure, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service. They have specialized equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive or hidden mold without spreading spores.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always ensure good ventilation when dealing with mold to disperse spores and fumes.
- Test any cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area of the wall first to check for discoloration or damage.
- Consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas of your home to maintain humidity levels below 60%.
- Never paint over mold without cleaning it first; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, goggles, and an N95 respirator mask.
- ⚠️ If you encounter extensive mold (larger than 10 square feet) or suspect hidden mold, contact a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Be cautious with bleach on colored or delicate surfaces, as it can cause discoloration.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you identify mold on walls?**

Mold on walls often appears as fuzzy, slimy, or powdery patches in various colors like black, green, white, or orange. It can also have a distinct musty odor. Look for discoloration or texture changes on the wall surface, especially in damp areas.

**What's the best way to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, a solution of undiluted white vinegar is often effective and less harsh than bleach. Spray it on, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly.

**Can I remove mold from inside walls myself?**

Removing mold from inside walls is generally not recommended for DIY. This often indicates a significant moisture problem and can involve opening up walls, which requires expertise to prevent spore spread. If you suspect mold inside walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediator.

**What cleaning solutions are safe for mold removal?**

Safe and effective cleaning solutions include undiluted white vinegar, a mild detergent mixed with warm water, or a solution of 1/4 cup bleach per gallon of water. Always test any solution in an inconspicuous area first and wear protective gear.

**How can I prevent mold from growing back on my walls?**

To prevent mold recurrence, address the source of moisture immediately, ensure good ventilation, use a dehumidifier in damp areas to keep humidity below 60%, and repair any leaks promptly. Regularly clean and dry surfaces in high-humidity rooms.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's black mold, if it returns quickly after cleaning, or if you suspect it's hidden inside walls or HVAC systems. Professionals have the tools and expertise for safe and thorough removal.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

Removing small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) is generally safe for DIY with proper precautions like wearing protective gear (gloves, mask, goggles) and ensuring good ventilation. However, for large areas, recurrent mold, or suspected hidden mold, professional help is recommended.
