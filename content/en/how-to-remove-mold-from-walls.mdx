To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. Then, clean the affected area with a diluted bleach or vinegar solution, scrub gently, and allow it to dry completely. Addressing the moisture source is crucial for preventing recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or sponges
- White vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan (for drying)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Open all windows and use fans to create cross-ventilation in the room, ensuring good airflow to disperse fumes and spores.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like tile or sealed concrete, mix 1 cup of bleach with 1 gallon of water in a bucket. For painted walls or a more natural approach, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. Avoid mixing bleach with other cleaners, especially ammonia, as it can create toxic fumes.

### Step 3: Apply the Solution to the Mold

Spray or dab your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. Ensure the entire affected surface is saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes; this gives it time to penetrate and kill the mold spores.

### Step 4: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled brush or a scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work from the outside edges of the moldy patch inward to prevent spreading spores to unaffected areas. Continue scrubbing until all visible mold is gone.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Wipe the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining cleaning solution and mold residue. Immediately follow with a dry cloth to absorb excess moisture. Use a fan or open windows to ensure the wall dries completely and quickly, as lingering moisture can encourage mold regrowth.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

After cleaning, identify and fix the source of moisture that led to the mold problem, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Improve ventilation in the area and consider using a dehumidifier to maintain humidity levels below 60%, making it harder for mold to thrive.

### Step 7: Dispose of Materials Safely

Once the cleaning is complete, seal all used cloths, sponges, and protective gear in a sturdy plastic bag. Dispose of the sealed bag in an outdoor trash can to prevent any remaining mold spores from contaminating your indoor environment.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia, as it can produce dangerous toxic gases.
- For persistent musty odors after cleaning, consider placing a bowl of activated charcoal or baking soda in the room to absorb lingering smells.
- If the mold returns quickly, it indicates a significant moisture problem that needs immediate attention from a professional.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have severe allergies, asthma, or a compromised immune system; hire a professional.
- ⚠️ Never use cleaning solutions near electrical outlets or switches without first turning off power to that circuit.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3 ft by 3 ft), or if you suspect it's black mold or growing behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls?**

Start by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation. Clean the mold with a solution of white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (for non-porous surfaces), scrub gently, and then rinse and dry the area thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, use undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle, as bleach can discolor paint. Spray the vinegar, let it sit for an hour, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a damp cloth and dry completely to prevent paint damage.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Mold growing inside walls often indicates a significant moisture problem and usually requires professional remediation. This typically involves opening up the wall to access and remove the moldy materials, as well as fixing the underlying water source. Self-remediation is not recommended for hidden mold.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While cleaning solutions kill existing mold, preventing its return permanently requires addressing the root cause of moisture. Fix leaks, reduce humidity, improve ventilation, and ensure surfaces dry quickly to prevent recurrence. For severe cases, consider consulting [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is growing behind walls, if the mold reappears quickly after cleaning, or if anyone in your household has severe allergies or respiratory issues that are exacerbated by the mold.

**Is it safe to clean mold myself?**

For small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), it is generally safe to clean yourself with proper protective gear and ventilation. However, for larger areas, suspected hidden mold, or if you have health concerns, it's safer to consult a professional.

**What's the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

White vinegar is a highly effective and natural option for most surface mold, especially on painted walls, as it kills many mold species without harsh chemicals. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can also be effective.
