Removing mold from walls is crucial for maintaining a healthy home environment and preserving structural integrity. Small areas of surface mold can often be tackled with common household cleaners and proper safety precautions. However, extensive mold or mold suspected to be hidden behind walls may require professional intervention.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Borax (optional)
- Dish soap
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting or old towels

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Lay down plastic sheeting or old towels to protect flooring and furniture from spores and cleaning solutions. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 mask to protect yourself from inhaling mold spores and coming into direct contact with the mold or cleaning agents.

### Step 2: 2. Isolate the Moldy Area

If possible, close off the affected room from the rest of your home to prevent mold spores from spreading. Turn off any HVAC systems or ceiling fans that could circulate air. This containment helps to minimize the risk of cross-contamination and keeps the spores localized to the area you are treating.

### Step 3: 3. Mix Your Cleaning Solution

For general mold, a simple solution of white vinegar and water works effectively. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. For tougher mold, you can create a paste with one part borax and two parts water, or use a mild dish soap solution for painted surfaces.

### Step 4: 4. Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray the vinegar solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes to allow the solution to penetrate and kill the mold. Then, use a stiff brush or scrub sponge to gently scrub away the mold from the wall surface. Avoid scrubbing too aggressively on painted walls to prevent damage.

### Step 5: 5. Rinse and Dry the Area Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean microfiber cloth dampened with plain water to wipe away any residual cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's crucial to dry the area completely to prevent mold from returning. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is bone dry within 24-48 hours.

### Step 6: 6. Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully gather all used cloths, sponges, plastic sheeting, and any other contaminated materials. Place them immediately into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of the bag outdoors in a trash bin to prevent mold spores from re-entering your home environment.

### Step 7: 7. Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the source of moisture that led to the mold growth. This might involve fixing leaks, improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, or using a dehumidifier. Maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60% is key to preventing mold from recurring on your walls.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never use bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it can be ineffective and potentially cause further damage.
- Ensure adequate ventilation in your home, especially in areas prone to moisture, to discourage mold growth.
- Act quickly when you spot mold; the sooner you address it, the easier it is to remove and prevent its spread.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning solutions, especially those containing ammonia with bleach, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For more information on dealing with severe cases, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consider professional help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a mild solution like white vinegar and water (50/50 mix) or a gentle dish soap with water. Spray or wipe the solution onto the mold, let it sit briefly, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly to avoid damaging the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. If you suspect mold behind drywall, it usually means there's a significant moisture problem that needs to be identified and fixed. Cutting into walls without proper containment can release large amounts of spores, posing health risks and spreading the mold.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

For most surface mold on walls, a solution of white vinegar and water (50/50) is highly effective and safe. Borax mixed with water is another good option for tougher mold. For non-porous surfaces like tiles, a diluted bleach solution can also be used, but avoid it on painted or porous walls.

**When should I call a professional for wall mold?**

You should call a professional for wall mold if the affected area is larger than 10 square feet, if the mold returns quickly after cleaning, if you suspect mold behind walls or in your HVAC system, or if anyone in your household has severe allergies or respiratory conditions that could be aggravated by mold exposure.

**Can mold on walls make you sick?**

Yes, mold on walls can potentially make you sick, especially if you're sensitive or allergic to mold. Symptoms can include nasal stuffiness, throat irritation, coughing, wheezing, eye irritation, or skin irritation. People with asthma or weakened immune systems may experience more severe reactions.

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls?**

Preventing mold on walls involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly clean and dry damp areas, and consider using mold-resistant paint in high-humidity rooms.
