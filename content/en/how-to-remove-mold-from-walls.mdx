Removing mold from walls is a manageable task for small, superficial areas, but it requires caution to prevent spore spread and protect your health. Always wear protective gear and ensure good ventilation. Addressing the moisture source is key to preventing its return.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar (undiluted) OR Borax (1 cup per gallon of water) OR Bleach (1 cup per gallon of water)
- Stiff scrub brush or sponge
- Old rags or disposable towels
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Put on your rubber gloves, N95 respirator mask, and safety goggles to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Avoid touching your face during the process.

### Step 2: Isolate and Prepare the Area

To prevent mold spores from spreading, close off the affected room from the rest of the house if possible. Remove any furniture or items from the immediate vicinity of the mold. Lay down old towels or plastic sheeting on the floor to catch any drips or falling mold particles, which can be disposed of later.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, white vinegar is an effective and natural option; pour it undiluted into a spray bottle. Alternatively, you can mix 1 cup of borax with a gallon of hot water, or 1 cup of bleach with a gallon of water (never mix bleach with other cleaners like ammonia or vinegar). For painted walls, vinegar or borax might be gentler than bleach, which can sometimes fade paint.

### Step 4: Apply Solution and Scrub Away Mold

Liberally spray or apply your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff scrub brush or sponge, firmly scrub the mold from the wall surface. For stubborn areas, reapply the solution and scrub again.

### Step 5: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp rag to wipe away any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to remove all moisture from the wall to prevent mold regrowth. Use dry towels to blot the area, then use a fan or dehumidifier to dry the wall completely. Ensure the area is bone dry before proceeding to any further steps like painting.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials Safely

Carefully gather all used rags, towels, plastic sheeting, and any other contaminated materials. Place them in a sealed plastic bag and immediately dispose of them in an outdoor trash can. This prevents any lingering mold spores from re-contaminating your indoor environment.

### Step 7: Address the Moisture Source to Prevent Recurrence

Identifying and fixing the source of moisture is the most critical step to prevent mold from returning. This could involve repairing leaky pipes, improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms, or using a dehumidifier in humid environments. Without addressing the moisture, mold will likely reappear.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For persistent odors after cleaning, use a dehumidifier or activated charcoal packets to absorb lingering moisture and smells.
- Never paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and drying the area first; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint.
- Consider using mold-resistant primer and paint in areas prone to dampness to provide an extra layer of protection.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For more information on identifying and dealing with black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness during mold removal, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.
- ⚠️ Do not use a dry brush or vacuum cleaner without a HEPA filter, as this can release mold spores into the air, spreading the problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows the same general steps: wear protective gear, ventilate the area, and use a gentle cleaning solution like white vinegar or borax. Test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first, as bleach can sometimes discolor paint. Scrub gently to avoid damaging the paint finish, then rinse and dry thoroughly.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Mold growing inside walls indicates a significant moisture problem, often hidden. This situation typically requires professional assessment and remediation, as it involves opening up the wall, addressing the moisture source, and ensuring proper drying. Attempting to clean mold inside walls without proper equipment and expertise can spread spores and worsen the problem.

**What kills mold on walls effectively?**

White vinegar (undiluted) is a natural and effective mold killer. Bleach (diluted 1 cup per gallon of water) is also effective, especially on non-porous surfaces. Borax (1 cup per gallon of hot water) is another good option that also inhibits future growth. Always ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear when using any of these solutions.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls myself?**

For small patches of superficial mold (less than 10 square feet) on non-porous surfaces, it is generally safe to remove it yourself with proper precautions. Always wear an N95 mask, gloves, and eye protection, and ensure the area is well-ventilated to prevent inhaling spores. If the mold is extensive, recurrent, or you have health concerns, consult a professional.

**How do I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

The key to preventing mold recurrence is controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, use exhaust fans, and consider a dehumidifier in humid climates. Ensure surfaces dry quickly after getting wet, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%.

**Can I just paint over mold on a wall?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, eventually resurfacing and causing more extensive damage. Always thoroughly clean, treat, and completely dry the moldy area before applying any primer or paint.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation specialist if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's recurrent, if you suspect it's black mold, or if you or family members experience health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely contain and remove large infestations and address hidden moisture sources.
