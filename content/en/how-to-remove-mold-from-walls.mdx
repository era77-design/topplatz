To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear. Then, use a solution of white vinegar or bleach diluted with water to scrub the affected area, allowing it to dry completely to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old towels
- White vinegar
- Water
- Optional: Bleach (for non-porous surfaces)
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct air outside. Put on your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling mold spores. Wear old clothes that you can wash immediately after or dispose of, as mold spores can cling to fabric.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Choose Your Solution

Examine the mold growth. For small areas (less than 10 square feet) of common surface mold, you can likely handle it yourself. If you suspect black mold or a larger infestation, consider consulting a professional. For cleaning, white vinegar is effective for many types of mold on various surfaces. For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but always test in an inconspicuous area first.

### Step 3: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Fill a spray bottle with your chosen solution. If using white vinegar, use it undiluted. If using bleach, ensure it's properly diluted. Lightly mist the moldy area, making sure the mold is thoroughly dampened but not dripping. This helps to prevent spores from becoming airborne during scrubbing. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate and kill the mold.

### Step 4: Scrub the Mold Away

Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. Avoid scrubbing too vigorously, as this can damage the wall or spread mold spores further. Dispose of any heavily soiled cloths or sponges in a sealed bag immediately after use.

### Step 5: Wipe and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the area with clean, damp microfiber cloths to remove any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate recurrence of mold. Use clean, dry towels, and consider using a fan or dehumidifier to accelerate the drying process. Ensure the area is bone dry before moving on.

### Step 6: Dispose of Materials and Clean Up

Place all used cloths, sponges, and protective gear (except reusable items like goggles and respirator, which should be cleaned) into a sealed plastic bag and dispose of it in an outdoor trash can. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water. Clean any tools used with hot, soapy water and allow them to dry completely.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the underlying cause of the mold, such as leaks or high humidity. Repair any plumbing issues and ensure proper ventilation in humid areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier can significantly reduce the risk of mold returning. Regular cleaning and inspection can also help catch new growth early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold odors, a spray of baking soda and water solution after cleaning can help neutralize smells.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and avoiding overcrowding furniture against walls.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer to painted walls in areas prone to dampness after cleaning and drying.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not attempt to remove mold if you have severe allergies or respiratory conditions; consult a professional.
- ⚠️ Never scrub dry mold, as this releases spores into the air, potentially spreading the problem and causing respiratory issues.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if it returns quickly after cleaning, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when using bleach solutions, as they can discolor certain painted surfaces and fabrics.
- ⚠️ If you suspect mold is growing behind drywall or wallpaper, it's often best to remove and replace the affected materials, as surface cleaning won't address the hidden issue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. Use a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle, or a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) for non-porous paints. Spray the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a stiff brush or sponge. Wipe clean and dry the area thoroughly with a fan.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the moldy materials. This is often a job for professionals, as it involves potential demolition, remediation, and reconstruction. If you suspect mold behind a wall (e.g., musty smell, visible growth on the other side), it's crucial to address the moisture source and consider professional assessment to prevent further spread and health risks.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While cleaning solutions like white vinegar or bleach can kill existing mold, the only way to prevent its permanent return is to eliminate the moisture source that allows it to grow. Repair leaks, reduce indoor humidity, improve ventilation, and ensure proper drainage around your home. Addressing these underlying issues is key to permanent mold control.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

It can be safe to remove small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you wear appropriate protective gear (gloves, goggles, N95 mask) and ensure good ventilation. However, if the mold covers a large area, is black mold, or if you have allergies or respiratory sensitivities, it's safer to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For information on black mold, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**Can mold on walls make you sick?**

Yes, mold on walls can potentially make you sick, especially if you are sensitive to mold or have respiratory conditions like asthma. Exposure to mold spores can cause allergic reactions, respiratory problems, skin irritation, and other health issues. It's important to remove mold promptly and safely to protect your health.

**What is the best homemade mold remover for walls?**

White vinegar is highly regarded as one of the best homemade mold removers for walls. It's effective at killing many types of mold and is generally safer than bleach for various surfaces. For a boost, you can add a tablespoon of baking soda to the vinegar, which also helps absorb odors and provides gentle abrasive action.

**How long does it take for mold to die after cleaning?**

After applying a mold-killing solution like vinegar or bleach, the mold typically starts to die within minutes to a few hours. However, it's crucial to allow the area to remain wet with the solution for 10-15 minutes to ensure penetration. The most important step after cleaning is to thoroughly dry the area to prevent new mold growth, which can take several hours depending on humidity and ventilation.
