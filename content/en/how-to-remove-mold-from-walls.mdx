To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. For small surface mold, a solution of white vinegar or a diluted bleach mixture can effectively kill and remove the growth. Always dry the area thoroughly afterward to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Protective gloves
- Eye protection
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Old towels or rags
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Dish soap (optional)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Before you begin, open windows and use a fan to ensure good ventilation in the room. Lay down plastic sheeting or old towels to protect the floor from cleaning solutions and mold spores. Most importantly, don protective gear including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling spores or contact with cleaning agents.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Choose Your Cleaner

Identify the extent of the mold. For small, surface-level mold (less than 10 square feet), DIY removal is generally safe. For larger infestations, or if you suspect black mold, it's safer to consult professionals. For cleaning, white vinegar is effective and non-toxic. For non-porous surfaces like tile or sealed painted walls, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can be used, but never mix bleach with vinegar or other cleaners.

### Step 3: Apply Your Chosen Cleaning Solution

If using vinegar, pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle and thoroughly saturate the moldy area. Let it sit for at least an hour to allow the vinegar to penetrate and kill the mold. If using a bleach solution, apply it with a spray bottle or sponge, ensuring good coverage. Let the bleach solution sit for 10-15 minutes.

### Step 4: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled brush or an abrasive sponge, scrub the mold from the wall. Apply firm but gentle pressure to avoid damaging the wall surface. For painted walls, be extra careful not to strip the paint. Wipe away the loosened mold and cleaning solution with clean, damp old towels or rags.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Area Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, water-dampened cloth to remove any remaining residue. It is crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use old towels to absorb as much moisture as possible, then use a fan or dehumidifier to air dry the area for several hours, or even a full day.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Carefully place all used towels, sponges, and plastic sheeting into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of this bag outside the home immediately to prevent the spread of mold spores. Clean any tools used with a disinfectant and allow them to dry completely before storing.

### Step 7: Implement Prevention Strategies

Address the source of the moisture that led to the mold growth. This might involve fixing leaks, improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, or using a dehumidifier. Maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60% is key to preventing future mold outbreaks on your walls.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For stubborn stains, a paste made from baking soda and water can be gently scrubbed onto the area after the initial cleaning.
- Consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to dampness after the mold has been completely removed and the moisture source addressed.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ NEVER mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or any other cleaning product, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to call a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Wear an N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling mold spores, which can cause respiratory issues.
- ⚠️ If mold returns quickly after cleaning, it indicates an ongoing moisture problem that needs to be professionally addressed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid stripping the paint. A solution of undiluted white vinegar sprayed directly onto the mold and allowed to sit for an hour is effective. Gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, then wipe clean and dry thoroughly to prevent damage to the painted surface.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a complex and potentially hazardous task that generally requires professional intervention. It indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be identified and repaired, and the mold itself can be extensive. DIY attempts can spread spores and lead to health issues or structural damage.

**What is the best household cleaner for mold on walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on walls, especially on porous surfaces, as it penetrates deeper than bleach. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can also be used, but vinegar is often preferred for safety and versatility.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you wear proper protective gear like gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask. However, for larger infestations, mold caused by contaminated water, or suspected [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold), it's advisable to call professionals.

**How do I stop mold from growing back on walls?**

To prevent mold regrowth, the key is to eliminate moisture. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and use a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Ensuring proper air circulation and drying wet surfaces quickly are also crucial steps.

**Can I paint over mold on walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow and spread underneath the paint, eventually resurfacing and potentially causing further damage to your walls and air quality. Always remove the mold completely and address the moisture source before repainting.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold typically grows on walls in the presence of moisture, warmth, and a food source (like drywall or paint). Common causes include high humidity, water leaks (from pipes, roofs, or windows), condensation, poor ventilation, and flooding. Addressing these moisture sources is essential for prevention.
