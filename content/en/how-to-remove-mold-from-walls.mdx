To remove mold from walls, it's crucial to prioritize safety and ensure proper ventilation. For small, surface mold, a simple cleaning solution like white vinegar or a mild detergent can be highly effective. Always wear protective gear and thoroughly dry the area afterward to prevent regrowth.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask or respirator
- Safety glasses
- Old clothes or apron
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Mild dish soap
- Baking soda (optional)
- Chlorine bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or old toothbrush
- Sponges or microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- Clean towels
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting or old newspaper

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Lay down plastic sheeting or old newspapers to protect the floor and nearby furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Put on your rubber gloves, N95 mask or respirator, and safety glasses to protect yourself from mold spores and harsh chemicals.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Choose Your Cleaner

Identify the extent and type of mold. For small patches of surface mold, white vinegar or mild dish soap is usually sufficient. If you encounter extensive mold growth or suspect it might be black mold, consult our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for specific precautions. For non-porous surfaces and stubborn mold, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but avoid it on painted walls unless you test it first.

### Step 3: Apply the Cleaning Solution

For vinegar, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray it directly onto the moldy area, ensuring full saturation. For dish soap, mix a few drops with water in a spray bottle. If using a bleach solution, apply it carefully with a sponge. Let the solution sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold.

### Step 4: Scrub the Mold Away

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush, an old toothbrush, or a sponge, scrub the mold from the wall surface. Apply firm but gentle pressure, working in small sections until the mold stains are gone. Be careful not to damage the wall material, especially if it's painted or delicate.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

Wipe down the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth or sponge to remove any residual cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use clean, dry towels, and consider running a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours until the wall is bone dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials and Prevent Recurrence

Place all used sponges, cloths, and protective gear that cannot be cleaned into a sealed plastic bag and dispose of them in the trash. Address the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leaky pipe, high humidity, or poor ventilation. Maintaining good airflow and controlling humidity are key to preventing future mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold odors after cleaning, sprinkle baking soda on the affected area, let it sit for a few hours, then vacuum or wipe it away.
- Consider using mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.
- Improve air circulation by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and avoiding overcrowding furniture against walls.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), or if you suspect it's hidden behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or a compromised immune system without consulting a doctor first.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) to avoid inhaling mold spores or coming into direct contact with cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure the area is extremely well-ventilated when using bleach, as its fumes can be irritating and harmful.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle solution to avoid damaging the paint. A mixture of white vinegar and water (50/50) or a few drops of mild dish soap in water are good choices. Spray the solution, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a clean, damp cloth and dry thoroughly.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex job that often indicates a significant moisture problem. It's generally recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist to properly identify the source, safely remove the mold, and repair the structural damage.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

For most surface mold on walls, white vinegar is an excellent natural and effective cleaner. For tougher mold on non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) can be used, but always test it first. Mild dish soap and water can also work well for lighter mold growth.

**Can I paint over mold on my walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow and can eventually resurface, causing peeling paint and further damage. Always thoroughly clean and dry the moldy area, and address the moisture source, before applying any primer or paint.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, control humidity levels in your home by using dehumidifiers and ensuring good ventilation, especially in bathrooms and kitchens. Fix any leaks immediately, keep surfaces dry, and consider using mold-resistant paint or primers in high-moisture areas.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it keeps returning after cleaning, or if you suspect it's hidden behind walls or in HVAC systems. Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise to safely and effectively address extensive or hidden mold issues.
