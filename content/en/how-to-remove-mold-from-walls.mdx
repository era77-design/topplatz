Removing mold from walls is crucial for maintaining a healthy home environment. Small areas of surface mold can often be tackled with simple household cleaners and proper safety precautions, preventing further spread and potential health issues.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety goggles
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old towels
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Dish soap (optional)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Situation and Prepare

Before you begin, determine the extent of the mold growth. If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), or if you suspect it's a more serious type like black mold, consider consulting a professional. For smaller areas, put on your rubber gloves, N95 respirator mask, and safety goggles to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Isolate and Ventilate the Area

Open windows and doors to ensure good airflow in the room. If possible, use a fan to direct air out of the room. Close off air vents to prevent mold spores from spreading to other parts of your home. This step is crucial for minimizing exposure during the cleaning process.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, a simple white vinegar solution is highly effective. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray it directly onto the moldy area. For tougher spots, you can make a paste with baking soda and a small amount of water, or add a few drops of dish soap to your vinegar solution for extra cleaning power. Do not mix vinegar with bleach, as this creates dangerous fumes.

### Step 4: Apply and Let it Sit

Spray the chosen cleaning solution generously over the moldy area, ensuring it's completely saturated. Allow the solution to sit for at least 30 minutes to give it time to penetrate and kill the mold spores. For baking soda paste, apply it thickly and let it dry slightly before scrubbing.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Clean

Using your stiff-bristled brush or sponge, gently scrub the mold from the wall. Apply consistent pressure to lift the mold without damaging the wall surface. Once the mold is visibly removed, wipe down the area with a clean, damp microfiber cloth to rinse away any residue. Repeat this step if necessary until the wall is clean.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After cleaning, it's essential to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. Use clean, dry cloths to absorb as much moisture as possible. You can also use a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process. Ensure the area is bone dry before considering any repainting or repairs.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is clean and dry, take steps to prevent recurrence. Address any underlying moisture issues, such as leaks or condensation. Improve ventilation in the room, especially in high-humidity areas like bathrooms or kitchens. Regularly clean and inspect walls for early signs of mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For stubborn mold stains that remain after cleaning, a mild solution of hydrogen peroxide (3%) can be used, but test it first and rinse thoroughly.
- If you're dealing with a large infestation or suspect mold behind the walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix different cleaning chemicals, especially bleach and ammonia or bleach and vinegar, as this can produce toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection to prevent exposure to mold spores and chemicals.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's recommended to consult a professional. For more serious cases, see our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows the same process as unpainted walls. Use a gentle cleaning solution like white vinegar or a mild dish soap and water solution. Be sure to scrub gently to avoid damaging the paint, and always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first.

**Can mold be removed from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls (behind the drywall or plaster) is a more complex issue. This usually indicates a significant moisture problem that requires professional intervention, as it often involves opening up the wall to properly clean, dry, and repair the affected area. Attempting to clean it yourself without addressing the source can lead to recurrence.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold grows on walls primarily due to excess moisture and a food source (like dust or drywall). Common causes include high humidity, water leaks from plumbing or roofs, condensation, and poor ventilation. Addressing these underlying moisture issues is key to preventing mold growth.

**Is it safe to clean mold yourself?**

For small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), it is generally safe to clean yourself using proper protective equipment and ventilation. However, if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or suspect a large infestation or toxic mold, it's safer to hire a professional.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), if you suspect the presence of toxic mold like black mold, or if the mold is due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage). Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively remediate larger or more dangerous infestations. For specific guidance on severe cases, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, control humidity levels in your home (ideally below 60%) using dehumidifiers or air conditioners. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and regularly clean and inspect your walls for early signs of moisture or mold.

**What type of mold commonly grows on walls?**

Various types of mold can grow on walls, often appearing as black, green, or white fuzzy patches. Common household molds include Cladosporium, Penicillium, and Aspergillus. While many are not inherently toxic, prolonged exposure to any mold can trigger allergic reactions or respiratory issues in sensitive individuals.
