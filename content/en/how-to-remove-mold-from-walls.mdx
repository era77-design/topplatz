Removing mold from walls is crucial for maintaining a healthy home environment and preventing further damage. For minor surface mold, you can often tackle the problem yourself with a few simple tools and cleaning solutions. Always ensure proper ventilation and protective gear before you begin.

## What You Need

- Protective gear (gloves, N95 mask, eye protection)
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%) OR household bleach
- Bucket
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- Fan (for ventilation)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using a fan to direct air outdoors. Put on protective gear, including rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and eye protection, to prevent contact with mold spores and cleaning solutions. Clear the area around the moldy wall to make it accessible.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold Extent and Type

Carefully examine the mold to determine its extent. If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet) or appears to be black mold, it's often best to consult a professional. For smaller, surface-level mold, you can proceed with DIY removal. For more information on dealing with specific types, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For porous surfaces like drywall, white vinegar is often effective as it penetrates deeper to kill mold. For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 part bleach to 10 parts water, or a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution, can be used. Avoid mixing bleach with other cleaners like ammonia, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Fill a spray bottle with your chosen solution. Lightly mist the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly dampened but not oversaturated, especially if dealing with drywall. Allow the solution to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the spores.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sponge, gently scrub the mold from the wall surface. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. Once the mold is loosened, wipe it away with clean cloths or paper towels. Dispose of these immediately in a sealed bag to prevent spreading spores.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a damp cloth to remove any residue from the cleaning solution. It is crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent mold from returning. Use a clean, dry cloth, and continue to run a fan in the room for several hours until the wall is entirely dry.

### Step 7: Address the Root Cause of Mold

Mold often indicates a moisture problem. Identify and fix any leaks, reduce humidity with a dehumidifier, or improve ventilation in the area. Without addressing the underlying moisture issue, mold is likely to return, requiring repeated cleaning efforts.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For stubborn mold stains, a paste made from baking soda and water can be gently applied, left for a few minutes, then scrubbed away.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and ensuring furniture isn't pushed too tightly against walls.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's part of a larger, hidden problem, consider hiring a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or other health problems while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek medical advice, then consider professional help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, opt for a gentle approach. A solution of white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide is often safer for paint than bleach. Test the solution on an inconspicuous area first, then gently scrub the mold with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a more complex and potentially hazardous task that usually requires professional intervention. If you suspect mold behind your walls due to persistent musty odors or visible damage, it indicates a significant moisture issue that needs to be properly assessed and remediated by experts to prevent structural damage and health risks.

**What is the best cleaning solution for mold on walls?**

The best cleaning solution depends on the wall surface. For porous surfaces like drywall, white vinegar is often preferred as it can penetrate and kill mold effectively. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) or 3% hydrogen peroxide can be highly effective.

**Can I use bleach to clean mold from walls?**

Yes, bleach can be used to clean mold from non-porous wall surfaces. Mix one cup of bleach with one gallon of water and apply it to the moldy area. Ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. However, bleach may not be as effective on porous materials like drywall as it doesn't always penetrate deep enough to kill the mold roots.

**How do I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks immediately, use a dehumidifier in humid areas, and ensure proper ventilation, especially in bathrooms and kitchens. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, and consider using mold-resistant paints in prone areas.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect mold is hidden behind walls, or if you or family members experience health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively address extensive or hidden mold problems.
