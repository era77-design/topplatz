Removing mold from walls typically involves identifying the moisture source, preparing the area, and cleaning the affected surface with an appropriate solution. For small, surface-level mold infestations, a DIY approach can be effective. Always prioritize safety and address the underlying cause to prevent future growth.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety glasses
- Old clothes or protective suit
- Bucket
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- White distilled vinegar
- Borax (optional)
- Mild dish soap (optional)
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting or tarp (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Protect yourself by wearing rubber gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask to avoid inhaling mold spores. If possible, turn off your HVAC system to prevent spores from spreading throughout your home. Remove any furniture or items from the affected area, or cover them with plastic sheeting.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives in damp conditions, so finding and fixing the moisture problem is crucial for long-term prevention. This could be a leaky pipe, a humid room, or condensation issues. Repair any leaks immediately and consider using a dehumidifier in humid environments to keep moisture levels low. Addressing the source is key to preventing mold from returning after cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold on walls, white distilled vinegar is an excellent natural option. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. Alternatively, you can mix 1 cup of borax with 1 gallon of hot water, adding a squirt of dish soap for extra cleaning power. For more stubborn mold, or if you suspect black mold, refer to specialized guides on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for appropriate solutions and precautions. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as it can create toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Liberally spray the chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, scrub the mold away from the wall. Work in small sections, applying gentle but firm pressure until the mold is visibly gone.

### Step 5: Rinse and Wipe Down the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residue from the cleaning solution and dead mold spores. Avoid over-saturating the wall with water during this step. If using vinegar, you generally don't need to rinse, as vinegar residue helps inhibit future mold growth.

### Step 6: Dry the Area Thoroughly

This is a critical step to prevent mold recurrence. Use clean, dry towels to blot the wall until it is as dry as possible. Then, use fans, open windows, or a dehumidifier to ensure the wall dries completely within 24-48 hours. Proper drying eliminates the moisture that mold needs to grow back.

### Step 7: Monitor and Prevent Future Growth

Regularly inspect the cleaned area for any signs of mold returning. Maintain good ventilation in your home, especially in bathrooms, kitchens, and basements. Fix any leaks promptly and keep indoor humidity levels between 30-50% using a dehumidifier if necessary. A dry environment is your best defense against mold.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For porous surfaces like drywall, mold can be harder to remove completely and may require professional intervention if deeply embedded.
- Improve air circulation by opening windows and using exhaust fans, especially after showers or cooking.
- Consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to dampness, like bathrooms.
- Regularly clean and dry areas that are frequently exposed to moisture.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this creates dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect black mold, or if the mold returns quickly, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Avoid touching mold directly with bare skin, as it can cause irritation or allergic reactions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a soft brush or sponge using a mild solution like white vinegar or borax and water. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint. Avoid harsh scrubbing that could strip the paint.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically indicates a significant and hidden moisture issue, often requiring professional remediation. This usually involves opening up the wall to find and fix the moisture source, remove damaged materials, and thoroughly clean and dry the cavity. Attempting this yourself without proper equipment and expertise can be risky.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold grows on walls when there's a consistent source of moisture, warmth, and organic material to feed on. Common causes include leaky pipes, high humidity, condensation from poor ventilation, water intrusion from roofs or foundations, and floods. Addressing the moisture source is paramount to preventing recurrence.

**Can I use bleach to remove mold from walls?**

While bleach can kill surface mold on non-porous surfaces, it's often not the most effective solution for porous walls. Bleach primarily removes the color, but the mold's roots can remain, allowing it to regrow. White vinegar is often preferred as it penetrates porous surfaces better and kills more types of mold without harsh chemicals.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation specialist if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect black mold (Stachybotrys chartarum), if you have health concerns related to mold exposure, or if the mold returns quickly after cleaning. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively address large or stubborn infestations.

**How can I prevent mold from returning after cleaning?**

To prevent mold from returning, ensure the wall dries completely after cleaning and address the original moisture source. Maintain good ventilation, keep indoor humidity levels below 50% using a dehumidifier if needed, and promptly repair any leaks or water damage. Regular cleaning of damp areas also helps.
