To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the type and extent of the growth, and address any underlying moisture issues. For small areas, a simple solution of white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide can be effective for cleaning. Always ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear during the cleaning process to protect your health.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- White vinegar
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
- Baking soda (optional, for odor)
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. This helps to disperse mold spores and fumes from cleaning solutions. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect your eyes, hands, and respiratory system from mold spores and cleaning agents.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Its Cause

Assess the size and location of the mold growth. Small, superficial patches (less than 10 square feet) are generally safe for DIY removal. Crucially, try to identify and fix the source of moisture causing the mold, such as a leaky pipe or high humidity, as this is essential for preventing recurrence. Do not proceed with DIY removal if the mold covers a large area or is due to sewage or contaminated water.

### Step 3: Choose and Apply Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, white vinegar is an excellent choice; spray it undiluted directly onto the mold and let it sit for at least an hour. Alternatively, a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution can be sprayed on the mold and left for 10-15 minutes. Both are effective at killing mold without the harshness of bleach, which is generally not recommended for porous surfaces or painted walls as it can damage the finish and isn't always effective on mold roots.

### Step 4: Scrub Away the Mold

After the solution has had sufficient time to work, use a stiff brush or scrub sponge to thoroughly scrub the moldy area. Apply gentle pressure to avoid damaging the wall surface, especially if it's painted. Ensure you remove all visible traces of mold, working from the top down to prevent spores from spreading to already cleaned areas.

### Step 5: Wipe and Dry Thoroughly

Once scrubbed, wipe the area clean with a damp microfiber cloth to remove any remaining mold spores or cleaning solution residue. Then, use a dry cloth to absorb as much moisture as possible. It is critical to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning; use a fan or dehumidifier to accelerate the drying process.

### Step 6: Address Stubborn Stains or Odors (Optional)

If any stains remain after cleaning, a paste made from baking soda and water can be applied, left for a few minutes, then gently scrubbed and wiped away. For lingering musty odors, a bowl of white vinegar or activated charcoal placed in the room can help absorb smells. Ensure the area remains dry to prevent future odor and mold issues.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

The best way to keep mold from returning is to control moisture in your home. Fix any leaks promptly, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier in humid areas. Aim to keep indoor humidity levels below 60% to create an environment less hospitable for mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix different cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet), or if you suspect mold behind walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it does not effectively kill mold at its roots and can even promote regrowth.
- ⚠️ Avoid painting over mold without first removing it, as this will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the new paint.
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a solution of undiluted white vinegar or a 3% hydrogen peroxide spray. Allow the solution to sit for a period (vinegar for an hour, peroxide for 10-15 minutes) before wiping clean with a damp cloth and drying thoroughly. Avoid harsh scrubbing that could damage the paint finish.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often indicates a significant, hidden moisture problem. It's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist for mold found inside walls, as it can be extensive and require specialized equipment for safe removal and repair.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold grows on walls primarily due to excessive moisture and humidity. Common causes include water leaks from pipes or roofs, condensation from poor ventilation, high indoor humidity levels, and past flooding. Identifying and fixing the source of moisture is crucial for preventing mold recurrence.

**Is it dangerous to have mold on walls?**

Yes, mold on walls can be dangerous, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Exposure to mold spores can cause respiratory problems, skin irritation, headaches, and other health issues. Prompt and safe removal is important for maintaining a healthy indoor environment.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), if it's due to sewage or contaminated water, or if you suspect mold is hidden behind walls or in HVAC systems. Professionals have the expertise and equipment to safely contain and remove extensive mold growth. For particularly stubborn or problematic mold, you might also find useful information in our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**Can I prevent mold from returning after cleaning?**

Yes, preventing mold's return is possible by controlling moisture. Ensure good ventilation in damp areas, fix any leaks immediately, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier if necessary. Regular cleaning and inspecting areas prone to moisture can also significantly help.
