To remove mold from walls, begin by ensuring good ventilation and wearing protective gear. Then, clean the affected area using a diluted solution of bleach, vinegar, or hydrogen peroxide. Thoroughly dry the surface to prevent mold from returning.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or protective suit
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Clean cloths or rags
- White distilled vinegar OR 3% hydrogen peroxide OR household bleach
- Water
- Fan (for ventilation)
- Plastic sheeting (optional, for floor/furniture protection)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Lay down plastic sheeting to protect floors and furniture from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Crucially, wear rubber gloves, safety goggles, an N95 respirator mask, and old clothes to protect yourself from mold spores and chemicals.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Its Cause

Examine the mold to determine its extent and type. For small patches (less than 10 square feet), DIY removal is usually safe. If you suspect black mold or find extensive growth, it's best to consult a professional for safe remediation. Address the underlying moisture issue, such as a leak or high humidity, to prevent recurrence.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

You have several effective options for killing mold. For a natural solution, use undiluted white distilled vinegar in a spray bottle. Alternatively, a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution can be sprayed directly onto the mold. For tougher mold, mix one cup of household bleach with one gallon of water in a bucket, ensuring good ventilation when using bleach. Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Spray or gently wipe your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. If using a spray bottle, ensure the area is thoroughly saturated. For bleach solutions, apply with a cloth or sponge. Allow the solution to sit on the mold for at least 10-15 minutes to effectively kill the spores.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, vigorously scrub the mold away from the wall surface. For painted walls, be gentle to avoid damaging the paint. Once the mold appears to be removed, wipe the area thoroughly with clean, damp cloths to remove any remaining residue and spores. Dispose of contaminated cloths and sponges carefully.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean, water-dampened cloth to rinse off any residual cleaning solution. This is especially important if using bleach. Then, use dry cloths to absorb as much moisture as possible from the wall. Crucially, allow the wall to air dry completely, using a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the process and ensure no moisture remains, which could encourage mold regrowth.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

The key to preventing mold is controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly inspect walls for signs of moisture or new mold growth, especially in areas prone to dampness. Maintaining a dry environment is your best defense against mold.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For persistent mold odors after cleaning, an open container of baking soda can help absorb smells in the room.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer to walls in areas prone to dampness after cleaning and before repainting.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ For large areas of mold (exceeding 10 square feet) or if you suspect black mold, it is strongly recommended to contact a professional mold remediation specialist. You can learn more about this in [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Always wear proper personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a soft brush or sponge using a diluted solution of white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide. Avoid harsh scrubbing that could damage the paint, and ensure the wall dries completely afterward.

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way involves first addressing the moisture source, then cleaning with a solution like white distilled vinegar, hydrogen peroxide, or a diluted bleach mixture. Ensure good ventilation, wear protective gear, and thoroughly dry the wall to prevent regrowth.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is complex and often requires professional help. It typically involves opening the wall, remediating the mold, addressing the moisture source, and then repairing the wall. DIY attempts are generally not recommended for hidden mold.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches of mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you wear appropriate protective gear and ensure good ventilation. For larger areas, extensive growth, or suspected black mold, professional remediation is advised.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Prevent mold by controlling humidity levels, fixing leaks promptly, and ensuring adequate ventilation, especially in moisture-prone areas. Using a dehumidifier and regularly cleaning damp surfaces can also significantly reduce the risk of mold recurrence.

**What kills mold on walls instantly?**

While no solution truly 'instantly' kills mold without some dwell time, undiluted white distilled vinegar, 3% hydrogen peroxide, and a diluted bleach solution are all highly effective at killing mold spores on contact after a short application period.
