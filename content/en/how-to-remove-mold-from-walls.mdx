To remove mold from walls, first identify the source of moisture and ensure proper ventilation. Then, prepare a cleaning solution of water and bleach or vinegar, apply it to the affected area, scrub gently, and allow the surface to dry completely to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber or nitrile gloves
- Eye protection (goggles)
- N95 face mask
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- White distilled vinegar
- Household bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Ventilation

Before you begin, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Put on your protective gear, including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask, to prevent contact with mold spores and cleaning solutions. Isolate the affected area by closing doors to other rooms to minimize spore spread.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives on moisture, so finding and fixing the source is crucial to prevent its return. Look for leaks from pipes, roofs, or windows, and address any condensation issues. If the moisture source isn't resolved, the mold will likely grow back even after cleaning.

### Step 3: Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a solution of 1 cup household bleach mixed with 1 gallon of water can be effective. For a natural alternative or for sensitive surfaces, use undiluted white distilled vinegar in a spray bottle. Never mix bleach and ammonia or other cleaning agents, as this can create dangerous fumes.

### Step 4: Apply Solution and Scrub the Mold

Liberally spray the chosen cleaning solution onto the moldy area, ensuring it's thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Then, using your stiff scrub brush or sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall surface.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the treated area, removing any residual cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's vital to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate re-growth. Use a fan, dehumidifier, or open windows to accelerate drying.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials

Place all used cloths, sponges, and brushes in a sealed plastic bag immediately after cleaning. Dispose of the bag in an outdoor trash receptacle to prevent mold spores from spreading within your home. Thoroughly clean any reusable tools you used.

### Step 7: Monitor and Prevent Recurrence

Regularly inspect the cleaned area for any signs of mold re-growth and maintain good ventilation in your home. Consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas like bathrooms or basements to keep humidity levels below 60%. Promptly address any new moisture issues to keep your walls mold-free.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For persistent musty odors after cleaning, consider using an air purifier with a HEPA filter to help remove lingering mold spores.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and avoiding clutter against walls.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this creates toxic gases that can be extremely dangerous.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. For more information on severe cases, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ If you experience respiratory issues, headaches, or dizziness while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consider professional help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, don protective gear, identify and fix the moisture source, then clean the area with a solution of white vinegar or bleach and water. Scrub the mold away, rinse, and ensure the wall dries completely to prevent its return.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls is similar to cleaning other non-porous surfaces. Use a mild solution of white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water), and gently scrub the affected area. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

Killing mold on walls permanently requires more than just cleaning; you must eliminate the moisture source that allows it to grow. Once the moisture problem is fixed, cleaning with vinegar or bleach can kill existing mold, but addressing the root cause is key to long-term prevention.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's toxic black mold, or if you have respiratory issues or allergies that could be aggravated. Professionals have specialized equipment and expertise to safely remove extensive mold growth.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a more complex task. This usually indicates a significant moisture problem, such as a hidden leak, and often warrants professional assessment and remediation to ensure it's safely and completely addressed.

**Can I paint over mold?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, eventually reappearing and potentially causing further structural damage or health issues. Always clean and thoroughly dry the moldy area before applying any paint.

**Is it safe to clean mold yourself?**

Cleaning small areas of mold (less than 10 square feet) is generally safe for most individuals, provided you wear appropriate protective gear like gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask. However, if you have respiratory conditions or allergies, or if the mold is extensive, it's safer to hire a professional.
