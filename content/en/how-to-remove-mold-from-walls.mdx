To remove mold from walls, begin by identifying and fixing the source of moisture, then ensure the area is well-ventilated. For surface mold, a solution of distilled white vinegar or a mild detergent can effectively clean and disinfect the affected area, helping to prevent its return and restoring your wall's appearance.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or coveralls
- Spray bottle
- Distilled white vinegar OR Mild detergent OR Borax
- Warm water
- Soft scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths/rags
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting or old towels

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Don personal protective equipment (PPE): safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Remove any furniture or items from the affected area and cover the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels to catch debris.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives on moisture, so cleaning it without addressing the underlying cause is a temporary fix. Locate and repair any sources of water intrusion, such as leaky pipes, roof leaks, or excessive condensation. Ensuring the area remains dry after cleaning is crucial for preventing mold from returning.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold, distilled white vinegar is an excellent choice; pour it undiluted into a spray bottle. Alternatively, you can mix one cup of borax with a gallon of warm water, or use a solution of mild dish soap and warm water. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it may not kill the mold's roots and can damage the wall material.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold and loosen its grip. Using a soft scrub brush or sponge, gently scrub the mold away. Be careful not to scrub too aggressively, especially on painted walls, to avoid damaging the surface.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe the cleaned area with a clean cloth dampened with plain water to remove any cleaning solution residue and dead mold. It is critical to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process and ensure no moisture is left behind.

### Step 6: Dispose of Contaminated Materials Safely

Carefully gather all used cloths, sponges, plastic sheeting, and any other contaminated materials. Place them immediately into a sealed plastic bag. Dispose of the sealed bag in an outdoor trash receptacle to prevent the spread of mold spores to other parts of your home.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is clean and dry, focus on long-term prevention. Maintain indoor humidity levels below 60% using a dehumidifier, especially in damp areas like basements and bathrooms. Ensure proper ventilation in kitchens and bathrooms, and promptly fix any new leaks or water damage. Consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to moisture.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners or vinegar, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold problems or areas larger than 10 square feet, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including an N95 respirator mask, safety goggles, and rubber gloves, to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.
- ⚠️ Do not scrub dry mold, as this can release a large number of spores into the air, potentially spreading the problem and posing health risks.
- ⚠️ If mold growth covers a large area (more than 10 square feet) or if it returns quickly after cleaning, it indicates a more significant issue that requires professional assessment.
- ⚠️ Never mix household cleaners, especially bleach and ammonia, as they can create highly toxic gases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of distilled white vinegar or mild detergent mixed with water is effective. Spray the solution, let it sit, then gently scrub with a soft sponge, followed by thorough drying to prevent regrowth.

**How do you get rid of black mold safely and effectively?**

Tackling black mold requires careful attention to safety and effective cleaning. For comprehensive guidance on safe and effective removal techniques, refer to our detailed guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While various solutions kill surface mold, preventing its return permanently hinges on eliminating the moisture source. After cleaning, ensure the area stays dry, maintain good ventilation, and fix any leaks or condensation issues to prevent future growth.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls, behind drywall, is a more serious issue that often requires professional intervention. It usually indicates a significant moisture problem and may involve opening up the wall to properly remediate the mold and fix the leak, which is best left to experts.

**Is it safe to clean mold yourself?**

Cleaning small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) can be done safely with proper personal protective equipment (PPE), including gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask. For larger infestations, if the mold returns, or if you have health concerns, it's safer to call a professional.

**Can I use bleach to clean mold?**

Bleach can kill surface mold on non-porous materials, but it's often less effective on porous surfaces like drywall as it may not penetrate to kill the mold roots. Additionally, it can damage surfaces and release harsh fumes, making vinegar or borax a safer and often more effective alternative for walls.

**How do I prevent mold from returning?**

To prevent mold from returning, the most critical step is to control moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, use dehumidifiers to keep indoor humidity below 60%, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and clean up spills immediately to maintain a dry environment.
