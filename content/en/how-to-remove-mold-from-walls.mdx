To remove mold from walls, begin by identifying the source of moisture and ensuring proper ventilation. For small areas, use a solution of diluted bleach or white vinegar, scrubbing the affected surface carefully while wearing protective gear. Always address the underlying moisture problem to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Bucket
- Scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Clean cloths or paper towels
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (if isolating the area)
- Drop cloths

## Steps

### Step 1: Isolate and Protect the Area

Before starting, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows or using fans. Close off the affected room from other parts of the house if possible, using plastic sheeting. Don protective gear including rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or contacting cleaning solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turn off your HVAC system to prevent spreading spores during cleaning.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold grows where there's moisture. Before cleaning, locate and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or excessive humidity. Without addressing the moisture, mold will inevitably return. Repairing leaks or improving ventilation is crucial for long-term prevention.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a solution of 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water can be effective. However, for a less harsh and equally effective option, especially on painted walls, use undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle. Vinegar is excellent for killing mold and inhibiting its return without the harshness of bleach.

> 💡 **Tip:** Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as it can create toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Spray the chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring complete saturation. If using bleach, be cautious of splashing and protect surrounding furniture. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes; this gives it time to penetrate and kill the mold spores effectively.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn spots, reapply the solution after five minutes.

### Step 5: Scrub and Remove the Mold

Using a stiff scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, thoroughly scrub the moldy surface. Apply firm pressure to dislodge the mold and its roots. Work in small sections to ensure complete coverage. Dispose of any heavily contaminated cloths or sponges immediately after use.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid excessive scrubbing that might damage the wall surface, especially on painted walls.

### Step 6: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining mold residue or cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent new mold growth. Use fans, open windows, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry before considering repainting or repairing any damaged areas.

### Step 7: Implement Preventative Measures

Once the mold is removed and the wall is dry, focus on prevention. Maintain good ventilation, especially in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Use exhaust fans, fix any leaks promptly, and consider mold-resistant paint in humid environments. Regularly inspect walls for any signs of moisture or new growth.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% using a hygrometer.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't damage or discolor your wall.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or black mold, consider calling a professional mold remediation service for safety. [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) offers more insights into professional handling.
- Improve air circulation by moving furniture slightly away from walls, especially in damp rooms.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use bleach on porous materials like drywall without proper ventilation and knowing it might not kill mold roots.
- ⚠️ If you suspect mold is growing inside walls, it's a more serious issue that may require professional intervention to avoid structural damage and health risks.
- ⚠️ Mold exposure can cause health problems, especially for those with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Always wear protective gear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle or a mild bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) for non-porous paint. Spray, let sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse and dry thoroughly.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

To permanently get rid of mold on walls, you must first eliminate the moisture source that allows it to grow. After cleaning the visible mold with a solution like vinegar or bleach, ensure the area stays dry and well-ventilated to prevent its return. Addressing the underlying water problem is key to permanent removal.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often involves professional mold remediation to ensure all mold is safely removed, the area is dried, and the moisture source is completely resolved to prevent recurrence.

**Can I use hydrogen peroxide to remove mold from walls?**

Yes, hydrogen peroxide can be an effective and less toxic alternative to bleach for removing mold from walls. Apply a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution directly to the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub and wipe clean. It's safe for most surfaces but always test in an inconspicuous spot first.

**What should I do if the mold keeps coming back on my walls?**

If mold repeatedly returns, it indicates an unresolved moisture issue. Re-evaluate your home for hidden leaks, condensation problems, or excessive humidity. You may need to consult a professional mold remediator or a home inspector to identify and fix the persistent moisture source.

**Is it safe to paint over mold on walls?**

No, it is not safe or effective to paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow beneath the paint, potentially causing more extensive damage and recurring health issues. Always remove all mold and address the moisture source before repainting.
