To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear. Then, clean the affected area with a diluted bleach solution or white vinegar, scrubbing gently to lift the mold before rinsing and drying thoroughly to prevent regrowth.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes or apron
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths or old towels
- White distilled vinegar OR household bleach
- Warm water
- Fan or dehumidifier (optional, for drying)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Turn off any HVAC systems to prevent mold spores from spreading. Don your safety goggles, rubber gloves, and N95 respirator mask to protect yourself from spores and cleaning solutions. Remove any furniture or items from the immediate area to prevent contamination.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold Type and Extent

Observe the mold to determine its characteristics. Small patches of surface mold can often be handled with DIY methods. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's a more hazardous type like black mold, it's best to consult a professional. For more information on identifying and dealing with specific types, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) is effective. For painted walls or if you prefer a natural option, white distilled vinegar (undiluted) is an excellent alternative. Never mix bleach with other cleaners like ammonia, as this can create toxic fumes. Test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Pour your chosen cleaning solution into a spray bottle. Liberally spray the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes, which gives it time to penetrate and kill the mold spores. Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, gently scrub the mold away from the wall. Be careful not to damage painted surfaces by scrubbing too aggressively.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean microfiber cloth dampened with warm water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residue from the cleaning solution and dead mold spores. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent the mold from returning. You can use clean, dry towels, or place a fan or dehumidifier in the room to speed up the drying process.

### Step 6: Address the Root Cause

Mold growth is often a symptom of excess moisture. After cleaning, take steps to identify and fix the source of the moisture. This could involve repairing leaky pipes, improving ventilation in bathrooms or kitchens, or using a dehumidifier in damp areas. Addressing the underlying issue is key to preventing future mold outbreaks.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always ventilate the area well during and after cleaning to disperse spores and fumes.
- For stubborn stains, a paste made of baking soda and water can be gently scrubbed on the area before applying your main cleaning solution.
- Consider using mold-resistant paint in areas prone to dampness to inhibit future growth.
- If the mold has penetrated drywall or insulation, those materials may need to be removed and replaced by a professional.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can produce dangerous, toxic gases.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to protect against mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you have health concerns, it's advisable to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when dealing with mold that appears furry, slimy, or has a strong musty odor, as these may indicate more severe types.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a solution of undiluted white distilled vinegar or a mild, diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water). Spray the solution onto the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly to prevent damage to the paint.

**What is the best way to remove mold from walls?**

The best way depends on the wall surface. For non-porous walls, a diluted bleach solution is highly effective. For painted or more delicate surfaces, undiluted white distilled vinegar is a safer, natural alternative. Always ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear, then scrub, rinse, and thoroughly dry the area.

**How do you prevent mold from growing back on walls?**

To prevent mold regrowth, address the root cause of moisture. This includes fixing leaks, improving ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60% with a dehumidifier. Ensure walls dry quickly after any moisture exposure.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

Removing small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) is generally safe for DIY. However, always wear protective gear (gloves, goggles, N95 mask) and ensure good ventilation. If the mold covers a large area, is deeply embedded, or if you have respiratory issues, it's best to hire a professional.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task and often indicates a significant moisture problem. It's usually best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist to safely and effectively address mold inside walls, as it can be extensive and pose health risks.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's deeply embedded in porous materials like drywall, if you suspect it's black mold, or if you experience persistent health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the expertise and equipment for safe and thorough remediation.
