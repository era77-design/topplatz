To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold type and ensuring proper ventilation. Use a mild bleach solution or vinegar mixture, gently scrubbing the affected area. Always wear protective gear to prevent exposure and ensure a thorough clean.

## What You Need

- Protective gear (rubber gloves, N95 respirator mask, safety glasses)
- Ventilation fan (optional)
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional)
- Mild dish soap
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or rags
- Bucket
- Water
- Mold-inhibiting primer (optional, for repainting)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. This helps to disperse mold spores and fumes from cleaning solutions. Don your protective gear, including rubber gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask, to prevent skin contact, eye irritation, and inhalation of mold spores or cleaning chemicals.

> 💡 **Tip:** If possible, close doors to other rooms to help prevent mold spores from spreading throughout your home.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Mold Source

Before cleaning, identify and fix the source of moisture that caused the mold growth, such as a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. For surface mold, proceed with cleaning. If you suspect you're dealing with extensive or potentially harmful mold, like certain types of black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) or consider professional help.

> 💡 **Tip:** Addressing the moisture source is crucial for preventing mold from returning after cleaning.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces such as painted walls, a solution of one part household bleach to three parts water is highly effective. Alternatively, for a natural, bleach-free option, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar. For more stubborn mold or a non-toxic approach, you can also use a solution of borax (one cup per gallon of water) with a few drops of dish soap.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply and Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes to penetrate and kill the mold. Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sponge, gently scrub the mold away. Avoid aggressive scrubbing that could damage the wall surface, especially on painted walls.

> 💡 **Tip:** Work in small sections to prevent the cleaning solution from drying on the wall before you can scrub it.

### Step 5: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residue from the cleaning solution and remaining mold spores. It is absolutely crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate recurrence of mold. Use clean, dry towels or a fan to speed up the drying process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the area is completely dry, which may take several hours, before considering any repainting or sealing.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is completely dry, consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting, especially if the area is prone to moisture. The most effective long-term prevention involves addressing underlying moisture issues, maintaining good ventilation, and controlling indoor humidity levels. Regularly inspect vulnerable areas for any signs of returning mold.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a dehumidifier in damp areas of your home to keep humidity levels below 60%.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear protective gear: gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask.
- Ensure adequate ventilation during and after cleaning to disperse spores and fumes.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as it can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or mold inside walls, consider professional help.
- Address the source of moisture immediately to prevent future mold recurrence.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes that are harmful if inhaled.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or if you suspect it's black mold, consider contacting a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Avoid breathing in mold spores or cleaning solution fumes. Always use appropriate personal protective equipment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, gently scrub the affected area with a solution of one part bleach to three parts water, or undiluted white vinegar. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint. Rinse thoroughly with clean water and dry completely.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a complex task that typically requires professional mold remediation. It often involves opening up the wall to access and safely remove the affected materials, as improper removal can spread spores and cause further damage or health issues.

**What is the best cleaner for mold on walls?**

For non-porous surfaces like most walls, a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 3 parts water) is highly effective at killing mold. For a natural alternative, undiluted white vinegar also works well. For porous surfaces or a non-bleach option, hydrogen peroxide or a borax solution can be used.

**How do you kill mold permanently on walls?**

To kill mold permanently, you must not only clean the visible mold but also address and eliminate the underlying source of moisture that allows it to grow. After cleaning, ensure the area remains dry, fix any leaks, and maintain good ventilation to prevent its return.

**Can I just paint over mold on walls?**

No, you should never paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow underneath the paint, eventually causing the paint to peel and potentially spreading further. Always clean and dry the mold completely before applying any primer or paint.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, if you have health concerns related to mold exposure, or if the mold is located in difficult-to-reach areas like inside walls or HVAC systems.
