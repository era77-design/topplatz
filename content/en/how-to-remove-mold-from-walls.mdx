To remove mold from walls, begin by ensuring the area is well-ventilated and protecting yourself with gloves, goggles, and a mask. Then, apply a suitable cleaning solution such as diluted bleach or vinegar, scrub gently to lift the mold, and dry the surface thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Bucket
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- White vinegar
- Household bleach (optional)
- Water
- Fan (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Ensure Ventilation

Before you begin, move any furniture or items away from the affected wall and cover the floor with old sheets or plastic sheeting to protect it from cleaning solutions. Open windows and doors to ensure good cross-ventilation, and if possible, use a fan to direct airflow outwards. Adequate ventilation is crucial for dissipating mold spores and fumes from cleaning products.

### Step 2: Protect Yourself with Proper Gear

Mold spores can be harmful if inhaled, and cleaning solutions can irritate skin and eyes. Always wear safety goggles to protect your eyes, rubber gloves to shield your hands, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling spores. This personal protective equipment (PPE) is essential for your safety during the mold removal process.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, you can use either a bleach solution or white vinegar. If using bleach, mix one cup of household bleach with one gallon of water in a bucket. For a bleach-free alternative, fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar, as its acidic nature effectively kills many mold types. For more severe cases or black mold, you might want to consult our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Carefully spray or wipe your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area of the wall. Ensure the entire affected surface is saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes; this dwell time is important to allow the active ingredients to penetrate and kill the mold spores. Do not scrub dry mold, as this can release more spores into the air.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

After the solution has sat, use a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge to gently scrub the mold off the wall. Work in small sections, applying consistent pressure until the visible mold disappears. Once the mold is removed, wipe the area clean with a damp microfiber cloth, rinsing the cloth frequently in a separate bucket of clean water. Avoid over-wetting the wall.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Thoroughly

Thorough drying is perhaps the most critical step to prevent mold from returning. Use clean, dry microfiber cloths to absorb as much moisture as possible from the wall. Then, leave windows open and use fans or a dehumidifier to ensure the wall is completely dry. Any residual moisture can lead to mold regrowth.

### Step 7: Address the Source of Moisture and Prevent Recurrence

Removing the mold is only a temporary fix if the underlying moisture problem isn't addressed. Identify and repair any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and control indoor humidity levels. Regularly inspect walls for early signs of moisture or mold growth to prevent future infestations.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage, especially on painted surfaces.
- For stubborn mold stains that remain after cleaning, you can try a second application of your chosen solution, letting it sit for an extended period before scrubbing again.
- Improve air circulation in your home by using exhaust fans, opening windows, and ensuring furniture isn't pushed too tightly against exterior walls, which can trap moisture.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ NEVER mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), or if you suspect mold growth inside walls, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or a weakened immune system; always defer to a professional in such cases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first protect yourself with PPE and ventilate the area. Apply a solution of diluted bleach or undiluted white vinegar, let it sit, then scrub and wipe the mold away. Finish by thoroughly drying the wall to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows the same process: ventilate, wear PPE, and apply a cleaning solution. You can use diluted bleach or white vinegar. Always test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't strip paint or cause discoloration, then gently scrub and dry.

**What kills mold on walls effectively?**

Household bleach diluted with water (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) is effective on non-porous surfaces like walls. Undiluted white vinegar is also a natural and effective alternative that kills many types of mold and helps prevent regrowth.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls myself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you wear proper protective gear (mask, gloves, goggles) and ensure good ventilation. For larger areas, hidden mold, or if you have health concerns, professional help is recommended.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls is a complex task that typically requires professional intervention. It often involves opening up the wall, identifying the moisture source, and extensive remediation. Attempting to clean mold inside walls without proper equipment and expertise can spread spores and worsen the problem.

**How do I prevent mold from coming back on my walls?**

To prevent mold recurrence, you must address the underlying moisture issue. This includes repairing leaks, improving ventilation in humid areas, using dehumidifiers, and ensuring walls are kept dry. Regularly clean and dry areas prone to moisture.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation specialist if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if it's due to contaminated water, if you suspect hidden mold behind walls, or if anyone in the household experiences persistent health issues related to mold exposure.
