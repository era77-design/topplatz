Mold on walls is a common issue that, if not addressed, can affect air quality and structural integrity. Fortunately, minor mold growth can often be removed safely with common household products and proper precautions. This guide will walk you through the steps to effectively clean mold from your walls.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety glasses
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Borax
- Dish soap
- Warm water
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Old cloths or rags
- Bucket
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Drop cloths or old towels
- Mold-resistant primer (optional)
- Hydrogen peroxide (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess the Mold and Prepare Safely

Before you begin, assess the extent of the mold. If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if it's due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), it's best to consult a professional. For smaller areas, don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety glasses, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling spores. Cover nearby furniture and flooring with drop cloths to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Ventilate and Isolate the Area

Open windows and doors to ensure good ventilation in the room. If possible, use a fan to direct airflow outwards. Close off the affected room from other parts of the house to prevent mold spores from spreading. Turn off your HVAC system to avoid circulating spores throughout your home.

### Step 3: Choose and Apply Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, a simple solution of white vinegar works wonders. Fill a spray bottle with undiluted white vinegar and spray it directly onto the moldy area, letting it sit for at least an hour. Alternatively, mix 1 cup of borax with 1 gallon of warm water and a few drops of dish soap; this solution can also be sprayed or applied with a sponge. For tougher spots, a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution applied directly can also be effective.

### Step 4: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

After allowing the solution to sit, use a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a rough sponge to gently scrub the mold away from the wall. Apply consistent pressure, working in small sections. Once the mold is removed, wipe the area thoroughly with clean, damp cloths to remove any remaining residue and spores. Rinse your cloths frequently in a separate bucket of clean water.

### Step 5: Dry the Area Completely

Thorough drying is crucial to prevent mold from returning. Use a clean, dry cloth to absorb as much moisture as possible from the cleaned wall. Then, run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours, or even overnight, to ensure the wall is completely dry. Any lingering moisture can lead to rapid mold regrowth.

### Step 6: Sanitize and Prevent Recurrence

Once dry, consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting, especially if the mold was extensive or if the area is prone to moisture. More importantly, identify and fix the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Addressing the root cause is the best long-term prevention strategy.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For minor mold, avoid using bleach, as it can damage surfaces and doesn't always kill mold at its root, especially on porous materials.
- Improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens by using exhaust fans or opening windows during and after moisture-producing activities.
- Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally between 30-50%) using a dehumidifier in humid climates or seasons.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If you encounter black mold, or suspect it, exercise extreme caution. For severe cases or persistent black mold, it's highly recommended to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. You can learn more about handling this specific type of mold by reading [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ If mold growth is extensive, returns quickly, or you experience health issues, seek professional help rather than attempting DIY removal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, first wear protective gear like gloves and a mask. Ventilate the area, then apply a cleaning solution like white vinegar or a borax-water mixture. Scrub the mold away with a brush, wipe clean with damp cloths, and ensure the area is thoroughly dried to prevent regrowth.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

Removing mold from painted walls follows the same general steps. Use a mild cleaning solution like undiluted white vinegar or a borax solution, as harsh chemicals can strip paint. Gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint, then rinse and dry completely.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

No single product kills mold permanently if the moisture source remains. Solutions like vinegar, hydrogen peroxide, and borax effectively kill existing mold. However, for permanent removal, you must identify and fix the underlying moisture problem that allowed the mold to grow in the first place.

**When should you not remove mold yourself?**

You should not attempt to remove mold yourself if the affected area is larger than 10 square feet, if it's caused by contaminated water (e.g., sewage), or if you have health conditions that make you sensitive to mold exposure. In these cases, it's best to hire a professional mold remediation specialist.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more complex task that often involves cutting out and replacing moldy drywall or insulation. Due to the potential for widespread contamination and structural damage, it's usually best to consult a professional for mold inside walls.

**Can mold on walls make you sick?**

Yes, mold on walls can potentially make you sick, especially if you have allergies, asthma, or a weakened immune system. Exposure to mold spores can cause respiratory problems, skin irritation, eye irritation, and other allergic reactions. Prompt removal and prevention are important for health.

**How do I prevent mold from growing back on walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, eliminate the moisture source, whether it's a leak, condensation, or high humidity. Ensure good ventilation, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider a dehumidifier. Applying mold-resistant primer before painting can also offer extra protection in prone areas.
