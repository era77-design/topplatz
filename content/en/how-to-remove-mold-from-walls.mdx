Removing mold from walls is crucial for maintaining a healthy home environment. Small areas of surface mold can often be tackled with household cleaners and proper safety precautions, but larger infestations or mold inside walls may require professional help. Always prioritize safety to prevent health issues and ensure effective removal.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub pad
- Clean cloths or sponges
- Mild dish soap
- White vinegar
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional, for containment)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Don protective gear including rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to avoid contact with mold spores. Clear the area around the moldy wall, moving furniture and covering nearby items with plastic sheeting to prevent cross-contamination. Ensure you're wearing old clothes that can be washed immediately after or disposed of.

### Step 2: Ventilate and Isolate the Area

Open windows and use fans to create good airflow, directing air *out* of the room if possible, but avoid blowing air directly onto the mold as this can spread spores. Close off the affected room from other parts of your home to minimize spore distribution during the cleaning process. This isolation helps contain the problem to one area.

### Step 3: Choose and Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

For most surface mold, a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle works well, as vinegar is a mild acid that can kill many types of mold. Alternatively, you can use a solution of 1/4 cup mild dish soap per gallon of water. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply and Gently Scrub the Mold

Spray your chosen solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring the surface is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 10-15 minutes to penetrate the mold. Using a stiff brush or scrub pad, gently scrub the mold away from the wall, working in small sections to avoid spreading spores. For stubborn spots, reapply the solution and scrub again.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's crucial to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a clean, dry towel, and then run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for several hours until the wall is bone dry.

### Step 6: Dispose of Materials and Prevent Recurrence

Carefully bag all used cloths, sponges, and any plastic sheeting, sealing them before disposing of them outside your home. To prevent mold from returning, identify and fix the underlying moisture problem that caused the mold in the first place, such as a leaky pipe or high humidity. Consider using mold-resistant paint in the future.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot of the wall first to ensure it doesn't damage the paint or finish.
- Do not paint over mold. This will not kill the mold and it will eventually grow through the new paint.
- Address the source of moisture immediately. Mold will always return if the moisture problem isn't fixed.
- Ensure the room is well-ventilated throughout the cleaning process to minimize inhalation of spores and fumes.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (roughly 3x3 feet), or if you suspect it's black mold, it's best to call a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Always wear an N95 respirator mask, safety goggles, and rubber gloves to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions.
- ⚠️ If you have health conditions like asthma, allergies, or a compromised immune system, it's advisable to have someone else remove the mold or hire a professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

You can remove surface mold from walls by first protecting yourself with gloves, goggles, and a respirator. Then, spray the affected area with a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water, let it sit, and scrub with a stiff brush. Rinse and dry the wall thoroughly to prevent regrowth.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of mild dish soap and water or a diluted white vinegar solution is often effective. Spray the area, let it sit briefly, and gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge, then rinse and dry completely. Test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first.

**What kills mold on walls permanently?**

While cleaning solutions like white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide can kill mold spores on surfaces, permanent removal relies on addressing the root cause of the moisture. If the moisture source isn't fixed, mold will likely return. Ensuring proper ventilation, fixing leaks, and controlling humidity are key to long-term prevention.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches (under 10 square feet) of surface mold yourself, provided you wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) like gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask. However, if the mold covers a large area, is black mold, or you suspect it's growing inside walls, it's safer to consult a professional. For more on black mold, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation service if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, or if the mold is hidden behind walls or under flooring. Professionals have the specialized equipment and expertise to safely and effectively remove extensive mold infestations and address hidden moisture issues.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area, which is a more involved process and often best left to professionals. If you suspect mold behind a wall (e.g., musty smell, visible staining on the exterior), it indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be fully investigated and remediated to prevent structural damage and health risks.

**Can mold come back after cleaning?**

Yes, mold can definitely come back after cleaning if the underlying moisture problem that caused its initial growth is not resolved. Mold spores are ubiquitous, and they will regrow in the presence of moisture, food sources (like drywall or wood), and the right temperature. Addressing leaks, condensation, and high humidity is vital for preventing recurrence.
