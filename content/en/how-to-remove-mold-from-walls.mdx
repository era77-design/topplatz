To remove mold from walls, first ensure proper ventilation and wear protective gear. For small areas, a solution of distilled white vinegar or a mild detergent and water can effectively kill and clean mold from surfaces. Always address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 mask
- Eye protection (goggles)
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Two buckets
- Distilled white vinegar
- Mild dish soap or detergent
- Water

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace and Yourself

Before tackling mold, ensure the area is well-ventilated by opening windows and doors. Don appropriate safety gear, including rubber gloves, an N95 mask to avoid inhaling spores, and eye protection. Clear any furniture or items away from the affected wall to prevent contamination and allow for easy access.

### Step 2: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a solution of undiluted distilled white vinegar is highly effective at killing most types of mold. Alternatively, you can use a mixture of 1 cup of mild dish soap or detergent with 1 gallon of warm water. Avoid using bleach, especially on painted walls, as it can damage the finish and isn't always effective on porous surfaces.

### Step 3: Apply the Solution to the Moldy Area

Pour your chosen cleaning solution into a spray bottle. Generously spray the mold-affected area, ensuring it is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes. This dwell time helps the cleaning agent penetrate the mold and makes it easier to remove, preventing spores from becoming airborne during scrubbing.

### Step 4: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or a sturdy sponge, gently scrub the mold from the wall. Start from the top of the moldy patch and work your way down. Be careful not to scrub too aggressively, which could damage the wall surface or spread mold spores further. Dispose of heavily soiled cloths or sponges.

### Step 5: Rinse and Thoroughly Dry the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the cleaned area, removing any residual cleaning solution and dead mold. It's crucial to then dry the wall completely with a clean, dry towel or allow it to air dry with good ventilation. Any remaining moisture can encourage mold to regrow.

### Step 6: Address the Moisture Source

Mold growth is a symptom of excess moisture. Identify and fix the source of the dampness, whether it's a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Repair any leaks promptly and consider using a dehumidifier in humid environments. Ensuring proper ventilation, especially in bathrooms and kitchens, is key to preventing future mold outbreaks.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix different cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, as this can create toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold or if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet (about 3x3 feet), it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and running a dehumidifier to keep humidity levels below 60%.
- If you suspect black mold, or the infestation is extensive, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for more detailed guidance or contact a professional.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on porous surfaces like drywall, as it often only discolors the mold and doesn't kill the roots, potentially leading to faster regrowth. It can also damage paint and finishes.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), or if you suspect it's caused by contaminated water (e.g., sewage), do not attempt to clean it yourself. Professional remediation is required.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including an N95 mask, rubber gloves, and eye protection, to prevent exposure to mold spores.
- ⚠️ If you experience allergic reactions, respiratory issues, or headaches while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air. Consult a doctor if symptoms persist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle solution like distilled white vinegar or a mild dish soap and water mixture. Spray the area, let it sit, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse with clean water and dry thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

**What is the best solution for removing mold from walls?**

For most common surface mold on non-porous walls, undiluted distilled white vinegar is highly effective as a natural fungicide. A solution of mild dish soap and water also works well. For tougher spots, a hydrogen peroxide solution (3% concentration) can be used, but always test on an inconspicuous area first.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention, as it indicates a significant moisture problem that has penetrated the wall structure. This usually involves opening up the wall, identifying and fixing the moisture source, and safely removing or replacing affected building materials. DIY removal is not recommended for mold inside walls.

**Is it safe to remove mold yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small patches of surface mold (less than 10 square feet) yourself, provided you take proper safety precautions. Always wear protective gear like gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection, and ensure good ventilation. For larger areas or suspected toxic mold, professional remediation is advised.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Preventing mold recurrence involves controlling moisture. Fix any leaks promptly, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and use a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly clean and dry surfaces that are prone to condensation.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional mold remediation service if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect black mold, or if the mold is due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage). Professionals have the equipment and expertise to safely contain and remove extensive mold growth.
