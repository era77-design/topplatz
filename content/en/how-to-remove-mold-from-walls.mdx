Removing mold from walls is a manageable DIY task for small, surface-level growth. It primarily involves identifying the mold, preparing the area safely, and using an appropriate cleaning solution to kill and remove the fungal spores. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation during the process.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Safety glasses
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Clean cloths or towels
- Bucket
- Mild dish soap
- White vinegar
- Borax (optional)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Gear Up and Ventilate

Before you begin, don your protective gear: rubber gloves, an N95 respirator mask, and safety glasses. Mold spores can irritate your respiratory system and skin. Open windows and doors in the affected area to ensure proper ventilation, or use a fan to direct airflow outdoors. This helps to disperse spores and fumes from cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Isolate and Prep the Area

To prevent mold spores from spreading to other parts of your home, close off the affected room if possible. Remove any furniture, rugs, or decorations from the vicinity of the moldy wall. If items cannot be moved, cover them with plastic sheeting to protect them from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For general mold removal, a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle is effective and non-toxic. For tougher mold or non-porous surfaces, you can use a solution of 1 cup bleach per gallon of water (never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia). Another option is a borax solution: 1 cup borax dissolved in a gallon of hot water. Test your chosen solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage the wall finish.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution to Mold

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy areas of the wall. Ensure the mold is thoroughly saturated. Allow the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes. This dwell time allows the active ingredients to penetrate and kill the mold spores effectively before scrubbing begins. Do not rinse yet.

### Step 5: Scrub Away the Mold

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge, vigorously scrub the mold from the wall. Apply firm pressure to dislodge all visible mold growth. For stubborn spots, reapply the solution and let it sit for a few more minutes before scrubbing again. Be thorough, as any remaining mold can quickly regrow.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, wipe down the cleaned area with a clean, damp cloth to remove any residue from the cleaning solution and remaining mold spores. Then, immediately dry the wall completely using clean, dry towels or a fan. Ensuring the wall is fully dry is crucial to prevent mold from returning, as moisture is its primary fuel.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the source of the moisture that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leaky pipe, poor ventilation, or high humidity. Use a dehumidifier in damp areas, ensure proper ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and promptly fix any leaks. Regularly inspect walls for signs of new moisture or mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, as it creates toxic fumes.
- For porous surfaces like drywall, mold may have penetrated deeper. If scrubbing doesn't remove all visible mold, or if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), consider professional remediation.
- Improve air circulation by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and ensuring furniture isn't pushed directly against exterior walls.
- Consider using mold-inhibiting primers and paints in areas prone to dampness.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear protective gear (gloves, mask, safety glasses) to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If you suspect the mold is 'black mold' or covers a large area (greater than 10 square feet), it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. Large infestations can pose significant health risks. For more information on dealing with severe cases, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Never attempt to clean mold if you have severe allergies, asthma, or a compromised immune system. Seek professional help instead.
- ⚠️ Ensure the area is well-ventilated during and after cleaning to minimize exposure to spores and fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first wear protective gear and ensure good ventilation. Use a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water, or a mild detergent and water, applied with a spray bottle. Gently scrub the mold with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint, then rinse with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly.

**How do you remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall cavity, which is a more extensive task. If you suspect mold behind drywall, it's often best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist. They can safely identify the source of moisture, remove affected materials, and ensure the area is properly cleaned and dried to prevent recurrence.

**What causes mold to grow on walls?**

Mold grows on walls when there is a persistent moisture source, combined with organic material (like dust or drywall) for it to feed on, and a lack of proper ventilation. Common causes include leaky pipes, high humidity, condensation, water intrusion from outside, and poor air circulation in damp areas like bathrooms or basements.

**Is it safe to remove mold from walls yourself?**

It is generally safe to remove small, surface-level mold patches (less than 10 square feet) from walls yourself, provided you wear appropriate protective gear (gloves, N95 mask, safety glasses) and ensure good ventilation. However, for large infestations, persistent mold, or 'black mold', it's safer and more effective to hire a professional.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, address the underlying moisture problem. Fix leaks promptly, use a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity below 60%, ensure good ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens, and regularly clean and dry surfaces prone to dampness. Improving air circulation also helps prevent condensation.
