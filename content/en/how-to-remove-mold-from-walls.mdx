Removing mold from walls involves identifying the source, preparing the area, and using appropriate cleaning solutions. Always prioritize safety by wearing protective gear and ensuring good ventilation to prevent health risks and mold recurrence. Thorough drying after cleaning is crucial for long-term success.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 respirator mask
- Bucket
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub pad
- Clean cloths or sponges
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Mild dish soap (optional)
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Drop cloths or old towels

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan to direct airflow outdoors. Lay down drop cloths or old towels to protect the floor and furniture from spills. Don your protective gear: rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or cleaning solution fumes.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold Type and Source

Determine if the growth is superficial mildew or deeper-seated mold. Mildew is usually powdery and white or gray, while mold is often fuzzy, black, green, or brown. More importantly, locate the moisture source causing the mold, such as a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Addressing the moisture problem is critical to prevent future mold growth.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, white vinegar is an effective natural fungicide. You can also create a paste using baking soda and water for tougher spots, or use a solution of mild dish soap and water. Avoid using bleach on porous surfaces, as it may not kill mold roots and can damage the material.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

If using vinegar, pour undiluted white vinegar into a spray bottle and saturate the moldy area. For baking soda, mix a quarter cup of baking soda with a small amount of water to form a thick paste, then apply it directly to the mold. If using dish soap, mix a few drops with water in a bucket.

### Step 5: Scrub and Rinse the Mold Away

Allow the vinegar or baking soda paste to sit on the mold for at least 15-30 minutes to penetrate and kill the spores. Then, use a stiff brush or scrub pad to vigorously scrub the mold off the wall surface. Wipe the area clean with a damp cloth, rinsing the cloth frequently in clean water.

### Step 6: Dry Thoroughly to Prevent Recurrence

After cleaning, it is essential to thoroughly dry the affected area to prevent mold from returning. Use clean, dry towels to blot excess moisture. Employ a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process and ensure the wall is completely dry before considering any repainting or sealing.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Once the wall is clean and dry, take steps to prevent future mold. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and consider using a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regular cleaning and monitoring can help catch new mold growth early.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Never mix bleach with other household cleaners, especially ammonia, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- For persistent mold odors after cleaning, place an open bowl of white vinegar or baking soda in the room to absorb the smell.
- Improve air circulation by keeping furniture slightly away from walls, especially in humid rooms, to prevent moisture buildup.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear proper protective gear, including gloves, goggles, and an N95 respirator mask, to avoid contact with mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, or if you suspect it's 'black mold' (Stachybotrys chartarum), it's best to consult a professional mold remediation service.
- ⚠️ Never paint over mold without thoroughly cleaning and addressing the moisture source, as the mold will simply grow through the new paint.
- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on drywall or other porous surfaces, as it often only bleaches the surface mold and does not kill the roots, potentially leading to regrowth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from walls safely?**

To safely remove mold from walls, always wear protective gear including gloves, goggles, and an N95 mask. Ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using fans. Use appropriate cleaners like white vinegar or a mild detergent, and thoroughly dry the area afterward to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like diluted white vinegar or a mild dish soap solution to avoid damaging the paint. Apply the solution, let it sit for a few minutes, then gently scrub with a soft brush or sponge. Rinse and dry the area completely.

**What's the best natural cleaner for mold on walls?**

White vinegar is an excellent natural cleaner for mold on walls. It's a mild acid that can kill many types of mold. Simply spray undiluted white vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for an hour, then scrub and wipe clean.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls after cleaning?**

Prevent mold recurrence by addressing the source of moisture. Fix any leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas, and use a dehumidifier to keep indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly inspect walls and promptly clean any new spots.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area and address the moisture source. This is often a job best left to professionals, especially if the mold is extensive or you suspect structural damage. They can safely identify and remediate hidden mold.

**Is it safe to clean black mold myself?**

While small patches of black mold (under 10 square feet) can sometimes be handled with caution, it's generally recommended to consult a professional for black mold, especially if it's extensive or you have health concerns. For more details on safe handling, refer to [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

**What should I do if the mold keeps coming back on my walls?**

If mold repeatedly returns, it indicates an unresolved underlying moisture problem. You'll need to thoroughly investigate and fix the source of moisture, whether it's a persistent leak, condensation issue, or high humidity. If you can't identify the cause, consider hiring a professional inspector.
