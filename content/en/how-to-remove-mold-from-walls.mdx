To remove mold from walls, start by ensuring proper ventilation and wearing protective gear. For small, non-porous areas, a simple solution of white vinegar or a mild bleach mixture can effectively kill and remove the mold. Always address the moisture source to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 mask or respirator
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Baking soda (optional)
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Clean cloths or sponges
- Stiff brush or scrub brush
- Bucket
- Fan
- Dehumidifier (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Put on rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 mask or respirator to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Wear old clothes that you don't mind getting stained or can easily wash afterward.

### Step 2: Identify and Address the Moisture Source

Mold thrives in damp conditions, so identifying and fixing the source of moisture is crucial to prevent its return. This could be a leaky pipe, condensation, or high humidity. Repair any leaks, improve ventilation, and consider using a dehumidifier in humid areas to keep the walls dry.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces like painted walls, you have a few options. White vinegar is a natural, effective mold killer; simply pour undiluted vinegar into a spray bottle. Alternatively, you can use a solution of 1 cup bleach mixed with 1 gallon of water, or a mild detergent and water. Avoid mixing bleach with vinegar or ammonia, as this creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution and Scrub

Spray your chosen cleaning solution directly onto the moldy area, ensuring it's fully saturated. Let the solution sit for 10-15 minutes to allow it to penetrate and kill the mold. Then, using a stiff brush or scrub brush, scrub the mold away from the wall. For extensive or black mold, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

### Step 5: Wipe and Rinse the Wall

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe down the treated area, removing any remaining mold residue and cleaning solution. It's important to rinse thoroughly to prevent any chemical residue from damaging the paint or attracting more dirt. Repeat the scrubbing and wiping process if necessary until all visible mold is gone.

### Step 6: Dry the Wall Completely

Once the mold is removed, it's critical to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate regrowth. Use a clean, dry cloth to blot excess moisture, and then use fans or a dehumidifier to ensure the area is thoroughly dry. Good air circulation is key at this stage.

### Step 7: Dispose of Materials and Monitor

Carefully dispose of all contaminated cloths, sponges, and brushes in a sealed plastic bag. Wash your gloves and goggles. Continue to monitor the area regularly for any signs of mold returning. Maintaining good ventilation and addressing humidity will help keep your walls mold-free.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For porous surfaces like drywall or insulation, mold often penetrates too deeply and may require professional removal or replacement.
- Consider applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting walls in areas prone to moisture.
- Improve air circulation in your home by opening windows, using exhaust fans, and avoiding overcrowding furniture against walls.
- Keep indoor humidity levels below 60% (ideally between 30-50%) using a dehumidifier, especially in basements and bathrooms.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous, toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment (gloves, goggles, N95 mask) to avoid contact with mold spores and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), or if you suspect it's a severe type of mold, it's best to consult a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Do not paint over mold without cleaning it first, as the mold will continue to grow underneath the paint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, first ensure good ventilation and wear protective gear. Use a solution of undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle or a mild bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water). Spray the mold, let it sit for 10-15 minutes, then scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge to avoid damaging the paint. Rinse and dry thoroughly.

**What is the best homemade solution to remove mold from walls?**

The best homemade solution for removing mold from walls is undiluted white vinegar. It's effective at killing many types of mold and is safe for most surfaces. Simply spray it on, let it sit, then scrub and wipe clean. For tougher stains, a paste of baking soda and water can be applied before cleaning.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet), if it's due to contaminated water (e.g., sewage), or if you or family members experience health issues related to mold exposure. Professionals have the specialized equipment and expertise to safely handle extensive mold contamination.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is often a sign of a significant, ongoing moisture problem that needs professional assessment and remediation. Attempting to clean mold from inside walls without addressing the moisture source or proper containment can spread spores and worsen the issue.

**Can I just paint over mold?**

No, you should never just paint over mold. Painting over mold will not kill it; the mold will continue to grow and spread underneath the paint, eventually reappearing and potentially causing structural damage or health issues. Always clean and thoroughly dry the moldy area completely before applying any primer or paint.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold from returning, address any sources of moisture like leaks, improve ventilation in damp areas, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, open windows when possible, and consider a dehumidifier in humid climates or basements. Ensure surfaces dry quickly after cleaning or getting wet.
