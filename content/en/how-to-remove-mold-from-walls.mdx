To remove mold from walls, start by identifying the mold type and the wall material. For surface mold on non-porous walls, a simple bleach or vinegar solution can be highly effective. Always wear protective gear and ensure good ventilation during the cleaning process.

## What You Need

- Safety goggles
- Rubber gloves
- N95 respirator mask
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using a fan. Clear the area around the mold, moving furniture and covering items you can't move. Don your protective gear: safety goggles, rubber gloves, and an N95 respirator mask to prevent inhaling mold spores or cleaning fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the area if possible by closing doors to other rooms to prevent spore spread.

### Step 2: Identify the Mold and Wall Type

Before cleaning, determine if the mold is superficial and what type of wall you're dealing with. This guide focuses on surface mold on painted, tiled, or other non-porous walls. For extensive black mold or mold on drywall/porous surfaces, professional help might be needed.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water) is effective. Alternatively, undiluted white vinegar in a spray bottle is a natural and safer option for most surfaces, including painted walls, as it kills a high percentage of mold species. Never mix bleach and vinegar as it creates toxic fumes.

### Step 4: Apply the Solution

Lightly spray the moldy area with your chosen solution. If using bleach, let it sit for 5-10 minutes to kill the mold. For vinegar, allow it to sit for at least an hour to ensure it penetrates and effectively kills the mold spores. Do not scrub yet, allowing the solution time to work.

### Step 5: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

Using a stiff brush or scrub sponge, gently scrub the affected area to loosen and remove the mold. Wipe away the loosened mold and solution with clean microfiber cloths, rinsing the cloths frequently in a separate bucket of clean water. Avoid excessive wetting of the wall, especially if it's drywall, to prevent damage.

### Step 6: Dry the Area Thoroughly

Once the mold is removed, ensure the wall is completely dry to prevent recurrence. Use a clean, dry cloth to absorb any excess moisture. Employ a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the drying process and lower the humidity in the room, which is crucial for long-term prevention.

### Step 7: Inspect and Prevent Future Growth

After the wall is dry, inspect the area thoroughly for any missed spots. Address the source of moisture that caused the mold in the first place, whether it's a leak, condensation, or high humidity. Maintaining good ventilation and promptly fixing water issues are key to preventing future mold growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test your cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For small, easily accessible mold spots, a simple solution of dish soap and water can sometimes be effective, followed by thorough drying.
- Consider using mold-inhibiting primers and paints in areas prone to moisture, such as bathrooms and basements, after cleaning.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or any other household cleaners, as this can create toxic and dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ If you suspect the mold is black mold or covers an area larger than 10 square feet, it's safer to consult a professional mold remediation service. For more specific advice, see [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).
- ⚠️ Avoid excessive wetting of drywall, as it can damage the material and encourage mold growth within the wall cavity, potentially requiring replacement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, a solution of undiluted white vinegar is generally safe and effective. Spray the vinegar directly onto the mold, let it sit for an hour, then gently scrub and wipe clean. Always test the solution in an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't affect the paint.

**What's the best way to remove mold from walls permanently?**

Removing mold permanently requires not just cleaning the visible mold but also addressing and fixing the underlying moisture problem that caused it. Without eliminating the moisture source, such as a leak or high humidity, mold is likely to return.

**Can I remove mold from inside walls myself?**

Removing mold from *inside* walls is much more complex and usually requires professional intervention, especially if it's extensive. This often involves opening up the wall, which can release a large number of mold spores and requires specialized equipment for safe removal and remediation.

**Is it safe to clean mold without a mask?**

No, it is not safe to clean mold without a mask. Mold spores can be irritating or harmful when inhaled, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or other respiratory issues. Always wear an N95 respirator mask to protect your lungs from inhaling spores and cleaning fumes.

**How can I prevent mold from growing on my walls again?**

To prevent mold recurrence, control indoor humidity levels (ideally below 60%), ensure good ventilation in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and kitchens, and promptly repair any leaks or water damage. Regular cleaning and reducing condensation also help significantly.

**What should I do if the mold keeps coming back after cleaning?**

If mold consistently returns after cleaning, it indicates that the underlying moisture problem has not been fully resolved, or the mold has penetrated deeper than surface cleaning can address. At this point, it's advisable to consult a professional mold remediation specialist to identify and fix the root cause.
