Removing mold from walls is a common household task that can be tackled safely with the right tools and techniques. The key is to address the mold quickly, protect yourself, and ensure the area dries completely to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Safety goggles
- N95 mask or respirator
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Spray bottle
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Dish soap (optional)
- Clean cloths or old towels
- Bucket of clean water
- Fan or dehumidifier

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Protect Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated. Open windows and doors, or use a fan to circulate air. Put on your rubber gloves, safety goggles, and an N95 mask or respirator to protect yourself from mold spores and cleaning solutions. If the mold covers a large area (more than 10 square feet) or you suspect it's black mold, consider calling a professional.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cover nearby furniture with plastic sheeting to protect it from spores and cleaning solutions.

### Step 2: Assess the Mold and Choose Your Cleaner

For small areas of surface mold, white vinegar is an excellent natural fungicide. For tougher spots or if you prefer an alternative, a solution of baking soda and water can also be effective. For painted walls, avoid harsh abrasive cleaners that could damage the paint finish. If you suspect you're dealing with a more serious type of growth, refer to guides on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the wall first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 3: Apply Your Cleaning Solution

For white vinegar, pour undiluted vinegar into a spray bottle and saturate the moldy area. Let it sit for at least an hour to allow the acetic acid to penetrate and kill the mold. If using baking soda, mix half a teaspoon of baking soda with a cup of water in a spray bottle, add a few drops of dish soap if desired, and spray generously onto the mold. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid scrubbing dry mold, as this can release spores into the air. Always dampen the mold first with your cleaning solution.

### Step 4: Scrub and Wipe Away the Mold

After the solution has sat, use a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge to scrub the mold from the wall. Apply firm but gentle pressure, working in small sections. Once the mold is visibly removed, wipe down the area thoroughly with a clean, damp cloth to remove any remaining residue and spores. Rinse your cloth frequently in a bucket of clean water.

> 💡 **Tip:** Dispose of used cloths or sponges in a sealed bag immediately after cleaning to prevent spore spread.

### Step 5: Rinse and Dry the Wall Thoroughly

After scrubbing, use a fresh, clean cloth dampened with plain water to wipe down the entire cleaned area. This helps to remove any lingering cleaning solution residue. The most crucial step is ensuring the wall is completely dry. Use a fan, open windows, or a dehumidifier to speed up the drying process. Mold thrives in moisture, so thorough drying is essential to prevent its return.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the wall is bone dry for at least 24-48 hours before painting or applying any finishes to prevent mold recurrence.

### Step 6: Address the Source of Moisture

Mold will return if the underlying moisture problem isn't resolved. Identify and fix any leaks, reduce humidity levels with a dehumidifier, or improve ventilation in damp areas like bathrooms and kitchens. Good air circulation and keeping humidity below 60% are key to preventing future mold growth on your walls.

> 💡 **Tip:** Regularly inspect areas prone to mold, such as bathrooms, basements, and around windows, for early signs of moisture or growth.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear protective gear (gloves, mask, goggles) when cleaning mold.
- Improve ventilation in your home to reduce humidity, especially in bathrooms and kitchens.
- Fix any leaks immediately, no matter how small, to prevent mold growth.
- Consider using mold-resistant paint in high-humidity areas as a preventative measure.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as it can create dangerous fumes.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not attempt to clean mold if you have severe allergies, asthma, or a compromised immune system; seek professional help instead.
- ⚠️ If mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet or appears to be black mold, it's safer to contact a professional mold remediation specialist.
- ⚠️ Avoid using bleach directly on porous surfaces like drywall, as it can damage the material and may not kill the mold's roots effectively.
- ⚠️ Ensure adequate ventilation to avoid inhaling mold spores or fumes from cleaning products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

To remove mold from painted walls, use a gentle cleaning solution like white vinegar or a baking soda paste. Avoid harsh scrubbing or abrasive cleaners that could damage the paint. Test the solution on an inconspicuous spot first, then gently scrub the mold, rinse with clean water, and thoroughly dry the area.

**What is the best homemade solution for removing mold from walls?**

The best homemade solution is often undiluted white vinegar, which is effective at killing many types of mold. Alternatively, a paste made from baking soda and a small amount of water, or a spray solution of half a teaspoon of baking soda per cup of water, can also work well.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires professional intervention. It often indicates a significant moisture problem that needs to be identified and fixed, and the mold itself may be extensive, requiring demolition and specialized remediation techniques.

**When should I call a professional for mold removal?**

You should call a professional for mold removal if the mold covers an area larger than 10 square feet, if you suspect it's black mold, if you have health issues like asthma or allergies that could be aggravated, or if the mold is hidden behind walls or in HVAC systems.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

Prevent mold from returning by addressing the source of moisture, improving ventilation, and maintaining indoor humidity levels below 60%. Fix leaks promptly, use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens, and consider a dehumidifier in damp areas to keep walls dry.

**Is it safe to clean mold myself?**

For small areas of surface mold (less than 10 square feet), it is generally safe to clean yourself with proper protective gear like gloves, a mask, and goggles. However, if you have respiratory issues, allergies, or if the mold is extensive, it's safer to consult a professional.
