To remove mold from walls, first ensure the area is well-ventilated and you are wearing proper protective gear. Clean the affected surface with a suitable mold-killing solution like white vinegar or diluted bleach, then scrub gently and dry thoroughly to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Eye protection (goggles)
- N95 respirator mask
- Old clothes
- Spray bottle
- Stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge
- Microfiber cloths
- Bucket
- White vinegar
- Bleach (optional, for non-porous surfaces)
- Water
- Fan or dehumidifier
- Plastic sheeting (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Yourself

Before you begin, ensure the room is well-ventilated by opening windows and using fans. Cover nearby furniture and flooring with plastic sheeting to protect them from mold spores and cleaning solutions. Don your protective gear: rubber gloves, eye protection, and an N95 respirator mask are essential to avoid inhaling spores or contact with cleaning agents.

> 💡 **Tip:** Isolate the area by closing doors to other rooms to prevent mold spores from spreading.

### Step 2: Identify Mold Type and Assess Damage

Carefully examine the mold to determine its extent. Small patches of surface mold on non-porous walls are often manageable with DIY methods. If you suspect extensive mold growth, particularly black mold, or if it covers an area larger than 10 square feet, consider consulting a professional. For more details on severe cases, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold).

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not scrape dry mold, as this can release a large number of spores into the air.

### Step 3: Choose Your Cleaning Solution

For porous surfaces like painted drywall, white vinegar is often preferred as it penetrates better than bleach and is less harsh. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. For non-porous surfaces like tiles or sealed concrete, a solution of 1 cup bleach to 1 gallon of water can be effective. Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaners, as this creates toxic fumes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test your chosen cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the wall.

### Step 4: Apply the Cleaning Solution

Generously spray your chosen mold-killing solution directly onto the moldy area. Ensure the entire affected surface is saturated. Allow the solution to sit for at least 10-15 minutes. This dwell time is crucial for the solution to effectively penetrate and kill the mold spores.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn mold, you might need to apply the solution a second time after the initial soak.

### Step 5: Scrub and Clean the Wall

Using a stiff-bristled scrub brush or sponge, gently but firmly scrub the mold from the wall. Work in small sections to ensure thorough cleaning. For painted walls, be careful not to scrub too aggressively, which could damage the paint finish. Wipe away the loosened mold and excess solution with clean microfiber cloths, rinsing the cloths frequently in a separate bucket of clean water.

> 💡 **Tip:** Dispose of contaminated cloths and sponges in a sealed bag immediately after use.

### Step 6: Rinse and Dry Thoroughly

After scrubbing, lightly rinse the cleaned area with plain water using a clean, damp cloth to remove any residue from the cleaning solution. The most critical step is to dry the wall completely to prevent immediate mold regrowth. Use a fan, dehumidifier, or open windows to ensure the wall is thoroughly dry. This process can take several hours.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the relative humidity in the room is below 60% to inhibit mold growth.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Mold Growth

Address the underlying moisture issue that caused the mold in the first place. This could involve fixing leaky pipes, improving ventilation, or managing condensation. Regularly inspect areas prone to moisture and ensure good air circulation. Applying a mold-inhibiting primer before repainting can also offer an extra layer of protection.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using moisture-absorbing products or a dehumidifier in damp areas like bathrooms or basements.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear proper personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, eye protection, and an N95 mask.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes.
- For extensive mold growth (larger than 10 square feet) or recurrent issues, consider hiring a professional mold remediation service.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Exposure to mold can cause allergic reactions, respiratory problems, and other health issues. Take precautions seriously.
- ⚠️ Mold growing on drywall or other porous materials may have roots deep within the material, requiring removal and replacement rather than just surface cleaning.
- ⚠️ If you experience headaches, dizziness, or respiratory distress while cleaning mold, stop immediately and seek fresh air.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you remove mold from walls?**

To remove mold from walls, prepare the area with ventilation and protective gear. Apply a solution of white vinegar or diluted bleach, let it sit, then scrub the mold away. Rinse the area and dry it completely to prevent recurrence.

**How to remove mold from painted walls?**

For painted walls, use a gentler approach to avoid damaging the paint. A solution of equal parts white vinegar and water is effective. Spray, let it soak, gently scrub with a soft brush, and then rinse and dry thoroughly. Always test on an inconspicuous spot first.

**What is the best homemade solution to remove mold from walls?**

White vinegar is highly effective for killing mold on porous and non-porous surfaces. Mix it with an equal amount of water in a spray bottle. For non-porous surfaces, a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach to 1 gallon water) can also be used, but never mix it with vinegar or other cleaners.

**How do you get rid of black mold on walls?**

Small patches of black mold can be treated similarly to other molds using vinegar or bleach solutions. However, black mold can be more hazardous. If the black mold covers a large area or is deep within the wall, it's best to consult a professional for safe and effective remediation. See our guide on [How to Get Rid of Black Mold Safely and Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-black-mold) for more information.

**How to remove mold from inside walls?**

Removing mold from inside walls typically requires opening up the wall to access the affected area. This is a more extensive remediation project and often indicates a significant moisture problem. It's usually best to hire a professional mold remediation specialist to handle mold inside walls due to potential health risks and the complexity of the repair.

**Can mold on walls make you sick?**

Yes, mold exposure can cause various health issues, especially for individuals with allergies, asthma, or compromised immune systems. Symptoms can include respiratory problems, skin irritation, headaches, and nasal congestion. It's crucial to address mold promptly and safely to protect your health.

**How can I prevent mold from returning to my walls?**

To prevent mold recurrence, identify and fix the source of moisture, improve ventilation in damp areas, and maintain indoor humidity levels below 60%. Regularly clean and dry surfaces, especially in bathrooms and kitchens. You can also use mold-inhibiting paints or primers in prone areas.
