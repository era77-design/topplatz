Planting an acorn is a rewarding way to grow a majestic oak tree from scratch. The process involves selecting a viable acorn, providing it with a period of cold stratification, and then planting it in suitable conditions. With a little patience and care, you can witness the beginning of a long-lived tree.

## What You Need

- Viable acorns
- Potting soil (well-draining)
- Planting pot (at least 6-8 inches deep) or garden spade
- Watering can
- Plastic bag (for stratification)
- Refrigerator
- Optional: Wire mesh or screen (for critter protection)

## Steps

### Step 1: Select Healthy Acorns

Gather fresh acorns, ideally shortly after they fall in autumn. Look for plump, firm acorns without any holes, mold, or discoloration, which indicate insect damage or rot. Perform a float test: place acorns in water; those that sink are generally viable, while those that float should be discarded.

### Step 2: Stratify Your Acorns

Most oak species require a period of cold stratification to break dormancy. Place your viable acorns in a plastic bag with a handful of damp (not wet) peat moss, sand, or sawdust. Store the bag in the refrigerator for 60-90 days at temperatures between 34-40°F (1-4°C), ensuring the medium stays consistently moist.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check the acorns periodically for any signs of mold; if found, remove the affected acorn and replace the medium.

### Step 3: Prepare the Planting Site or Pot

If planting directly outdoors, choose a location with well-draining soil and ample sunlight, away from structures or utility lines. For starting indoors, select a pot at least 6-8 inches deep with good drainage holes. Fill the pot with a quality, well-draining potting mix, leaving about an inch of space from the rim.

### Step 4: Plant the Acorn

Once stratified, plant the acorn on its side, about 1-2 inches deep in the prepared soil. The root will emerge from the pointed end, so orienting it on its side or slightly pointed-end down is fine. Gently cover the acorn with soil and lightly pat it down. This step is crucial for success. For more detailed insights on general planting success, refer to [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow an Oak Tree Successfully](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-successfully).

### Step 5: Water and Provide Initial Care

Immediately after planting, water the soil thoroughly until water drains from the bottom of the pot (if using one). Place the pot in a location with bright, indirect sunlight or outdoors in a sheltered spot. Keep the soil consistently moist but not waterlogged.

### Step 6: Protect from Pests and Critters

Young oak sprouts are tempting snacks for squirrels, mice, and other animals. If planting outdoors, consider placing a wire mesh cage or screen over the planting site to protect the emerging seedling. For potted acorns, ensure they are in a secure location. For a comprehensive guide from selection to growth, see [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow a Mighty Oak Tree](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-guide).

### Step 7: Transplanting Your Seedling (Optional)

If you started your acorn in a pot, plan to transplant it to its permanent outdoor location once it has developed a strong root system and a few sets of true leaves, typically after one to two years. Choose a time when the weather is mild, such as early spring or fall, to minimize transplant shock. To fully understand the process of growing an oak tree, including long-term care, read [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow a Mighty Oak Tree](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-for-beginners).


## Tips & Tricks

- Collect more acorns than you intend to plant, as not all will germinate.
- Research your specific oak species to understand its unique stratification and growing requirements.
- Protect young seedlings from harsh sunlight, strong winds, and extreme temperatures during their first year.
- Be patient; germination can take several weeks or even months after stratification.
- Use a deep pot to accommodate the oak's taproot.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Ensure acorns are free from pests before bringing them indoors for stratification.
- ⚠️ Avoid overwatering, which can lead to rot and fungal diseases.
- ⚠️ Do not plant acorns too deep, as this can hinder germination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is the best time to plant an acorn?**

The best time to plant an acorn after stratification is in early spring, once the danger of hard frost has passed. If you are planting directly outdoors without prior stratification, autumn is ideal, allowing nature to stratify the acorn over winter.

**How long does it take for an acorn to sprout?**

After proper stratification and planting, an acorn can take anywhere from a few weeks to several months to sprout. The exact timing depends on the oak species, environmental conditions, and the health of the acorn.

**Do all acorns need stratification?**

Most oak species, particularly those native to temperate climates, require a period of cold stratification to break dormancy. However, some species, especially those from warmer regions, may germinate without it. It's best to research your specific oak type.

**What kind of soil is best for planting acorns?**

Acorns thrive in well-draining soil that mimics their natural forest environment. A good quality potting mix for seedlings, or a blend of loamy soil with some organic matter, is suitable. Avoid heavy clay soils that retain too much water.

**How often should I water a newly planted acorn?**

Keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged. Check the soil moisture daily by feeling the top inch; if it feels dry, it's time to water. Reduce frequency as the seedling establishes, but ensure it doesn't dry out completely.

**Can I plant acorns directly into the ground?**

Yes, you can plant acorns directly into the ground in the fall, allowing them to naturally stratify over winter. However, direct planting increases vulnerability to pests like squirrels and fluctuating environmental conditions, potentially reducing germination success.

**What should I do if my acorn doesn't sprout?**

If your acorn doesn't sprout after a reasonable period, it might not have been viable, or the stratification process was incomplete. Try planting more acorns, ensuring they are fresh, pass the float test, and receive adequate cold treatment and consistent moisture.
