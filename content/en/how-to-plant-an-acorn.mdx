To plant an acorn, start by selecting viable acorns and performing a float test. Stratify them if necessary, then plant them in a suitable pot or directly in the ground, ensuring proper depth and moisture. With patience and consistent care, your acorn can grow into a majestic oak.

## What You Need

- Healthy acorns
- Bowl of water
- Potting soil or garden soil
- Small pots with drainage holes (if starting indoors)
- Shovel or trowel
- Watering can
- Mesh or wire cage (optional, for squirrel protection)
- Peat moss or sand (for stratification)

## Steps

### Step 1: Gather and Inspect Healthy Acorns

Collect acorns directly from under an oak tree, ideally in the fall when they are ripe. Look for plump, firm acorns without any holes, cracks, or mold, as these can indicate pest damage or disease. Different oak species have different germination requirements, so identify your oak if possible. Starting with the best quality acorns significantly increases your chances of success in growing an oak tree. For more detailed guidance on selecting the best acorns, refer to our article on [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow an Oak Tree Successfully](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-successfully).

> 💡 **Tip:** Collect more acorns than you plan to plant, as not all will be viable or germinate.

### Step 2: Perform a Viability Float Test

Place your collected acorns in a bowl of water. Healthy, viable acorns will sink to the bottom, indicating they are dense and likely contain a healthy embryo. Acorns that float are often hollow, damaged, or infested with weevils and should be discarded. This simple test helps you select the strongest candidates for planting, saving you time and effort on non-viable seeds.

### Step 3: Stratify Your Acorns (If Needed)

Many oak species require a period of cold stratification to break dormancy and encourage germination, mimicking natural winter conditions. Place viable acorns in a plastic bag with moist (not wet) peat moss or sand, then store them in the refrigerator (at 34-40°F or 1-4°C) for 60-90 days. Check periodically to ensure the medium remains moist and there's no mold. Some acorns may even sprout a taproot during this period, indicating they are ready for planting.

### Step 4: Choose Your Planting Method and Location

Decide whether to plant your acorn directly in the ground or start it in a pot. For direct planting, select a spot with well-draining soil, full to partial sunlight, and ample space for a mature oak tree. If using a pot, choose one at least 6-8 inches deep with good drainage holes, filled with a high-quality potting mix. Starting in a pot allows for better control over the seedling's early environment. For beginners, starting in a pot can be a great way to ensure the best conditions, as detailed in [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow a Mighty Oak Tree](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-for-beginners).

### Step 5: Plant the Acorn Properly

Whether in a pot or the ground, plant the acorn on its side, about 1-2 inches deep. The depth should be roughly twice the diameter of the acorn. If you notice a small root (radicle) emerging from stratification, orient the acorn so the root points downwards. Gently cover the acorn with soil and lightly pat it down. Proper planting depth and orientation are key for successful root development and emergence of the shoot, as explained in [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow a Mighty Oak Tree](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn-properly).

### Step 6: Provide Essential Post-Planting Care

Immediately after planting, water the soil thoroughly until it's evenly moist but not waterlogged. Maintain consistent moisture, especially during dry periods, but allow the top inch of soil to dry out slightly between waterings to prevent rot. Protect your newly planted acorn from pests like squirrels, which are notorious for digging up acorns. A wire mesh cage or plastic bottle with the bottom cut out can offer effective protection while allowing light and air circulation.

### Step 7: Monitor Growth and Consider Transplanting

Once planted, be patient. Germination can take weeks to several months, depending on the oak species and environmental conditions. Look for a small sprout emerging from the soil. If you started your acorn in a pot, plan to transplant the seedling to its permanent outdoor location once it has developed a strong root system and is about 6-12 inches tall, typically after one growing season. Transplant carefully to minimize root disturbance.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always plant more acorns than you need, as not all will germinate or survive.
- Protect young seedlings from squirrels, deer, and other pests immediately after planting.
- Ensure good drainage in pots and planting sites to prevent root rot.
- Be patient; oak trees grow slowly, and germination can take time.
- Use native oak species for your region to ensure better adaptation and ecological benefit.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not overwater your acorns or seedlings, as this can lead to rot and fungal diseases.
- ⚠️ Avoid using fertilizers on newly planted acorns; they have enough stored energy for initial growth.
- ⚠️ Ensure any protective barriers do not restrict the seedling's growth or air circulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is the best time to plant an acorn?**

The best time to plant an acorn is in the fall, shortly after they drop from the tree. This allows them to undergo natural cold stratification over winter, preparing them for spring germination. If you've stratified them artificially, you can plant them in early spring.

**How deep should I plant an acorn?**

Plant acorns approximately 1 to 2 inches deep, or roughly twice the diameter of the acorn itself. This depth provides adequate protection while allowing the shoot to emerge without too much difficulty.

**How long does it take for an acorn to sprout?**

The time it takes for an acorn to sprout varies significantly by oak species and environmental conditions. Some can sprout within a few weeks after stratification, while others might take several months, often emerging in the spring after a winter dormancy period.

**What kind of soil is best for planting acorns?**

Oak trees generally prefer well-draining, loamy soil. If planting in a pot, use a quality potting mix. For direct ground planting, ensure the soil isn't compacted and drains well to prevent waterlogging, which can cause the acorn to rot.

**How much water does an acorn need?**

After planting, water the acorn thoroughly. Subsequently, keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged. Allow the top inch of soil to dry out slightly between waterings to prevent fungal issues and root rot, especially for potted acorns.

**Can I plant an acorn directly in the ground?**

Yes, you can plant an acorn directly in the ground, provided you choose a suitable location with good soil and sunlight. Ensure it's protected from pests like squirrels, which are notorious for digging up freshly planted acorns.

**Why aren't my acorns sprouting?**

Several factors can prevent acorns from sprouting, including non-viable acorns (failed float test), insufficient cold stratification, improper planting depth, inconsistent moisture (too dry or too wet), or pest damage. Patience is also key, as some species take longer to germinate.
