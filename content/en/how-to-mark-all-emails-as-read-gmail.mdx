To mark all emails as read in Gmail, first navigate to your Gmail inbox. Then, use the search bar to filter for unread messages, select all conversations matching your search, and finally click the 'Mark as read' icon. This process helps you quickly clear your inbox and manage your emails more effectively, whether you're a new user or a long-time Gmail enthusiast.

## What You Need

- Computer or mobile device
- Internet connection
- Gmail account

## Steps

### Step 1: Access Your Gmail Inbox

Open your web browser and go to mail.google.com, then log in with your Gmail credentials. If you're using the Gmail mobile app, simply open the app on your device. Ensure you're logged into the correct account where you wish to mark emails as read. If you're new to Gmail and haven't set up an account yet, you might want to learn [How to Create a Gmail Account Quickly and Easily](/en/how-to-create-gmail-account) first.

### Step 2: Search for Unread Emails

In the search bar at the top of your Gmail interface, type 'is:unread' and press Enter. This will filter your inbox to display only messages that you have not yet opened or marked as read. For mobile users, locate the search icon within the app and type the same query.

### Step 3: Select Visible Unread Emails

Above your list of emails, you'll see a small checkbox. Click this checkbox to select all the emails currently visible on the page. Typically, Gmail displays 50 conversations per page by default. This action is the first step in preparing your emails for bulk action.

### Step 4: Select All Matching Conversations

After clicking the checkbox, a banner will appear just above your email list, stating something like 'All 50 conversations on this page are selected.' Crucially, it will also include a link that says, 'Select all conversations that match this search.' Click this link to extend your selection beyond the current page, encompassing every unread email in your entire inbox or search results.

### Step 5: Mark Emails as Read

Once all unread emails are selected, look for the 'Mark as read' icon in the toolbar above your emails. It typically looks like an open envelope. Click this icon to change the status of all selected emails from unread to read. This is the primary action that will clear your unread count.

### Step 6: Confirm the Action

Gmail may ask you to confirm this bulk action, especially if you're marking a large number of emails as read. A small pop-up or notification might appear asking 'Confirm bulk action?' Click 'OK' or 'Confirm' to proceed. This ensures that the change is applied across all selected emails.

### Step 7: Verify Your Inbox

After confirming, your unread email count should drop significantly, or even to zero, depending on how many unread emails you had. You can refresh your browser or check your inbox again to confirm that the changes have been applied successfully. Your inbox should now appear much tidier.


## Tips & Tricks

- Use the 'is:unread' search operator frequently to keep track of new messages.
- If you only want to mark emails from a specific sender as read, combine 'from:sender@example.com is:unread' in the search bar.
- Regularly marking emails as read can contribute to 'Inbox Zero,' a popular email management strategy.
- Remember that marking as read does not archive or delete emails; they simply lose their 'unread' status.
- You can also use the 'Mark as read' option on individual emails by right-clicking them or selecting them and using the toolbar icon.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Ensure you have selected 'Select all conversations that match this search' to mark all unread emails, not just those visible on the current page. Accidentally performing another bulk action like archiving or deleting could remove emails you still need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does 'mark all emails as read' actually do?**

Marking all emails as read changes their status from unread to read. This means the bold formatting typically associated with unread messages disappears, and they no longer contribute to your unread email count. It doesn't archive, delete, or move them.

**Can I mark emails as read on the Gmail mobile app?**

Yes, you can. On the mobile app, use the search bar to find 'is:unread'. Then, long-press one email to select it, tap the checkbox at the top to select all visible, and then look for the three-dot menu or an icon to 'Mark as read'. The 'select all conversations that match this search' feature is less prominent on mobile, so you might need to scroll and select multiple pages.

**Will marking emails as read delete them?**

No, marking emails as read will not delete them. It only changes their status. Your emails will remain in your inbox or their respective labels/folders. If you wish to delete emails, you need to specifically choose the 'Delete' option.

**What if I accidentally mark an important email as read?**

If you accidentally mark an important email as read, you can easily find it and mark it as unread again. Simply search for the email, open it, and then click the 'Mark as unread' icon (often an envelope with a downward arrow or an unopened envelope symbol) in the toolbar. You can also right-click the email and select 'Mark as unread'.

**Why would I want to mark all emails as read?**

Marking all emails as read is often done to achieve 'Inbox Zero,' reduce visual clutter, and reset your unread count. It's useful after a vacation, a busy period, or when you simply want a fresh start, ensuring that only truly new incoming emails appear as unread.

**Is there a way to mark specific categories or labels as read?**

Yes, you can combine the 'is:unread' search operator with other filters. For example, to mark all unread emails in your 'Promotions' tab, you could search for 'category:promotions is:unread'. Similarly, for a custom label, use 'label:yourlabelname is:unread' then apply the bulk mark as read action.

**Does this work for emails in other folders like 'Sent' or 'Spam'?**

The 'is:unread' search operator generally works across all your Gmail folders and labels. However, emails in 'Sent' are typically sent by you and are never 'unread' in the traditional sense. For 'Spam' emails, Gmail usually handles their status automatically, but you could technically apply the same method if needed, though it's rarely necessary.
