To mark all emails as read in Gmail, use the search bar to filter for "is:unread", then select all visible emails. If you have more, click "Select all conversations that match this search" and finally use the "Mark as read" button. This process allows you to quickly clear your unread count across your entire inbox.

## What You Need

- An active Google Account with Gmail access (If you need to set one up, learn [How to Create a Gmail Account Quickly and Easily](/en/how-to-create-gmail-account))
- An internet connection
- A computer or mobile device

## Steps

### Step 1: Open Gmail in Your Web Browser

Navigate to mail.google.com and log in to your Google Account if prompted. Ensure you are viewing your primary inbox or the specific label/category where you wish to mark emails as read. This is the starting point for managing your email status.

> 💡 **Tip:** Make sure you are logged into the correct Gmail account if you manage multiple.

### Step 2: Search for Unread Emails

In the search bar located at the very top of your Gmail interface, type `is:unread` and press Enter. This powerful search operator will filter your entire inbox, displaying only those emails that are currently marked as unread, regardless of their location (inbox, promotions, social, etc.).

> 💡 **Tip:** For more specific results, you can use `is:unread in:inbox` to only show unread emails in your main inbox.

### Step 3: Select All Visible Unread Emails

Locate the small checkbox at the top-left corner of your email list, positioned just above the first displayed email. Click this checkbox once. This action will select all unread emails that are currently visible on the page, typically up to 50 or 100 conversations.

> 💡 **Tip:** Double-check that you've selected the correct checkbox, as there are often others for individual emails.

### Step 4: Select All Matching Conversations (If Applicable)

If you have more unread emails than can be shown on a single page, a message will appear just above your email list. It will state something like, "All 50 conversations on this page are selected. Select all [number] conversations that match this search." Click the blue text within this message to extend your selection to *all* unread emails found by your search query, not just those on the current page.

> 💡 **Tip:** This step is crucial for marking a large volume of emails as read in one go.

### Step 5: Mark Emails as Read

After selecting all your desired unread emails, look for the "Mark as read" icon in the toolbar located just above your email list. This icon typically looks like an open envelope. Click this icon to change the status of all selected emails from unread to read.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you don't see the 'Mark as read' icon, it might be under the 'More' menu (three vertical dots) depending on your Gmail view.

### Step 6: Confirm the Action

A small notification will usually appear at the bottom-left of your screen, confirming that the conversations have been marked as read. Your unread count, often displayed next to your inbox or labels, should now reflect the change, or disappear entirely if all emails are marked read. This indicates your task is complete.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the count doesn't update immediately, try refreshing your browser page.


## Tips & Tricks

- Use specific search operators like `is:unread in:inbox` or `is:unread category:promotions` to target unread emails in particular sections of your Gmail.
- Regularly archiving or deleting old emails can prevent your inbox from becoming overwhelmed, reducing the need for bulk 'mark as read' actions.
- If you accidentally mark an important email as read, you can quickly find it again by searching with keywords or sender, then marking it as unread.
- Consider using Gmail's "Categories" (Primary, Social, Promotions) or custom "Labels" to better organize incoming mail and reduce the perceived clutter of your main inbox.
- For new users, understanding Gmail's interface can be key to efficient management. Take time to explore settings and features.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Be cautious when performing bulk actions. Ensure you have selected only the emails you intend to mark as read to avoid accidentally archiving or deleting important messages.
- ⚠️ Marking all emails as read might cause you to overlook genuinely new or important communications that require your immediate attention, especially if you rely on the unread indicator.
- ⚠️ This action is generally not reversible for a very large number of emails if you later realize you needed to review them individually.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I mark all emails as read on the Gmail mobile app?**

Yes, the process is similar on the Gmail mobile app. Search for `is:unread`, then long-press one email to select it, tap the checkbox at the top to select all, and finally tap the three-dot menu or relevant icon to 'Mark as read'.

**What if I only want to mark unread emails in a specific folder or label?**

You can combine search operators for this. For example, to mark unread emails only in your 'Promotions' tab, search for `is:unread category:promotions`. For a custom label, use `is:unread label:YourLabelName`.

**Will marking all emails as read delete them or archive them?**

No, marking emails as read only changes their status from unread to read. It does not delete or archive them; they will remain in your inbox or their respective labels, just without the unread indicator.

**How do I find emails I've accidentally marked as read?**

You can use the search bar with specific keywords, sender names, or dates to locate them. If you know roughly when it arrived, you can search `after:YYYY/MM/DD before:YYYY/MM/DD is:read` to narrow down your search results.

**Is there a way to automatically mark certain incoming emails as read?**

Yes, you can set up Gmail filters to do this. Go to Settings > See all settings > Filters and Blocked Addresses, then create a new filter specifying criteria for incoming emails and choose the action 'Mark as read'.

**Why does my unread count still show emails after I marked them all as read?**

This might happen if you have emails in other categories (like Promotions or Social) or in other labels that weren't included in your initial `is:unread` search. Ensure your search is broad enough (e.g., just `is:unread`) to cover all locations, or repeat the process for specific categories.
