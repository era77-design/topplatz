To mark all emails as read in Gmail, use the search bar to filter for unread messages, select all displayed conversations, and then choose the 'Mark as read' option from the toolbar. This process quickly clears your unread count, making it easier to manage a busy inbox.

## What You Need

- Computer or mobile device
- Internet access
- Gmail account

## Steps

### Step 1: Access Your Gmail Account

First, open your preferred web browser (like Chrome, Firefox, or Safari) and navigate to mail.google.com. Log in with your Google account credentials if you aren't already signed in. If you're a new user or need to set up an account, you can find a comprehensive guide on [How to Create a New Gmail Account - Step-by-Step Guide](/en/how-to-create-new-gmail-account).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure you're logging into the correct Gmail account, especially if you manage multiple inboxes.

### Step 2: Search for Unread Emails

In the Gmail search bar at the top of the screen, type 'is:unread' and press Enter or click the magnifying glass icon. This action will filter your entire inbox, displaying only the emails that are currently marked as unread. This is the crucial first step to gather all unread messages in one view.

> 💡 **Tip:** You can combine 'is:unread' with other search terms, like 'is:unread label:promotions', to narrow down your search.

### Step 3: Select All Displayed Unread Conversations

Above your list of unread emails, you'll see a small checkbox. Click this checkbox to select all the emails currently visible on the page (usually 50 conversations). This acts as a 'select all' for the current view, preparing them for the next action.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you only want to mark a few specific emails as read, manually click the checkbox next to each individual email instead of the main 'select all' box.

### Step 4: Select All Unread Messages (Across Pages)

If you have more than 50 unread emails, after clicking the 'select all' checkbox, a message will appear just above your email list stating, 'All 50 conversations on this page are selected.' Crucially, it will also offer a link: 'Select all conversations that match this search.' Click this link to extend your selection to *all* unread emails in your entire inbox, not just those visible on the current page.

> 💡 **Tip:** This step is vital for clearing a large backlog of unread emails quickly. Don't skip it if you have hundreds or thousands of unread messages.

### Step 5: Mark Selected Emails as Read

Once all your unread emails are selected (either on the page or across all pages), look for the toolbar located just above your email list. Click the 'More' (three vertical dots) icon, or sometimes a direct 'Mark as read' icon (an open envelope) might be visible. From the dropdown menu, select 'Mark as read.'

> 💡 **Tip:** Gmail might ask for confirmation if you're marking a very large number of emails as read. Confirm to proceed.

### Step 6: Verify Your Inbox Status

After clicking 'Mark as read,' Gmail will process the action. You should see a confirmation message, and your unread count should significantly decrease or disappear entirely. You can remove the 'is:unread' search filter to return to your normal inbox view and confirm that your unread count is now zero, or much lower.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you still see unread emails, repeat the process. Sometimes, emails in other categories (like Promotions or Social) might not be caught by a general 'is:unread' search if you're primarily in your Primary tab.


## Tips & Tricks

- Regularly manage your inbox to prevent a large buildup of unread emails. Consider unsubscribing from unwanted newsletters.
- Use Gmail's categories (Primary, Social, Promotions, Updates, Forums) to organize incoming mail and tackle unread messages in batches.
- Marking emails as read doesn't archive or delete them. They will remain in your inbox or their respective labels.
- For specific unread emails, you can select them individually and then use the 'Mark as read' option.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always double-check your selection before marking a large number of emails as read, especially if you might have important unread messages you haven't reviewed yet.
- ⚠️ Marking as read is generally irreversible in terms of the 'unread' status, though you can manually mark individual emails as unread again if needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I undo marking emails as read?**

Once emails are marked as read, you cannot universally 'undo' the action for all of them at once. However, you can manually select individual emails and mark them as unread again if you realize you missed something important.

**Does marking emails as read delete them?**

No, marking emails as read does not delete them. It simply changes their status from 'unread' to 'read.' The emails will remain in your inbox or their respective labels until you choose to archive or delete them.

**Can I mark emails as read on the Gmail mobile app?**

Yes, the Gmail mobile app allows you to mark multiple emails as read. Tap and hold an email to select it, then tap additional emails. Once selected, look for the 'Mark as read' icon (usually an open envelope) or a three-dot menu with the option.

**Why do I have so many unread emails?**

A high number of unread emails often results from subscribing to many newsletters, receiving frequent notifications, or simply not checking your inbox regularly. Consider unsubscribing from unnecessary lists and using Gmail's filtering features.

**How do I mark only specific emails as read?**

To mark only specific emails as read, simply check the box next to each individual email you wish to mark. Once selected, click the 'Mark as read' icon or choose the option from the 'More' menu.

**What's the difference between 'read' and 'archived'?**

'Read' status simply indicates you've viewed an email, but it remains in your inbox. 'Archived' moves an email out of your inbox into the 'All Mail' folder, effectively hiding it from your primary view without deleting it. An archived email can still be unread or read.

**Can I automate marking emails as read?**

Gmail's filters allow you to automate certain actions. You can create a filter that automatically marks specific incoming emails as read based on sender, subject, or keywords. This is useful for newsletters or notifications you don't need to actively read.
