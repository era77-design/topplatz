To get rid of cat pee smell, immediately blot the affected area to remove as much urine as possible, then clean thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner. These specialized cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, eliminating it at its source rather than just masking it.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean absorbent cloths
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor remover
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Black light (UV flashlight, optional for finding hidden spots)
- Stiff brush (for carpets/upholstery)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil

## Steps

### Step 1: Act Quickly and Blot the Area

The faster you act, the easier it is to remove the smell. Put on rubber gloves and immediately blot the fresh urine stain with several layers of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth. Press down firmly to soak up as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

### Step 2: Locate All Affected Areas

Even if you see one spot, there might be others. Use your nose or, for dried or hidden stains, a black light (UV flashlight) in a darkened room. Urine stains will glow a yellowish-green under the UV light, making them visible on carpets, upholstery, and even walls. Mark these areas for thorough treatment.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with Household Solutions (Optional)

For initial odor reduction, you can pre-treat the area. Mix a solution of one part white vinegar to one part water and spray it onto the affected area, allowing it to sit for 10-15 minutes before blotting. Alternatively, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the damp spot and let it sit for several hours or overnight to absorb odors, then vacuum it up.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

This is the most crucial step for permanent odor removal. Thoroughly saturate the affected area with a high-quality enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Allow the cleaner to sit for the time recommended on its label (often 10-15 minutes or longer) to allow the enzymes to break down the uric acid crystals. For a deeper dive into achieving permanent odor removal, you can refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

After the enzymatic cleaner has worked its magic, blot up any excess liquid and allow the area to air dry completely. This can take several hours or even a day or two, depending on humidity and ventilation. To prevent your cat from returning to the spot while it's drying, cover it with plastic sheeting, aluminum foil, or an upside-down laundry basket.

### Step 6: Re-evaluate and Repeat if Necessary

Once the area is fully dry, check for any lingering odor. If a faint smell remains, it means not all the uric acid crystals were neutralized. You may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process, ensuring even deeper saturation this time. Patience is key for stubborn odors.

### Step 7: Address the Root Cause of Urination

To prevent future accidents, it's vital to understand why your cat is urinating outside the litter box. This could be due to medical issues (like a UTI), stress, an unclean litter box, or dissatisfaction with the litter type or location. Consult your veterinarian to rule out health problems and ensure your cat's environment meets their needs.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ensure good ventilation in the room while cleaning and drying to help dissipate odors and speed up the drying process.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the same spot.
- Consider professional cleaning for severe or long-standing urine stains, especially on carpets or upholstery, as the urine may have soaked into padding or subflooring.
- If the pee is on washable items, pre-soak them in a solution of enzymatic cleaner or white vinegar before washing on a cold cycle.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning products, especially those containing ammonia with bleach, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves to protect your hands from urine and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and any cleaning residues have been removed or are safe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so strong and linger?**

The strong odor comes from uric acid crystals in cat urine that don't dissolve in water. As these crystals break down, they release pungent mercaptans, especially when exposed to moisture, causing the notorious lingering smell.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and how does it work for cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine, eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it with fragrance.

**Can I use household bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat urine. Bleach can react with ammonia in urine to create toxic fumes, and it doesn't effectively remove the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering smell, potentially making it worse.

**How can I find old, dried cat urine spots that I can't see?**

Use a UV or black light in a darkened room. Urine stains will glow a distinctive yellowish-green under the UV light, making them easy to identify on carpets, walls, and furniture even when they are invisible to the naked eye.

**What if the cat pee smell persists after cleaning?**

If the smell persists, it likely means not all the uric acid crystals were neutralized. You may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process, ensuring thorough saturation, or consider professional cleaning for odors deeply embedded in materials like carpet padding or subflooring.

**Is cat pee smell harmful to humans?**

While not directly toxic in small amounts, prolonged exposure to strong cat urine odors can be irritating to the respiratory system, especially for those with asthma or allergies. The ammonia can cause discomfort and headaches.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box in the future?**

First, consult a vet to rule out medical issues. Then, ensure the litter box is clean (scoop daily), has enough boxes (one per cat plus one extra), is in a quiet location, and try different litter types.
