To get rid of cat pee smell, immediately blot up as much urine as possible, then thoroughly saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner. Allow the cleaner to air dry completely, as these specialized solutions break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- Rubber gloves
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Spray bottle (optional)
- Black light (optional, for finding dried stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Blot the Stain Immediately

The key to successful odor removal is acting fast. Use paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth to blot up as much fresh urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the stain and push the urine deeper into fibers. For older, dried stains, a black light can help you pinpoint the exact location in a dark room.

### Step 2: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner Generously

Once the excess urine is removed, liberally apply an enzymatic pet odor cleaner to the affected area. These specialized cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are responsible for the strong, lingering smell. Ensure the cleaner soaks deep into any carpet padding or upholstery to reach all urine.

### Step 3: Allow the Area to Air Dry Completely

Do not rinse the enzymatic cleaner immediately. The enzymes need time to work their magic, often requiring several hours or even overnight. Cover the treated area with a clean towel or plastic wrap to prevent your pet from re-soiling it while it dries. Air circulation can help speed up the drying process. ### Step 4: Repeat Treatment If Necessary

For stubborn or old stains, a single treatment might not be enough. If you still detect a faint odor after the area is completely dry, repeat the application of the enzymatic cleaner. Persistence is key to completely eradicating the smell, especially in areas that have been repeatedly soiled. ### Step 5: Address Lingering Odors with Household Solutions

After enzymatic treatment, if a faint odor persists, sprinkle baking soda over the dry area and let it sit for several hours or overnight before vacuuming. For hard surfaces, a solution of one part white vinegar to one part water can be used to wipe down the area, followed by a clean water rinse. Always test these solutions on an inconspicuous spot first.

### Step 6: Prevent Future Accidents

To prevent recurrence, thoroughly clean and sanitize the litter box regularly and ensure you have enough litter boxes for the number of cats you own (one per cat plus one extra). Identify and address any underlying medical or behavioral issues with your veterinarian. Placing a deterrent like aluminum foil on previously soiled spots can also discourage your cat from returning.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness.
- Use a black light to locate dried, invisible urine stains, especially in carpets and upholstery.
- Consider professional cleaning for very old or deeply set stains in carpets or upholstered furniture.
- Ensure proper litter box hygiene to prevent future accidents and reduce stress for your cat.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell is similar to urine and can attract your cat back to the spot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and don't dissolve easily in water. When these crystals dry, they reactivate with moisture in the air, releasing a strong, pungent ammonia-like odor that can persist for a long time.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat pee?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat pee. Mixing bleach with ammonia (found in urine) can create toxic chlorine gas, which is very dangerous to inhale. Bleach also doesn't effectively break down uric acid crystals, so the smell may return.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work on cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into harmless, odorless compounds, effectively eliminating the source of the smell rather than just masking it.

**What if the smell is in my mattress or couch?**

For mattresses and couches, blot as much urine as possible, then saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak deep into the material. You may need to press down with towels to absorb the cleaner and urine. Allow it to air dry completely, repeating if necessary.

**How can I find old cat pee stains I can't see?**

The most effective way to find old, dried cat pee stains is by using a UV or black light. Turn off all other lights in the room, and the urine stains will glow a dull yellowish-green, making them easy to spot even on dark surfaces.

**Will the cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with proper and persistent use of enzymatic cleaners, the cat pee smell can be completely eliminated. These cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor, ensuring it doesn't reactivate and the smell is gone permanently.

**My cat keeps peeing in the same spot, what should I do?**

Thoroughly clean the spot with an enzymatic cleaner to remove all scent traces. Then, address potential reasons for repeat soiling: consult a vet to rule out medical issues, ensure litter box cleanliness and sufficient boxes, and consider behavioral deterrents or professional advice.
