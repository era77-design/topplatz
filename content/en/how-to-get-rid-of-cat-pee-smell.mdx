To get rid of cat pee smell, promptly locate and blot the soiled area, then use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine. Allow the cleaner to air dry completely to break down uric acid crystals and neutralize the odor at its source.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar (optional, for pre-treatment)
- Baking soda
- Stiff brush (for carpets/upholstery)
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating dried stains)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Smell

Begin by identifying all affected areas. Cat urine can seep into carpets, upholstery, wood, and even walls, making the smell pervasive. If the stain is old or dry, a UV blacklight can be incredibly helpful in revealing hidden urine spots that are invisible to the naked eye. This initial step is critical for effective treatment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check less obvious places like baseboards, furniture legs, and under rugs, as cats often spray or urinate in secluded spots.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh urine, act fast! Place several layers of paper towels or an old, absorbent cloth over the wet spot and press down firmly to soak up as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. Repeat this process until no more moisture transfers to the towels.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels for a few seconds to maximize absorption, especially on carpets.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Pet Odor Cleaner

Once the area is blotted, generously saturate the affected spot with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor cleaner. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Follow the product instructions carefully regarding application and dwell time. For a comprehensive guide on effective solutions, you might find this article helpful: [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: Effective Solutions](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did, especially in carpets or thick upholstery, for thorough odor neutralization.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Air Dry Completely

Do not rush this step. For the enzymes to fully work and neutralize the odor, the treated area must air dry completely. This can take several hours, or even a day or two, depending on the humidity and the material. Covering the area with plastic wrap can help keep the cleaner wet longer, allowing the enzymes more time to act.

> 💡 **Tip:** Open windows or use fans to improve air circulation and aid in drying, which also helps dissipate any remaining odors.

### Step 5: Rinse and Extract (If Necessary)

After the enzymatic cleaner has dried, some products may recommend a light rinse with water to remove any residue. If you have a wet vac or carpet cleaner, this is an excellent opportunity to extract any remaining moisture and cleaner from carpets or upholstery. Ensure the area dries thoroughly again to prevent mold or mildew.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always check your cleaner's instructions; some enzymatic cleaners are designed to be left to dry without rinsing.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional but Recommended)

For an extra layer of odor protection, once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any lingering smells. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, before vacuuming it up. For persistent odors, you might need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process, as discussed in [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** For tough odors on fabric, gently work the baking soda into the fibers with a brush before letting it sit.

### Step 7: Address Persistent Odors or Large Areas

If the smell persists after multiple treatments, especially in porous materials like wood subflooring, drywall, or very old, deeply saturated carpets, professional cleaning or even replacement of affected materials might be necessary. Sometimes the urine has seeped too deeply for topical treatments to be fully effective. For quick and effective solutions, consider reviewing [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast).

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider sealing subfloors with an odor-blocking primer after cleaning if replacement isn't an option for severe saturation.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness and prevent damage.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell is similar to urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- Address the root cause of inappropriate urination with your vet or a feline behaviorist to prevent future accidents.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves to protect your hands from urine and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation by opening windows or using fans when using cleaning products.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and safe according to product instructions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that are very difficult to break down with water alone. These crystals reactivate with moisture, causing the smell to return even after cleaning. Enzymatic cleaners are essential to neutralize them permanently.

**Can I use homemade remedies like vinegar or baking soda?**

While white vinegar and baking soda can help with general deodorizing and can be used as a pre-treatment or post-treatment, they are usually not sufficient on their own to eliminate cat urine smell permanently. They don't break down uric acid crystals like enzymatic cleaners do.

**What if the smell is in my hardwood floor?**

For hardwood, clean the surface thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner designed for hard surfaces. If the urine has soaked into the wood, you might need to sand and reseal the affected boards, or in severe cases, replace the wood entirely.

**How do I find old, dried cat urine stains?**

A UV blacklight (also known as a 'blacklight flashlight') is the most effective tool for locating old, dried cat urine stains. In a darkened room, the uric acid crystals in the urine will fluoresce under the UV light, appearing as yellowish or greenish spots.

**How long does it take for the smell to go away?**

With proper enzymatic cleaning and thorough drying, the smell should dissipate within a few days to a week. If the smell persists, it usually indicates that the cleaner didn't penetrate deep enough or that there are still untreated areas.

**Will the smell attract my cat back to the spot?**

Yes, if the urine smell isn't completely eliminated, your cat may be drawn back to the same spot to urinate again, as it marks their territory. This is why thorough cleaning with an enzymatic cleaner is crucial to break the cycle.

**When should I call a professional cleaner?**

You should consider calling a professional cleaner if the urine has saturated large areas, seeped into subflooring or walls, or if multiple attempts with enzymatic cleaners haven't fully removed the odor. Professionals have stronger equipment and specialized solutions.
