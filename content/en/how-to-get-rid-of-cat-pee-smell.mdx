To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, you must neutralize the uric acid crystals left behind by cat urine, not just mask the odor. This involves using specialized enzymatic cleaners designed to break down these crystals, followed by thorough drying and proper ventilation to ensure the smell is permanently eliminated from your home.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar (optional for pre-treatment)
- Baking soda
- Plastic gloves
- UV black light (optional but highly recommended)
- Scrub brush (for carpets)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional)
- Fan or open windows

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Urine Source

Before you can treat the odor, you need to find all the affected areas. Use a UV black light in a darkened room to easily spot dried urine stains, which will glow yellowish-green. Don't forget to check vertical surfaces like walls or furniture legs, as cats sometimes spray.

> 💡 **Tip:** Even if you think you know where the accident happened, a UV light can reveal hidden or older stains you might otherwise miss.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, the quicker you act, the better. Place several layers of paper towels or old rags over the wet spot and press down firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into carpets or upholstery fibers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels to apply maximum pressure, changing them frequently until no more moisture transfers.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are crucial because they contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering cat pee smell. Saturate the soiled area generously with the cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully for dwell time.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn or old stains, you might need to leave the enzymatic cleaner on for several hours or even overnight, keeping the area moist. For a truly lasting solution, learn more about how to get rid of cat pee smell permanently with enzymatic treatments: [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently).

### Step 4: Allow to Air Dry Completely

After the recommended dwell time, blot up any excess cleaner and allow the treated area to air dry completely. This step is vital, as the enzymes continue to work as long as the area is damp. Using fans or opening windows can significantly speed up the drying process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not rush the drying process; if the area remains damp, the odor may not be fully neutralized, and mold could develop.

### Step 5: Apply Baking Soda (Optional)

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural odor absorber and can help draw out any remaining faint smells. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, to maximize its effectiveness.

> 💡 **Tip:** For extra absorption, cover the baking soda with a towel and place a heavy object on top. This helps press the baking soda deeper into the fibers.

### Step 6: Vacuum Thoroughly and Repeat if Necessary

After the baking soda has had time to work, vacuum it up completely. Sniff the area to check if the odor has been fully eliminated. For older, more saturated, or persistent stains, you may need to repeat the entire cleaning process one or more times to achieve complete odor removal. For comprehensive strategies, explore [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-effectively).

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't be discouraged if the smell isn't completely gone after the first attempt; old urine stains often require multiple treatments.

### Step 7: Address Underlying Causes and Ventilate

Once the immediate area is clean, consider why your cat is urinating outside the litter box and address any behavioral or medical issues. Ensure good ventilation in your home to refresh the air. Continuously ventilating can help remove any lingering airborne odor particles and improve overall air quality.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consult your veterinarian to rule out any medical conditions if your cat suddenly starts urinating inappropriately.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the material.
- Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fibers.
- For hard surfaces like tile or linoleum, a solution of one part white vinegar to one part water can be used after blotting to neutralize odors, followed by an enzymatic cleaner.
- Consider professional carpet or upholstery cleaning for very old, widespread, or deeply set urine stains.
- Ensure your cat's litter box is clean and accessible, and consider adding an extra litter box if you have multiple cats or a large home.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning products, especially ammonia-based cleaners with bleach, as this can create toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using any cleaning products, and keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry.
- ⚠️ Avoid using products that contain ammonia, as its smell can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Wear gloves to protect your hands when handling cat urine and cleaning products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals that are very difficult to break down with regular cleaners. These crystals react with bacteria, creating mercaptans, which are responsible for the strong, pungent odor that can reactivate with humidity.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat pee?**

No, you should never use bleach on cat pee. Not only can it create harmful fumes when mixed with urine, but it also won't break down the uric acid crystals, making it ineffective for permanent odor removal and potentially damaging surfaces.

**How can I find old, dried cat pee stains?**

The most effective way to find old, dried cat pee stains is by using a UV black light in a dark room. The uric acid crystals in dried urine will fluoresce, appearing as a yellowish-green glow on surfaces that look clean in normal light.

**What if the cat pee smell is in my carpet pad or subfloor?**

If the urine has soaked into the carpet pad or subfloor, simply treating the carpet surface won't be enough. You might need to lift the carpet, clean the pad and subfloor with enzymatic cleaner, or in severe cases, replace the affected sections of pad or seal the subfloor.

**Will the cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with the correct approach using enzymatic cleaners, the smell can be permanently eliminated. These cleaners break down the uric acid crystals at a molecular level, removing the source of the odor rather than just masking it.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Prevention involves ruling out medical issues with a vet visit, ensuring multiple clean litter boxes in accessible locations, using preferred litter, and reducing stress in your cat's environment. Scent deterrents or moving food bowls to previously soiled areas can also help.

**Is white vinegar effective for cat pee odors?**

White vinegar can help neutralize some of the alkaline salts in cat urine and reduce odor temporarily, but it doesn't break down uric acid crystals. For complete and permanent removal, an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine is essential.
