To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly to blot up fresh urine, then saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner to break down uric acid crystals. For dried stains, pre-treat with a vinegar solution or baking soda before applying the enzymatic cleaner.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar (optional, for pre-treatment)
- Baking soda (optional, for odor absorption)
- Spray bottle (optional)
- Stiff brush or scrub brush (optional)
- Vacuum cleaner
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating dried stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Urine Source

Even if you can smell it, pinpointing the exact spot is crucial for effective treatment. Use your nose or, for dried and invisible stains, a UV blacklight in a dark room to make the urine glow. This step ensures you treat the correct area thoroughly and don't miss any hidden spots.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is key to preventing the odor from setting. Don rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or an old rag to blot up as much of the liquid as possible. Press down firmly to absorb the urine, replacing towels as they become saturated, until no more liquid transfers.

### Step 3: Rinse and Blot the Area (Carpets/Upholstery)

If the stain is on carpet or upholstery, gently pour a small amount of clean, cool water over the affected spot. Do not rub, as this can spread the stain and push the urine deeper into fibers. Immediately blot the water back up with clean towels, repeating this process a few times to dilute and extract more urine from the material.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

This is the most critical step for eliminating the smell permanently. Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that consume the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor. Liberally saturate the entire affected area with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor remover, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine penetrated. Follow the product's instructions for dwell time, which often ranges from 15 minutes to several hours, or even overnight. For tips on ensuring a complete and permanent removal, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

After the recommended dwell time for the enzymatic cleaner, blot up any excess liquid and allow the area to air dry completely. Do not try to speed up drying with heat, as this can inadvertently set the odor. Placing a fan nearby or opening windows can help facilitate natural drying. The enzymes continue to work as long as the area remains damp, so patience is key.

### Step 6: Repeat Treatment for Stubborn Odors

For old or deeply set stains, a single treatment may not be enough to fully eliminate the smell. If the odor persists after the area is completely dry, repeat the entire cleaning process from step 3 or 4. Sometimes, multiple applications are necessary to fully break down all the odor-causing compounds. If you're looking for [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast-methods), repeated application of enzymatic cleaners is often the most efficient route.

### Step 7: Clean Hard Surfaces

For non-porous surfaces like tile, hardwood, or linoleum, wipe up fresh urine thoroughly with paper towels. Then, clean the area with a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water, or an enzymatic cleaner. Rinse with clean water and dry completely. Ensure the cleaner gets into any grout lines or crevices where urine might have seeped, as these can harbor odors.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act immediately: The sooner you clean a fresh accident, the easier it will be to remove the smell completely.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners: Ammonia is a component of urine and can actually attract your cat back to the same spot, perpetuating the problem.
- Consider a professional cleaner: For severe or widespread contamination, especially in carpets or upholstery, a professional cleaner with specialized equipment may be the best solution.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness or potential damage to the surface.
- ⚠️ Wear rubber gloves to protect your hands from urine and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products, especially enzymatic cleaners and vinegar solutions.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and safe, and all cleaning products have been removed or dissipated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to remove?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals that bind strongly to surfaces and become more pungent when exposed to humidity. Regular cleaners often cannot break down these crystals, leading to the lingering odor.

**Can I use bleach to remove cat pee smell?**

No, you should never use bleach. Bleach can react with ammonia in urine to create toxic fumes that are dangerous to breathe. Additionally, bleach can damage many surfaces and fabrics.

**What if the smell is deeply embedded in my couch or mattress?**

For deeply embedded odors in upholstery or mattresses, thorough saturation with an enzymatic cleaner is essential, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine. You may need to repeat the process multiple times, and in some severe cases, professional cleaning or replacement might be the only permanent solution.

**Does baking soda help with cat pee smell?**

Yes, baking soda is an excellent natural odor absorber. After cleaning with an enzymatic cleaner and allowing the area to dry, you can sprinkle a generous layer of baking soda over the spot, let it sit for several hours (or overnight), then vacuum it up to further neutralize residual odors.

**How do I prevent future cat pee accidents?**

Prevention involves addressing the root cause: ensure the litter box is clean and accessible, consider if your cat needs more litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), rule out medical issues with a vet visit, and reduce any sources of stress in your cat's environment.

**How long does it take for enzymatic cleaners to work?**

Most enzymatic cleaners require several hours, often 12-24, to fully break down the odor-causing compounds. They work best as the area slowly air-dries, allowing the enzymes sufficient time to act.

**Can I just air out the room to get rid of the smell?**

Airing out the room can temporarily reduce the perceived smell by diluting the odor in the air. However, it will not remove the embedded uric acid crystals from surfaces, meaning the smell will return, especially in humid conditions, until the source is properly treated.
