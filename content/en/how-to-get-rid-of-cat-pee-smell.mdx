To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, you need to neutralize the uric acid crystals left behind by the urine. This typically involves using an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet odors, as regular cleaners often only mask the smell temporarily. Prompt action and thorough cleaning are key to preventing the odor from setting in permanently.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)
- UV black light (optional, for locating old stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Pinpoint the Cat Urine Stain

The first crucial step to get rid of cat pee smell is to accurately locate all affected areas. Cat urine can soak into surprising places, and even a small missed spot can perpetuate the odor. If the stain isn't visible, use your nose to sniff out the strongest odor, or a UV black light in a darkened room, which will make dried urine glow.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you have multiple pets or aren't sure which pet is causing the issue, a black light can often help distinguish urine from other stains.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh urine, immediate action is vital. Place several layers of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth over the wet area and press down firmly to soak up as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into the material. Repeat with fresh towels until no more moisture transfers.

### Step 3: Rinse the Affected Area

After blotting, gently rinse the area with a small amount of cool, clean water, especially for carpets and upholstery. This helps to dilute the remaining urine. Blot again thoroughly with clean towels to remove as much moisture as possible. For hard surfaces, a simple wipe with a damp cloth is sufficient.

### Step 4: Treat with an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals and proteins in cat urine that cause the persistent odor. Saturate the stained area with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully regarding saturation and dwell time, which can range from 10 minutes to several hours. This is critical for [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

> 💡 **Tip:** Test the cleaner on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage to your material.

### Step 5: Allow for Complete Drying

After the enzymatic cleaner has had sufficient time to work, blot up any excess liquid and allow the area to air dry completely. This can take several hours, or even a day or two, depending on humidity and material. Do not rush this step, as residual moisture can encourage mold growth and sometimes reactivate the odor. You can use fans or open windows to speed up drying.

### Step 6: Neutralize Residual Odor with Baking Soda

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot, especially on carpets or fabric. Baking soda is excellent at absorbing lingering odors. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, to maximize its deodorizing effect.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very stubborn odors, you can mix baking soda with a small amount of white vinegar to create a paste, apply it, let it dry, then vacuum.

### Step 7: Vacuum Up Residue

After the baking soda has absorbed odors, vacuum the area thoroughly to remove all traces of the powder. For carpets, use a powerful vacuum cleaner and go over the spot multiple times. If any odor persists, repeat the enzymatic cleaning and baking soda steps, as it may indicate that the urine penetrated deeper than initially treated.


## Tips & Tricks

- **Act Fast:** The sooner you address a cat urine stain, the easier it will be to remove the smell permanently.
- **Don't Use Ammonia:** Ammonia is a component of urine and can actually attract your cat back to the spot to urinate again.
- **Consider Professional Help:** For severe or old stains, especially in subflooring or extensive carpeting, professional cleaning or even replacement might be necessary.
- **Ventilate:** Ensure good air circulation during and after cleaning to help dry the area and dissipate odors.
- **Address the Cause:** If your cat is repeatedly urinating outside the litter box, consult a vet to rule out medical issues or a behaviorist to address stress or litter box preferences.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning agents.
- ⚠️ Never mix different cleaning products, especially ammonia-based ones with bleach, as this can create dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Ensure proper ventilation when using cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and cleaned up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains concentrated uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent odor that ordinary cleaners cannot break down, making the smell very persistent.

**Can white vinegar really remove cat urine smell?**

White vinegar can help neutralize the alkaline salts in cat urine and is effective for mild or fresh stains, but it doesn't break down the uric acid crystals. It's best used as a follow-up or in conjunction with an enzymatic cleaner for best results.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it important?**

An enzymatic cleaner contains beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes specifically target and break down the uric acid and protein molecules in cat urine, effectively eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a couch or mattress?**

For couches or mattresses, blot thoroughly, then saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak deep into the padding. After the dwell time, blot again and let it air dry completely, using fans if needed. You may need to repeat the process multiple times for deep saturation.

**What if the cat pee smell is in my subfloor or drywall?**

If urine has soaked into subflooring or drywall, simply cleaning the surface won't be enough. You may need to remove affected sections of carpet, padding, or even drywall. The subfloor can then be treated with an enzymatic cleaner or sealed with an odor-blocking primer.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to completely disappear?**

With proper and thorough treatment using an enzymatic cleaner, the smell can dissipate within a few hours to a few days. However, for deep or old stains, it might take several applications and complete drying for the odor to be fully gone.

**Will regular carpet shampoo remove cat urine smell?**

Regular carpet shampoo is generally not effective at removing cat urine smell permanently. It might clean the surface and dilute the odor temporarily, but it doesn't break down the uric acid crystals, which are the primary cause of the lingering smell. An enzymatic cleaner is essential.
