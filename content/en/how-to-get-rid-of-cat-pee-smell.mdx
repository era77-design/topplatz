To get rid of cat pee smell, immediately locate the source, blot up as much urine as possible, and then treat the area with an enzymatic cleaner. This breaks down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, effectively neutralizing the smell at its source.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Black light (optional, for locating old stains)
- Vacuum cleaner
- Old toothbrush or scrub brush

## Steps

### Step 1: Find the Urine Stain

The first crucial step is to accurately locate all affected areas. Cat urine can soak into various surfaces, and if you miss even a small spot, the odor will persist. Use your nose or, for dried stains, a UV black light in a darkened room to make hidden urine glow.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check vertical surfaces and furniture legs, as cats sometimes spray.

### Step 2: Absorb Fresh Urine Quickly

For fresh accidents, put on rubber gloves and immediately blot the area with several layers of paper towels or clean, old rags. Press down firmly to absorb as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels to apply maximum pressure and extract more liquid.

### Step 3: Rinse with Water (If Applicable)

For carpets and upholstery, after blotting, lightly rinse the area with cool, clean water. Blot again thoroughly to extract the water. This helps dilute and remove some of the urine before applying a cleaner. For hard surfaces, a simple wipe-down is often sufficient after blotting.

### Step 4: Treat with an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically designed to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the main cause of the persistent odor. Saturate the affected area according to the product instructions, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine penetrated. Allow it to air dry completely. For a deeper dive into effective solutions, consider reading our [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: Effective Solutions](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not use other harsh chemicals or steam cleaners before an enzymatic cleaner, as heat can permanently set the stain and odor.

### Step 5: Use Vinegar and Baking Soda for Extra Power

After the enzymatic cleaner has dried, a homemade solution can provide extra odor-neutralizing power. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle and lightly spray the area. Sprinkle baking soda generously over the damp spot, letting it sit for several hours or overnight to absorb odors.

> 💡 **Tip:** White vinegar is acidic and helps neutralize the alkaline salts in urine, while baking soda is an excellent odor absorber.

### Step 6: Remove Residue and Air Out

Once the baking soda is completely dry, vacuum it up thoroughly. Ensure good ventilation in the room by opening windows or using fans. This helps to dissipate any remaining airborne odors and speeds up the drying process. Proper ventilation is key to completely eliminating the smell.

> 💡 **Tip:** Repeat vacuuming if any baking soda residue remains.

### Step 7: Address Persistent Odors

If a faint odor persists, repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. For stubborn cases or larger affected areas, you might need professional cleaning services. ## Tips & Tricks

- Act quickly! The sooner you clean a cat urine accident, the easier it is to remove the smell.
- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Consider investing in a good quality enzymatic cleaner specifically formulated for pet urine, as these are far more effective than general household cleaners for this type of odor.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as ammonia is a component of urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when handling cat urine to protect your skin from bacteria.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that are insoluble in water and bind tightly to surfaces. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent odor that persists even after the liquid dries.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should not use bleach on cat urine. Bleach can react with ammonia in urine to produce toxic fumes and may also damage or discolor surfaces. It does not effectively neutralize the uric acid crystals.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work on cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria and enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine into harmless, odorless compounds. They effectively neutralize the odor at a molecular level.

**What if the smell is coming from the subfloor?**

If the urine has soaked into the subfloor, you might need to remove the carpet and padding to directly treat the subfloor. Use an enzymatic cleaner, and if the damage is severe, sealing the subfloor or replacing affected sections might be necessary.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing (right type of litter, enough boxes). Rule out medical issues with a vet visit. Stress, territorial marking, or litter box aversion can also be factors.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to go away?**

With proper cleaning using an enzymatic cleaner, the smell should dissipate significantly within a few days as the cleaner works and the area dries. For very old or saturated stains, it might take a few repeat treatments and patience.

**Is white vinegar safe for all surfaces?**

White vinegar is generally safe for most surfaces, including carpets, upholstery, and hard floors. However, always test it on an inconspicuous area first, especially on delicate fabrics or natural stone, to ensure it doesn't cause damage or discoloration.
