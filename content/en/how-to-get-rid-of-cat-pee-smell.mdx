Getting rid of cat pee smell requires more than just masking it; you need to neutralize the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor. Enzymatic cleaners are key, as they break down the odor-causing molecules effectively. Prompt action and proper cleaning techniques can permanently eliminate the lingering scent.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Gloves
- Enzymatic pet urine cleaner
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Plastic wrap (optional)
- Vacuum cleaner
- UV blacklight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

Even if you think you know where the accident happened, cat urine can spread or soak into unexpected areas. Use your nose, or for more precise detection, a UV blacklight in a darkened room to reveal hidden urine stains, which will glow. This step is crucial for ensuring you treat all affected spots thoroughly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Hidden stains are often the reason why the smell persists after cleaning.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, wear gloves and use several layers of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth to blot as much urine as possible. Press down firmly to absorb moisture from carpets or upholstery. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into the fibers.

### Step 3: Rinse the Area with Clean Water (Optional for Fresh Stains)

After blotting, lightly rinse the affected area with cool, clean water. This helps dilute any remaining urine that hasn't fully soaked in. Blot the area dry again thoroughly with clean towels, repeating until no more moisture comes up.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Pet Urine Cleaner

This is the most critical step for neutralizing cat pee smell. Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that literally 'eat' the uric acid crystals, which are the source of the lingering odor. Saturate the soiled area generously, following the product's instructions carefully. This ensures the enzymes can reach all the urine that has soaked in for effective and [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell) removing the odor.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner fully saturates the entire affected area, including the padding under carpets.

### Step 5: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Air Dry

For the enzymatic cleaner to work effectively, it needs time. Cover the treated area with plastic wrap to keep it moist and prevent your cat from returning to the spot while it works. Let it sit for the recommended time, usually several hours or even overnight. After dwelling, remove the plastic and allow the area to air dry completely, which can take 24-48 hours.

### Step 6: Reassess and Reapply if Necessary

Once the area is completely dry, do a sniff test. If any odor remains, it indicates that some urine crystals were missed or not fully neutralized. Repeat the enzymatic cleaning process, ensuring you saturate the area thoroughly. Stubborn odors might require multiple treatments to be fully eradicated for [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor) from your home.

### Step 7: Address Hard Surfaces and Walls

For non-porous surfaces like tile, linoleum, or sealed wood, wipe up urine with paper towels. Then, clean with a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water, followed by a rinse with clean water. For painted walls, use a mild enzymatic cleaner or a vinegar solution. Ensure you clean beyond the visible stain, as urine can run down walls or under baseboards, using [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast-methods) for quick action on these surfaces.


## Tips & Tricks

- Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and make it harder to remove.
- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't cause discoloration or damage to your surfaces.
- Consider using a pet-specific odor eliminator spray or air purifier in the room to help neutralize any residual airborne smells.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning products, especially ammonia-based cleaners with bleach, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.
- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your skin from bacteria and cleaning agents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to remove?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and become reactivated by moisture. These crystals are incredibly pungent and require specialized enzymatic cleaners to break them down effectively, unlike simple soap and water.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat urine. Bleach can react with the ammonia in urine to create toxic fumes. It also doesn't effectively neutralize the uric acid crystals, and its strong scent might even attract your cat back to the spot.

**Do homemade remedies like vinegar and baking soda work?**

White vinegar and baking soda can help with general odors and some fresh urine stains by neutralizing ammonia. However, for the uric acid crystals that cause the persistent, strong cat pee smell, an enzymatic cleaner is usually necessary for complete and permanent removal.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a mattress?**

For a mattress, blot as much urine as possible, then douse the area with an enzymatic cleaner. Let it soak for several hours, then blot dry. Sprinkle baking soda generously over the area, let it sit for a day or two, then vacuum. You may need to repeat this process.

**What if the cat keeps peeing in the same spot?**

Persistent inappropriate urination often indicates an underlying issue. It could be a medical problem, stress, or dissatisfaction with the litter box. Consult your vet to rule out health concerns and review litter box hygiene, type, and placement to address behavioral causes.

**How long does it take for cat pee smell to go away?**

If cleaned properly with an enzymatic cleaner, the smell can be significantly reduced or eliminated within a few hours to a couple of days, depending on the severity and saturation. However, complete drying and full enzyme action can take up to 24-48 hours.

**Will the smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with consistent and correct application of enzymatic cleaners, it is absolutely possible to completely eliminate cat pee smell. The key is to ensure the cleaner reaches and breaks down all the uric acid crystals wherever the urine has soaked.
