To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly to blot up the urine, then use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet odors. Follow up with a baking soda application to absorb remaining odors and ensure thorough drying of the affected area.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet urine cleaner
- Baking soda
- Vacuum cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Spray bottle (optional)
- Black light (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Blot the Stain Immediately

The key to effective odor removal is quick action. As soon as you discover a fresh cat urine spot, locate the affected area. Don your rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or clean, absorbent rags to blot up as much of the urine as possible. Press down firmly and repeatedly, replacing saturated towels, until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into carpet fibers or upholstery.

### Step 2: Pre-Treat with White Vinegar (Optional but Recommended)

For particularly strong odors or fresh stains, a white vinegar solution can be an effective pre-treatment. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Lightly spray the affected area, allowing the solution to sit for 5-10 minutes. Vinegar helps to neutralize the ammonia in the urine. After the dwell time, blot the area again with clean paper towels to remove excess moisture and the vinegar solution.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner Thoroughly

The most crucial step for eliminating cat pee smell permanently is using a high-quality enzymatic cleaner. These cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor. Saturate the stained area generously with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. For a comprehensive approach to tackling the smell, consider reading more on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Air Dry Completely

After applying the enzymatic cleaner, it's vital to let it air dry completely. This process can take several hours, or even up to a day, depending on the product instructions and the saturation level. Do not attempt to speed up drying with heat, as this can set the stain and odor. The enzymes need adequate time and moisture to effectively break down the odor-causing compounds. Patience here is key to success.

### Step 5: Sprinkle with Baking Soda to Absorb Odors

Once the treated area is fully dry, generously sprinkle baking soda over the entire spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that excels at absorbing residual odors. Let the baking soda sit on the area for several hours, or ideally overnight, to give it ample time to work its magic. This step helps to pull out any remaining faint smells that the enzymatic cleaner might have missed.

### Step 6: Vacuum Thoroughly

After the baking soda has had sufficient time to absorb odors, use a vacuum cleaner to thoroughly remove all traces of the powder. Ensure your vacuum has strong suction to lift all the baking soda and any dried residue from the carpet or upholstery fibers. This final step leaves the area clean, dry, and free of both visible residue and lingering odors. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage to your surfaces.
- Use a black light in a dark room to locate old, dried urine stains that might not be visible to the naked eye; they will often glow a yellowish-green.
- If the smell persists after multiple attempts, especially on deeply saturated items, consider professional cleaning or replacement of the affected material.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic urine and encourage your cat to mark the spot again.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fibers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and do not dissolve easily in water. These crystals react with moisture in the air, releasing strong, unpleasant ammonia-like odors that can persist for a very long time if not properly neutralized.

**What is the best type of cleaner for cat pee?**

Enzymatic cleaners are generally considered the most effective for cat pee. They contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes to break down the uric acid and other odor-causing components in the urine, truly eliminating the source of the smell rather than just masking it.

**Can I use homemade remedies like vinegar or baking soda alone?**

While white vinegar and baking soda are excellent for neutralizing odors and are safe to use, they may not fully eliminate the deep-set uric acid crystals on their own. They work best as pre-treatments or follow-ups to enzymatic cleaners for comprehensive odor removal.

**How do I find old cat pee stains I can't see?**

Old, dried cat urine stains often fluoresce under a UV (black) light. To find them, turn off all the lights in the room and shine the black light over suspected areas; the stains will typically glow a yellowish-green, making them easy to spot.

**What if the cat pee smell is in my concrete or hardwood floors?**

For concrete, enzymatic cleaners can still work, but may require multiple applications and longer dwell times. For hardwood, the urine can soak into the wood and subfloor, potentially requiring sanding, sealing, or even replacement of affected boards if the smell is deeply embedded.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is clean and scooped daily, try different types of litter, and consider having multiple litter boxes. Stress, medical issues (like UTIs), or changes in the home can also contribute to inappropriate urination, so consult a vet to rule out underlying health problems.

**Will cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with the right approach and diligent cleaning, cat pee smell can be completely eliminated. The key is to use enzymatic cleaners that break down the uric acid crystals, rather than just masking the odor, and to ensure thorough drying and follow-up treatments.
