To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, you need to neutralize the uric acid crystals, not just mask the odor. The most effective method involves using an enzymatic cleaner that breaks down the urine at a molecular level, combined with thorough cleaning and odor absorption techniques.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Spray bottle (optional)
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Vacuum cleaner
- Plastic sheeting or foil (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

Before you can eliminate the smell, you must find all affected areas. Cat urine can seep deep into carpets, padding, and even subflooring, or soak into furniture. Use a black light (UV light) in a dark room to help reveal dried urine stains, which often glow under UV light.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check walls, baseboards, and vertical surfaces, as cats sometimes spray.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, act quickly. Place several layers of paper towels over the wet spot and press down firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Stand on the towels or use a heavy object to maximize absorption. Repeat with fresh towels until no more liquid transfers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid rubbing the stain, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with White Vinegar (Optional)

For dried or stubborn stains, a white vinegar solution can help break down some of the urine salts. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Generously spray the affected area, letting it sit for 10-15 minutes. Blot dry thoroughly before moving to the next step.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test vinegar on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't discolor the material.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

This is the most critical step for permanent odor removal. Enzymatic cleaners contain bacteria that produce enzymes to break down uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine. Saturate the affected area with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine has penetrated. Follow the product's instructions carefully for application and dwell time. For a deeper dive into making sure the smell is gone for good, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not use other cleaning products before or after applying enzymatic cleaner, as they can deactivate the enzymes.

### Step 5: Allow the Area to Air Dry Completely

After applying the enzymatic cleaner, allow the area to air dry naturally. This process can take several hours or even days, depending on the saturation and humidity. Do not try to speed up drying with heat, as this can 'set' the odor. Cover the area with plastic sheeting or foil to prevent your cat from re-soiling while it dries.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure good ventilation in the room to aid drying and dissipate any lingering odors.

### Step 6: Vacuum or Rinse Away Residue

Once the area is completely dry, vacuum carpets thoroughly to remove any dried residue from the cleaner. For hard surfaces, wipe with a clean, damp cloth. If the odor persists, you may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process, as the urine might have penetrated deeper than initially treated.

> 💡 **Tip:** A stiff brush can help lift dried cleaner residue from carpet fibers before vacuuming.

### Step 7: Apply Baking Soda for Extra Odor Absorption

If a faint odor remains, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the dried area. Baking soda is an excellent natural odor absorber. Let it sit for several hours or even overnight to draw out any residual smells. Vacuum up the baking soda thoroughly afterward. For a comprehensive approach to tackling persistent smells, consult [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** For furniture, you can sprinkle baking soda, let it sit, and then vacuum it off.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous spot first to check for colorfastness or damage.
- Invest in a good quality enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine; not all cleaners are equally effective.
- Address the underlying cause of your cat's inappropriate urination with your vet to prevent future accidents.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as the smell is similar to urine and can attract your cat to re-mark the spot.
- ⚠️ Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and become more pungent as they break down over time. These crystals bind to surfaces and release strong ammonia-like odors, especially in humid conditions.

**Can I use household cleaners like bleach to remove cat pee smell?**

No, you should never use bleach or other harsh household cleaners on cat urine. Bleach can damage surfaces, and its fumes are dangerous when mixed with ammonia-like substances in urine. It also doesn't effectively break down uric acid crystals.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it important?**

An enzymatic cleaner contains beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes specifically target and break down the uric acid crystals and other organic matter in cat urine at a molecular level, permanently eliminating the odor rather than just masking it.

**How do I know if the cat pee smell is completely gone?**

The best way to tell is by smell, especially on a humid day, which can reactivate dormant odors. A UV black light can also help identify any remaining urine residue that might be contributing to the smell.

**What if the cat pee smell is in my subfloor?**

If the urine has soaked into the subfloor, you may need to remove the affected carpet and padding. The subfloor itself might need to be cleaned with an enzymatic cleaner, sealed with a specialized odor-blocking primer, or in severe cases, replaced.

**Can I make my own cat pee cleaner?**

While white vinegar and baking soda can help with initial cleaning and odor absorption, they generally cannot fully break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the persistent smell. A commercial enzymatic cleaner is usually necessary for complete removal.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Consult a veterinarian to rule out medical issues. Ensure the litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing to your cat. Provide enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), try different types of litter, and reduce household stressors.
