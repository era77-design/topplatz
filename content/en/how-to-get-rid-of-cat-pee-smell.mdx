To get rid of cat pee smell effectively, start by locating the source and thoroughly blotting fresh urine. Then, apply an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals, which are the main cause of the persistent odor. For older stains, repeated treatments may be necessary.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush (optional, for carpets)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional, for large areas)
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating old stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Act Fast: Locate and Blot Fresh Urine

The quicker you act, the easier it is to remove the smell. Use paper towels or old rags to blot as much of the fresh urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. Stand on the towels to absorb maximum liquid.

> 💡 **Tip:** For older, invisible stains, a UV blacklight can help reveal hidden urine spots in a dark room.

### Step 2: Rinse the Affected Area

After blotting, gently rinse the area with clean, cool water. This helps to dilute any remaining urine and flush it out. Blot the area thoroughly again to remove as much moisture as possible before applying any cleaning solutions.

### Step 3: Treat with an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are crucial because they break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine that cause the stubborn smell. Saturate the affected area generously with the cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product instructions for dwell time, which is usually 10-15 minutes or more. For a thorough approach to eliminating the odor, consider specific methods for [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner fully saturates the stained area, matching the depth of the urine penetration.

### Step 4: Let the Cleaner Work and Air Dry

After the recommended dwell time, blot away any excess cleaner. Do not rinse immediately. Allow the area to air dry completely, as the enzymes continue to work as long as the area is damp. This process can take several hours or even a day, depending on the humidity and saturation.

### Step 5: Neutralize with Vinegar and Baking Soda (If Needed)

For stubborn odors or as an initial treatment if you don't have an enzymatic cleaner, a vinegar and baking soda solution can help. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle, spray the area, let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then blot dry. Once dry, sprinkle baking soda generously over the area and let it sit for several hours or overnight before vacuuming it up. For quick and effective solutions, see [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast-methods).

> 💡 **Tip:** Always ensure the area is completely dry before applying baking soda to maximize its odor-absorbing capabilities.

### Step 6: Re-treat Stubborn Odors

Cat urine smell can be tenacious, especially from old or deeply saturated stains. If the odor persists after the first treatment, repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. Multiple applications may be required to completely eliminate the smell and prevent your cat from returning to the same spot. For persistent issues, learning [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently) is essential.

> 💡 **Tip:** Patience is key; some deep stains may require 2-3 treatments for full odor removal.

### Step 7: Prevent Future Accidents

While cleaning the smell is important, addressing why your cat is urinating outside the litter box is crucial for long-term success. Consult with a veterinarian to rule out medical issues. Ensure litter boxes are clean, accessible, and in sufficient number (one per cat plus one extra). Reduce stress factors in your cat's environment.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness.
- Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the spot.
- Ensure the area is completely dry before allowing your cat back to prevent re-soiling.
- Consider using an air purifier with a carbon filter to help neutralize lingering airborne odors.
- Ventilate the area well by opening windows or using fans during and after cleaning.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning solutions.
- ⚠️ Keep cleaning products out of reach of children and pets.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different cleaning chemicals, especially bleach and ammonia, as this can create dangerous fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that don't dissolve easily in water. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent odor that can persist for a long time if not properly broken down by enzymatic cleaners.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, it's generally not recommended to use bleach. Bleach can damage surfaces and, more importantly, can react with ammonia in urine to create dangerous chloramine gas. It also doesn't effectively break down uric acid crystals.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into harmless, odorless compounds, effectively eliminating the source of the smell.

**How long does it take for the smell to go away?**

The time it takes can vary. Fresh stains treated quickly might be odor-free in a day or two. Older, deeply saturated stains may require multiple treatments over several days or even a week to fully eliminate the smell.

**Will the smell come back after cleaning?**

If the uric acid crystals are not completely broken down, the smell can reappear, especially in humid conditions. Thorough treatment with an enzymatic cleaner is key to ensuring the smell is permanently gone and doesn't resurface.

**What if the cat keeps peeing in the same spot?**

If your cat repeatedly soils the same area, it indicates the scent hasn't been fully removed, or there's an underlying behavioral or medical issue. Ensure thorough cleaning, then consult a vet to rule out medical problems and address behavioral triggers.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar alone to get rid of the smell?**

While baking soda and vinegar can help neutralize and absorb some odors, they are not as effective as enzymatic cleaners at breaking down the uric acid crystals that cause the stubborn cat pee smell. They can be good temporary solutions or boosters, but enzymatic cleaners are best for permanent removal.
