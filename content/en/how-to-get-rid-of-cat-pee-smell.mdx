To get rid of cat pee smell, immediately blot up as much urine as possible, then apply an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed to break down uric acid crystals. For best results, allow the cleaner to air dry completely, and consider repeating the process for stubborn odors. Addressing the source and cleaning thoroughly are key to permanent odor removal.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- White vinegar (optional pre-treatment)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Black light (optional, for finding hidden spots)
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Wet vac or extractor (optional, for deep cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Locate the Urine Source

Finding the exact spot where your cat has urinated is the crucial first step. Cat urine can be difficult to see, especially on carpets or dark surfaces. A black light can be incredibly helpful for illuminating dried urine stains, making them glow in the dark. Thoroughly inspect common areas like corners, under furniture, and near litter boxes.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you have multiple cats, observe which cat is marking, as this may indicate a behavioral or health issue requiring veterinary attention.

### Step 2: 2. Blot Up Excess Urine Immediately

Act quickly to prevent the urine from soaking deeper into surfaces. Using paper towels or old rags, press firmly onto the wet spot to absorb as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it further into materials. Continue blotting until no more moisture transfers to the towels, changing rags as needed.

### Step 3: 3. Rinse the Area (if applicable)

For carpets and upholstery, lightly rinse the affected area with cool, clean water. This helps dilute the urine and wash away some of the odor-causing compounds. After rinsing, blot the area again thoroughly to remove as much moisture as possible, preparing it for the cleaning solution. Ensure you don't over-saturate.

### Step 4: 4. Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Saturate the soiled area with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully for dwell time, which is typically 10-15 minutes or more. For comprehensive advice on selecting the right products, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

### Step 5: 5. Allow to Air Dry Completely

Do not rush the drying process. Enzymatic cleaners work as they dry, so it's essential to let the treated area air dry completely, which can take several hours or even a day, depending on humidity and material thickness. You can place a fan nearby to speed up drying, but resist the urge to use heat, as this can set the stain and odor permanently.

### Step 6: 6. Neutralize Remaining Odors with Baking Soda

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any lingering odors. Leave the baking soda on for several hours or overnight, then vacuum it up thoroughly. This step is particularly effective for carpets and fabrics, providing an extra layer of odor removal.

### Step 7: 7. Address Stubborn Stains and Odors

If the smell persists after the first treatment, repeat the entire cleaning process, focusing on saturating the area adequately with the enzymatic cleaner. For very old or strong odors, it may take multiple applications. Consider using a wet vac or extractor for a deeper clean, especially on carpets, to ensure all urine residues are removed. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't cause discoloration or damage.
- Place aluminum foil, plastic sheeting, or an upside-down laundry basket over the cleaned area to deter your cat from returning to the same spot while it dries.
- Consider professional cleaning for severe or widespread urine contamination, especially on expensive rugs, upholstery, or subflooring.
- Address the root cause of the peeing, such as litter box issues, stress, or medical problems, to prevent future accidents.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell is similar to cat urine and can attract your cat to re-mark the area.
- ⚠️ Do not use steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fabrics and surfaces.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when cleaning urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning solutions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat pee has a strong, pungent odor due to concentrated uric acid, urea, and mercaptans. As it dries, bacteria break down the urea, releasing an ammonia-like smell, and the uric acid crystals bond to surfaces, reactivating the odor when re-wet or exposed to humidity.

**Can I use household cleaners like bleach or ammonia to get rid of cat pee smell?**

No, it's strongly advised against using bleach or ammonia-based cleaners. Ammonia's scent is similar to cat urine, which can attract your cat back to the spot. Bleach can cause discoloration and, when mixed with urine, can create harmful fumes.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it essential for cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine, effectively eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it. They are crucial for permanent odor removal.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a couch or mattress?**

For couches and mattresses, blot thoroughly to remove excess urine. Then, apply an enzymatic cleaner, ensuring deep saturation into the affected material. Allow it to air dry completely, which may take longer for thick items, and consider sprinkling baking soda on top once dry, then vacuuming it up later.

**How can I find old, dried cat urine spots that aren't visible?**

The most effective way to find old, dried cat urine spots is by using a black light (UV flashlight) in a dark room. The uric acid crystals in the urine will glow under the UV light, making hidden stains visible on carpets, upholstery, and other surfaces.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to go away completely?**

With proper and immediate cleaning using an enzymatic cleaner, the smell can be significantly reduced or eliminated within a few hours to a day. For older or deeply set stains, multiple treatments and complete drying may be required over several days to ensure complete odor removal.

**What if my cat keeps peeing in the same spot after cleaning?**

If your cat repeatedly pees in the same spot, it often indicates a behavioral issue, stress, or an underlying medical problem. Consult your veterinarian to rule out health issues, and consider addressing environmental factors like litter box cleanliness, location, or the number of litter boxes available.
