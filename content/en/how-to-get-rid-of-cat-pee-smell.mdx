To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly to blot the urine, then thoroughly clean the area with an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals. Follow up with proper drying and ventilation to eliminate lingering odors effectively and permanently.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar (optional, for pre-treatment on some surfaces)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle (optional, for vinegar solution)
- Vacuum cleaner
- UV black light (optional, for locating old stains)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil (for deterrence)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

The first critical step is to accurately identify all areas affected by cat urine. For fresh accidents, your nose might be sufficient, but for dried or older stains, a UV black light flashlight can reveal hidden urine spots that glow in the dark. Thoroughly checking all potential areas ensures no spot is missed, which is key to complete odor removal.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh urine, immediate action is crucial. Place several layers of paper towels or an old, clean rag over the wet area and press down firmly to absorb as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into carpet fibers or upholstery. Continue blotting until no more moisture transfers to the towels.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Saturate the soiled area generously with an enzymatic cleaner specifically formulated for pet urine. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the potent cat pee odor, effectively neutralizing it rather than just masking it. Ensure you follow the product's instructions for application and dwell time for best results. For a more comprehensive approach to tackling these odors, refer to our [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

### Step 4: Allow to Dwell and Re-Blot

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit on the stain for the recommended period, typically 10-15 minutes or longer, to allow the enzymes sufficient time to work on the uric acid. After the dwell time, use clean paper towels or a rag to blot up the excess cleaner and any remaining urine residue. Do not rinse the area unless the product instructions specifically state to do so.

### Step 5: Ensure Thorough Drying

Proper drying is essential to prevent mildew and ensure the odor doesn't reactivate. You can aid the drying process by placing a fan near the treated area, opening windows for ventilation, or using a dehumidifier. Avoid covering the area until it is completely dry to the touch, which might take several hours or even overnight.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional but Recommended)

Once the treated area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the spot. Baking soda is a natural odor absorber and can help neutralize any lingering faint smells. Let it sit for several hours, or ideally overnight, to maximize its deodorizing effect. Afterwards, thoroughly vacuum up the baking soda.

### Step 7: Address Underlying Behavioral Issues

If your cat continues to urinate outside the litter box, cleaning the spot is only part of the solution. Consult your veterinarian to rule out any medical conditions. Also, evaluate your cat's litter box habits: ensure the box is clean, in a quiet location, and that you have enough litter boxes for the number of cats in your household. This can prevent future accidents.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning product, including enzymatic cleaners, on an inconspicuous area of the material first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ensure good ventilation by opening windows and using fans during and after cleaning to help dissipate odors and speed up drying.
- Never mix different cleaning chemicals, especially ammonia-based products, as they can create harmful fumes and ammonia can actually attract cats back to the same spot.
- For very old, deeply saturated stains, or large areas of contamination, professional carpet or upholstery cleaning may be necessary to fully eradicate the odor. For truly permanent solutions, check out [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).
- Consider placing aluminum foil, plastic sheeting, or an upside-down laundry basket over a freshly cleaned spot temporarily to deter your cat from returning to that area before the smell is completely gone.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic cat urine and attract your cat to re-mark the area.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when cleaning urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning agents.
- ⚠️ Ensure the area is well-ventilated when using cleaning products to avoid inhaling fumes.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fibers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat urine contains highly concentrated uric acid crystals, which do not dissolve easily in water. As these crystals dry, they release a strong, pungent ammonia-like odor, which can be reactivated by humidity and persist indefinitely if not properly neutralized.

**What is the best product for cat pee smell?**

Enzymatic cleaners are widely considered the most effective products for cat pee smell. They contain beneficial bacteria and enzymes that specifically break down the uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine, permanently eliminating the odor rather than just masking it.

**Can baking soda alone remove cat pee smell?**

While baking soda is an excellent natural deodorizer that can absorb some odors, it is typically not strong enough to completely eliminate the deeply set uric acid crystals from cat urine on its own. It works best as a supplementary treatment after an enzymatic cleaner has broken down the core components.

**How do I get old cat pee smell out of carpet?**

For old stains, first locate them using a UV black light. Then, thoroughly saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to dwell for an extended period as per product instructions. Multiple treatments, combined with deep cleaning and ensuring complete drying, are often necessary for stubborn old odors.

**Will cat pee smell eventually go away on its own?**

No, cat pee smell rarely goes away completely on its own. The uric acid crystals can remain in surfaces indefinitely, reactivating and releasing odor with changes in humidity. Proper cleaning with an enzymatic cleaner is essential for permanent removal and a fresh-smelling home.

**How do I stop my cat from peeing in the same spot?**

After thoroughly cleaning the spot, make it less appealing to your cat. You can use deterrents like aluminum foil, plastic sheeting, or a cat-safe citrus spray. Crucially, address the root cause by ensuring a clean, accessible litter box, adequate litter boxes, and consulting a vet for any underlying medical or behavioral issues.

**Is cat pee harmful to humans?**

While cat pee itself isn't directly toxic, prolonged exposure to its strong ammonia fumes, especially in poorly ventilated areas, can cause respiratory irritation, headaches, and nausea. It's also unhygienic and can harbor bacteria, so prompt and thorough cleanup is always recommended for health and sanitation.
