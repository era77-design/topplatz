To get rid of cat pee smell, immediately clean the affected area thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner designed for pet odors. Blot, do not rub, and allow the cleaner to air dry to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor. Repeat as necessary for complete neutralization.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle (optional)
- UV blacklight (optional)
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Blot the Stain Immediately

The quicker you act, the easier it is to get rid of cat pee smell. If the stain is fresh, use several layers of paper towels or a clean cloth to blot up as much urine as possible. Press firmly, standing on the towels if necessary, to absorb liquid from deep within carpets or upholstery. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

> 💡 **Tip:** For old, dried stains, use a UV blacklight in a darkened room to pinpoint the exact location, as dried urine glows under UV light.

### Step 2: Rinse the Area (If Possible)

For carpets or washable fabrics, gently rinse the affected area with cool water. This helps dilute the urine and flush some of the salts and proteins out of the material. Blot the water thoroughly again with clean towels, ensuring you remove as much moisture as possible before applying any cleaning solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** A wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner can be highly effective at extracting both urine and rinse water from carpets and upholstery.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are crucial for breaking down the uric acid crystals in cat urine that cause the persistent odor. Saturate the soiled area generously with the enzymatic cleaner, following the product's instructions. Ensure the cleaner reaches deep into the carpet padding or fabric where the urine might have soaked. For more in-depth solutions, see [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** Look for cleaners specifically formulated for pet urine, as they contain enzymes that target the components of cat pee.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Air Dry

Do not immediately blot or clean up the enzymatic solution. For the enzymes to work effectively, they need time to break down the urine at a molecular level. Allow the cleaner to dwell for the time recommended on the product label, often 10-15 minutes or longer. Then, let the area air dry completely; this can take several hours or even a day.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cover the treated area with an upside-down laundry basket or foil to prevent pets or children from walking on it while it dries.

### Step 5: Re-evaluate and Repeat if Necessary

Once the area is completely dry, perform a sniff test. If any odor persists, it means some urine crystals remain, and the process needs to be repeated. Reapply the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring thorough saturation, and allow it to dry again. Persistence is key to completely eliminating the smell.

> 💡 **Tip:** Sometimes, stubborn odors require multiple applications to fully neutralize all the uric acid.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda

After the area is fully dry and the odor is gone, you can sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any lingering, faint smells. Let it sit for several hours or overnight before vacuuming it up thoroughly.

> 💡 **Tip:** For extra freshening, mix a few drops of essential oil (like lavender or tea tree) with the baking soda before sprinkling, but ensure it's safe for pets.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't cause discoloration or damage.
- Consider investing in a good quality enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine, as household cleaners often only mask the smell temporarily.
- Address the underlying reason for the inappropriate urination (e.g., litter box issues, stress, medical problems) to prevent future incidents.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as ammonia is a component of urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia, as this can create dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Always ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals that are insoluble and bond tightly to surfaces. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent odor, making it challenging to eliminate without specialized cleaners.

**Can white vinegar help get rid of cat pee smell?**

Yes, white vinegar can be effective as a pre-treatment or for mild odors because its acidity helps neutralize the alkaline salts in urine. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water, apply, let it sit, then blot dry before using an enzymatic cleaner.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it important?**

An enzymatic cleaner contains beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, proteins, and other organic compounds in cat urine, permanently eliminating the odor rather than just masking it.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a mattress?**

For mattresses, blot as much urine as possible, then thoroughly saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner. Let it air dry completely, potentially for several days, using fans to aid drying. Repeat if necessary, and sprinkle baking soda afterward.

**Will regular carpet shampoo remove cat urine odor?**

Regular carpet shampoos often lack the specific enzymes needed to break down uric acid crystals in cat urine. While they might clean the surface, they typically won't eliminate the deep-seated odor, which can resurface later.

**How do I prevent my cat from peeing in the same spot again?**

After thoroughly cleaning and deodorizing, make the spot undesirable by placing food, toys, or furniture there. Also, consult a vet to rule out medical issues, and ensure litter box hygiene, location, and number are appropriate for your cat. For more effective solutions, check out [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: Effective Solutions](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

It is generally not recommended to use bleach on cat urine. Bleach can damage fabrics and carpets, and its strong smell might deter your cat temporarily but doesn't break down the uric acid. More importantly, mixing bleach with ammonia (which is in urine) creates dangerous toxic fumes.
