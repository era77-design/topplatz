To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by blotting up fresh urine, then saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals. Allow the cleaner to air dry completely, and repeat if necessary for stubborn odors.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle (for vinegar, if used)
- Rubber gloves
- Blacklight (optional, for old stains)
- Vacuum cleaner

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Identify the Urine Stain

Begin by thoroughly inspecting the affected area. If the stain is fresh, it will be easier to spot. For older, dried stains, a blacklight can be incredibly helpful in revealing hidden urine deposits, which glow under UV light. Mark the areas you find for targeted treatment to ensure no spot is missed.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check underneath furniture, along baseboards, and in less trafficked corners where pets might hide.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

Speed is key when dealing with fresh cat urine. Use paper towels or old rags to blot as much liquid as possible from the affected surface. Press firmly and replace soiled towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers, making removal harder.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels to apply maximum pressure, especially on carpets, to absorb more liquid.

### Step 3: Saturate the Area with an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Liberally saturate the stained area, ensuring the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully for best results. For a comprehensive approach to tackling the smell, learn [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-complete-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner makes contact with all affected layers, especially in carpets and padding, for thorough treatment.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Air Dry Completely

Do not rush this step, as enzymatic cleaners need time to work their magic, often several hours or even overnight. Cover the area with a piece of plastic or foil to prevent pets from re-soiling it while it dries. Air circulation can significantly help speed up the drying process and dissipate odors.

> 💡 **Tip:** Open windows or use a fan to improve ventilation in the room and aid in faster drying.

### Step 5: Rinse and Reapply for Stubborn Odors

Once the area is completely dry, perform a sniff test. If a faint odor persists, you may need to rinse the area with clean water (blotting thoroughly afterwards) and then reapply the enzymatic cleaner. Persistent smells often indicate that the initial application didn't reach all the urine. Repeat the treatment until the odor is completely gone.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very tough spots or deeply absorbed urine, a second or third application may be necessary to completely eliminate the odor.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional but Recommended)

After the enzymatic cleaner has dried and the odor is significantly reduced, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated area. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any remaining faint smells. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, before vacuuming it up thoroughly.

> 💡 **Tip:** For extra deodorizing power, mix a few drops of pet-safe essential oil (like lavender or frankincense) with the baking soda before sprinkling.

### Step 7: Address Porous Materials and Furniture

For items like cushions, mattresses, or untreated wood, the process can be more challenging. If the urine has soaked deep, consider removing fabric covers and washing them according to care instructions, adding a cup of white vinegar to the wash cycle. For wood, after cleaning, you might need to sand and reseal the affected area with an odor-blocking primer to completely encapsulate the odor.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always spot-test cleaners on an inconspicuous area of fabric or wood before full application to prevent damage or discoloration.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act fast: The quicker you address a fresh stain, the easier it is to remove the odor permanently and prevent deep saturation.
- Test cleaners: Always spot-test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to prevent discoloration or damage to your surfaces.
- Ventilate well: Ensure good airflow in the room while cleaning and drying to dissipate odors and fumes effectively.
- Consider the source: If your cat is repeatedly urinating outside the litter box, consult a veterinarian to rule out medical issues or a behaviorist for stress-related causes.
- Avoid ammonia-based cleaners: Ammonia smells similar to cat urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot, perpetuating the problem.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when handling cat urine and cleaning solutions to protect your skin.
- ⚠️ Ensure the area is well-ventilated when using cleaning products to avoid inhaling fumes.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until completely dry and cleaned, especially after using strong solutions.
- ⚠️ Never mix different cleaning products, especially those containing bleach and ammonia, as this can create dangerous and toxic fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat urine contains concentrated uric acid crystals, which are difficult to break down with regular cleaners. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent odor that can linger for a long time, even after the visible stain is gone.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat urine. Bleach can react negatively with ammonia in urine, creating dangerous fumes. It can also damage surfaces and is not effective at breaking down the uric acid crystals that cause the persistent odor.

**What if the cat pee smell is in my wooden floor or subfloor?**

For wooden floors, clean the surface thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak in. For severe cases where the urine has soaked into the subfloor, you might need to remove and replace the affected wood, or at least seal it with an odor-blocking primer after cleaning to prevent the smell from returning.

**How do I get rid of cat pee smell from a couch or mattress?**

For couches and mattresses, blot as much urine as possible, then thoroughly saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner. Allow it to air dry completely, potentially using a fan or dehumidifier to speed up the process. For deeply saturated items, professional cleaning might be necessary.

**Does white vinegar really help with cat pee smell?**

White vinegar can help neutralize some of the alkaline salts in cat urine and acts as a mild deodorizer. While it can reduce the smell, it's generally not as effective as an enzymatic cleaner for completely breaking down the uric acid crystals responsible for the most stubborn odors.

**How often should I clean the litter box to prevent accidents?**

Scoop the litter box at least once a day, and completely change the litter and clean the box with mild soap and water weekly. A dirty or smelly litter box is a common reason for cats to urinate elsewhere in the home.

**When should I call a professional cleaner for cat urine?**

Consider calling a professional cleaner if the urine has deeply saturated carpets, upholstery, or subflooring, or if home remedies haven't completely eliminated the odor after multiple attempts. Professionals have stronger equipment and specialized solutions for severe cases.
