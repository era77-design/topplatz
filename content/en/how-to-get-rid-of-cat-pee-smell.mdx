To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, the key is to act quickly and use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed to break down uric acid crystals. Locating the source of the odor and thoroughly saturating the area with the cleaner, followed by proper drying, is crucial for permanent removal. Addressing the underlying cause of the cat's inappropriate urination can also help prevent future incidents.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- Old toothbrush or scrub brush (optional)
- Black light (UV light) (optional)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil (optional)
- Fan or open windows (for ventilation)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Vacuum cleaner

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

The first and most critical step is to accurately pinpoint where the cat urine odor is coming from. Even if you think you know, sometimes there are hidden spots. Use your nose, but for greater accuracy, a black light (UV light) in a dark room can reveal dried urine stains, which often glow. Circle the affected areas with chalk or painter's tape to ensure thorough treatment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cat urine can soak deep into carpets, padding, and even subflooring, so be prepared to treat a larger area than initially visible.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

If the urine is still wet, put on rubber gloves and blot up as much as possible using paper towels or old rags. Press firmly to absorb the liquid, but avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. Replace saturated towels until no more moisture transfers.

> 💡 **Tip:** The quicker you act, the easier it will be to completely eliminate the odor and prevent permanent staining. Speed is your best friend when dealing with fresh urine.

### Step 3: Pre-Test Your Enzymatic Cleaner

Before applying any cleaning product to a visible area, always test it on an inconspicuous spot of the material (e.g., a hidden corner of carpet or upholstery). Apply a small amount, wait for a few minutes, and then blot it dry. Check for any discoloration or damage to ensure the cleaner is safe for your specific surface.

### Step 4: Saturate the Area with Enzymatic Cleaner

Once you've blotted up excess urine and tested your cleaner, generously apply an enzymatic pet urine cleaner to the entire affected area. Ensure the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did, which might mean soaking carpets or upholstery thoroughly. Enzymatic cleaners contain special enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the primary cause of the lingering smell. For a complete guide on effective removal, you might find more detailed information in our article [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

> 💡 **Tip:** Follow the specific instructions on your chosen cleaner's label for optimal results and recommended dwell times.

### Step 5: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Air Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit on the stain for the time recommended by the manufacturer, typically 10-30 minutes. This allows the enzymes sufficient time to work their magic. After the dwell time, do not rinse immediately. Instead, allow the area to air dry completely. You can cover the treated spot with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil to prevent your cat from revisiting the area while it dries.

> 💡 **Tip:** Proper drying is crucial. If the area remains damp, it can encourage mold growth and reactivate the urine odor. Using a fan or opening windows can speed up the drying process.

### Step 6: Repeat if Necessary and Vacuum

Once the area is completely dry, sniff test it. If a faint odor remains, you may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. For persistent odors, multiple applications might be necessary. Once satisfied, vacuum the treated area to remove any residue left by the cleaner.

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn odors, consider sprinkling baking soda over the dry, treated area, letting it sit overnight, and then vacuuming it up. Baking soda is excellent for absorbing residual smells.

### Step 7: Address the Cause of Inappropriate Urination

While cleaning the smell is important, preventing future incidents is key. Consider why your cat might be urinating outside the litter box. Factors include litter box cleanliness, type of litter, location of the box, stress, or underlying medical issues. Consulting a veterinarian to rule out health problems is always recommended.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure you have enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), keep them scooped daily, and try different litter types if your cat seems particular.


## Tips & Tricks

- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as ammonia is a component of cat urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- For very old or deeply set stains, you might need to lift carpets, treat the padding, and even seal the subfloor with an odor-blocking primer after cleaning.
- Consider professional cleaning for severe or widespread cat urine contamination, especially on expensive carpets or upholstery.
- Ensure good ventilation during and after cleaning by opening windows or using fans.
- Always clean up accidents as soon as they happen to prevent the odor from setting in permanently.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when handling cat urine and cleaning products to protect your skin.
- ⚠️ Ensure adequate ventilation when using cleaning products, especially enzymatic cleaners, to avoid inhaling fumes.
- ⚠️ Keep all cleaning products out of reach of children and pets.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different cleaning products, as this can create dangerous fumes or ineffective solutions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and notoriously difficult to break down with regular cleaners. These crystals re-crystallize when exposed to moisture, reactivating the strong, pungent odor.

**What is the best cleaner for cat pee smell?**

Enzymatic cleaners are the most effective because they contain specialized enzymes that specifically target and break down the uric acid crystals and proteins in cat urine, eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**Can I use vinegar or baking soda to get rid of cat pee smell?**

While vinegar and baking soda can help neutralize odors in some situations, they are generally not strong enough to completely eliminate the uric acid crystals in cat urine. They can be used as a supplementary step after enzymatic cleaning or for very minor, fresh accidents.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a couch or mattress?**

For couches or mattresses, the process is similar to carpets: blot up fresh urine, use a black light to locate hidden spots, then thoroughly saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner. Ensure deep penetration and allow it to air dry completely, potentially repeating the process.

**What if the cat pee smell is in the subfloor or walls?**

If urine has soaked into subflooring or walls, you may need to remove affected carpet, padding, or drywall. Clean the underlying structure with an enzymatic cleaner, allow it to dry, and then seal it with an odor-blocking primer before replacing materials.

**How long does it take for cat pee smell to go away?**

With proper and thorough enzymatic cleaning, the smell can be significantly reduced or eliminated within a few days of drying. However, for deeply saturated or old stains, it might require multiple treatments and longer drying times to completely dissipate.

**Will the cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with the correct approach using enzymatic cleaners that break down uric acid, the smell can be permanently eliminated. The key is thoroughness, ensuring the cleaner reaches every bit of the contamination, and allowing it to completely dry.
