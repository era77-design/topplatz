To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by blotting up fresh urine and then applying an enzymatic cleaner. These cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor, which ordinary cleaners cannot do effectively. For stubborn smells, repeated treatments and ensuring the cleaner saturates the affected area are key to complete removal.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet urine cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- UV black light (optional, for dried stains)
- Stiff brush or scrubber (for carpets)
- Plastic sheeting or foil (to cover treated areas)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

Even if you can smell it, pinpointing the exact spot of cat urine, especially dried stains, can be tricky. For fresh stains, visual inspection is usually enough. For older, invisible stains, use a UV black light in a darkened room; urine will glow yellowish-green, helping you identify all affected areas.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

Speed is crucial when dealing with fresh cat pee. Place several layers of paper towels or an old, absorbent cloth over the wet spot and press down firmly to soak up as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. Repeat with fresh towels until no more liquid is absorbed.

### Step 3: Pre-treat with White Vinegar (Optional)

For an initial attack on the odor and to help neutralize alkaline salts in the urine, you can pre-treat the area with a white vinegar solution. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water, then generously spray or pour it over the affected area. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes before blotting it dry again. This step is optional but can be beneficial before applying enzymatic cleaners.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Pet Urine Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine that cause the lingering smell. Follow the product instructions carefully, ensuring you saturate the affected area, including padding beneath carpets if necessary. For detailed guidance on effective and fast cleaning, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast).

### Step 5: Allow the Cleaner to Work and Air Dry

Do not rush this step. Enzymatic cleaners need time to work their magic, often several hours or even overnight. Cover the treated area with plastic sheeting or foil to prevent it from drying out too quickly, which allows the enzymes more time to break down the urine. Ensure good ventilation in the room. Once the recommended time has passed, remove the covering and allow the area to air dry completely.

### Step 6: Absorb Residual Odor with Baking Soda (Optional)

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over it. Baking soda is a natural odor absorber and can help draw out any remaining smells. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, then vacuum it up thoroughly. This step is a good follow-up, especially for carpets or upholstery.

### Step 7: Re-evaluate and Repeat if Necessary

After the area is dry and you've vacuumed any baking soda, check for any lingering odor. If a faint smell remains, it means some uric acid crystals are still present. You may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. For persistent or deeply set odors, exploring more comprehensive solutions for permanent removal might be beneficial, as discussed in [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaner on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ensure enzymatic cleaners fully saturate the affected area, as the urine likely soaked deep into carpets or upholstery.
- Consider professional cleaning for severe or old stains, especially on delicate fabrics or large affected areas.
- Address the underlying cause of your cat's inappropriate urination to prevent recurrence, such as litter box issues or vet check-ups.
- Use an air purifier with an activated carbon filter to help remove airborne odors during and after the cleaning process.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent is similar to cat urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor into fibers and padding.
- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when cleaning urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning agents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to remove?**

Cat pee is difficult to remove due to uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong odor that traditional cleaners cannot break down.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and how does it work?**

An enzymatic cleaner contains beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into gases that then dissipate, effectively eliminating the odor at its source.

**Can I use homemade remedies instead of commercial cleaners?**

While white vinegar and baking soda can help neutralize and absorb some odors, they are generally not as effective as enzymatic cleaners for completely breaking down uric acid crystals. For best results, enzymatic cleaners are recommended.

**How do I find dried cat urine stains that I can't see?**

Dried cat urine stains can be found using a UV black light in a dark room. The uric acid crystals in the urine will fluoresce, appearing as a yellowish-green glow, making invisible stains visible.

**What if the cat pee smell is in wood or concrete?**

For porous surfaces like wood or concrete, the urine can soak deeply. After enzymatic treatment, you may need to use an odor-sealing primer or paint to encapsulate any remaining smell, or in severe cases, replace affected sections.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box again?**

Prevention involves addressing the cause. Ensure the litter box is clean, has enough boxes for your cats (one per cat plus one extra), and is in a quiet, accessible location. Consult a vet to rule out medical issues.

**Is cat pee smell harmful to humans or other pets?**

While not directly harmful in terms of toxicity, the strong ammonia smell can be irritating to respiratory systems, especially for those with allergies or asthma. It also signals poor hygiene and can attract pests.
