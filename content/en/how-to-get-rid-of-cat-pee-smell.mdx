To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, the most crucial step is to use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor. Blotting up fresh urine quickly and thoroughly preparing the affected area are also key to success. For a truly fresh home, addressing both the visible stain and the lingering odor is essential.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (pet-specific)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Stiff brush or scrub brush
- Plastic wrap or foil (optional)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Identify the Source

Before you can eliminate the smell, you must find all affected areas. Cat urine can soak into carpets, padding, upholstery, and even subflooring, so use a black light in a darkened room to reveal hidden urine stains. The salts in dried urine will glow under UV light, helping you pinpoint every spot that needs treatment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check vertical surfaces like walls or furniture legs, as cats sometimes spray to mark territory.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is key. Put on rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or an old, absorbent cloth to blot as much urine as possible. Press down firmly and replace towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels to exert more pressure and absorb maximum liquid.

### Step 3: Rinse the Area (If Applicable)

If the stain is on a washable item or a carpet that can handle a bit more moisture, lightly rinse the area with cool water. This helps dilute the urine and makes it easier for the enzymatic cleaner to work. After rinsing, blot the area thoroughly again to remove excess water.

> 💡 **Tip:** For carpets, a wet/dry vac can be very effective at extracting rinse water and urine.

### Step 4: Apply Enzymatic Cleaner Generously

Saturate the affected area with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor remover, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine did. The enzymes in these cleaners break down the uric acid crystals that cause the persistent smell. Follow the product's instructions carefully for application and dwell time.

> 💡 **Tip:** For truly stubborn or older stains, understanding [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently) is crucial for long-term success.

### Step 5: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit for the recommended time, which can range from 10-15 minutes to several hours, or even overnight. Covering the treated area with plastic wrap or foil can prevent it from drying out too quickly, allowing the enzymes more time to work. Do not rinse the cleaner off prematurely.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the area is well-ventilated during the drying process to help dissipate any remaining odors.

### Step 6: Blot and Air Dry Completely

After the dwell time, blot up any remaining liquid from the enzymatic cleaner with clean paper towels. Allow the area to air dry completely. This might take several hours or even a day or two, depending on the material and humidity. Do not use heat to dry, as it can set the odor.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a fan nearby to speed up the drying process, especially for carpets and upholstery.

### Step 7: Address Lingering Odors with Baking Soda or Vinegar (Optional)

If a faint odor persists after the enzymatic treatment and drying, you can try a baking soda or vinegar solution. Sprinkle baking soda generously over the dry area and let it sit for several hours or overnight before vacuuming. Alternatively, for hard surfaces, a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water can be used to wipe down the area, followed by a clean water rinse.

## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness or damage.
- Consider a professional carpet cleaning service for severe or widespread urine contamination, especially in padding or subflooring.
- Prevent future accidents by addressing the root cause of your cat's inappropriate urination, such as litter box issues, stress, or medical problems.
- Place a piece of aluminum foil or plastic over the cleaned spot for a few days to deter your cat from re-marking the area while it's still drying.
- Ensure your litter boxes are clean, accessible, and numerous enough (one per cat plus one extra) to prevent accidents.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent is similar to cat urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Do not use steam cleaners on cat urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into the fibers.
- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands from bacteria and strong odors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and do not dissolve easily in water. These crystals react with humidity and bacteria, causing a strong, pungent ammonia-like odor that can reactivate even after seemingly being cleaned.

**Do homemade remedies like vinegar and baking soda really work?**

While white vinegar and baking soda can neutralize some odors and clean stains, they are not as effective as enzymatic cleaners for breaking down uric acid crystals. They can be good secondary treatments for lingering odors or for hard, non-porous surfaces after initial enzymatic treatment.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it important?**

An enzymatic cleaner contains beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes specifically designed to break down the organic matter in cat urine, including the uric acid crystals. This process eliminates the odor at its source, rather than just masking it, making it crucial for permanent odor removal.

**How do I remove cat pee smell from furniture or mattresses?**

For furniture and mattresses, the process is similar to carpets: blot thoroughly, apply an enzymatic cleaner, allow it to dwell and dry completely. You may need to apply the cleaner multiple times and ensure it soaks deep enough to reach all contamination. A wet vac can assist with extraction.

**What if the cat pee smell is in hardwood floors or subflooring?**

For hardwood, clean the surface with an enzymatic cleaner. If the odor persists, the urine may have seeped into the wood or subfloor, requiring more extensive treatment like sanding, sealing, or even replacing affected sections of wood or subflooring for complete removal.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Preventative measures include ensuring you have enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), keeping them impeccably clean, trying different litter types, reducing stress in your cat's environment, and ruling out medical issues with a vet visit. Addressing the underlying cause is key.

**Can I use a steam cleaner to remove cat pee smell?**

No, it's strongly advised not to use a steam cleaner. The heat from a steam cleaner can actually bond the uric acid crystals to the carpet fibers or upholstery, permanently setting the stain and the odor, making it much harder to remove later.
