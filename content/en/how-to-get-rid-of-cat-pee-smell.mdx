To get rid of cat pee smell, identify the source, thoroughly clean the area with an enzymatic cleaner designed for pet odors, and allow it to dry completely. Repeated treatments may be necessary for stubborn odors, ensuring you neutralize the uric acid crystals.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating invisible stains)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil (to deter re-soiling)
- Wet vac or carpet extractor (optional, for deep cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source

Even if you think you know where the smell is coming from, cat urine can spread beyond visible marks. Use your nose to sniff out affected areas, or for hidden stains, a UV blacklight in a darkened room can reveal dried urine that glows under the light. Mark all identified areas for thorough treatment to ensure no spot is missed.

### Step 2: Blot and Absorb Fresh Urine

For fresh urine, immediate action is key. Immediately blot up as much liquid as possible using paper towels or old rags. Press down firmly to absorb deeply embedded moisture, changing towels frequently until no more liquid is transferred. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers, making it harder to remove.

### Step 3: Rinse the Area (if applicable)

For hard, non-porous surfaces, rinse the area with clean water and blot dry. For carpets or upholstery, gently pour a small amount of clean water onto the stain, then blot again to dilute and remove residual urine. This step helps to flush out some of the urine and prepares the area for the enzymatic cleaner by making it more receptive.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Saturate the affected area with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor cleaner, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine did. These specialized cleaners contain enzymes that break down uric acid crystals and odor-causing bacteria, which are the primary sources of the persistent smell. Follow the product instructions carefully, often requiring several hours or overnight to work effectively for a complete solution. For a more detailed approach, consider reading [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-complete-guide).

### Step 5: Allow to Dry Completely

This is a crucial step that should not be rushed. Do not allow pets back into the area until it is completely dry, as residual moisture can reactivate odors and attract your cat back to the spot. You might need to cover the area with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil to deter re-soiling while it dries. Ensure the area is thoroughly dry before proceeding or allowing pets back.

### Step 6: Neutralize Residual Odor with Vinegar and Baking Soda

Once the enzymatic cleaner has dried, if a faint odor persists, sprinkle baking soda generously over the area. Let it sit for several hours or overnight to absorb any remaining smells. For an extra boost, you can lightly spray a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water over the baking soda, allowing it to fizz and further neutralize. Vacuum up the baking soda once everything is completely dry.

### Step 7: Address Persistent Odors & Prevention

If the smell lingers, repeat the enzymatic cleaning process. Sometimes, deep saturation or old stains require multiple treatments for complete eradication. To prevent future accidents, ensure your litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing, and consider placing food or toys in previously soiled spots to deter re-soiling. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to ensure they don't cause discoloration or damage to your surfaces.
- Consider investing in a small wet vac or carpet extractor for deeply saturated areas, as it can help remove more urine and cleaning solution.
- Ensure your cat's litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing to prevent future accidents. Sometimes, a change in litter type or box location can help deter inappropriate urination.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell is similar to urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot, perpetuating the problem.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently bond the odor to fibers and make it much harder to remove.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products, especially in enclosed spaces, to avoid inhaling strong fumes.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and all cleaning residues have been removed or are safe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains concentrated uric acid crystals, which are very difficult to break down with regular cleaners. These crystals can reactivate when exposed to moisture, causing the odor to return even after cleaning. Enzymatic cleaners are essential because they specifically break down these crystals and the odor-causing bacteria.

**Can I use homemade remedies like vinegar and baking soda alone?**

While vinegar and baking soda are excellent for neutralizing odors, they are generally not strong enough to completely eliminate the uric acid crystals in cat urine, especially in older or deeply saturated stains. They work best as a secondary treatment after an enzymatic cleaner has been applied or for very fresh, minor accidents.

**How do I find old, dried cat urine stains?**

The most effective way to find old, dried cat urine stains is by using a UV blacklight. Turn off all other lights in the room, and the urine will glow a yellowish-green under the UV light, even if it's invisible to the naked eye. This helps ensure you treat all affected areas thoroughly.

**What if the smell is in my couch or mattress?**

For couches and mattresses, the cleaning process is similar but requires extra care to avoid over-saturating the material. Apply enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine, then blot thoroughly. You might need to repeat the process and allow for extended drying times, possibly using fans or a dehumidifier.

**How long does it take for the smell to completely disappear?**

The time it takes for the smell to completely disappear depends on the severity and age of the stain, as well as the effectiveness of your cleaning. For fresh stains, it might be a day or two. For older, deeply embedded odors, it could take several days of repeated treatments and thorough drying. Patience is key.

**My cat keeps peeing in the same spot, what should I do?**

First, ensure the area is thoroughly cleaned to eliminate any residual scent that attracts them. Then, try placing their food bowl, toys, or even aluminum foil over the spot to deter them. Address any underlying issues like litter box cleanliness, location, type of litter, or consult a vet to rule out medical problems.

**Is it possible to permanently remove cat pee smell?**

Yes, it is absolutely possible to permanently remove cat pee smell, especially if you use the right approach. The key is to break down the uric acid crystals and odor-causing bacteria using an enzymatic cleaner. Consistent application and ensuring the area is completely dry are crucial for permanent elimination.
