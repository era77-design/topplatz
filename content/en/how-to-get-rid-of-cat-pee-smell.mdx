To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by blotting up fresh urine and then treating the area with an enzymatic cleaner. These cleaners contain special enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, making them highly effective. Proper ventilation and repeat treatments are key to ensuring complete odor elimination.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- Rubber gloves
- Old towel or plastic sheet (optional)
- Brush (for carpet fibers, optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Vacuum cleaner (optional)
- UV blacklight (for old stains, optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Blot the Stain Immediately

The quicker you address a cat pee stain, the easier it is to remove the odor. Use paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth to blot the area, pressing firmly to soak up as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers. If the stain is old, use a UV blacklight in a darkened room to pinpoint its exact location, as dried urine crystals glow under UV light.

### Step 2: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner Liberally

Once you've blotted the excess urine, liberally apply an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the source of the persistent odor. Make sure to saturate the area thoroughly, extending beyond the visible stain to treat any spread urine.

### Step 3: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell

For the enzymatic cleaner to work effectively, it needs sufficient contact time. Let the cleaner sit on the affected area for the duration recommended by the manufacturer, often 10-30 minutes, or even longer for severe odors. For carpets or upholstery, you might cover the area with plastic wrap to prevent it from drying out too quickly, allowing the enzymes more time to penetrate. For a thorough approach, see our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

### Step 4: Blot and Clean the Area

After the recommended dwell time, use a clean, damp cloth or paper towels to blot up the excess cleaner and any remaining urine residue. For carpets, you can gently scrub with a soft brush to work the cleaner deeper into the fibers before blotting. Rinse the cloth frequently to avoid redepositing the urine or cleaner onto the surface.

### Step 5: Air Dry and Ventilate Thoroughly

Allow the treated area to air dry completely. Good ventilation is crucial to help dissipate any remaining moisture and odors. Open windows, use fans, or run an air purifier in the room to speed up the drying process. Ensure the area is fully dry before allowing pets back on it to prevent them from being attracted to residual moisture. ### Step 6: Reassess and Repeat if Necessary

Once the area is completely dry, perform a sniff test to check if the odor is gone. Cat urine odor can be very persistent, especially if it has soaked deep into materials. If a faint smell remains, repeat the entire process from applying the enzymatic cleaner. Multiple treatments may be necessary for older or heavily saturated stains to achieve complete odor elimination. For stubborn cases, our article [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-effectively) offers further insights.

### Step 7: Address the Root Cause of Accidents

While cleaning is essential, preventing future accidents is key to a fresh-smelling home. Consult with your veterinarian to rule out any underlying medical issues contributing to inappropriate urination. Ensure your cat has a clean litter box, enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), and that they are in accessible, stress-free locations. Stress, changes in routine, or territory marking can also lead to accidents.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test cleaners on an inconspicuous area first to check for colorfastness.
- Avoid using ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the same spot.
- For very stubborn odors on carpets, consider using a wet-vac or steam cleaner *after* applying an enzymatic cleaner, but ensure the cleaner has had enough time to work first.
- Sprinkle baking soda over the dried area after treatment and let it sit for several hours (or overnight) before vacuuming to absorb any residual odors.
- Consider using a pet-specific odor eliminator spray as a final touch after the area is completely dry.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear rubber gloves when handling cat urine and cleaning products.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products.
- ⚠️ Keep cleaning products out of reach of children and pets.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different cleaning products, especially ammonia and bleach, as this can create dangerous fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals that bind to surfaces and become reactivated by moisture, continuously releasing a strong, unpleasant odor. These crystals are not easily removed by water or standard cleaners alone, requiring specific enzymatic solutions.

**Can I use vinegar or baking soda instead of enzymatic cleaner?**

While vinegar and baking soda can help neutralize some odors, they typically don't break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine as effectively as enzymatic cleaners. For complete and permanent odor elimination, enzymatic cleaners are usually the most reliable solution.

**How do I find old, invisible cat urine stains?**

Use a UV (blacklight) flashlight in a darkened room. Dried cat urine crystals will glow a yellowish-green under the UV light, making them visible even on surfaces where they've long since dried and are otherwise undetectable.

**Is it safe to use enzymatic cleaners around pets?**

Most enzymatic cleaners are formulated to be pet-safe once dry, but always check the product label for specific instructions and warnings. It's generally best to keep pets away from the treated area until it's completely dry and any residue has been blotted away.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure multiple clean litter boxes (one per cat + one extra), use preferred litter, and place boxes in quiet, accessible locations. Rule out medical issues with a vet, and address any stress or territorial concerns that might be contributing to the behavior.

**What if the smell is in concrete or wood floors?**

For concrete, heavy saturation with an enzymatic cleaner followed by thorough drying can work, potentially requiring multiple applications. For wood, the urine may have soaked deeply, possibly requiring sanding and sealing, or even replacement of affected boards in severe cases, after initial enzymatic treatment.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to go away completely?**

With immediate and proper enzymatic treatment, the smell can be significantly reduced or eliminated within hours to a day. For older, deeply saturated stains, multiple treatments over several days may be required for complete and permanent odor removal.
