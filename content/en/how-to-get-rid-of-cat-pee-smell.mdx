To get rid of cat pee smell, start by locating the source using a black light, then blot up as much urine as possible. Treat the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor. Repeat treatment if necessary, ensuring the area is completely dry afterward.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner specifically for pet odors
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Black light (UV flashlight) - optional but highly recommended
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Plastic sheeting or foil (to prevent re-soiling)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source

The first crucial step is to accurately find all affected areas, as cat urine can spread and seep into underlying materials. Use your nose, but for hidden or old stains, a black light (UV flashlight) in a darkened room will make urine glow, revealing the exact spots. This is especially important for carpets and upholstery.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check walls, furniture legs, and even curtains, as cats can spray in unexpected places.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine

If the stain is fresh, act immediately to absorb as much urine as possible. Put on rubber gloves and use a stack of paper towels or clean, absorbent rags. Press firmly onto the wet spot, standing on the towels if needed, and replace them until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine deeper into the fibers.

> 💡 **Tip:** The more urine you can blot up manually, the less work the cleaner has to do, leading to faster and more effective odor removal.

### Step 3: Rinse the Area (Optional, for carpets)

For carpets and some upholstery, after blotting, you can gently rinse the area with a small amount of clean, cool water. This helps dilute the remaining urine. Blot dry again thoroughly, repeating the blotting process until the area is as dry as possible. This step helps remove soluble urine components before applying cleaners.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are essential because they contain beneficial bacteria that consume the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering cat pee smell. Saturate the affected area generously, ensuring the cleaner reaches as deep as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully for application and dwell time. For a comprehensive approach, consider reading [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not use steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor.

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

Let the enzymatic cleaner air dry completely, which can take several hours or even a day or two depending on humidity and ventilation. It's crucial for the enzymes to work their magic. While drying, keep pets away from the treated area, perhaps by covering it with an upside-down laundry basket or aluminum foil.

> 💡 **Tip:** Adequate ventilation, such as opening windows or using a fan, will speed up the drying process and help dissipate any remaining fumes.

### Step 6: Re-treat if Necessary

Stubborn odors or deeply saturated areas may require a second or even third application of the enzymatic cleaner. Re-evaluate the area once it's completely dry. If you still detect a faint odor, repeat the application process from step 4, allowing for full drying between treatments. Persistence is key for complete odor elimination. For persistent odors or older stains, you might find more advanced strategies in [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently).

> 💡 **Tip:** Sometimes, the odor seems to disappear only to return with humidity; complete dryness and enzyme action are vital.

### Step 7: Address Hard Surfaces and Prevent Future Accidents

For hard surfaces like tile, wood, or linoleum, wipe up fresh urine, then clean with a mixture of 1 part white vinegar to 1 part water, or an enzymatic cleaner. To prevent future accidents, ensure litter boxes are clean, accessible, and plentiful (one per cat plus one extra). Address any underlying behavioral or medical issues with your veterinarian.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider using a litter box attractant or trying different types of litter if your cat is particular.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area of the material (carpet, fabric, wood) to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ventilate thoroughly by opening windows and using fans during and after cleaning to air out the space and help dissipate odors and cleaning solution fumes.
- For severe, pervasive, or long-standing cat urine odors, especially those that have soaked into subflooring or walls, professional cleaning and restoration services may be necessary.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never mix cleaning products, especially ammonia-based cleaners with bleach, as this can create dangerous toxic fumes. Cat urine contains ammonia, so be cautious when cleaning.
- ⚠️ Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently bond the uric acid to the fibers, making the odor impossible to remove.
- ⚠️ Ensure treated areas are completely dry before allowing pets back to prevent them from being attracted to residual moisture or cleaner residue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and become reactivated by moisture. These crystals bind to surfaces and release strong ammonia-like odors, making them notoriously difficult to eliminate without specialized cleaners.

**Can I use regular household cleaners like bleach or ammonia?**

No, you should avoid bleach and ammonia-based cleaners. Ammonia is a component of urine, so using ammonia cleaners can actually attract your cat back to the spot. Bleach can be dangerous when mixed with urine (which contains ammonia) and may damage surfaces.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine, effectively neutralizing the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**What if the smell is in my subfloor or walls?**

If the urine has soaked into porous materials like subfloor, drywall, or wood, surface cleaning may not be enough. You might need to seal the affected areas with a specialized odor-blocking primer or even replace the saturated materials to fully eliminate the smell.

**How do I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box again?**

To prevent future accidents, ensure you have enough clean litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), consider litter type and box location, and rule out medical issues with a vet. Stress or behavioral problems can also be causes, which a cat behaviorist can help address.

**Does white vinegar really help with cat pee smell?**

Yes, white vinegar can be effective for initial cleaning and neutralizing some of the alkaline salts in cat urine due to its acidic nature. However, it doesn't break down the uric acid crystals like enzymatic cleaners do, so it's best used as a pre-treatment or for mild, fresh stains.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to disappear after cleaning?**

The time it takes for the smell to disappear depends on the severity of the stain, the effectiveness of the cleaning, and the drying time. With proper use of enzymatic cleaners, the odor should significantly diminish as the area dries, typically within a few days. Persistent odors may require re-treatment.
