To get rid of cat pee smell effectively, start by locating the source and blotting up as much urine as possible. Then, apply an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Allow the cleaner to penetrate and air dry completely for the best results.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic cleaner (pet specific)
- Spray bottle (optional, for DIY solutions)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Old toothbrush or scrub brush
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)
- Black light (UV flashlight, optional for locating stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Locate and Contain the Source

The first crucial step is to accurately find all affected areas. Cat urine can soak deep into carpets, padding, and even subflooring, so a thorough search is essential. A black light (UV flashlight) can be extremely helpful in a darkened room, as dried urine stains will fluoresce. Once located, contain the area to prevent your cat from re-soiling the spot.

### Step 2: 2. Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh urine, put on rubber gloves and use paper towels or old rags to blot up as much liquid as possible. Press firmly and repeatedly, replacing saturated towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into the material and spread the smell. This initial blotting is vital for preventing the odor from setting in.

### Step 3: 3. Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine that cause the persistent odor. Saturate the soiled area generously with the cleaner, making sure to cover the full extent of the stain and any areas where urine may have seeped. Follow the product instructions for application and dwell time. For a deeper clean and to ensure permanent removal, consider exploring more comprehensive solutions found in guides like [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently).

### Step 4: 4. Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Air Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit for the recommended time, usually 10-30 minutes, or even longer for severe stains. This allows the enzymes to work their magic and break down the odor-causing compounds. Do not rinse immediately. After the dwell time, blot up any excess liquid, then allow the area to air dry completely. This might take several hours or even a day, depending on humidity and material.

### Step 5: 5. Deodorize with Baking Soda

Once the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any remaining odors. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, to maximize its effectiveness. You can also mix baking soda with a few drops of essential oil (like lavender or tea tree) for an extra fresh scent, if desired.

### Step 6: 6. Vacuum Thoroughly

After the baking soda has had ample time to absorb odors, vacuum it up thoroughly. Make sure to use a strong vacuum cleaner to remove all baking soda particles from carpets or upholstery. For hard surfaces, a damp cloth can be used to wipe away the residue. This step helps to physically remove any remaining odor-causing particles and leave the area clean.

### Step 7: 7. Repeat if Necessary

Stubborn or old cat urine smells may require multiple treatments. If the odor persists after the first attempt, repeat the entire process, focusing on saturating the area well with the enzymatic cleaner and allowing it to dry completely. Persistence is key for truly eliminating the smell. For more immediate action on fresh stains, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: Quick & Effective Solutions](/en/how-to-remove-cat-pee-smell-quickly).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ventilate the area by opening windows or using fans to speed up drying and help dissipate odors.
- Consider placing aluminum foil or plastic over the cleaned area temporarily to deter your cat from re-soiling the spot while it dries.
- For very old or deeply soaked stains, you may need to lift carpets to treat the padding and subfloor directly, or consult a professional cleaner.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use bleach or ammonia-based cleaners on cat urine. Ammonia is a component of urine and can actually attract your cat to re-mark the same spot, making the problem worse.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on fresh urine stains, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into the fabric.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly pungent and do not dissolve easily in water. These crystals react with moisture in the air, reactivating the strong odor over time, making it seem like the smell never truly goes away.

**Can I use white vinegar to get rid of cat pee smell?**

Yes, white vinegar is effective for neutralizing the alkaline salts in cat urine. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water, spray the area, let it sit for 15-30 minutes, then blot dry. It's a good preliminary step before using an enzymatic cleaner or for minor odors.

**How long does it take for cat pee smell to go away naturally?**

Cat pee smell rarely goes away naturally without proper treatment, especially if the uric acid crystals are not broken down. The odor can persist for months or even years, reactivating with humidity, until thoroughly cleaned with enzymatic solutions.

**What surfaces are hardest to clean cat pee from?**

Porous surfaces like unsealed wood, concrete, and deeply padded carpets with multiple layers are the hardest. Urine can soak deep into these materials, requiring extensive treatment or even replacement of affected sections.

**Will enzyme cleaners work on old, dried cat pee stains?**

Yes, enzyme cleaners are specifically designed to break down the uric acid crystals found in both fresh and old cat urine. For older stains, you may need to re-saturate the area multiple times and allow for longer dwell times to be effective.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing to your cat. Provide enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), try different litter types, and rule out any underlying medical issues with a vet visit. Reducing stress can also help.
