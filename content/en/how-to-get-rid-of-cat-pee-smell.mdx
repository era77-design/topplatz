To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, the key is to act quickly and use enzymatic cleaners that break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor. Thoroughly blot fresh urine, apply the cleaner, and allow it to air dry completely for the best results, ensuring the smell is gone for good.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- Gloves
- Plastic wrap or foil (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Vacuum cleaner
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating old stains)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

Even if you think you know where the accident happened, cat urine can spread or soak into unexpected areas. Use your nose to sniff out the strongest odor, or for older, dried stains, a UV blacklight in a dark room can make the urine glow. This step is crucial for ensuring you treat the entire affected area and don't miss any hidden spots.

> 💡 **Tip:** Mark the edges of the stain with chalk or tape to ensure complete coverage during cleaning.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is your ally in preventing the smell from setting in. Place several layers of paper towels or clean rags over the wet spot and press down firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into the material. Repeat with fresh towels until no more moisture transfers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels for extra pressure to draw out maximum liquid from carpets or upholstery.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine that cause the lingering odor. Follow the product instructions carefully, typically by saturating the soiled area. Ensure the cleaner reaches as deep as the urine penetrated, especially in carpets and padding, for thorough odor elimination. For more in-depth solutions, consider reading our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-complete-guide).

> 💡 **Tip:** Test the cleaner on an inconspicuous spot first to ensure it doesn't discolor the material.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Work and Air Dry

Most enzymatic cleaners require time to work their magic. Cover the treated area with plastic wrap or foil to keep it moist, preventing the cleaner from evaporating too quickly, which allows the enzymes more time to break down the urine. Let it sit for the recommended time, often several hours or overnight. After the waiting period, remove the covering and allow the area to air dry completely.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a fan near the treated area to speed up the drying process and improve air circulation.

### Step 5: Address Lingering Odors (Optional)

Once the area is completely dry, if a faint odor persists, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the spot. Baking soda is an excellent natural deodorizer that can absorb residual smells. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight, then vacuum it up thoroughly. This can be a great final step for persistent smells.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very stubborn odors, you might need to repeat the enzymatic cleaner application process, as outlined in guides like [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

### Step 6: Prevent Future Accidents

After cleaning, it's essential to address the root cause of the peeing behavior to prevent recurrence. Ensure your cat's litter box is clean and accessible, and consider if there are any stressors in their environment. Providing multiple litter boxes, especially in multi-cat households, can also help. Thorough cleaning is vital to remove scent markers that might attract your cat back to the same spot.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a litter box or a food bowl near the previously soiled area temporarily to deter your cat from using it as a toilet again.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed for pet urine, as general household cleaners may only mask the smell or set the stain.
- Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain.
- Ensure proper ventilation when cleaning by opening windows or using fans.
- Consider professional cleaning for severe or widespread urine contamination, especially on carpets with thick padding.
- Don't punish your cat for accidents; instead, focus on identifying and resolving the underlying cause of the inappropriate urination.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always read and follow the instructions and safety warnings on all cleaning products.
- ⚠️ Wear gloves to protect your hands when handling urine and cleaning agents.
- ⚠️ Keep cleaning products out of reach of children and pets.
- ⚠️ Avoid mixing different cleaning chemicals, as this can produce dangerous fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are very difficult to break down with water or regular cleaners. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong ammonia-like odor that can persist for a long time if not properly treated.

**Can I use vinegar or baking soda instead of an enzymatic cleaner?**

While vinegar and baking soda can help with general odors, they are not as effective as enzymatic cleaners for breaking down uric acid crystals in cat urine. They might mask the smell temporarily but often won't eliminate it permanently at the molecular level.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a mattress or couch?**

For mattresses and couches, the process is similar: blot thoroughly, then saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine. Allow it to air dry completely, using fans if possible. You may need to repeat the process multiple times for complete odor removal.

**My cat keeps peeing in the same spot, even after I clean it. What should I do?**

This often indicates that the previous cleaning wasn't thorough enough, and residual scent attracts the cat back. Ensure you're using an enzymatic cleaner effectively. Also, investigate potential behavioral or medical reasons with your vet, and consider making the spot less appealing by placing food or a litter box there temporarily.

**Will the smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with proper and persistent treatment using enzymatic cleaners, the smell can be completely eliminated. The key is to break down the uric acid crystals, not just mask them. It might take several applications and patience, but permanent removal is achievable.

**How can I find old, dried cat pee stains?**

Old, dried cat pee stains are often invisible to the naked eye. The most effective way to locate them is by using a UV blacklight in a darkened room. The uric acid crystals in the urine will fluoresce, appearing as yellowish-green spots that are easy to identify.

**Is it safe to use enzymatic cleaners around pets?**

Most enzymatic cleaners designed for pet odors are safe to use around pets once they are dry. However, it's always best to keep pets away from the treated area while it's wet and ventilate the space well. Always check the product's specific safety instructions for guidance.
