To get rid of cat pee smell, you need to neutralize the uric acid crystals and bacteria responsible for the pungent odor. This involves locating the source, thoroughly cleaning the area, and applying an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down the urine components. Patience and proper technique are key to permanently eliminating the smell.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- UV blacklight (highly recommended)
- Enzymatic cleaner specifically for pet urine
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Clean water
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil
- Spray bottle

## Steps

### Step 1: Pinpoint the Odor Source

Even dried cat urine can emit a strong odor. To find invisible stains, dim the lights and use a UV blacklight, which will make dried urine glow. Carefully mark all affected areas on carpets, upholstery, and walls, as you'll need to treat every spot to truly get rid of the cat pee smell.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check vertical surfaces like baseboards and furniture legs, as cats often spray upwards.

### Step 2: Absorb Fresh Urine Quickly

If the urine is still wet, act immediately. Don your rubber gloves and use several layers of paper towels or old rags to blot up as much of the liquid as possible. Press down firmly and replace towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with Vinegar Solution

After blotting, mix a solution of equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Spray the affected area generously, ensuring it saturates the spot. Vinegar helps to neutralize the alkaline salts in cat urine and also acts as a natural deodorizer. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes before blotting it dry.

> 💡 **Tip:** Vinegar is safe for most surfaces but always test in an inconspicuous area first.

### Step 4: Apply Specialized Enzymatic Cleaner

This is the most crucial step to get rid of cat pee smell permanently. Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that break down the uric acid crystals, which are the main cause of the lingering odor. Follow the product instructions carefully, saturating the area thoroughly. For more detailed guidance on selecting and using these products, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner makes contact with all the urine, even if it has soaked deep into carpet padding or upholstery.

### Step 5: Allow for Adequate Dwell Time and Drying

Enzymatic cleaners need time to work their magic. Leave the cleaner on for the recommended duration, often several hours or even overnight. To prevent your cat from re-soiling the area while it's drying, cover it with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil. Air drying is essential for the enzymes to fully break down the odor-causing molecules.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a fan or open windows to improve air circulation and speed up the drying process.

### Step 6: Rinse and Deodorize with Baking Soda

Once the area is completely dry, you can lightly rinse it with clean water and blot again if desired, although many enzymatic cleaners don't require rinsing. For an extra layer of odor absorption, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the dried area. Let it sit for several hours or overnight, then vacuum it up.

### Step 7: Re-Treat Stubborn Odors (If Needed)

If a faint odor persists after the first treatment, it likely means some urine was missed or the cleaner didn't fully penetrate. Repeat steps 1-6, paying extra attention to saturation and dwell time. For very old or deep stains, multiple applications may be necessary to completely eliminate the smell. Consider consulting a professional cleaner for extensive damage.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act Quickly: The sooner you address a fresh cat urine stain, the easier it will be to remove the odor permanently.
- Test Cleaners: Always test any cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Prevent Re-soiling: After cleaning, make the area undesirable for your cat by placing toys, food bowls, or furniture over it temporarily, or using pet deterrent sprays.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Avoid Ammonia-Based Cleaners: Ammonia is a component of urine and can actually attract your cat back to the same spot to urinate again.
- ⚠️ Do Not Use Steam Cleaners: The heat from steam cleaners can permanently set the cat urine proteins and odor into carpet fibers and upholstery, making it much harder to remove.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and persist for so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, urea, and pheromones. While urea breaks down quickly, the uric acid crystals are insoluble and reactivate with moisture, continuously releasing a pungent, ammonia-like odor that can last for years if not properly neutralized.

**What is an enzymatic cleaner and why is it important for cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain live bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine into harmless, odorless compounds, effectively eliminating the source of the smell rather than just masking it.

**Can I use home remedies like vinegar and baking soda alone to get rid of cat pee smell?**

While vinegar and baking soda are excellent for neutralizing odors and are good initial steps, they often cannot completely break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine. For a permanent solution, especially for dried or old stains, an enzymatic cleaner is usually essential.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to go away after cleaning?**

The initial strong odor should dissipate relatively quickly after applying an enzymatic cleaner. However, the full process of the enzymes breaking down all the uric acid crystals and for the area to completely dry can take anywhere from a few hours to a few days.

**What if the smell persists even after multiple treatments?**

Persistent odors usually mean the urine has penetrated very deeply (e.g., into subflooring or furniture padding), or not all affected areas were treated. Consider using a stronger enzymatic cleaner, repeating the process with longer dwell times, or consulting a professional odor removal specialist.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Address the underlying cause. Ensure the litter box is clean, accessible, and there are enough boxes (one per cat plus one extra). Rule out medical issues with a vet visit. Reduce stress and try different litter types or box styles.

**Is the smell of cat urine harmful to humans?**

While the strong odor of cat urine, particularly the ammonia, can be irritating to the respiratory system, especially for those with asthma or allergies, it's generally not considered acutely harmful in typical household concentrations. Prolonged exposure to very high concentrations in poorly ventilated areas could potentially be problematic.
