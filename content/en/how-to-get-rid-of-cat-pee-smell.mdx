To get rid of cat pee smell, the most effective method involves immediately blotting up fresh urine and then saturating the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner. These specialized cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the persistent and strong odor. For older, set-in stains, multiple applications may be necessary to fully neutralize the smell.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Stiff brush (for carpets/fabric)
- Plastic sheeting or foil (optional)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)
- UV black light (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate All Affected Areas

Even if you've found one spot, it's crucial to check for others, especially for older, dried urine. Use a UV black light in a dark room to reveal hidden urine stains, which will glow yellowish-green. Mark all identified spots with tape or chalk so you don't miss any during cleaning. This step is vital for complete odor elimination.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, put on rubber gloves and use paper towels or old rags to blot up as much urine as possible. Press firmly, replacing towels as they become saturated, until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into the material fibers. The quicker you act, the easier it will be to neutralize the odor.

### Step 3: Apply Enzymatic Cleaner

Saturate the soiled area thoroughly with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor cleaner, following the product's instructions carefully. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the source of the persistent smell. Ensure the cleaner reaches as deep as the urine penetrated to effectively neutralize the odor at its source. For deeper insights into effective and permanent solutions, refer to our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

### Step 4: Allow Cleaner to Dwell and Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner sit for the recommended time, usually 10-30 minutes, or even longer for severe odors. For carpets or upholstery, you can cover the area with plastic sheeting or foil to prevent it from drying out too quickly, allowing the enzymes to work longer. Do not rinse the cleaner off immediately; it needs time to break down the urine components. Allow the area to air dry completely, which can take several hours or even a day.

### Step 5: Rinse and Extract (Optional)

Once the enzymatic cleaner has worked and the area is dry, you can rinse the spot with a small amount of clean water and blot it dry. For carpets or upholstery, a wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner can be used to extract the cleaner and any residual urine, but ensure you don't use hot water, as heat can set the stain. Always make sure the area is completely dry afterward.

### Step 6: Apply Baking Soda for Odor Absorption

Once the area is completely dry from the enzymatic cleaner, sprinkle a generous layer of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is excellent at absorbing residual odors. Let it sit for several hours, or ideally overnight, to draw out any remaining smells. This step helps ensure a fresh and clean scent.

### Step 7: Vacuum Thoroughly

After the baking soda has had ample time to absorb odors, vacuum it up thoroughly. Make sure to go over the area multiple times to remove all baking soda particles. If any faint odor persists, you may need to repeat the enzymatic cleaning and baking soda steps. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ensure the treated area is completely dry before allowing pets or children back onto it.
- Ventilate the area well by opening windows or using fans during and after cleaning.
- Consider addressing the root cause of the peeing, such as a trip to the vet for medical issues or reviewing litter box hygiene and placement.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as ammonia is a component of urine and can attract your cat to pee in the same spot again.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical cleaners or bleach on cat urine, as they can damage surfaces, create toxic fumes, and are generally ineffective against the uric acid crystals.
- ⚠️ Do not use steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into the fabric or carpet fibers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so difficult to get rid of?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals that are insoluble and bond strongly to surfaces. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing the strong, unpleasant odor, making it challenging to eliminate without specific enzymatic cleaners.

**Does white vinegar effectively remove cat pee smell?**

White vinegar can help neutralize some of the ammonia components in cat urine, offering temporary relief. However, it does not break down the uric acid crystals, so it's less effective than enzymatic cleaners for complete and permanent odor removal.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes break down the uric acid, urea, and other organic compounds in cat urine into harmless gases and liquids, effectively eliminating the odor at its source.

**What if the cat pee smell is old and set-in?**

For old, set-in smells, you may need multiple applications of an enzymatic cleaner. Ensure deep saturation and allow extended dwell times for the enzymes to fully penetrate and break down the concentrated uric acid crystals. A UV black light is essential to find all hidden spots.

**Can cat pee smell be permanently removed?**

Yes, cat pee smell can be permanently removed by thoroughly breaking down the uric acid crystals. This requires using effective enzymatic cleaners and ensuring the entire affected area, including padding or subflooring, is treated and allowed to dry completely.

**What surfaces are hardest to clean cat pee from?**

Porous materials like carpet, upholstery, mattresses, and untreated wood are the most challenging. Urine can soak deep into these materials, requiring extensive treatment to reach and neutralize all the embedded uric acid crystals.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Prevention involves ensuring litter boxes are clean, accessible, and sufficient in number (one per cat plus one extra). Rule out medical issues with a vet visit, consider stress factors, and use deterrents or make soiled areas unattractive by thoroughly cleaning them.
