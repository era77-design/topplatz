To get rid of cat pee smell, you need to locate the source, blot up as much urine as possible, and then treat the area thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner. This process breaks down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, ensuring the smell is truly eliminated, not just masked. Acting quickly is key to preventing the odor from setting in permanently.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Old toothbrush or scrub brush
- UV blacklight (optional, for locating stains)
- Plastic sheeting or aluminum foil

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Urine Stain

Before you can clean, you must find all affected areas. Cat urine can soak into surprising places, and the smell can linger if even a small spot is missed. Use your nose to sniff out faint odors, or for dried stains, a UV blacklight in a darkened room can reveal hidden urine spots that glow yellow or green. Mark these areas with chalk or tape for easy identification.

### Step 2: Blot Up Excess Urine Immediately

As soon as you find a fresh stain, put on rubber gloves and cover the area with several layers of paper towels or an old, absorbent rag. Press down firmly to absorb as much urine as possible, standing on the towels if necessary. Continue blotting with fresh towels until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into fibers.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are essential because they contain beneficial bacteria that consume the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the primary cause of the strong, lingering odor. Generously saturate the stained area with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine penetrated. For a truly effective and lasting removal, it's crucial to use these specialized cleaners. For more in-depth methods on permanent removal, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Dry

Follow the product instructions for dwell time, which is typically 10-15 minutes or longer. During this period, the enzymes are actively breaking down the urine. To prevent your cat from re-soiling the treated area while it's wet, cover it with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil. Allow the area to air dry completely, which might take several hours or even a day, depending on the saturation and humidity.

### Step 5: Rinse and Air Dry Thoroughly

Once the enzymatic cleaner has had sufficient time to work and the area is mostly dry, lightly rinse the spot with clean water using a damp cloth, blotting to remove any residue. Avoid oversaturating. For carpets or upholstery, place a clean, dry towel over the area and weigh it down to absorb remaining moisture. Ensure the area dries completely to prevent mildew.

### Step 6: Deodorize with Baking Soda (Optional)

If a faint odor persists after the area is completely dry, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated spot. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that can absorb any remaining smells. Let it sit for several hours or overnight, then vacuum it up thoroughly. This step can provide an extra layer of freshness, especially on carpets and fabrics.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Act quickly: The sooner you address a fresh urine stain, the easier it will be to remove the odor completely.
- Address the root cause: If your cat is repeatedly urinating outside the litter box, consult your vet to rule out medical issues or consider behavioral factors.
- Ensure good ventilation while cleaning by opening windows or using fans.
- For severe or old stains, multiple applications of enzymatic cleaner may be necessary for complete odor elimination.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent is similar to cat urine and can attract your cat to re-mark the same spot.
- ⚠️ Avoid mixing different cleaning products, especially bleach, as this can create dangerous fumes.
- ⚠️ Do not rub urine stains; always blot to prevent spreading the odor and pushing the urine deeper into surfaces.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and become more pungent as they break down over time. These crystals bind to surfaces, making the smell very difficult to remove without specialized cleaners.

**Will cat pee smell ever go away on its own?**

No, cat pee smell will not go away on its own. The uric acid crystals will reactivate with humidity, causing the odor to return, often stronger than before. Proper cleaning with enzymatic cleaners is essential for permanent removal.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat urine. Bleach can react with ammonia in urine to create dangerous, toxic fumes. It also doesn't effectively break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor.

**What if the smell is still there after cleaning?**

If the smell persists, it usually means the urine has penetrated deeper than you treated, or some crystals remain. Reapply the enzymatic cleaner generously, ensuring it saturates the entire affected area and let it dwell for longer periods.

**How do I stop my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

First, consult a vet to rule out medical issues. Then, consider behavioral factors like litter box cleanliness, type of litter, number of litter boxes, stress, or location. Addressing these can help prevent future accidents.

**How long does it take for the cat pee smell to disappear completely?**

With proper enzymatic cleaning, the smell should begin to dissipate within hours to a day after the first treatment. For older or deeply saturated stains, complete odor elimination might take a few days and potentially multiple applications.

**Can enzymatic cleaners be used on all surfaces?**

Most enzymatic cleaners are safe for carpets, upholstery, and hard surfaces. However, always test on an inconspicuous area first, especially on delicate fabrics or finishes, to ensure no discoloration or damage occurs.
