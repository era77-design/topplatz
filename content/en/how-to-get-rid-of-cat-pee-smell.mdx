Getting rid of cat pee smell can be challenging due to its potent, crystal-forming nature, but it's entirely possible with the right approach. The key is to act quickly and use enzymatic cleaners to break down the urine's odor-causing compounds effectively. This guide will walk you through the steps to eliminate the smell from various surfaces.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Plastic wrap or foil (optional)
- UV blacklight (optional, for finding hidden spots)
- Carpet brush or stiff scrub brush (for carpets)
- Wet vac or carpet cleaner (optional, for deeper cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Odor

Begin by thoroughly inspecting the area where you suspect the cat has urinated. Fresh spots are easier to deal with, but dried urine can be invisible. For hidden or old stains, a UV blacklight can be incredibly helpful in revealing dried urine, which glows under its light. Check carpets, upholstery, walls, and even hard floors, as urine can seep into grout or floorboards.

> 💡 **Tip:** Sometimes, the smell is stronger than the visual evidence, so trust your nose!

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

As soon as you discover a fresh accident, don rubber gloves and blot as much of the urine as possible using paper towels or old rags. Press down firmly to absorb the liquid from carpets or fabrics. Do not rub the stain, as this can push the urine deeper into the fibers and spread the odor. Continue blotting until the area feels almost dry.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels for extra pressure to draw out more liquid.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are essential for getting rid of cat pee smell because they contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals and proteins in cat urine, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Saturate the affected area generously with a high-quality enzymatic pet odor remover. Be sure to follow the product's instructions carefully for best results. > 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner fully saturates the area, reaching as deep as the urine has penetrated.

### Step 4: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell and Work

For the enzymatic cleaner to be effective, it needs sufficient time to break down the urine compounds. Cover the treated area with plastic wrap or foil to prevent it from drying out too quickly, especially on carpets or upholstery. Allow the cleaner to sit for at least 10-15 minutes, or even several hours for older, more stubborn odors, as recommended by the product's instructions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Resist the urge to scrub vigorously at this stage; let the enzymes do their job.

### Step 5: Blot and Air Dry the Area

After the recommended dwell time, remove the plastic covering and blot the area again with clean paper towels or a clean rag to absorb any remaining moisture and cleaner. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so strong and hard to remove?**

Cat urine contains highly concentrated urea, uric acid, creatinine, and pheromones. When it dries, the uric acid crystallizes and bonds to surfaces, making it very difficult to remove with just water or standard cleaners. These crystals react with humidity, reactivating the strong, unpleasant odor.

**Can I use bleach to clean cat urine?**

No, you should never use bleach to clean cat urine. Mixing bleach with ammonia (a component of urine) creates toxic chloramine gas, which is very dangerous to inhale. Additionally, bleach doesn't effectively break down the uric acid crystals that cause the persistent odor.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a mattress?**

To get cat pee smell out of a mattress, blot up as much urine as possible, then generously spray an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak deep into the mattress. Cover with plastic to prevent drying, let it sit for several hours, then blot dry. Finish by sprinkling baking soda to absorb residual odors before vacuuming.

**What if the cat pee smell returns after cleaning?**

If the smell returns, it usually means the urine wasn't fully neutralized or you missed some spots. The uric acid crystals can reactivate with moisture. Re-treat the area with a high-quality enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it saturates deeply enough to reach all the urine, and allow ample dwell time before drying.

**How do I find old, invisible cat urine stains?**

Old, invisible cat urine stains can be located using a UV blacklight in a darkened room. The uric acid crystals in dried cat urine will fluoresce and glow a yellowish-green under the blacklight, making them visible and allowing you to target your cleaning efforts precisely.

**Is white vinegar safe for all surfaces when cleaning cat urine?**

White vinegar is generally safe for most surfaces, especially hard floors and washable fabrics, as it's a mild acid that neutralizes alkaline urine odors. However, it's always wise to test it on an inconspicuous area first, especially on delicate fabrics, natural stone, or sealed wood, to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

**How often should I clean the litter box to prevent accidents outside of it?**

To prevent accidents, scoop the litter box at least once daily, and completely change the litter and clean the box with mild soap and water weekly. Some cats prefer even more frequent cleaning. A dirty litter box is a common reason for cats to urinate elsewhere, so maintaining a pristine litter environment is crucial.
