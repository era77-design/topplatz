To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, immediately blot the affected area to remove as much urine as possible, then apply an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals. For a truly lasting solution, it's crucial to ensure the cleaner penetrates deeply into the material. ## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle
- Black light (optional, for hidden stains)
- Stiff brush or scrub brush (for carpets)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Act Quickly and Blot the Area

The sooner you address a fresh cat urine stain, the easier it will be to remove the odor. Put on rubber gloves and use several layers of paper towels or old rags to blot up as much of the urine as possible. Press down firmly to absorb liquid from deep within carpets or upholstery, but avoid rubbing the stain, as this can spread it and push it deeper into the fibers.

### Step 2: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are essential because they contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes to break down uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the primary cause of the lingering odor. Follow the product's instructions carefully for application, ensuring the cleaner saturates the area as deeply as the urine did. For a comprehensive approach to tackling these odors, you might find our article [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide) helpful.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test the enzymatic cleaner on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage to your material.

### Step 3: Allow the Cleaner to Dwell

Give the enzymatic cleaner sufficient time to work. Most products recommend letting it sit for 10-15 minutes, or even longer for older or more severe stains. Covering the area with plastic wrap can help keep the cleaner moist and active for longer, enhancing its effectiveness in breaking down the odor-causing compounds.

### Step 4: Rinse and Blot Again

After the dwell time, use clean, damp paper towels or a cloth to blot up any remaining cleaner and moisture. You can lightly spray the area with clean water and then blot again to rinse thoroughly. If you have a wet/dry vacuum, it can be very effective for extracting moisture from carpets and upholstery, speeding up the drying process.

### Step 5: Address Lingering Odors with Vinegar & Baking Soda (Optional)

For particularly stubborn odors or as an extra measure, you can create a DIY treatment. After the enzymatic cleaner has been blotted up, spray the area with a mixture of equal parts white vinegar and water. Let it air dry, then generously sprinkle baking soda over the dried area. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that will absorb any remaining smells. Let it sit for several hours or overnight before vacuuming it up.

### Step 6: Ensure Thorough Drying

Proper drying is crucial to prevent mold or mildew, especially in carpets and upholstery. Open windows to improve ventilation, use fans, or even a dehumidifier to help the area dry completely. A damp environment can reactivate urine crystals or foster new odors, so patience during this stage is key.

### Step 7: Locate Hidden Stains with a Black Light (Optional)

If you can still smell cat pee but can't find the source, a black light (UV light) can be invaluable. Turn off all the lights in the room and scan surfaces; dried cat urine will glow yellowish-green under UV light. This allows you to pinpoint previously unnoticed soiled areas and treat them directly, ensuring no odor sources are missed.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always wear rubber gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands and prevent the spread of bacteria.
- For old, set-in stains, multiple treatments with an enzymatic cleaner may be necessary to fully eliminate the odor.
- Consider the underlying reason for your cat peeing outside the litter box, such as a medical issue (vet visit recommended) or litter box aversion (cleanliness, type, number of boxes).
- Ensure proper ventilation during and after cleaning by opening windows or using fans to air out the space.
- Avoid using steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fabrics and carpets.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as ammonia is a component of urine and can attract your cat to re-mark the same spot.
- ⚠️ Do not use bleach on cat urine stains, as it can create harmful fumes when mixed with ammonia and may damage surfaces.
- ⚠️ Always test cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous area first to prevent damage or discoloration.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is cat pee smell so hard to remove?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and become reactivated by moisture. These crystals bind to surfaces and release strong, persistent odors that standard cleaners cannot break down.

**Can I use household cleaners like dish soap or all-purpose cleaner?**

While these cleaners might temporarily mask the smell or clean the surface, they do not break down the uric acid crystals. This means the odor will likely return once the area gets damp again, as the crystals remain.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes specifically target and break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in cat urine, permanently eliminating the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**What if the cat pee smell is in concrete or wood?**

For porous surfaces like concrete or unfinished wood, the urine can soak deeply. You'll need to apply generous amounts of enzymatic cleaner and allow it to penetrate. For severe cases, sealing the surface after cleaning with an odor-blocking primer might be necessary.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

First, rule out medical issues with a vet visit. Then, ensure the litter box is impeccably clean, consider offering multiple litter boxes, try different types of litter, or move the box to a more private or accessible location. Reduce stress for your cat if possible.

**How long does cat pee smell last if not treated?**

If not properly treated, cat pee smell can last indefinitely. The uric acid crystals can remain dormant for years and reactivate whenever they come into contact with moisture, releasing the strong odor again.

**Is cat pee smell harmful to humans?**

While not directly toxic in small amounts, prolonged exposure to strong cat urine odors can cause respiratory irritation, headaches, and nausea in sensitive individuals. It can also contribute to an unhealthy indoor air quality.
