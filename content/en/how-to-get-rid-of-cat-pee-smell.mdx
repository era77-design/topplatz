To effectively get rid of cat pee smell, you need to use an enzymatic cleaner that breaks down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor. Start by locating the source, blotting up fresh urine, and then applying the cleaner as directed to neutralize the smell permanently.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor remover
- Stiff brush or old toothbrush (optional)
- Baking soda
- Vacuum cleaner
- Black light (optional)
- White vinegar (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source

Before you can effectively clean, you must find all affected areas. Fresh urine is usually easy to spot, but old, dried stains can be invisible. Use a black light in a darkened room to make dried urine glow, helping you pinpoint hidden spots on carpets, upholstery, and walls.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine

For fresh accidents, wear gloves and use several layers of paper towels or an old rag to blot up as much urine as possible. Press firmly, replacing towels as they become saturated, until no more moisture transfers. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into the fibers.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with Vinegar (Optional)

For particularly strong or older stains, a pre-treatment can be beneficial. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water, then lightly spray the affected area. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes. Vinegar helps neutralize some of the ammonia smell, but always follow up with an enzymatic cleaner. Test on an inconspicuous spot first.

### Step 4: Apply Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are crucial because they contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the main cause of the persistent odor. Saturate the stain thoroughly with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it reaches as deep as the urine did. For a comprehensive guide on choosing and using these products, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

> 💡 **Tip:** Always follow the specific instructions on your chosen enzymatic cleaner for best results, as dwell times can vary.

### Step 5: Allow to Dwell and Air Dry

Let the enzymatic cleaner dwell for the time recommended on its label, which can range from 15 minutes to several hours, or even overnight. It's essential not to rush this step, as the enzymes need time to work. Cover the area with plastic wrap to keep it moist and prevent pets from disturbing it, then allow it to air dry completely.

### Step 6: Remove Residue and Deodorize

Once the area is completely dry, you can blot any remaining residue with a clean, damp cloth. For extra deodorizing power, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda over the treated area. Let it sit for several hours or overnight to absorb any lingering odors, then vacuum it up thoroughly.

### Step 7: Address the Root Cause

To prevent future accidents, it's vital to understand why your cat is peeing outside the litter box. This could be due to a medical issue, litter box aversion (e.g., cleanliness, type of litter, location), stress, or territorial marking. Consult your vet to rule out health problems and consider behavioral solutions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are not water-soluble and bind to surfaces. As these crystals break down, they release thiols, the compounds responsible for the strong, pungent, and long-lasting odor.

**What is the best type of cleaner for cat pee?**

Enzymatic cleaners are the most effective because they contain specialized enzymes that chemically break down the uric acid crystals and other organic components in cat urine, neutralizing the odor at its source rather than just masking it.

**Can cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with the correct cleaning methods, especially using enzymatic cleaners, cat pee smell can be completely eliminated. The key is to thoroughly saturate the affected area to ensure the enzymes reach all the uric acid crystals.

**How can I find old, invisible cat pee stains?**

The most effective way to find old, dried cat pee stains is by using a UV black light in a darkened room. The uric acid crystals in the urine will fluoresce under the black light, making the stains visible.

**Is white vinegar effective at removing cat pee smell?**

White vinegar can help neutralize some of the ammonia components in cat urine and can be a good pre-treatment for fresh stains. However, it does not break down the uric acid crystals, so it should always be followed by an enzymatic cleaner for complete odor removal.

**What if the cat pee smell is in wood or concrete?**

For porous surfaces like wood or concrete, the urine can soak deep into the material. You'll need to apply an enzymatic cleaner repeatedly and ensure it penetrates deeply. In severe cases, sanding and sealing wood or even replacing subflooring might be necessary.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing (e.g., right type of litter, enough boxes). Rule out medical issues with a vet visit. Address stress factors, provide enrichment, and clean previous accident spots thoroughly to remove scent markers.
