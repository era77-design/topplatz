To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by blotting up fresh urine and then applying an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals. For stubborn odors, repeat treatments and consider specialized products or professional cleaning. Identifying and addressing the source of the spraying is also crucial to prevent recurrence.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- Gloves
- Plastic wrap (optional)
- UV black light (optional)
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate and Assess the Affected Area

Even if you can smell it, pinpointing the exact location of the cat urine is crucial for effective cleaning. Use your nose or, for dried or old stains, a UV black light in a darkened room can reveal hidden spots. Inspect carpets, upholstery, walls, and even hard surfaces like wood or tile, as urine can seep into grout or floorboards.

> 💡 **Tip:** A UV black light will make dried urine glow, making it much easier to find all affected spots, especially on light-colored surfaces or in dark corners.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

For fresh accidents, speed is key. Place several layers of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth over the wet spot and press down firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can push the urine deeper into fibers. Repeat with fresh towels until no more moisture transfers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stand on the paper towels to apply maximum pressure, drawing more liquid out of carpets or fabrics.

### Step 3: Rinse the Area (if applicable)

After blotting, lightly rinse the area with cool water, especially for carpets and upholstery. You can use a spray bottle or a damp cloth. This helps dilute any remaining urine. Immediately blot the area dry again, removing as much of the rinse water as possible. This step prepares the surface for the enzymatic cleaner.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner on its rinse setting to extract water more effectively from carpets without oversaturating them.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are essential because they contain beneficial bacteria that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are responsible for the lingering odor. Saturate the affected area thoroughly with the cleaner, following the product's instructions carefully. Ensure the cleaner reaches as deep as the urine did to neutralize the odor at its source. For advice on deeper cleaning, see our guide on [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

> 💡 **Tip:** For stubborn odors, cover the treated area with plastic wrap after applying the enzymatic cleaner to keep it wet longer, allowing the enzymes more time to work.

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

Do not rush the drying process. The enzymatic cleaner needs time to work its magic as it dries. Keep pets and children away from the treated area until it is fully dry. You may want to open windows or use a fan to improve air circulation and speed up drying, but ensure the cleaner has had adequate contact time with the stain.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid using heat to dry the area, as it can set the stain and odor. Air drying is best.

### Step 6: Repeat Treatment if Necessary

If a faint odor persists after the first treatment and the area is completely dry, don't hesitate to repeat the entire process. Cat urine can be incredibly potent, and multiple applications of an enzymatic cleaner may be required to fully eliminate the smell, especially for older or heavily saturated stains. Consistency is key for achieving a fresh home. For a truly comprehensive approach to persistent odors, consider exploring methods to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-effectively).

> 💡 **Tip:** After the area is dry, a final sprinkle of baking soda left for several hours can absorb any lingering moisture and odors before vacuuming.

### Step 7: Address the Underlying Cause

To prevent future accidents, it's crucial to understand why your cat is urinating outside the litter box. This could be due to medical issues (e.g., UTIs), stress, litter box aversion (dirty box, wrong litter type, inconvenient location), or territorial marking. Consult your veterinarian to rule out health problems first, then consider behavioral solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure you have enough litter boxes (one per cat plus one extra), keep them clean, and experiment with different litter types or box locations.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning product on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage or discolor the material.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the same spot.
- For severe or widespread odors, especially in carpets or subflooring, professional cleaning services specializing in pet odor removal may be necessary.
- Consider using an odor neutralizer spray after cleaning to provide an extra layer of freshness, but ensure it's pet-safe.
- Clean the litter box daily and perform a full litter change weekly to encourage proper litter box use.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning chemicals.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation by opening windows or using fans when using cleaning products.
- ⚠️ Keep all cleaning chemicals out of reach of pets and children.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and persist?**

Cat urine contains urea, uric acid, creatinine, and pheromones. The uric acid crystals don't dissolve easily and reactivate with moisture, releasing a strong, pungent ammonia-like odor that can linger for a long time.

**What is the best type of cleaner for cat urine?**

Enzymatic cleaners are highly recommended for cat urine. These products use beneficial bacteria and enzymes to break down the uric acid crystals and proteins in urine, effectively neutralizing the odor rather than just masking it.

**Can cat pee smell ever truly be removed permanently?**

Yes, with the right approach, cat pee smell can be permanently removed. The key is to use enzymatic cleaners that break down uric acid and to ensure the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Multiple treatments may be necessary.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a couch or upholstered furniture?**

First, blot as much urine as possible. Then, thoroughly saturate the affected area with an enzymatic cleaner, letting it soak deep into the upholstery and padding. Allow it to air dry completely, repeating the process if necessary.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of concrete or hardwood floors?**

For concrete, clean the surface, then apply an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak into the porous material. For hardwood, clean the surface, then use an enzymatic cleaner specifically safe for wood, letting it penetrate without oversaturating. You might need to seal concrete or refinish wood if the smell has deeply permeated.

**Will the cat pee smell come back if it gets wet again?**

If the uric acid crystals are not fully broken down by an enzymatic cleaner, yes, the smell can reactivate when it gets wet again. This is why thorough cleaning with an appropriate enzymatic product is crucial for permanent odor elimination.

**Why is my cat peeing outside the litter box?**

Cats may urinate outside the litter box due to medical issues (like UTIs), stress, an aversion to their litter box (e.g., it's dirty, too small, or in a noisy location), or marking behavior. A vet check-up is always the first step to rule out health problems.
