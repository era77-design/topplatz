To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by cleaning the affected area thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner designed to break down uric acid crystals. Blot the area, apply the cleaner, let it sit, and then blot again to neutralize the odor. Repeat if necessary for stubborn smells.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean cloths
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic cleaner (specifically for pet urine)
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda (optional)
- Spray bottle (optional)
- UV blacklight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source of the Smell

The first crucial step is to accurately find all areas affected by cat urine. Fresh stains are often visible, but dried or older stains can be invisible to the naked eye. A UV blacklight can be incredibly useful; in a dark room, urine stains will glow under the UV light, revealing even hidden spots.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't forget to check hidden areas like baseboards, under furniture, or carpet edges where accidents might occur unnoticed.

### Step 2: Blot Up Fresh Urine Immediately

If the urine is fresh, prompt action is key to preventing the odor from setting. Place several layers of paper towels or a thick, clean cloth over the wet spot and press firmly to absorb as much liquid as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into the material. Continue blotting until no more moisture transfers to the towels.

> 💡 **Tip:** Standing on the paper towels can provide extra pressure for maximum absorption, especially on carpets.

### Step 3: Pre-Treat with a Vinegar Solution (Optional)

For an initial pre-treatment, especially on carpets or upholstery, mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Generously spray the affected area, ensuring the solution soaks into the fibers as deeply as the urine did. Vinegar helps to break down some urine components and acts as a natural deodorizer. Let it sit for about 15-20 minutes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test the vinegar solution on an inconspicuous area of the material first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.

### Step 4: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

After blotting up any pre-treatment, apply a high-quality enzymatic cleaner specifically formulated for pet urine. These cleaners contain beneficial enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the persistent cat pee smell. Follow the product instructions carefully regarding the amount to use and the required dwell time. Ensure the cleaner saturates the area as deeply as the urine did.

> 💡 **Tip:** For comprehensive removal, ensure the cleaner penetrates deep into the material. For more detailed instructions on lasting results, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell).

### Step 5: Allow to Air Dry Completely

Once the enzymatic cleaner has had its recommended dwell time, resist the urge to rinse the area unless the product instructions specifically advise it. Allow the treated area to air dry completely. The enzymes continue working as they dry, which is crucial for fully neutralizing the odor. Using fans or opening windows can help speed up the drying process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid using heat (like a hairdryer) to dry the area, as heat can inadvertently set the stain and make the odor harder to remove.

### Step 6: Re-Evaluate and Re-Treat if Necessary

After the area is thoroughly dry, check for any lingering odor. Sometimes, especially with older, deeply saturated, or multiple stains, a single treatment may not be sufficient. If you still detect a smell, repeat the entire enzymatic cleaning process. Persistent odors may require multiple applications or a more in-depth approach.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check the area at different times of the day and under varying humidity levels, as moisture can sometimes reactivate dormant odors. For a complete strategy, consult [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-guide).

### Step 7: Consider Deep Cleaning or Professional Help

For very stubborn stains that have permeated deep into carpet padding, subflooring, or upholstery, a more intensive approach may be needed. This could involve using a wet-vac to extract the cleaner and urine residue, or even replacing affected materials. If the odor persists after several attempts, professional carpet or upholstery cleaners specializing in pet odors can often provide effective solutions.

> 💡 **Tip:** Professionals have access to stronger, commercial-grade products and equipment that can tackle the toughest cat urine odors.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act fast: The sooner you address the cat pee, the easier it will be to remove the smell completely.
- Never use ammonia-based cleaners: Ammonia smells similar to urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot, encouraging repeat accidents.
- Consider using baking soda as an odor absorbent after cleaning: Sprinkle it generously, let it sit for several hours (or overnight), then vacuum it up.
- Address the root cause: Consult your vet to rule out medical issues, and ensure your litter box is clean, accessible, and appealing to prevent future accidents.
- For hard surfaces like tile or linoleum, a simple solution of dish soap and water followed by an enzymatic cleaner usually works well.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it does not cause discoloration or damage to the material.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products, especially in enclosed spaces, to avoid inhaling strong fumes.
- ⚠️ Keep pets and children away from treated areas until they are completely dry and safe to prevent ingestion or skin irritation.
- ⚠️ Do not mix different cleaning products unless explicitly stated by the manufacturer, as this can create harmful or toxic fumes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and last so long?**

Cat pee contains uric acid crystals, which are highly pungent and difficult to break down with water alone. These crystals can reactivate with moisture, causing the smell to return even after initial cleaning, making it seem like the odor is permanent.

**Can I use baking soda to get rid of cat pee smell?**

Baking soda can help absorb and neutralize some surface odors, acting as a deodorizer. After cleaning the area with an enzymatic cleaner, sprinkling baking soda, letting it sit, and then vacuuming it up can be a helpful supplemental step for odor absorption.

**What if the smell is in concrete or wood?**

For concrete, specialized enzymatic cleaners designed for porous surfaces are essential, often requiring multiple applications. For wood, urine can soak deeply, potentially requiring sanding and sealing the wood, or in severe cases, even replacing the affected sections.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of laundry?**

Pre-soak affected items in a solution of enzymatic cleaner or white vinegar and water before washing. Then, wash with your regular detergent and add a cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle. Air dry the items, as machine drying can potentially set the odor.

**Are homemade remedies always effective for cat pee smell?**

While homemade remedies like white vinegar and baking soda can help with some surface odors and act as deodorizers, they often aren't enough to fully break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine. Enzymatic cleaners are generally the most effective solution for permanent odor removal.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box?**

Ensure the litter box is impeccably clean, accessible, and appealing to your cat (e.g., correct litter type, enough boxes). Rule out any underlying medical conditions with a vet visit. Also, address any sources of stress or provide more environmental enrichment.

**How long does it take for the smell to completely disappear?**

With proper and thorough application of an enzymatic cleaner, the smell should dissipate as the area dries, typically within 24-48 hours. For very deep or old stains, multiple treatments and patience may be required for complete eradication of the odor.
