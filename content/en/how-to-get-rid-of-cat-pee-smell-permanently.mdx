To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly to blot up fresh urine and then apply an enzymatic cleaner. Enzymatic cleaners break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the lingering odor, effectively neutralizing the smell at its source. For older stains, repeated treatments and patience are often necessary to achieve permanent odor removal, as detailed in a [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell: A Complete Guide](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-complete-guide).

## What You Need

- Paper towels or clean rags
- Rubber gloves
- Enzymatic pet odor cleaner
- White vinegar (optional)
- Baking soda
- Spray bottle (optional, for vinegar solution)
- Black light (optional, for locating old stains)
- Stiff brush (for carpets)
- Plastic sheeting or foil (to cover treated area)
- Vacuum cleaner

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source

The first crucial step is to accurately locate all areas affected by cat urine. Fresh stains are usually visible, but dried or older stains can be elusive. Use your nose to sniff out affected spots, paying close attention to corners, baseboards, furniture, and carpet edges. For hidden or old stains, a UV black light in a darkened room can make urine crystals glow, revealing the exact location.

> 💡 **Tip:** Circle detected spots with chalk or tape to ensure you treat the entire area thoroughly.

### Step 2: Blot Fresh Urine Immediately

If the urine is fresh, quick action is key to prevent it from soaking deeper into surfaces. Put on rubber gloves and use a thick stack of paper towels or a clean, absorbent cloth to blot the area firmly. Do not rub, as this can spread the stain and push the urine deeper. Continue blotting until no more moisture transfers to the towels; you may need to stand on the towels to apply sufficient pressure.

> 💡 **Tip:** Replace soiled towels frequently with fresh ones to maximize absorption and prevent spreading the odor.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

Enzymatic cleaners are specifically formulated to break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the primary cause of the lingering odor. Saturate the affected area thoroughly with the enzymatic cleaner, ensuring it penetrates as deeply as the urine did. Follow the product's instructions carefully regarding application and dwell time. This step is vital for truly getting rid of the cat pee smell.

> 💡 **Tip:** For best results and to ensure permanent odor removal, choose a high-quality enzymatic cleaner designed specifically for pet urine.

### Step 4: Allow Cleaner to Dwell and Dry

After applying the enzymatic cleaner, allow it to sit for the recommended time, which can vary from 15 minutes to several hours, or even overnight. It's crucial not to rinse or scrub it away too soon, as the enzymes need time to work. To prevent your cat from returning to the spot while it's wet, cover the area with plastic sheeting or aluminum foil. Once the dwell time is complete, blot up any excess moisture and allow the area to air dry completely.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the area is completely dry before proceeding to the next steps; patience is key for effective odor removal.

### Step 5: Neutralize with Baking Soda (Optional but Recommended)

Once the enzymatic cleaner has dried, a generous application of baking soda can help absorb any residual odors and moisture. Sprinkle a thick layer of baking soda over the treated area, ensuring good coverage. Let it sit for several hours, or ideally overnight, to draw out any remaining smells. Baking soda is a natural deodorizer that complements the enzymatic cleaning process.

> 💡 **Tip:** For very stubborn odors, you can gently work the baking soda into carpet fibers with a stiff brush before letting it sit.

### Step 6: Vacuum Thoroughly

After the baking soda has had ample time to work, use a powerful vacuum cleaner to thoroughly remove all the baking soda from the surface. For carpets, you may need to go over the area several times to ensure all powder is lifted. This step not only removes the baking soda but also helps to lift any remaining dried urine crystals or residue.

> 💡 **Tip:** Empty your vacuum cleaner's canister or bag immediately after use to prevent the absorbed odors from lingering in your vacuum.

### Step 7: Address Washable Items and Repeat if Necessary

For washable items like bedding, clothing, or small rugs, presoak them in a solution of enzymatic cleaner or a cup of white vinegar mixed with water before washing on a cold cycle. Always air dry these items, as heat can set the odor. For persistent or old stains, you may need to repeat the entire cleaning process, especially the enzymatic cleaner application, to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Permanently](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell). Deeply saturated areas may require multiple treatments to fully eliminate the odor.

> 💡 **Tip:** Never put items with a lingering urine smell into a hot dryer, as the heat can permanently bond the odor to the fabric fibers.


## Tips & Tricks

- Act quickly: The sooner you address a fresh urine stain, the easier it will be to remove the odor permanently.
- Test cleaners: Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- Ventilate the area: Open windows and use fans to help with drying and air circulation during the cleaning process.
- Address the cause: If your cat is repeatedly urinating outside the litter box, consult with your vet to rule out medical issues or a feline behaviorist for behavioral problems.
- Avoid ammonia: Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their smell can mimic urine and attract your cat back to the spot.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not use steam cleaners on cat urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain into fibers.
- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when handling cat urine to protect yourself from bacteria and odors.
- ⚠️ Ensure good ventilation when using cleaning products, especially in enclosed spaces.
- ⚠️ Keep cleaning products out of reach of children and pets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so bad and linger for so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and don't dissolve easily in water. These crystals react with moisture in the air, releasing strong, unpleasant ammonia-like odors that can reactivate even after seemingly drying, making the smell very persistent.

**What is the best cleaner for cat pee smell?**

The most effective cleaners for cat pee are enzymatic pet odor removers. These cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that break down the uric acid crystals, proteins, and other organic matter in urine, thereby neutralizing the odor at its source rather than just masking it. For more detailed methods, refer to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-remove-cat-urine-odor).

**Can baking soda really remove cat urine smell?**

Baking soda is an excellent natural deodorizer that can absorb and neutralize many odors, including some components of cat urine. While it's very helpful for surface odors and as a follow-up to enzymatic cleaners, it may not fully break down deeply embedded uric acid crystals on its own.

**How do I get cat pee smell out of a mattress?**

For a mattress, blot up as much urine as possible, then generously saturate the area with an enzymatic cleaner, allowing it to soak in deeply. Cover with plastic and let it dwell for several hours or overnight, then blot and let air dry completely. You may need to repeat the process multiple times, and consider placing baking soda on top to absorb residual moisture and odor once dry.

**How do I find old, invisible cat urine stains?**

Old cat urine stains, particularly on carpets or upholstery, often become invisible once dry. The most effective way to locate them is by using a UV (black) light in a completely darkened room. The uric acid crystals in dried cat urine will fluoresce under the black light, appearing as yellowish or greenish-white glow.

**Will the cat pee smell ever truly go away?**

Yes, with proper and persistent treatment using enzymatic cleaners, the cat pee smell can be permanently eliminated. The key is to ensure the cleaner penetrates as deeply as the urine did and to allow the enzymes sufficient time to break down all the uric acid crystals. For severe cases, multiple applications may be necessary to achieve complete odor removal.

**What if my cat keeps peeing outside the litter box?**

If your cat is consistently urinating outside the litter box, it's crucial to consult a veterinarian first to rule out any underlying medical conditions like a urinary tract infection. If medical issues are excluded, the behavior might be stress-related, a litter box aversion, or territorial marking, which may require behavioral modification or changes to their environment.
