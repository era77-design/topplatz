To fix a clogged toilet, first turn off the water supply to prevent overflow. Then, use a flange plunger with strong, consistent thrusts to dislodge the obstruction. For persistent clogs, alternative methods like dish soap and hot water or a toilet auger can be highly effective.

## What You Need

- Rubber gloves
- Flange plunger (toilet plunger)
- Old towels or newspaper
- Bucket
- Liquid dish soap
- Hot (not boiling) water
- Toilet auger (closet auger) (optional)
- Protective eyewear (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Area and Prevent Overflow

Before you start, turn the water supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water. This prevents the bowl from overflowing if you flush again. Lay old towels or newspaper around the base of the toilet to protect your floor from splashes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wear rubber gloves to maintain hygiene throughout the process.

### Step 2: Use a Flange Plunger Effectively

Position the plunger so its rubber flange creates a tight seal over the drain hole at the bottom of the toilet bowl. Ensure there's enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger's cup. Push down firmly to create a vacuum, then pull up sharply without breaking the seal. Repeat this plunging action 15-20 times with strong, consistent thrusts. After plunging, remove the plunger and see if the water drains; if it does, flush to check.

### Step 3: Try the Dish Soap and Hot Water Method

If plunging doesn't work or if you don't have a plunger, pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the toilet bowl. Follow this with a gallon of very hot (but not boiling) water, poured slowly from waist height. The hot water helps break down the clog, while the soap lubricates it. Allow this mixture to sit for 20-30 minutes; the combination should help the clog slide down the drain. If the water level drops, try a gentle flush.

### Step 4: Employ a Toilet Auger for Stubborn Clogs

For persistent clogs that resist plunging and the soap method, a toilet auger (also known as a closet auger or snake) is your best bet. Place the auger's curved end into the toilet drain, feeding the cable into the trap. Crank the handle to extend the cable until you feel resistance. Once you hit the clog, twist and push the auger to break it apart or hook onto it. Slowly retract the auger, pulling out any debris. Flush the toilet to confirm the clog is gone. For more detailed instructions, see [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: Easy Steps & Solutions](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet-easy-steps).

### Step 5: Confirm Drainage and Restore Water Supply

Once the water level in the bowl drops and drains normally, you can restore the water supply. Turn the water supply valve counter-clockwise until it's fully open. Perform a test flush to ensure the toilet flushes properly and the clog is completely cleared. If the toilet still seems sluggish, you may have a partial clog that requires another round of your chosen method or professional help.

### Step 6: Clean Up Your Workspace

After the clog is cleared, thoroughly clean your plunger, auger, and any tools used with disinfectant. Dispose of any soiled towels or newspaper. Wipe down the toilet bowl and surrounding area with a disinfectant cleaner to ensure hygiene.


## Tips & Tricks

- **Prevent Future Clogs:** Avoid flushing anything other than human waste and toilet paper. Never flush wipes (even "flushable" ones), feminine hygiene products, cotton balls, or excessive amounts of toilet paper.
- **Choose the Right Plunger:** A flange plunger (with a rubber extension at the bottom) is specifically designed for toilets and creates a better seal than a cup plunger.
- **Know When to Call a Professional:** If you've tried all methods and the clog persists, or if you suspect a deeper issue like a main sewer line blockage, it's time to call a licensed plumber. [How to Fix a Clogged Toilet: DIY Solutions & Easy Steps](/en/how-to-fix-a-clogged-toilet) has more advice on when to seek professional help.
- **Regular Maintenance:** Occasionally pour a bucket of hot water down the toilet (without soap) to help clear any potential buildup before it becomes a full clog.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ **Do NOT Use Chemical Drain Cleaners:** These can damage your toilet's porcelain, pipes, and septic system, and often don't work on toilet clogs. They also pose a significant health risk due to corrosive fumes and skin contact.
- ⚠️ **Avoid Repeated Flushing:** If the toilet bowl is full and not draining, do not flush again. This will only cause an overflow.
- ⚠️ **Be Gentle with Augers:** While effective, an auger can scratch the porcelain if not used carefully. Avoid forcing it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a clogged toilet without a plunger?**

You can often fix a clogged toilet without a plunger by using a combination of dish soap and hot water. Pour about half a cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes to help lubricate and dissolve the clog.

**How do you fix a clogged toilet with poop in it?**

To fix a clogged toilet with poop in it, first turn off the water supply to prevent overflow. Then, use a flange plunger with firm, consistent thrusts. If plunging doesn't work, try the dish soap and hot water method to help break down the organic matter before attempting to flush.

**What is the best homemade solution for a clogged toilet?**

The best homemade solution involves pouring half a cup of dish soap into the toilet bowl, followed by a gallon of hot (not boiling) water. Let it sit for 20-30 minutes. The soap helps lubricate the clog, and the hot water can aid in breaking it down, allowing it to slide through the pipes.

**Can I use baking soda and vinegar to unclog a toilet?**

While baking soda and vinegar create a fizzing reaction, they are generally not powerful enough to clear a significant toilet clog. They are more effective for minor drain maintenance or deodorizing. For actual clogs, a plunger or auger is usually required.

**When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?**

You should call a plumber if your toilet remains clogged after trying plunging, the dish soap and hot water method, and a toilet auger. This indicates a more severe blockage possibly deeper in your plumbing system or a main sewer line issue that requires professional tools and expertise.

**How do I prevent my toilet from getting clogged?**

To prevent toilet clogs, only flush human waste and toilet paper. Avoid flushing "flushable" wipes, feminine hygiene products, cotton swabs, dental floss, or excessive amounts of toilet paper. Regularly check for proper flushing and address any slow drainage promptly.

**Is it safe to use a chemical drain cleaner in a toilet?**

No, it is generally not safe or recommended to use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet. They can damage the toilet's porcelain, corrode pipes, and release harmful fumes. Moreover, they are often ineffective on toilet clogs and can make the situation worse, posing a hazard to anyone attempting to clear the clog afterward.
