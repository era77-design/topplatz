To get rid of cat pee smell, act quickly by locating the source and blotting up as much urine as possible. Then, apply an enzymatic cleaner specifically designed to break down the uric acid crystals responsible for the odor.

## What You Need

- Paper towels or old rags
- Enzymatic cleaner for pet urine
- Gloves
- UV black light (optional, for locating)
- Plastic wrap (optional)
- Weight (optional, for pressure)

## Steps

### Step 1: Locate the Source Quickly

The first and most crucial step is to find where your cat has urinated. Fresh urine is easier to remove and prevents the smell from setting in. If you can't see or smell it clearly, a UV black light can help reveal dried urine stains, which glow under the light, especially in the dark.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check hidden spots like behind furniture, along baseboards, and in corners, as cats often seek privacy for accidents.

### Step 2: Blot, Don't Rub

Once you've found the spot, immediately grab paper towels or clean rags and blot the area firmly to absorb as much urine as possible. Avoid rubbing, as this can spread the urine and push it deeper into carpets or upholstery fibers, making it harder to remove. Continue blotting until no more moisture transfers to the towels.

### Step 3: Apply an Enzymatic Cleaner

An enzymatic cleaner is essential for truly neutralizing cat urine odor. These cleaners contain enzymes that break down the uric acid crystals in cat urine, which are the main cause of the persistent smell. Follow the product instructions carefully, ensuring you saturate the affected area thoroughly enough for the cleaner to reach all the urine. For deeper stains, you might want to learn more about [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Permanently & Effectively](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-permanently).

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure the cleaner has enough contact time, often 10-15 minutes or more, as per instructions, for the enzymes to work effectively.

### Step 4: Allow to Dwell and Absorb

After applying the cleaner, let it sit for the recommended time. For very tough or old stains, you can cover the treated area with plastic wrap to keep it moist, then place a weighted object on top to help the cleaner penetrate deeper. This extended contact can significantly improve the cleaner's effectiveness, especially in carpets and fabrics. This method contributes to [How to Get Rid of Cat Pee Smell Effectively & Fast](/en/how-to-get-rid-of-cat-pee-smell-fast-methods).

### Step 5: Blot Up Excess Cleaner and Air Dry

Once the dwell time is complete, use fresh paper towels or a clean cloth to blot up any remaining cleaner and moisture. Again, blot, don't rub. Allow the area to air dry completely. You can use a fan or open windows to speed up drying and further ventilate the area. Ensure the spot is completely dry before assessing if the odor is gone.

### Step 6: Re-evaluate and Repeat if Necessary

After the area is dry, check for any lingering odor. If you still detect a faint smell, it means some uric acid crystals remain. Don't be discouraged; cat urine can be very stubborn. Repeat the entire cleaning process, focusing on saturating the area well with the enzymatic cleaner. Multiple applications are sometimes necessary for complete odor elimination. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always test any cleaning product on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't cause discoloration or damage.
- For severe or old stains, consider renting a wet/dry vacuum or carpet cleaner, but still pre-treat with an enzymatic cleaner.
- Ensure good ventilation during and after cleaning to help dissipate odors and dry the area faster.
- Address the root cause of why your cat is urinating outside the litter box (e.g., vet check, litter box issues, stress).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Never use ammonia-based cleaners, as their scent is similar to urine and can attract your cat back to the same spot.
- ⚠️ Avoid steam cleaners on fresh urine, as the heat can permanently set the odor and stain.
- ⚠️ Always wear gloves when cleaning cat urine to protect your hands from bacteria and cleaning agents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does cat pee smell so strong and last so long?**

Cat urine contains uric acid crystals, which are highly concentrated and notoriously difficult to break down with regular cleaners. These crystals reactivate with moisture, releasing the strong, unpleasant odor even after the liquid has dried.

**Can I use household products like vinegar or baking soda?**

While vinegar and baking soda can help neutralize some odors, they are generally not effective against the uric acid crystals in cat urine. They might mask the smell temporarily, but an enzymatic cleaner is required for permanent elimination.

**What surfaces are hardest to get cat pee smell out of?**

Porous surfaces like carpet padding, untreated wood subfloors, upholstery, and mattresses are the most challenging. The urine can soak deep into these materials, making it difficult for surface treatments to reach all the affected areas.

**How do enzymatic cleaners work?**

Enzymatic cleaners contain beneficial bacteria that produce enzymes. These enzymes specifically target and break down the uric acid and other organic compounds in cat urine into smaller, odorless molecules, effectively eliminating the source of the smell.

**What if the cat pee smell is old and dried?**

Old, dried cat pee smell is much harder to remove but still possible. You'll need to use a UV black light to locate all affected areas, then apply an enzymatic cleaner generously and allow for a longer dwell time, potentially repeating the process multiple times.

**How can I prevent my cat from peeing outside the litter box again?**

Prevention involves addressing the underlying cause. Rule out medical issues with a vet visit, ensure litter boxes are clean, accessible, and sufficient in number (one per cat plus one extra), and reduce any potential stressors in your cat's environment.

**When should I call a professional cleaner?**

Consider calling a professional if the urine has soaked into subflooring, walls, or large areas of carpet padding, or if multiple attempts with enzymatic cleaners haven't fully removed the odor. Professionals have specialized equipment and stronger solutions for deep penetration.
