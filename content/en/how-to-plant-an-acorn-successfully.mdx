To plant an acorn, first select a healthy, viable acorn, typically in the fall. Next, prepare the acorn through cold stratification to simulate winter conditions, encouraging germination. Finally, plant the stratified acorn in suitable soil, either in a pot or directly outdoors, providing consistent moisture and protection.

## What You Need

- Healthy acorns (from a local oak tree)
- Small pot (if starting indoors)
- Well-draining potting soil
- Watering can
- Trowel or small spade
- Plastic bag or container (for stratification)
- Sand or peat moss (for stratification)

## Steps

### Step 1: Select Viable Acorns

Gather acorns from healthy oak trees, ideally in the fall after they've dropped naturally. Look for acorns that are plump, firm, and free from holes, mold, or discoloration. A simple float test can help: place acorns in water; those that sink are generally viable, while those that float are likely hollow or damaged and should be discarded. Collecting several good acorns increases your chances of success.

> 💡 **Tip:** Collect acorns from local oak species, as they are best suited for your climate.

### Step 2: Perform Cold Stratification

Most oak species require a period of cold and moisture to break dormancy, a process called stratification. Place viable acorns in a plastic bag or container with a moist medium like peat moss, sand, or even damp paper towels. Seal the container and refrigerate for 60-90 days at temperatures between 35-40°F (1-4°C). Ensure the medium remains consistently moist but not waterlogged to prevent mold.

> 💡 **Tip:** Check on your acorns periodically during stratification, misting the medium if it begins to dry out. Discard any acorns that show signs of mold.

### Step 3: Prepare Your Planting Site or Pot

Once stratified, your acorns are ready for planting. If planting in a pot, choose one at least 6-8 inches deep with good drainage holes and fill it with well-draining potting soil. If planting directly outdoors, select a site with full to partial sun and well-draining soil, ensuring it's not too close to structures or other trees where the mature oak might eventually interfere. Clear the area of weeds and debris. For more detailed guidance, see [How to Plant an Acorn & Grow a Mighty Oak Tree](/en/how-to-plant-an-acorn).

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider starting acorns in pots indoors, especially if you live in an area with harsh winters or a high squirrel population, to give them a protected start.

### Step 4: Plant the Acorn Correctly

Plant the acorn on its side, about 1-2 inches deep (2.5-5 cm), ensuring the root end (if visible) faces downwards. Cover it gently with soil. If planting multiple acorns, space them several inches apart in a pot or at least 10 feet apart if planting directly into the ground to allow for future growth. Mark your planting spot to avoid disturbing it later.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not plant acorns too deeply, as this can hinder germination. The depth should be roughly twice the acorn's diameter.

### Step 5: Provide Initial Watering and Protection

Immediately after planting, water the soil thoroughly until it is evenly moist. Continue to water regularly, especially during dry periods, keeping the soil consistently damp but not waterlogged. Protect your newly planted acorns or sprouts from pests like squirrels, deer, and rodents using mesh cages, chicken wire, or tree shelters.

> 💡 **Tip:** Squirrels are notorious for digging up acorns. Consider placing a piece of hardware cloth over potted acorns or a protective cage around outdoor plantings until the sprout is established.

### Step 6: Monitor for Germination and Growth

Patience is key, as germination can take several weeks to a few months after planting, depending on the oak species and conditions. Look for a small sprout emerging from the soil. Once the seedling appears, ensure it continues to receive adequate sunlight and water. Protect it from weeds, which can compete for resources, by gently hand-weeding around the base.

> 💡 **Tip:** Some oak species, particularly red oaks, may germinate in the spring but not send a shoot above ground until the following year. Don't give up too soon!

### Step 7: Care for Your Young Oak Tree

As your seedling grows, continue to provide consistent care. If started in a pot, plan to transplant it to its permanent outdoor location after one or two growing seasons, once it has developed a strong root system and is about 1-2 feet tall. Ensure the final planting site offers sufficient space for the mature oak. Regular watering during dry spells and protection from pests will help your young oak thrive. Learn more about successfully growing your oak from an acorn at [How to Plant an Acorn: Grow Your Own Oak Tree Successfully](/en/how-to-grow-an-oak-from-acorn).

> 💡 **Tip:** When transplanting, dig a wide hole and gently loosen the roots to encourage outward growth. Water thoroughly after transplanting.


## Tips & Tricks

- Choose acorns from healthy, mature oak trees in your local area for the best chance of success.
- Never let your acorns dry out, especially during stratification, as this can kill the embryo.
- Protect young seedlings from hungry wildlife like squirrels, rabbits, and deer.
- Be patient; growing an oak from an acorn is a long-term project that requires time and consistent care.
- Consider planting several acorns, as not all will germinate or survive to become mature trees.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not allow acorns to dry out before or during stratification, as this significantly reduces their viability.
- ⚠️ Ensure proper drainage in pots and planting sites to prevent root rot.
- ⚠️ Protect young seedlings from extreme weather, pests, and accidental damage from lawnmowers or foot traffic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is the best time to plant an acorn?**

The best time to plant a stratified acorn is in early spring, after the last risk of hard frost. Acorns are typically collected in the fall and then stratified over winter, preparing them for spring germination.

**Do all acorns sprout?**

No, not all acorns will sprout. Many factors affect viability, including acorn health, proper stratification, planting depth, and environmental conditions. It's recommended to plant several acorns to increase your chances of success.

**How deep should I plant an acorn?**

Plant an acorn about 1 to 2 inches (2.5-5 cm) deep, or roughly twice the diameter of the acorn itself. Planting too deeply can prevent the sprout from reaching the surface.

**What kind of soil is best for planting acorns?**

Well-draining soil is crucial. For potted acorns, use a good quality potting mix. If planting outdoors, ensure the site has loamy, well-aerated soil that doesn't stay waterlogged. Oak trees are adaptable but prefer slightly acidic to neutral soil.

**How long does it take for an acorn to sprout?**

After proper stratification and planting, an acorn can take anywhere from a few weeks to several months to sprout, depending on the oak species and environmental conditions. Some may even take over a year to show above-ground growth.

**Can I plant an acorn directly outside?**

Yes, you can plant an acorn directly outdoors, but it's important to choose a suitable site with good soil and protect it from pests like squirrels and rodents. Ensure the acorn has undergone proper cold stratification before outdoor planting.

**How often should I water a newly planted acorn or seedling?**

Keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged. Water deeply when the top inch of soil feels dry to the touch. The frequency will depend on your climate and soil type, but usually every few days to once a week is sufficient for young seedlings during dry periods.
