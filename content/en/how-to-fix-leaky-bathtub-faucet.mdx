A leaky bathtub faucet is commonly caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet assembly. Fixing it involves turning off the water supply, carefully disassembling the faucet, replacing the defective parts, and then reassembling it. This straightforward DIY repair can prevent water waste and potential water damage to your home.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge (check faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or towel
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck parts)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house, or at least the shut-off valves for the bathroom. This crucial step prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process. Test the faucet afterward to confirm the water is completely off.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that conceals a screw. Carefully pry off this cap using a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver. Use the appropriate screwdriver to loosen and remove the screw, then pull the handle straight off the faucet stem. If the handle is stuck, a little penetrating oil might help loosen it.

### Step 3: Disassemble Faucet Body

With the handle removed, you'll likely see a decorative escutcheon or trim plate, followed by a bonnet nut. Remove any screws holding the escutcheon, then use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen the large bonnet nut. Once the nut is off, you can extract the faucet stem or cartridge assembly from the faucet body. Note the orientation of all parts as you remove them.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the rubber washers, O-rings, and the stem or cartridge for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for a leaky faucet. Replace any worn components with new ones, ensuring they match the original parts in size and type. For cartridge-style faucets, it's often easiest to replace the entire cartridge unit.

### Step 5: Lubricate and Reassemble Faucet

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new washers, O-rings, or the cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a better seal. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly seated. Then, reattach the bonnet nut, tightening it snugly with your adjustable wrench, but avoid overtightening.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Once all components are reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or spout. Operate the faucet handle through its full range (hot and cold) to ensure proper function and to flush out any air. Watch for any drips from the spout or leaks from the handle base.

### Step 7: Tighten and Finish Up

If no leaks are present after testing, reattach the faucet handle and secure it with its screw. Replace any decorative caps or escutcheons. Give the faucet one final check for drips after a few minutes of operation. If leaks persist, you may need to re-examine the components or consider professional help.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone during disassembly to help you remember the order of parts during reassembly.
- When buying replacement parts, it's helpful to bring the old components to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match.
- Avoid overtightening nuts and screws, as this can strip threads or damage plastic components, leading to more leaks.
- Keep a small bucket or towel under the faucet during disassembly to catch any residual water or dropped parts.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Use caution when prying off caps or loosening nuts to avoid scratching the faucet finish or stripping screw heads.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance when disassembling or reassembling, do not force parts, as this can cause damage. Consult a professional plumber if you're unsure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, carefully disassemble the faucet handle and body to access the internal components like washers, O-rings, or the cartridge. Replace any worn-out parts, apply plumber's grease, and reassemble the faucet before turning the water back on to test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to drip or leak from the spout or base.

**Can I fix a bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common bathtub faucet leaks, especially those caused by worn washers or O-rings, can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. However, if the leak is complex, involves internal pipe issues, or you're uncomfortable with the repair, it's always best to call a professional plumber.

**What parts commonly need replacing when I want to fix a bathtub faucet?**

When you want to fix a bathtub faucet, the parts most frequently requiring replacement are the rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire faucet cartridge. These are the sealing components that prevent water from escaping and wear down with regular use and exposure to water.

**How can I fix a bathtub faucet that is constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is usually a sign of a worn-out washer or O-ring within the faucet's stem or a faulty cartridge. To fix this, you'll need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet, identify the worn component, and replace it with a new one to restore a proper seal.

**What if fixing my leaky bathtub faucet doesn't work?**

If you've attempted to fix your leaky bathtub faucet by replacing the washers, O-rings, or cartridge and it's still leaking, there might be a more significant issue. It could be due to corrosion inside the faucet body, damaged valve seats, or a problem with the pipes. In such cases, you might consider replacing the entire faucet unit or consulting a professional plumber. You can find guidance on replacement here: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking from the base?**

If your bathtub faucet is leaking from its base, where it meets the tub or wall, it often indicates an issue with the O-rings or seals around the faucet body or stem. These seals prevent water from seeping out when the faucet is operating. Replacing these specific O-rings or seals should resolve the leak.
