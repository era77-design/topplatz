A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that can waste water and increase your utility bill. Fortunately, most leaks are caused by worn-out washers or cartridges and can be fixed with basic tools and a little DIY effort. This guide will walk you through the process.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- New O-rings, washers, or replacement cartridge (match your faucet model)
- Plumber's tape
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Clean rags
- Penetrating oil (optional)
- Bucket or bowl

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the main water supply to your home or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for your bathroom. Turn on the faucet to drain any residual water from the lines. This prevents accidental flooding and ensures a safe working environment.

### Step 2: Cover the Drain and Remove Faucet Handle

Place a rag or stopper over the bathtub drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling into it. Locate the decorative cap on top or front of the faucet handle and carefully pry it off with a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver. This will expose the handle screw. Unscrew it and gently pull the handle straight off.

### Step 3: Remove the Escutcheon and Cartridge Nut

Beneath the handle, you'll typically find a decorative escutcheon plate or trim. This might be held by screws or simply threaded on. Remove it to expose the faucet body and the cartridge or stem. You'll then see a large nut holding the cartridge or stem in place; use your adjustable wrench to loosen and remove this nut.

### Step 4: Extract and Inspect the Cartridge or Valve Stem

Carefully pull out the cartridge or valve stem. Some may require a specific cartridge puller tool, but many can be wiggled out by hand or with pliers. Inspect the O-rings and washers on the cartridge or stem for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Parts or the Entire Cartridge

If the O-rings or washers are worn, carefully remove them and replace them with new, matching ones. If the entire cartridge appears damaged or if new washers don't solve the leak, you may need to replace the whole cartridge. Take the old cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you purchase an identical replacement. For more extensive replacement, you might find guidance in an article like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet Components

Once new parts are installed, carefully reinsert the cartridge or valve stem into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Hand-tighten the large nut, then use your adjustable wrench to snug it up, being careful not to overtighten. Replace the escutcheon, handle, and decorative cap in reverse order of removal.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check the faucet thoroughly for any leaks, turning it on and off multiple times. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components for proper seating or consider if a different part is faulty. A persistent drip might indicate a deeper issue.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or bowl handy to catch any unexpected drips when disassembling.
- Take photos at each step of disassembly to aid in reassembly.
- Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for easier installation and better sealing.
- Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body before reassembling.
- If the cartridge is stuck, a little penetrating oil applied around its base can help loosen it.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely shut off before starting work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or are unsure about a step, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle and trim, remove the valve stem or cartridge, and replace worn-out O-rings, washers, or the entire cartridge. Then, reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Over time, these parts can deteriorate due to mineral deposits, age, or constant use, leading to water escaping.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. You'll need to identify the type of faucet (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge) and then replace the appropriate worn parts, such as washers or the cartridge, to stop the drip.

**Do I need a plumber to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most common leaks caused by worn washers or cartridges, a DIY fix is entirely possible for someone with basic tools and a little patience. However, if you're uncomfortable with plumbing tasks, the leak persists after your attempts, or the issue seems more complex, hiring a professional plumber is advisable.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can usually be completed within 15 to 45 minutes, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet. Most of the time is spent on disassembly and reassembly, once you have the correct replacement parts.

**What tools are essential to fix a leaky faucet?**

Essential tools for fixing a leaky faucet include screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and the correct replacement O-rings, washers, or a new cartridge. Plumber's tape and clean rags are also very useful.

**Can a leaky bathtub faucet increase my water bill?**

Yes, even a slow drip from a leaky bathtub faucet can significantly increase your water bill over time. A faucet dripping at one drip per second can waste thousands of gallons of water per year, leading to unnecessary expenses and environmental impact.
