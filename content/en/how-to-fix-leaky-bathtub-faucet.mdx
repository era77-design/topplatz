A leaky bathtub faucet is a common nuisance that can waste water and drive up utility bills. Fortunately, most drips are caused by worn-out washers or cartridges, making it a straightforward DIY repair that anyone can tackle with basic tools and a little patience.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (specific to your faucet model)
- Utility knife or putty knife (optional, for removing caps)
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck screws)
- Cleaning cloth or sponge
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the faucet on briefly after shutting off the water to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find a local shut-off for the bathroom, turn off the main water supply to your entire home to prevent any unexpected floods.

### Step 2: Disassemble the Faucet Handles

Most bathtub faucets have decorative caps covering the handle screws. Use a utility knife or flathead screwdriver to carefully pry off these caps. Once exposed, use the appropriate screwdriver to remove the screws holding the handles in place. Gently pull the handles off the valve stems. Some handles may be stuck and require a bit of wiggling or gentle prying.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the faucet assembly before you start disassembling it. This will be a helpful reference during reassembly.

### Step 3: Remove the Escutcheon and Valve Stem/Cartridge

Beneath the handles, you'll likely find a decorative plate called an escutcheon. Unscrew or pry this off to reveal the valve body. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, cartridge, or ball), you'll then need to remove the retaining nut or clip holding the valve stem or cartridge in place. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully unscrew the packing nut or pull out the cartridge.

> 💡 **Tip:** If parts are corroded or stuck, apply some penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying to loosen them again.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Once the valve stem or cartridge is removed, carefully examine it for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or damaged plastic components. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any brittle, cracked, or compressed washers and O-rings with new ones of the exact same size. If you have a cartridge-style faucet, you'll need to replace the entire cartridge unit. Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you get perfect matches.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider replacing all rubber components (washers, O-rings) on both hot and cold sides, even if only one side is leaking, as they are likely the same age and will wear out similarly.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body and the removed parts. This helps ensure a tight seal and smooth operation. Insert the new washers, O-rings, or cartridge into position. Carefully reassemble the faucet components in reverse order, ensuring all screws and nuts are tightened securely but not overtightened, which can damage parts.

> 💡 **Tip:** Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and washers to help them slide into place and create a better seal.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Once everything is reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Turn on the bathtub faucet and let the water run for a minute or two to flush out any air in the pipes. Carefully inspect the faucet for any signs of dripping or leaking around the handles or spout. If the leak persists, you may need to re-check your work or consider professional assistance.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak continues, double-check that all components are correctly seated and tightened, and that you used the correct replacement parts for your specific faucet model.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always identify your faucet's brand and model before buying replacement parts. A photo of the old part can help immensely.
- Keep a small container or cloth handy to catch any drips or small parts during disassembly.
- If you encounter heavily corroded or seized components, consider using a specialized faucet puller tool, available at most hardware stores.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Wear appropriate safety glasses, especially when dealing with potentially brittle or corroded parts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to replace worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. This involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet handles, removing the valve stem or cartridge, replacing the faulty parts, and then reassembling everything.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common cause of a leaky bathtub faucet is worn-out rubber washers or O-rings inside the valve stem, which lose their elasticity over time. Other causes can include a faulty or cracked cartridge, mineral buildup, or corrosion within the faucet body.

**How do I fix a bathtub faucet that won't stop dripping?**

If your bathtub faucet won't stop dripping, it's usually a sign that the internal rubber seals or the cartridge are no longer creating a watertight seal. Replacing these components, as outlined in the steps above, should resolve the persistent drip.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast. The process usually involves basic tools and readily available replacement parts. However, if you encounter significant corrosion, complex internal mechanisms, or if the leak persists after your attempts, calling a plumber is advisable.

**When should I consider replacing instead of fixing my leaky bathtub faucet?**

You should consider replacing your faucet if it's very old, heavily corroded, if replacement parts are unavailable, or if the leak persists despite multiple repair attempts. Sometimes, a full replacement is more cost-effective and provides a longer-lasting solution. For guidance on this, refer to our articles like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

**What type of replacement parts do I need for my bathtub faucet?**

The type of replacement parts depends on your faucet's style: compression faucets use washers, cartridge faucets use a single cartridge, and disc or ball faucets have different internal mechanisms. It's best to remove the old part and take it to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match for your specific faucet model.
