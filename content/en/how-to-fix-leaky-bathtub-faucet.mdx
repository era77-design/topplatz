A leaky bathtub faucet can be a constant annoyance, wasting water and driving up your utility bills. Often, the culprit is a worn-out washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge, a relatively simple fix for most DIYers. This guide will walk you through the steps to identify the problem and get your faucet drip-free again.

## What You Need

- Screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Rag or old towel
- Wire brush or sandpaper (fine-grit)
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck screws)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Bucket or basin

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off valves for your bathtub, if available. Turn the water completely off to prevent flooding. Once the water is off, turn on the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Drain the Faucet and Protect the Tub

Place a rag or old towel over the drain opening in your bathtub to prevent small screws or parts from falling into the drain. Use a bucket or basin to catch any water that might still drip from the faucet. Ensure the tub surface is protected from any tools or dropped components.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that can be pried off with a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to reveal a screw. Unscrew this handle screw, usually a Phillips head, and carefully pull the handle off. Some older handles might simply pull off or be held by a set screw on the underside.

### Step 4: Remove the Cartridge or Stem

Once the handle is off, you'll see either a cartridge or a valve stem. If it's a cartridge, there's usually a retaining nut or clip that needs to be removed with pliers or a wrench. If it's a stem, you'll likely need an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut, allowing you to pull out the stem assembly. Pay attention to the order of components as you remove them.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed cartridge or stem for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any damaged components with new ones, ensuring they are the correct size and type for your faucet. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and moving parts to ensure smooth operation and a good seal. Clean any mineral deposits from the faucet body using a wire brush or fine-grit sandpaper.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet components in the reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all parts are seated correctly and screws are tightened snugly, but do not overtighten, as this can damage the new components or the faucet body. Make sure the handle is properly aligned before securing it.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water is fully on, check the faucet for any signs of leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water, to ensure the repair was successful and there are no drips. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Before purchasing replacement parts, take photos of your faucet and the disassembled components, or bring the old parts to the hardware store to ensure you get the correct replacements.
- Keep a small container or magnetic tray nearby to hold tiny screws and parts, preventing them from getting lost down the drain.
- If screws are stuck due to corrosion, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before attempting to unscrew them.
- Consider replacing washers or cartridges on both the hot and cold handles, even if only one is leaking, as they are likely the same age and condition.
- Always use plumber's grease (silicone-based) on rubber O-rings and seals to help them slide into place and prolong their lifespan.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to drop small parts down the drain. Always cover the drain opening with a rag.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or can't identify the source of the leak, it's best to consult a professional plumber to avoid further damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the handle, remove the internal cartridge or stem, and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully, then restore the water supply and check for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. Over time, these components can degrade, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to drip.

**How do you fix a bathtub faucet that drips when off?**

A bathtub faucet that drips when off usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. The most common fix is to replace the rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge/stem assembly that controls water flow, as these parts are responsible for creating a watertight seal when the faucet is closed.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need basic tools like screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, a utility knife, and replacement parts specific to your faucet. Plumber's grease, rags, and a wire brush for cleaning are also very helpful.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common leaky bathtub faucet issues, such as replacing washers or a cartridge, can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. However, if the leak persists after basic repairs, the faucet is severely corroded, or you're uncomfortable with the process, it's best to call a professional plumber. If the faucet is old or extensively damaged, replacing it might be a better long-term solution. You can find guidance on replacement here: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's still leaking after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet is still leaking after replacing the washers or cartridge, double-check that all components were installed correctly and that the new parts are the exact match for your faucet model. Ensure there are no cracks in the faucet body or valve seat. Sometimes, mineral buildup can prevent a good seal, so thorough cleaning is crucial. If all else fails, the faucet body itself might be damaged, or it may be time to consider a full faucet replacement.

**How can I prevent my bathtub faucet from leaking in the future?**

To help prevent future leaks, avoid overtightening your faucet handles, as this can prematurely wear out washers and seals. Periodically inspect your faucet for drips and address them promptly. Using high-quality replacement parts and ensuring proper installation can also extend the life of your faucet's components.
