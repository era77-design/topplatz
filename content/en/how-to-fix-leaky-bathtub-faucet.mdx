Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out components like rubber washers or O-rings, which degrade over time. By carefully disassembling the faucet handle and valve, you can access these parts and install new ones, effectively stopping the drip and saving water. This process is generally straightforward for most homeowners.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement washer/O-ring kit (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rag or old towel
- Penetrating oil (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before beginning any plumbing repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off valves for your bathroom. Turn the water completely off to prevent flooding. Once the water is off, turn on the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find individual shut-off valves for the bathtub, you'll need to turn off the main water supply to your entire house.

### Step 2: Identify Your Faucet Type

Bathtub faucets come in several types, including compression, cartridge, ball, and ceramic disc. Identifying your faucet type will help you determine which replacement parts you'll need. Compression faucets often have two handles (hot/cold), while single-handle faucets are usually cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of your faucet before disassembling it, which can be helpful when buying replacement parts.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Use a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to carefully pry off the decorative cap on top of the faucet handle, revealing the screw underneath. Use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to remove this screw and then pull the handle straight off. If it's stubborn, gently wiggle it or use penetrating oil.

### Step 4: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

For compression faucets, you'll see a packing nut at the base of the stem. Use an adjustable wrench to loosen and remove this nut, then unscrew the stem. For cartridge faucets, there might be a retaining clip or nut holding the cartridge in place; remove it and pull the cartridge straight out with pliers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Pay close attention to the order of parts as you remove them, as they will need to go back in the same sequence.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. Use a small screwdriver or utility knife to carefully remove these old, hardened parts. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new replacement washers and O-rings before installing them onto the stem or cartridge in the correct positions. If the entire faucet mechanism is old and corroded, you might consider a full replacement. For guidance, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge, ensuring it is properly aligned. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Reattach the faucet handle, tighten the handle screw, and replace the decorative cap. Make sure all connections are snug but avoid over-tightening.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, either at the main shut-off or the bathroom valve. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the drip has stopped.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use replacement parts specifically designed for your faucet's make and model to ensure a proper fit and long-lasting repair.
- Keep a bucket or a rag handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If you encounter a part that won't budge, try applying penetrating oil and letting it sit for a few minutes before attempting to loosen it again.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent damage and flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using tools, especially screwdrivers and wrenches, to avoid slipping and injury or damaging your fixtures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply, then disassemble the handle and stem/cartridge. Replace any worn washers, O-rings, or seals with new ones, apply plumber's grease, and reassemble the faucet. Finally, restore water and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals inside the faucet valve. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to drip.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish. With basic tools and the correct replacement parts, you can usually stop a drip within an hour.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's constantly dripping?**

If your bathtub faucet is constantly dripping, it's likely due to a faulty washer or cartridge. Follow the steps of turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, replacing the worn internal components, and then reassembling it. This should resolve the constant drip.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set, an adjustable wrench, pliers, a utility knife, and replacement washers or a cartridge. Having a rag and plumber's grease on hand is also very helpful for the repair.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most standard faucet types, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour. The exact time depends on your experience level and how easily the faucet components can be disassembled and reassembled.
