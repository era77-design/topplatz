A leaky bathtub faucet is not only annoying but can also waste water and increase your utility bills. Fortunately, fixing a dripping faucet is often a straightforward DIY task that requires only a few basic tools and replacement parts. Most leaks are caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge.

## What You Need

- Screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Utility knife or putty knife
- New washers, O-rings, or replacement cartridge (match your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Rag or towel
- Penetrating oil (optional)
- Small wire brush (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your bathroom or the entire house. Turn both the hot and cold water handles on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines. Place a rag or towel over the drain opening to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: 2. Remove the Faucet Handles and Trim

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that covers a screw. Pry off this cap gently with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw and pull the handle straight off. You might need to use some penetrating oil if it's stuck. Remove any escutcheon plates or trim that cover the faucet body, typically held on by screws or threaded collars.

### Step 3: 3. Extract the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handles and trim are off, you'll expose the faucet stem (for compression faucets) or cartridge (for single-handle or disc faucets). Use an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem or cartridge in place. Gently pull the stem or cartridge straight out of the faucet body. Note its orientation for reassembly.

### Step 4: 4. Inspect and Replace Worn Components

For compression faucets, examine the rubber washer at the end of the stem and the O-rings along its body. These are common culprits for leaks. Remove any worn washers or O-rings using a utility knife or small screwdriver and replace them with new ones of the exact same size. For cartridge faucets, the entire cartridge usually needs to be replaced if it's faulty. Take the old cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you get a matching replacement.

### Step 5: 5. Clean and Lubricate Parts

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet body and the stem/cartridge using a small wire brush or an old toothbrush. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new washers, O-rings, or the rubber seals on the new cartridge. This helps prevent future leaks and makes reassembly easier.

### Step 6: 6. Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the repaired stem or new cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip with your wrench, but do not overtighten. Replace the escutcheon plate, handles, and decorative caps. Ensure all screws are snug but not excessively tight.

### Step 7: 7. Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks. Turn the faucet handles on and off a few times to ensure proper function and to check for any drips. If the leak persists, you may need to recheck the parts or consider professional help.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use genuine replacement parts specific to your faucet's make and model for the best fit and longevity.
- Take photos during disassembly to help you remember the order and orientation of parts for reassembly.
- If a part is stuck due to corrosion, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying to loosen it again.
- Keep a small container or cup handy during disassembly to hold small screws and parts, preventing them from getting lost.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old parts, as they can be brittle or sharp.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, remove the faucet handles and internal components like the stem or cartridge. Inspect and replace worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components. Common culprits include deteriorated rubber washers, hardened O-rings, or a faulty cartridge that no longer seals properly. Mineral deposits can also build up, preventing a tight seal.

**How do I fix a bathtub faucet that drips when off?**

A bathtub faucet that drips when off typically indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. For compression faucets, replace the seat washer and O-rings. For single-handle or disc faucets, replacing the cartridge is usually the solution to stop the drip.

**What tools are needed to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, a utility knife, and the appropriate replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge. Plumber's grease is also recommended for lubrication.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of repairing it?**

Consider replacing your bathtub faucet if it's very old, constantly breaking, or if replacement parts are difficult or impossible to find. If the faucet body itself is corroded or damaged beyond repair, a full replacement is often more cost-effective and reliable in the long run. For guidance, see our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

**Can I fix a leaky single-handle bathtub faucet?**

Yes, a leaky single-handle bathtub faucet can usually be fixed by replacing the internal cartridge. These cartridges are specific to the faucet brand and model, so you'll need to remove the old one and match it exactly at a hardware store for a successful repair.
