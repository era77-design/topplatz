A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water supply, carefully disassembling the faucet handle, and replacing the faulty internal components. This straightforward DIY repair can save water and prevent potential damage to your bathroom.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips, flathead, Allen wrench)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Cleaning cloth
- White vinegar (for mineral deposits)
- Safety glasses
- Small container for parts

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or the specific shut-off valves for your bathroom if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped. This is a critical first step to prevent flooding during the repair process.

> 💡 **Tip:** After turning off the water, open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

Depending on your faucet type, you'll need to remove a decorative cap or screw cover to access the handle screw. Use a screwdriver or Allen wrench to loosen and remove the screw holding the handle in place. Carefully pull the handle straight off the faucet stem to expose the internal components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the faucet's assembly before you start disassembling it. This will be helpful when putting it back together.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Body

Once the handle is off, you'll typically see a decorative escutcheon (cover plate) and then a packing nut or retaining clip. Use an adjustable wrench to loosen and remove the packing nut. For single-handle faucets, you'll likely remove a retaining clip or nut to access the cartridge. Gently pull out the faucet stem or cartridge.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all removed screws and small parts in a small container to prevent them from getting lost down the drain.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any damaged components with exact matches. If it's a cartridge faucet, replace the entire cartridge unit. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for a better seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacements. Faucet brands and models often require specific components.

### Step 5: Clean Faucet Components

Before reassembling, take the opportunity to clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body and components. Use a cleaning cloth and a solution of white vinegar to scrub away calcium buildup, which can also contribute to leaks and poor faucet function.

> 💡 **Tip:** A small brush, like an old toothbrush, can be effective for cleaning tight spaces and threads.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new or cleaned stem/cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Replace the escutcheon, then position the handle back onto the stem and tighten its screw. Don't overtighten components, as this can cause damage.

> 💡 **Tip:** Refer back to the photos you took during disassembly if you're unsure about the order of parts.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water is fully restored, check the faucet for any leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it's functioning correctly and that the drip has stopped. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full replacement.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the repair proves too challenging or the faucet is very old and worn, you might consider a complete replacement. For guidance on this, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Complete DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use the correct tools for the job to avoid stripping screws or damaging faucet finishes.
- If you have an older faucet, consider replacing it entirely if parts are hard to find or it continues to leak after repair. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet) can help.
- Keep spare washers and O-rings on hand if you know your faucet's model, for quicker future repairs.
- Do not overtighten screws or nuts; snug is usually sufficient to prevent damage to plastic or brass components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when handling tools to avoid injury or damaging the porcelain or finish of your tub and faucet.
- ⚠️ If you are unsure at any point or uncomfortable with the repair, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Mineral buildup can also prevent parts from sealing properly, leading to drips.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and the correct replacement parts for your specific faucet model. Plumber's grease and cleaning supplies are also helpful.

**How do I identify the right replacement parts for my faucet?**

The best way is to remove the old, faulty part (washer, O-ring, or cartridge) and take it to a hardware store. Many parts are specific to the faucet brand and model, so having the old part helps ensure a perfect match.

**How do I fix bathtub faucet drips that won't stop?**

Persistent drips usually indicate a worn washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge that needs to be replaced. Ensure the water supply is off, disassemble the faucet, replace the identified worn component, and reassemble carefully.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common leaky bathtub faucet issues, especially those involving washers or cartridges, can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and instructions. However, if the problem is complex or you're uncomfortable, calling a plumber is always an option.

**What if replacing the washer or cartridge doesn't stop the leak?**

If the leak persists after replacing the common parts, the faucet body itself might be corroded or damaged, or you might have misidentified the source of the leak. In such cases, a complete faucet replacement might be necessary, or professional help should be sought.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most DIYers, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and the complexity of your faucet's design. Gathering the correct replacement parts can sometimes take additional time.
