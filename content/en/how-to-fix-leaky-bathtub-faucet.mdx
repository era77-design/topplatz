A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Fortunately, fixing it is a common and straightforward DIY plumbing task that can save water and prevent further damage. This guide will walk you through the steps to stop the drip.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Utility knife (optional, for stubborn caulk)
- Rag or old towel
- Small container or stopper for drain

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house or, if available, the specific shut-off valve for your bathroom. This prevents flooding once you begin disassembling the faucet. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines.

### Step 2: Prepare the Faucet Area

Place a rag or small container over the bathtub drain. This crucial step prevents small screws, washers, or other parts from falling down the drain during disassembly. You'll thank yourself later for this simple precaution.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap or a small screw cover that needs to be popped off or unscrewed. Use a flathead screwdriver or your fingernail to pry off the cap, then use the appropriate screwdriver (usually Phillips) to remove the screw holding the handle in place. Gently pull the handle straight off.

### Step 4: Access the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll likely see a trim plate (escutcheon) and the valve stem or cartridge. Unscrew any retaining screws on the trim plate and remove it. Then, use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully unscrew or pull out the valve stem or cartridge, depending on your faucet type. Note its orientation for reassembly.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any degraded parts with new ones, making sure they match the originals exactly. For cartridge-style faucets, you'll typically replace the entire cartridge unit.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a better seal. Carefully reinsert the valve stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated. Reattach the trim plate, then secure the faucet handle back in place with its screw and decorative cap.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check the faucet thoroughly for any drips or leaks. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure everything is working correctly and the leak has stopped. If you still see a drip, you may need to re-check your parts or ensure everything is tightened correctly.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take a photo of your faucet before disassembling it, and at each stage of disassembly, to help with reassembly.
- When buying replacement parts, it's best to take the old, worn parts with you to the hardware store to ensure an exact match.
- Apply plumber's grease to new rubber O-rings or washers before installation to help them seat properly and last longer.
- Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can damage components and lead to new leaks.
- If you have an older faucet, consider replacing all rubber components (washers, O-rings) at once, even if only one seems faulty.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply or the specific valve for the bathroom before starting any work to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to strip screws or overtighten components during reassembly, as this can cause new leaks or damage the faucet fixtures.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance when removing or tightening parts, stop and re-evaluate. Forcing components can cause irreparable damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What causes a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Most leaky bathtub faucets are caused by worn-out internal components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a deteriorating cartridge. Over time, these parts can degrade, losing their ability to create a watertight seal and allowing water to seep out.

**Can I fix bathtub faucet leaks myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY task that most homeowners can accomplish with basic tools and a little patience. The key is correctly identifying the type of faucet and the specific worn part that needs replacement.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, and potentially a utility knife. You'll also need the appropriate replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model.

**How do I know which replacement parts to buy for my faucet?**

The best way to ensure you get the correct replacement parts is to carefully disassemble your faucet, remove the worn components, and take them to a hardware store. Many parts are specific to faucet brands and models, so having the old part helps immensely.

**When should I consider replacing the entire faucet instead of just fixing the leak?**

If your faucet is old, severely corroded, or you're having trouble finding replacement parts, it might be more cost-effective and efficient to replace the entire unit. For a comprehensive guide on replacement, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a first-timer, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet usually takes between 30 minutes to an hour, assuming you have the right replacement parts on hand. If you need to go to the store for parts, the total time will be longer.
