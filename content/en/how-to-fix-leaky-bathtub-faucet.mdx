To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off your home's water supply, then identify the source of the leak (usually the handle or spout). Most common leaks are resolved by replacing a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge inside the faucet mechanism.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or towel
- Penetrating oil (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the main water supply to your house. This prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair. Turn off the hot and cold water valves, typically located near your water heater or main water meter. If you have separate shut-off valves for the bathroom, use those first.

### Step 2: Identify the Leak Source

Observe your faucet carefully to determine where the leak is originating. Is water dripping from the spout, or is it leaking around the handles? Leaks from the spout often indicate a faulty washer or cartridge, while leaks from the handle usually point to a worn O-ring or stem packing. Knowing the source will guide your repair efforts.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle(s)

Carefully pry off the decorative cap on the faucet handle, usually with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. This will expose a screw, which you'll need to remove with a Phillips or flathead screwdriver. Once the screw is out, pull the handle straight off. You might need to wiggle it gently or use penetrating oil if it's stuck due to mineral buildup.

### Step 4: Access and Replace Worn Components

With the handle removed, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut and remove the stem. Replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and any O-rings. For cartridge faucets, remove the retaining clip or nut, then pull out the old cartridge. Take the old components to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the cartridge for smoother operation and a better seal.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet in reverse order. Ensure all components are seated correctly and screws are tightened securely, but do not overtighten. Overtightening can damage parts and potentially cause new leaks. Replace the handle and its decorative cap. Double-check that all connections are snug before turning the water back on.

### Step 6: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Return to your bathtub faucet and check for any signs of leakage. Turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full faucet replacement. If the leak is gone, congratulations – you've successfully fixed your leaky faucet!


## Tips & Tricks

- Before heading to the store, take a photo of your faucet and any removed parts. This helps ensure you get the correct replacement components.
- If mineral deposits are making it hard to remove parts, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again.
- Keep a bucket or towel handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- Consider replacing all washers and O-rings, even if only one appears worn, as they tend to age similarly.
- If your faucet is old or severely corroded, fixing a leak might be a temporary solution. Sometimes, it's more cost-effective in the long run to replace the entire faucet unit. For guidance on this, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you're unsure about any step, consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet that drips from the spout?**

A leaky bathtub faucet dripping from the spout is most commonly caused by a worn-out washer or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet body. You'll typically need to disassemble the handle, remove the stem or cartridge, and replace the old, worn component with a new one specific to your faucet model.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Bathtub faucets typically leak due to worn-out internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or ceramic cartridges. Over time, these parts degrade from normal use, mineral deposits, and water pressure, losing their ability to create a watertight seal.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's leaking around the handle?**

If your bathtub faucet is leaking around the handle, the issue is usually with a worn O-ring or the stem packing. After turning off the water, remove the handle and then the packing nut to access and replace the O-rings or packing material on the faucet stem. Apply plumber's grease to the new parts before reassembly.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish. With basic tools and the correct replacement parts, you can often resolve the issue in under an hour. However, always ensure the water supply is off before you begin.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if it's very old, heavily corroded, or if multiple repair attempts have failed to stop the leak. Sometimes, the cost of replacement parts for an old or obscure model can approach the cost of a new faucet, making replacement a more practical long-term solution.
