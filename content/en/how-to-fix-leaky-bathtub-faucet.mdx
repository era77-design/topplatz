Most leaky bathtub faucets are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which are relatively simple to replace. Addressing these issues promptly can save water and prevent further damage to your plumbing system. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and fixing the leak.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or flat-blade screwdriver (for prying)
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck parts)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (match your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Rags or towels
- Bucket or small container
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's essential to shut off the main water supply to your house or, if possible, just the water supply to the bathroom. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This prevents accidental flooding once you begin disassembling the faucet.

### Step 2: Prepare the Faucet Area

Place a rag or towel over the drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling into it. Also, have a bucket nearby to catch any residual water. Make sure you have good lighting and all your tools within reach, as some components can be small and easy to lose.

### Step 3: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles to expose the retaining screws. Use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to loosen and remove these screws, then pull the handles straight off. Next, remove the escutcheon or trim plate, which may be held in place by screws or simply threaded onto the faucet body. You might need to gently pry it off with a utility knife.

### Step 4: Access and Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Beneath the handles and trim, you'll find the faucet stem or cartridge, which controls the water flow. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem/cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge, being careful not to damage it. Note its orientation for reassembly.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for any signs of wear, such as cracked washers, brittle O-rings, or corroded parts. For compression faucets, replace the rubber washers and O-rings. For cartridge faucets, you'll typically replace the entire cartridge unit. Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you get exact matches. Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings before installation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Once you have the new components, carefully insert the repaired or new stem/cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Replace the escutcheon, then reattach the faucet handles, securing them with their screws and decorative caps. Do not overtighten any screws.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check for any leaks around the handles, spout, and trim. If you notice any drips, gently tighten the packing nut a quarter turn at a time until the leak stops. Operate the faucet handles to ensure smooth movement and proper water flow. If the leak persists after replacing the internal components, it might be time to consider replacing the entire faucet unit. ## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during disassembly to help remember the order of parts for reassembly.
- Keep all screws and small parts organized in a cup or on a magnetic tray to prevent loss.
- If components are stuck, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying to loosen them again.
- When buying replacement parts, it's helpful to know your faucet's brand and model, or bring the old part with you to the hardware store.
- Applying plumber's grease to threads and O-rings during reassembly can prolong the life of the repair and make future maintenance easier.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair to avoid flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or trim plates to avoid scratching the faucet finish.
- ⚠️ If you encounter ceramic cartridges, handle them with care as they can be brittle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet that's constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is most often caused by worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. You'll need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handles and trim, remove the internal stem or cartridge, and replace the worn components with new ones, then reassemble.

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking from the spout?**

A leak from the bathtub faucet spout typically indicates that the internal components responsible for sealing the water flow when the faucet is off are compromised. This usually means the washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge assembly needs to be replaced due to wear and tear.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll generally need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, a utility knife, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge specific to your faucet, and plumber's grease. Rags and a bucket are also helpful for cleanup.

**How do you fix bathtub faucet when it's leaking from the base of the handle?**

If your bathtub faucet is leaking from the base of the handle, the issue is likely with the packing nut or O-rings around the faucet stem. You'll need to remove the handle and trim, then tighten the packing nut or replace the O-rings/washers on the stem to create a proper seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Most common leaky bathtub faucet issues, such as those caused by worn washers or cartridges, can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. However, if the leak persists after multiple attempts, or if you're uncomfortable with plumbing work, it's best to call a professional plumber.

**How long does it typically take to fix bathtub faucet drips?**

Fixing bathtub faucet drips usually takes between 30 to 60 minutes for most DIYers, assuming you have the correct replacement parts on hand. The time can vary depending on your faucet type, how easily components can be removed, and your experience level.
