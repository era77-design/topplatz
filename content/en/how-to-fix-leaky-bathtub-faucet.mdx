A leaky bathtub faucet is usually caused by worn-out internal components like washers or cartridges. Fixing it involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet handles, replacing the faulty part, and reassembling everything. This simple repair can save water and prevent further damage to your bathroom.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife (optional, for decorative caps)
- Replacement O-rings, washers, or cartridge (match existing type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Vinegar or lime dissolver (for cleaning)
- Clean rags or towels
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Leak Source

First, observe your faucet carefully to determine where the leak is originating. Is it dripping from the spout, leaking around the base of the handles, or from the shower diverter? This initial assessment will help you narrow down the potential cause and the internal components that might need attention.

### Step 2: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before you begin any work, locate and turn off the main water shut-off valve for your house, or the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom if available. Once the water is off, turn on the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure. This prevents unexpected water flow during the repair.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handles

Carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles to expose the retaining screws. Use an appropriate screwdriver (usually Phillips) to remove these screws and then pull the handles straight off. You might also need to remove an escutcheon (a decorative metal plate) that covers the valve body behind the handles.

### Step 4: Remove the Cartridge or Stem Assembly

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either have a cartridge, a stem assembly with washers, or a ball valve. For cartridge faucets, unscrew the retaining nut or remove the clip, then pull the cartridge out. For compression faucets, unscrew the packing nut and remove the stem, which will have a rubber washer at its base.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed O-rings, washers, or the entire cartridge for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. Take the old part to a hardware store to ensure you purchase an exact match. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings before installation to aid in sealing and smooth operation. Clean any mineral deposits from inside the faucet body using vinegar or a lime dissolver.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reverse the disassembly process, ensuring all components are seated correctly. Tighten screws and nuts snugly, but avoid overtightening, which can strip threads or crack plastic parts. Replace the escutcheon, handles, and any decorative caps.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet. Turn on the bathtub faucet to test its operation and confirm that the leak has stopped. If the leak persists after replacing the internal components, or if the faucet body appears damaged, you might need to consider a full faucet replacement. For guidance on this, refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This visual record will be invaluable when you're putting everything back together.
- Keep a bucket or a stack of old towels handy to catch any residual water that might come out of the pipes when you open up the faucet.
- If you can't find an exact replacement part, consider taking the old component to a local hardware store. Their experts can often help you identify and match the correct replacement.
- While the faucet is disassembled, take the opportunity to clean away any mineral deposits or grime that may have accumulated inside the faucet body. This can improve water flow and extend the life of new components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent flooding and water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or damage the faucet body.
- ⚠️ If you are uncomfortable with any step or if the repair seems too complex, it is always best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet that is constantly dripping from the spout?**

A constant drip from the spout typically indicates a worn-out washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet's valve body. You'll need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handles, and replace the specific internal component responsible for sealing the water flow.

**What should I do if my bathtub faucet is leaking from the handle?**

If your bathtub faucet is leaking from around the handle, the issue is often with the O-rings or packing nuts on the faucet stem. After shutting off the water supply, remove the handle and any decorative plates to access and replace these worn seals, which are crucial for preventing leaks around the stem.

**Can I fix bathtub faucet leaks myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common bathtub faucet leaks, such as those caused by worn washers or cartridges, can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and a little patience. However, if the leak persists after your attempt, or if you encounter complex issues, it's always wise to consult a professional plumber.

**What tools are essential for fixing a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll typically need a set of screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, and pliers. Having replacement parts like new O-rings, washers, or a matching cartridge on hand before you start is also crucial for a successful repair.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if the repair doesn't stop the leak?**

If replacing the internal components doesn't stop the leak, the issue might be more extensive, such as corrosion or damage to the faucet body itself. In such cases, repairing the faucet may not be sufficient, and you might need to consider replacing the entire bathtub faucet unit. For a complete replacement guide, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Complete DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy-guide).

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a simple repair like replacing a washer or cartridge, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet usually takes about 30 minutes to an hour. This includes time for shutting off water, disassembly, replacing parts, and reassembly. More complex issues or difficulty finding parts can extend this time.
