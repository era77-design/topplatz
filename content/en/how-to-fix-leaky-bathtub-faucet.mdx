A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can be irritating. Fortunately, most leaks are caused by worn-out parts and can be fixed with basic tools and a little know-how.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge (match your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease
- Rags or towels
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your bathroom or the entire house. Turn on the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and ensure the water is completely off before proceeding.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles to expose the screws beneath. Use a screwdriver to remove these screws and pull off the handles. Next, remove the escutcheon or trim plate; this might be screwed in or simply snap into place.

### Step 3: Access the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Beneath the handles and trim, you'll find the faucet stem (in compression faucets) or a cartridge (in disc, ball, or cartridge faucets). Use an adjustable wrench to loosen and remove the packing nut holding the stem in place, or follow specific instructions for cartridge removal, often requiring a special tool or just pulling it out after removing a clip.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Once the stem or cartridge is removed, inspect the rubber washers, O-rings, and springs for any signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any worn components with new ones that match your faucet's specifications. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often easiest to replace the entire cartridge unit.

### Step 5: Clean and Lubricate Components

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body using a wire brush or old toothbrush. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and the threads of the stem or cartridge. This helps ensure a watertight seal and smoother operation, preventing future leaks.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new stem or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly seated. Tighten the packing nut or secure the cartridge clip. Replace the trim plate and reattach the faucet handles, tightening their screws. Avoid overtightening, as this can damage components.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on to your bathroom or house. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or handles. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has been successfully stopped.


## Tips & Tricks

- Identify your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or disc) before buying replacement parts; this will guide your repair process and save trips to the hardware store.
- Take pictures at each step of disassembly to help you remember how to put everything back together correctly, especially if it's your first time.
- When buying replacement parts, bring the old ones to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match, especially for O-rings and cartridges.
- If the faucet is very old, extensively corroded, or you find repairs are frequent, consider replacing the entire unit for a lasting solution. You can find a guide here: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet | DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent accidental flooding or water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or lead to further leaks.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance, cannot remove a part, or are unsure at any step, it's best to consult a professional plumber to avoid causing more extensive damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply to prevent flooding. Then, disassemble the faucet handles and trim to access the internal components like washers, O-rings, or the cartridge. Most leaks are resolved by replacing these worn-out parts.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes of a bathtub faucet leak are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these components lose their ability to create a watertight seal, leading to drips or steady leaks. Mineral buildup and corrosion can also contribute to the problem.

**How do you fix bathtub faucet drip?**

To effectively fix bathtub faucet drip, you'll typically need to replace the internal rubber components like washers or O-rings, or swap out the entire faucet cartridge. These parts wear down over time, compromising the seal and causing water to slowly escape.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky faucet?**

You'll generally need a screwdriver (both Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and potentially a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver for decorative caps. Crucially, you'll also need the specific replacement parts for your faucet type, such as new washers, O-rings, or a cartridge.

**Can I fix a leaky faucet myself?**

Yes, most common bathtub faucet leaks can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and a bit of patience. The process involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, replacing worn internal parts, and then reassembling everything carefully.

**When should I call a plumber for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a plumber if you're unable to identify the source of the leak, if the faucet is severely corroded, or if you encounter significant difficulties during disassembly or reassembly. Persistent leaks after attempting a repair or issues with the main water shut-off valve are also good reasons to seek professional help.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes about 15 to 45 minutes for a moderately experienced DIYer, depending on the faucet type and the specific issue. The time can vary based on the availability of replacement parts and any unexpected complications like corroded screws or stubborn components.
