A leaky bathtub faucet is a common plumbing issue that wastes water and can be annoying. Most leaks are caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or seals inside the faucet handle or spout. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, and replacing the faulty components.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rag or towel
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub, if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water supply is completely off. Once off, turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and ensure the water flow has stopped completely.

### Step 2: Identify Faucet Type and Disassemble Handle

Bathtub faucets come in various types, including compression, cartridge, ceramic disc, and ball faucets. Examine your faucet to determine its type, as this will dictate the specific repair parts needed. For most faucets, you'll need to remove the decorative cap or screw cover on the handle, then use a screwdriver to loosen and remove the handle screw. Carefully pull the handle off to expose the internal components.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

With the handle removed, you'll see a escutcheon plate or a packing nut. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut. For compression faucets, you'll then be able to pull out the stem assembly. For cartridge or ceramic disc faucets, you might need to remove a retaining clip or nut before pulling out the cartridge. Keep track of the order of parts as you remove them.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Once the stem or cartridge is out, inspect all rubber washers, O-rings, and seals for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any worn parts with new ones, ensuring they are the correct size and type for your faucet. Apply a thin coat of plumber's grease to new O-rings for easier reassembly and better sealing. If your faucet uses a cartridge, it's often easiest to replace the entire cartridge unit if it's faulty.

### Step 5: Clean Components and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body and components using a small brush or utility knife. This helps ensure a tight seal with the new parts. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated. Tighten the packing nut or retaining clip, then reattach the handle, making sure it's aligned correctly. Don't overtighten screws, as this can damage components.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Do not turn it on too quickly, as this can cause a surge that might dislodge newly installed parts. Once the water is fully on, test the faucet by turning it on and off several times. Check thoroughly for any drips or leaks around the handle, spout, or base of the faucet. If you still have a leak from the spout, you may need to investigate the diverter or spout itself. For issues with the spout, you might find our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet Spout: A Quick DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-spout) helpful.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly to help with reassembly.
- Place a rag over the drain to prevent small parts from falling into the pipes.
- If you're unsure about the type of faucet or parts needed, take the old parts to a hardware store for an exact match.
- Consider replacing both hot and cold side components even if only one is leaking, as they are likely the same age.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to overtighten nuts and screws, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or can't remove a part, do not force it, as you may cause further damage. Consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet that drips from the spout?**

A drip from the spout of a bathtub faucet usually indicates worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet handle assembly. You'll need to shut off the water, disassemble the handle, and replace the deteriorated components to stop the leak.

**What parts do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The specific parts depend on your faucet type, but common replacements include rubber washers, O-rings, a new stem assembly for compression faucets, or a new cartridge for cartridge-style faucets. It's often helpful to have a faucet repair kit or the old parts to match at a hardware store.

**Can I fix my bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Most common leaky bathtub faucet repairs, such as replacing washers or cartridges, can be done by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools. If the leak persists after component replacement, or if you encounter complex issues, it's best to call a professional plumber.

**How to fix a bathtub faucet that won't turn off completely?**

If your bathtub faucet won't turn off completely, it's likely due to worn internal components that no longer seal properly. This often means replacing the stem washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that controls water flow. Follow the steps for disassembling and replacing the internal parts.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a bathtub faucet leak are worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals that have hardened, cracked, or deteriorated over time. Mineral buildup can also prevent components from sealing correctly, leading to drips.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a typical DIY repair involving replacing washers or a cartridge, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet usually takes between 30 to 60 minutes. This includes time for shutting off the water, disassembling, replacing parts, and reassembling the faucet.
