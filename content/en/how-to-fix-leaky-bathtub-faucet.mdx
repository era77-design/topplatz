Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which deteriorate over time. By following a few simple steps, you can often resolve the issue yourself, saving water and preventing further damage. This DIY guide will walk you through the process, even for common bathtub faucet problems.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet)
- Plumber's grease
- Old rags or towels
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck screws)
- Pliers (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the faucet on briefly after shutting off the water to drain any remaining water from the pipes, preventing unexpected leaks during the repair.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a towel over the drain or insert a stopper to prevent small parts from falling into the drain during disassembly.

### Step 2: Identify Your Faucet Type

Bathtub faucets come in various styles, most commonly compression, cartridge, or ceramic disc. Compression faucets have two handles and use rubber washers. Cartridge faucets typically have a single handle and use a cylindrical cartridge. Ceramic disc faucets also often have a single handle and use two ceramic discs. Knowing your type helps you get the right replacement parts.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a picture of your faucet before disassembling. This can be helpful when buying replacement parts or if you need to reference how it looked.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle(s)

For two-handle faucets, pry off the decorative caps on top of each handle using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screws, then pull the handles straight off. For single-handle faucets, locate the set screw (often on the underside or back of the handle) and loosen it with an Allen wrench or screwdriver, then pull the handle off.

> 💡 **Tip:** If screws are stuck due to mineral buildup, apply a small amount of penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again.

### Step 4: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll expose the faucet stem (for compression faucets) or the cartridge (for single-handle faucets). For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut, then pull out the stem. For cartridge faucets, remove any retaining clip or nut holding the cartridge in place, then pull the cartridge straight out, sometimes with pliers.

> 💡 **Tip:** Carefully observe or take photos of how the components are oriented before removal to ensure correct reassembly.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a cracked cartridge. For compression faucets, replace the rubber washer at the end of the stem and the O-ring around the stem. For cartridge faucets, replace the entire cartridge with a new one that matches your faucet model. Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings and moving parts to ensure smooth operation and a good seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old parts to your local hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements. This is especially important for cartridges.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet components in the reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all parts are seated correctly and screws are tightened securely but not overtightened, which can strip threads or crack plastic. Make sure the handles are aligned properly before securing them. If you find the repair too complex or parts are hard to find, you might consider replacing the entire faucet. For guidance, see our article on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

> 💡 **Tip:** Test the movement of the handles before fully tightening everything to ensure they operate smoothly.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the bathtub faucet handles to flush any air from the pipes, then close them. Carefully inspect around the faucet base, handles, and spout for any signs of leaks. If a leak persists, you may need to re-check your connections or consider replacing more components. Sometimes, a full faucet replacement is more efficient. You can find steps for this in [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep a dry paper towel handy to quickly identify any small drips or leaks that might not be immediately visible.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always identify your faucet's brand and model if possible (often on the faucet body or handle) before buying replacement parts.
- Keep a small container or cup handy during disassembly to hold small screws and parts, preventing them from getting lost.
- If your faucet is old and corroded, consider cleaning it thoroughly while disassembled to improve its overall function and lifespan.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before starting any plumbing work to avoid flooding and potential damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or damage the faucet body.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or are unsure about a step, it's best to consult a professional plumber to avoid causing further damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the internal stem or cartridge, and replace worn components like washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet usually indicates a worn-out washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet body. Replacing these internal components is the most common and effective way to stop the drip and restore proper function.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The primary cause of a leaky bathtub faucet is the wear and tear of internal components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or the ceramic disc/cartridge. Over time, these parts degrade, lose their elasticity, or become corroded, preventing a complete seal and leading to drips or steady leaks.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is often a straightforward DIY project that most homeowners can accomplish. With basic tools and the correct replacement parts, you can usually repair common leaks without needing a professional plumber.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife or small flathead for prying caps, and possibly pliers. You'll also need the specific replacement washers, O-rings, or a cartridge for your faucet model.

**How do I know if I need to replace a washer or a cartridge?**

The type of repair depends on your faucet's design. Two-handle compression faucets use washers and O-rings, while single-handle faucets typically use a cartridge or ceramic disc assembly. Disassemble your faucet to identify the internal mechanism and the specific worn part.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're unable to identify the faucet type, can't find the correct replacement parts, if components are severely corroded and stuck, or if the leak persists after attempting the repair. Also, if you're uncomfortable working with plumbing, a pro can ensure a proper fix.
