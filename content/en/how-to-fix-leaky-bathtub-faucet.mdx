Most leaky bathtub faucets are caused by worn-out washers or cartridges. Fixing them usually involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet handle, and replacing the faulty component. This DIY guide will walk you through the process step-by-step.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips, flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (specific to faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or towel
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off for your bathroom. Turn it off completely to prevent flooding. Test the faucet by turning it on briefly to ensure the water flow has stopped before proceeding.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find a specific bathroom shut-off, the main house valve is usually in the basement, utility closet, or near the water meter.

### Step 2: Access the Faucet Handle

Use a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver to carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles. Once removed, you'll typically find a screw underneath. Unscrew this to detach the handle from the faucet body.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place removed screws and caps in a safe place, like a small container, to avoid losing them during the repair.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

With the handle removed, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type, you might need an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut or a cartridge puller tool to remove the cartridge. Note its orientation before removal, as it's important for reassembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the components as you disassemble them; this visual reference can be invaluable when putting everything back together.

### Step 4: Identify and Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks. For older compression faucets, replace the seat washer at the bottom of the stem. For cartridge faucets, replace the entire cartridge with a new, identical one. Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings before installation.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old part to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match for your faucet model.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet components in reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all parts are seated correctly and screws are tightened. Avoid over-tightening, which can strip threads or crack plastic components. Reattach the handle and decorative caps.

> 💡 **Tip:** Double-check that any packing nuts are snug but not overly tight, as this can restrict handle movement.

### Step 6: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the bathtub faucet to release any trapped air, then close it completely. Carefully check around the handle and spout for any new leaks. If the leak persists, you may need to recheck your work or consider further investigation.

> 💡 **Tip:** Wipe the area dry before testing to easily spot any small drips that might appear.


## Tips & Tricks

- Keep a bucket handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If components are difficult to remove due to mineral buildup, try applying a penetrating oil (being careful to avoid contact with plastic parts).
- Use plumber's grease on O-rings and threads to ensure a good seal and make future repairs easier.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts; it can strip threads or crack components, leading to more problems.
- If unsure about your faucet type or the required replacement parts, consult the manufacturer's website or a plumbing professional.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding and water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or removing old parts, as they can be brittle and break easily.
- ⚠️ If you encounter a seized or corroded component that won't budge with reasonable force, avoid excessive force, which could damage the plumbing. Consider calling a professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet if it's dripping from the spout?**

A continuous drip from the spout usually indicates a worn-out washer or cartridge inside the faucet handle assembly. Replacing these internal components, often the seat washer or the entire cartridge, is typically the solution.

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet handle?**

If the leak is around the faucet handle, it's typically due to worn O-rings or a loose packing nut on the faucet stem. Disassemble the handle, replace the O-rings, or tighten the packing nut to resolve this issue.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and potentially a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver for decorative caps. Crucially, you'll also need the specific replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge.

**How can I identify the type of faucet to fix a bathtub faucet?**

Bathtub faucets are commonly compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc types. Compression faucets have separate hot and cold handles, while cartridge faucets often have a single handle. Removing the handle will usually reveal the internal mechanism, helping you identify it and get the correct replacement parts.

**Is it better to repair or replace a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For minor leaks caused by worn washers or cartridges, repair is generally more cost-effective and simpler. However, if the faucet body is corroded, parts are hard to find, or you're experiencing multiple issues, replacing the entire faucet might be a better long-term solution. For guidance on replacement, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a DIY enthusiast, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes about 30 minutes to an hour, assuming you have the right tools and replacement parts readily available. More complex issues or seized components can extend this time.

**What if my bathtub faucet continues to leak after I've tried to fix it?**

If the leak persists, double-check that all components are correctly installed and tightened. The issue might be a more complex problem, such as a damaged valve seat, a crack in the faucet body, or an incorrect replacement part, which might necessitate professional help or a full faucet replacement.
