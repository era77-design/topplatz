Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing a worn-out washer or cartridge, which are common culprits for drips. This practical DIY repair can help you save water, prevent costly damage, and is often achievable with basic tools and a little patience.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers (needle-nose and/or groove-joint)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Cleaning cloth
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)
- Safety glasses

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house or, if possible, the shut-off valve specifically for the bathtub. Run the faucet briefly to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This crucial first step prevents flooding and ensures a safe working environment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Confirm the water is off by turning on the faucet handles – no water should come out.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Use a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to gently pry off any decorative caps on top of the faucet handles. Unscrew the handle screws, then pull the handles straight off. Depending on your faucet type, you might also need to remove a decorative escutcheon plate or trim cover, usually held in place by screws or simply threaded on.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all removed screws and parts organized in a small container to prevent losing them.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

With the handles removed, you'll expose the faucet stems or a cartridge. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut, then carefully pull out the stem. For single-handle or disc faucets, you'll typically remove a retaining nut or clip to extract the cartridge. Note the orientation of all parts as you remove them.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly to aid in reassembly.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracked plastic components. For compression faucets, the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem is usually the culprit. Carefully remove the old, hardened washer and replace it with a new one of the exact same size. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new rubber components and O-rings before installation. If you have a cartridge faucet, you'll likely need to replace the entire cartridge unit.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old washer or cartridge to the hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacement part for your specific faucet model.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet in reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all components are seated correctly and screws are tightened securely but do not overtighten, as this can strip threads or damage plastic parts. Double-check that all washers and O-rings are in their proper places and lubricated with plumber's grease. If you're having trouble identifying the correct parts or if the leak persists, considering a full replacement might be necessary. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy) might be helpful in that case.

> 💡 **Tip:** Before fully tightening, ensure all parts align perfectly to prevent future leaks or operational issues.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Once the faucet is fully reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or handles. Turn on both the hot and cold water to ensure proper flow and temperature control, and then turn them off completely. Observe the faucet for a few minutes to confirm that the leak has stopped.

> 💡 **Tip:** Listen for any unusual sounds as the water fills the pipes, which could indicate air trapped in the lines.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use a silicone-based plumber's grease on new rubber washers and O-rings to extend their lifespan and ensure a smooth operation.
- If screws or components are stuck, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again. Avoid forcing parts, as this can cause further damage.
- When purchasing replacement parts, it's helpful to know your faucet's brand and model number, often found on the faucet body or in your home's original plumbing documentation.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repairs to prevent water damage or injury.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when handling plumbing tools, especially wrenches and pliers, to avoid pinching fingers or damaging fixtures.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant corrosion, stripped screws, or are unsure about any step, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the handles, and replacing worn components like rubber washers or a cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully and test for leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet?**

To fix a bathtub faucet, first shut off the water supply. Then, remove the decorative caps and handles to access the internal mechanism. Replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, and then reassemble the faucet.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their seal, allowing water to drip.

**Do I need a plumber to fix a leaky faucet?**

For simple leaks caused by worn washers or cartridges, most homeowners can fix a leaky faucet themselves with basic tools. However, if the leak is complex, involves significant corrosion, or you're uncomfortable with the repair, calling a plumber is advisable.

**How do I know what replacement part I need for my leaky faucet?**

The best way to identify the correct replacement part is to disassemble your faucet and remove the worn component (washer, O-ring, or cartridge). Take the old part to a hardware store or plumbing supply shop to match it, or consult your faucet's manual for specific part numbers.

**Can I fix a leaky faucet without turning off the main water?**

No, it is highly recommended to always turn off the main water supply to your house or the specific shut-off valve for the bathtub before attempting any faucet repair. Failing to do so can result in significant water leaks and potential damage.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

A typical leaky bathtub faucet repair can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet. Gathering tools and replacement parts might add additional time.
