A leaky bathtub faucet is usually caused by a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge, leading to annoying drips and wasted water. Fixing it is a common DIY task that can save you money and prevent potential water damage. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and repairing a leaky faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge (match your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Old rags or towels
- Bucket (optional, for drips)
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn parts)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting any repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house, or the shut-off valves for the bathroom if available. This crucial first step prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process. Test the faucet afterward to confirm the water is completely off.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find individual shut-off valves for the tub, turning off the main house supply is the safest option.

### Step 2: Drain Faucet and Cover Drain

Once the water is off, open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and relieve pressure. Place a rag or cover over the drain opening to prevent small screws or parts from falling into the drain during disassembly. This small step can save you a lot of frustration later.

> 💡 **Tip:** Use a small plastic bag or a piece of cloth secured with a rubber band over the drain.

### Step 3: Remove Faucet Handle and Trim

Carefully pry off the decorative cap on the faucet handle (if present) using a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver. This will expose the handle screw. Unscrew the handle and pull it off. Then, remove any escutcheon plate or trim covering the faucet body, typically held by screws or simply threaded on.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos with your phone as you disassemble the faucet; it will help immensely during reassembly.

### Step 4: Disassemble the Faucet Body

Depending on your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or disc), you'll need to access the internal components. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut and pull out the stem. For cartridge faucets, remove the retaining clip or nut holding the cartridge in place, then pull the cartridge straight out. Ball and disc faucets have their own specific disassembly methods, often involving a cap and cam assembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** If parts are stuck, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the removed components for wear. The most common culprits for leaks are worn rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Replace any visibly damaged or hardened rubber components. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often easiest to replace the entire cartridge with a new one that matches your old one exactly. Apply a thin coat of plumber's grease to new rubber parts before installation.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old washer, O-ring, or cartridge to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reassemble the faucet components in the reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all parts are seated correctly and screws are tightened snugly but do not overtighten, as this can damage components. Replace the trim and faucet handle, making sure the handle operates smoothly. Double-check that all connections are secure.

> 💡 **Tip:** Refer to the photos you took during disassembly if you get stuck on the reassembly process.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Once the water is fully restored, open the faucet and check for any leaks around the handle, spout, and base. Run both hot and cold water for a minute to flush out any air and ensure proper function. If leaks persist, you may need to re-tighten parts or re-examine the seals.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep a dry paper towel handy to quickly spot any new or lingering drips.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always identify your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc) before buying replacement parts, as they are not interchangeable.
- If you encounter a particularly stubborn screw or part, a little penetrating oil can work wonders. Let it soak for 10-15 minutes.
- Keep a small container or magnetic tray for screws and small parts to prevent them from getting lost during the repair.
- Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet's internal components while it's disassembled; this can improve performance.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when prying off decorative caps or handles, especially on older fixtures, to avoid scratching or breaking them.
- ⚠️ If you are unsure about any step or if the leak persists after your repair, consider consulting a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I identify why my bathtub faucet is leaking?**

The location of the leak often indicates the cause. A drip from the spout usually means a worn washer or cartridge inside the faucet body. Leaks around the handle or base might point to a faulty O-ring or packing nut. Observing when and where the leak occurs is the first step to diagnosis.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife or small flathead for prying caps, and replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge. Old rags, plumber's grease, and potentially penetrating oil are also very helpful.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Most common leaky bathtub faucet issues, such as replacing washers or a cartridge, are manageable DIY tasks for someone with basic tools and a little patience. This guide provides the steps you need. However, if the problem is complex, involves significant rust, or you're uncomfortable with plumbing, calling a professional is always an option.

**How do you fix a bathtub faucet that drips constantly?**

To fix a bathtub faucet that drips constantly, you'll generally need to replace the internal components responsible for sealing the water flow. This most commonly involves replacing worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge assembly. Following the steps in this guide will walk you through the process.

**What's the most common cause of a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The most common cause of a leaky bathtub faucet is typically a worn-out rubber washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or degrade due to mineral deposits and regular use, losing their ability to create a watertight seal.

**When should I consider replacing the entire faucet instead of just fixing a leak?**

You should consider replacing the entire faucet if it's very old, corroded, or if replacement parts are difficult or impossible to find. If you've attempted multiple repairs and the leaks persist, or if you simply want to update the look of your bathroom, a full replacement might be more cost-effective in the long run. For guidance on this, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most common leaks, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes between 30 minutes to an hour. This time can vary depending on your experience level, the type of faucet, and how easily the components can be disassembled and reassembled.
