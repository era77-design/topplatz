A leaky bathtub faucet can be an annoying waste of water and money. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which can be easily replaced with basic tools. Follow these steps to diagnose and stop the drip.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or flat-head screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Old towel or rag
- Wire brush or sandpaper (for cleaning)
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck parts)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Prepare and Shut Off Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to turn off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the shut-off valve specifically for the bathroom. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines. Place a rag or towel over the drain opening to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: 2. Access the Faucet Handle and Trim

Identify your faucet type (single-handle, two-handle, or three-handle). For most faucets, you'll need to remove a decorative cap (often plastic) from the center of the handle to expose a screw. Use a screwdriver to remove this screw, then gently pull the handle straight off. Next, remove any decorative escutcheons or trim plates to expose the valve components underneath.

### Step 3: 3. Remove the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either have a valve stem (common in older two-handle compression faucets) or a cartridge (common in newer single-handle and some two-handle faucets). For valve stems, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut, then pull out the stem. For cartridges, you might need to remove a retaining clip or nut before pulling the cartridge straight out with pliers.

### Step 4: 4. Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Carefully examine the removed valve stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or gaskets. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Use a utility knife or small screwdriver to pry off old washers. Replace them with new ones that exactly match the size and shape. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often best to replace the entire cartridge unit, as internal wear can be hard to spot.

### Step 5: 5. Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet body using a wire brush or sandpaper. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease or silicone lubricant to new washers, O-rings, or the new cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a better seal. Carefully reinsert the valve stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly aligned. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip.

### Step 6: 6. Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Reattach the handle and any decorative trim in reverse order of disassembly. Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Turn the bathtub faucet on and off several times, checking carefully for any drips or leaks around the handle and spout. If you still see a drip, you may need to slightly tighten the packing nut (for compression faucets) or recheck your component installation.

### Step 7: 7. Troubleshooting Persistent Leaks

If your faucet continues to leak after replacing the internal components, the problem might be more complex, such as damage to the faucet body itself, or the wrong replacement parts were used. In some cases, especially with older faucets, parts may be obsolete, making a full replacement a more practical solution. Consider consulting a professional plumber or exploring options for a new fixture if repairs prove unsuccessful.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly. This will be invaluable when putting everything back together.
- When buying replacement parts, take the old ones (or the entire cartridge) to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match for your specific faucet model.
- Keep a small container of plumber's grease or silicone lubricant handy for any future faucet repairs; it helps prolong the life of rubber seals.
- If dealing with very stubborn or corroded parts, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a while before attempting to loosen them.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely shut off before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when dealing with hot water lines, as residual hot water can cause burns.
- ⚠️ Avoid overtightening screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to new leaks or damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to shut off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the internal valve stem or cartridge, and replace any worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components. Over time, rubber washers, O-rings, and cartridges can degrade, crack, or become stiff, losing their ability to create a watertight seal, leading to drips from the spout or leaks around the handle.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. The process usually involves replacing inexpensive internal parts. However, if the faucet body is damaged or parts are obsolete, professional help or a full replacement might be necessary.

**How to fix bathtub faucet drips?**

To fix bathtub faucet drips, the most common solution is to replace the worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge inside the faucet's valve assembly. These components are responsible for sealing the water flow, and when they fail, drips occur.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set, an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, pliers, and possibly a utility knife to fix a leaky faucet. You'll also need the correct replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge) specific to your faucet model.

**When should I replace the entire faucet instead of repairing it?**

You should consider replacing the entire faucet if internal components are corroded beyond repair, the faucet body itself is cracked or damaged, replacement parts are no longer available, or if you've attempted repairs multiple times without success. If you find yourself needing to replace the entire fixture, a guide like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) can provide detailed steps.

**What if my bathtub faucet spout is leaking?**

If your bathtub faucet spout is leaking, it usually indicates a problem with the internal valve (washers, O-rings, or cartridge) rather than the spout itself. However, if the spout itself is visibly cracked or damaged, you might need to replace just the spout.
