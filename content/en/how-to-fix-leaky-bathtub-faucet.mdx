A leaky bathtub faucet is usually caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the handle. Fixing it involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet, replacing the compromised component, and reassembling everything. This guide will walk you through the process, helping you save water and prevent annoyance.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Plumber's grease
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (match your faucet type)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying)
- Rag or towel
- Wire brush or old toothbrush (for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the main water supply to your house or, if available, the specific shut-off valve for the bathroom. Turn on the faucet briefly after shutting off the water to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process.

### Step 2: Cover the Drain and Prepare the Area

Place a rag or towel over the bathtub drain opening. This simple step is vital to prevent small screws, O-rings, or other tiny components from falling down the drain. Lay down a towel in the tub as well to protect the finish and provide a softer surface for tools.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that can be pried off with a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver. Underneath, you'll find a screw (often Phillips-head) holding the handle in place. Unscrew it and carefully pull the handle straight off. Some older faucets might have a set screw on the side of the handle instead.

### Step 4: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type, you might need an adjustable wrench to loosen a packing nut or a specific cartridge puller tool. Carefully unscrew or pull out the stem/cartridge. Pay attention to how it's oriented for easier reassembly.

### Step 5: Identify and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or damaged springs. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Remove the old components and replace them with new ones that precisely match your faucet's specifications. If the entire cartridge appears damaged or corroded, it's best to replace the whole cartridge unit.

### Step 6: Clean and Lubricate Parts

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet body and the stem/cartridge using a wire brush or old toothbrush. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new O-rings, washers, and the threads of the stem or cartridge. This helps create a better seal and makes future repairs easier.

### Step 7: Reassemble and Test for Leaks

Carefully reassemble the faucet in reverse order, ensuring all components are seated correctly and screws are tightened snugly (but don't overtighten). Once everything is back together, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check the faucet thoroughly for any drips or leaks, both when turned off and on.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This visual guide will be invaluable when putting everything back together.
- When buying replacement parts, it's best to take the old washer, O-ring, or cartridge with you to the hardware store to ensure an exact match.
- If you find significant corrosion or damage to the faucet body itself, or if the leak persists after replacing components, consider replacing the entire faucet. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy)
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts. This can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to more significant problems.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply to avoid flooding. Confirm it's off by running water before starting work.
- ⚠️ Be careful when handling tools to avoid scratching or damaging the finish of your bathtub or faucet.
- ⚠️ If you encounter extreme difficulty, stubborn parts, or are unsure at any point, it's safer to call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the stem or cartridge, and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Finally, reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty internal cartridge. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and causing drips.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that is constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet typically indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. You'll need to turn off the water, remove the faucet handle and stem/cartridge, and replace the worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that is no longer sealing properly.

**Do I need special tools to fix a leaky faucet?**

Most leaky faucet repairs can be done with common household tools like screwdrivers and an adjustable wrench. However, for some cartridge-style faucets, a specific cartridge puller might be helpful. Having plumber's grease and matching replacement parts is also essential.

**When should I replace my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if the body is severely corroded, if parts are obsolete and hard to find, or if the leak persists even after replacing washers and cartridges multiple times. Sometimes, a full replacement is more cost-effective and durable. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy)

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a DIY enthusiast, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes about 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet. Gathering the correct replacement parts beforehand can significantly speed up the process.

**Is it worth fixing a dripping faucet?**

Yes, it is definitely worth fixing a dripping faucet. A constant drip can waste hundreds of gallons of water per year, leading to higher water bills. It can also cause staining in your tub and, if left unaddressed, potentially lead to more significant water damage over time.
