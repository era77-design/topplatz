A persistent drip from your bathtub faucet can be annoying and wasteful. Most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed with basic tools by replacing worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges. This guide will walk you through the process to restore your faucet to proper working order.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- New O-rings, washers, or replacement cartridge (match existing parts)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house or, preferably, just the supply to the bathtub itself if there's a dedicated shut-off valve. This crucial first step prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process. Turn on the faucet briefly after shutting off the water to relieve any residual pressure in the pipes.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find a dedicated shut-off for the tub, turn off the main water supply to your entire home.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucet handles have a decorative cap covering a screw. Carefully pry off this cap using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw, then gently pull the handle straight off. If it's stuck, a little wiggling or a handle puller might be necessary, but avoid forcing it to prevent damage.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all removed screws and parts in a small container to avoid losing them.

### Step 3: Access and Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Beneath the handle, you'll find a decorative escutcheon or trim plate, which you may need to unscrew and remove. This will expose the faucet stem or cartridge, which is the core component that controls water flow. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully unscrew and remove the retaining nut or clip holding the stem/cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the assembly before disassembling further; it can be a helpful reference during reassembly.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Once the stem or cartridge is removed, carefully inspect it for any signs of wear, such as cracked O-rings, hardened rubber washers, or mineral buildup. These are the most common culprits for a leaky faucet. Remove the old, worn parts and replace them with new, identical ones. If you have a cartridge-style faucet, it's often easier and more effective to replace the entire cartridge with a new one that matches the old part exactly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, take a moment to clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body using a wire brush or old toothbrush. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and the moving parts of the stem or cartridge. Carefully insert the new or repaired stem/cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Reattach the retaining nut or clip, then the escutcheon, and finally the handle.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't overtighten components, as this can damage threads or crack plastic parts.

### Step 6: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on, listening for any hissing sounds that might indicate a new leak. Open the bathtub faucet fully to flush out any air in the lines, then close it completely. Observe the faucet for a few minutes to ensure the leak has stopped. If the leak persists or if the faucet is old and extensively corroded, consider replacing the entire unit for a more lasting solution. For a complete replacement guide, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

> 💡 **Tip:** If a small drip remains, it might be due to a tiny piece of debris or a slightly misaligned component; repeat the test or re-check connections.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or towel handy to catch any drips when disassembling the faucet.
- If you're unsure about the type of cartridge or washer needed, take the old part to a plumbing supply store for an exact match.
- Lubricate new O-rings and rubber washers with plumber's grease to ensure a good seal and prolong their lifespan.
- Avoid using harsh chemical cleaners on faucet finishes, as they can cause damage.
- For stubborn handles, a dedicated faucet handle puller can prevent damage to the handle or stem.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off caps or handles to avoid scratching the faucet finish or injuring yourself.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts during reassembly, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter lead pipes, exercise extreme caution and consider calling a professional plumber due to potential health risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the handle, and replace worn internal components like O-rings, washers, or the entire cartridge. These parts are usually responsible for controlling water flow and often wear out over time, causing drips.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a bathtub faucet leak are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Mineral buildup, corrosion, or loose packing nuts can also contribute to leaks, preventing a tight seal within the faucet mechanism.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that keeps dripping after turning off the water?**

If your bathtub faucet continues to drip after the water is off, it's almost certainly due to a worn-out washer, O-ring, or a damaged cartridge inside the faucet body. Replacing these internal components will restore the seal and stop the drip.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and a little patience. Replacing washers, O-rings, or cartridges is a common home repair. However, if the leak is severe, the faucet is very old, or you're uncomfortable with the process, calling a professional plumber is always a safe option.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, and potentially a utility knife to pry off decorative caps. You'll also need replacement parts like O-rings, washers, or a new cartridge that matches your faucet's specific model.

**When should I replace my bathtub faucet instead of just fixing the leak?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if the leak persists after multiple repair attempts, if the faucet body itself is corroded or damaged, or if replacement parts are difficult or impossible to find. Sometimes, an old, extensively worn faucet is beyond simple repair, and a new unit offers better long-term reliability and water efficiency. For guidance on replacement, refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).
