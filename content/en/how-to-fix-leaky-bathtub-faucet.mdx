A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can lead to higher utility bills. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers or cartridges, and can often be fixed with basic tools and a bit of DIY effort.

## What You Need

- Phillips and flathead screwdrivers
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or small flat-blade screwdriver
- Replacement O-rings, washers, or cartridge (matching your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease
- Towel or rag
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub. Turn them off completely to prevent water flow during the repair. Run the faucet briefly to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

### Step 2: 2. Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that can be pried off with a utility knife or small flat-blade screwdriver to reveal a screw. Use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to remove this screw, then gently pull the handle straight off. Some handles may simply pull off or unscrew without a visible cap.

### Step 3: 3. Access the Faucet Cartridge or Stem

Once the handle is off, you'll typically see a decorative escutcheon or trim plate held by screws. Remove these screws and pull off the plate. Beneath this, you'll find the faucet's main mechanism, which could be a stem assembly held by a large nut or a cartridge secured by a retaining clip or nut.

### Step 4: 4. Remove and Inspect Components

Carefully remove the stem or cartridge. If it's a stem, use an adjustable wrench to loosen the large nut holding it in place, then pull it out. If it's a cartridge, remove any retaining clips or nuts and gently pull it straight out. Inspect all rubber O-rings, washers, and the cartridge itself for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks.

### Step 5: 5. Replace Worn Parts

Take the old O-rings, washers, or cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements. If replacing O-rings, carefully pry off the old ones and slide on the new ones, applying a thin layer of plumber's grease. If replacing a cartridge, ensure the new one matches the old one perfectly. This is the crucial step to fix a leaky bathtub faucet.

### Step 6: 6. Reassemble the Faucet

Insert the new or repaired stem/cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's correctly aligned. Tighten any nuts or replace retaining clips. Reattach the escutcheon or trim plate, then secure the faucet handle with its screw and replace the decorative cap. Make sure all connections are snug but do not overtighten.

### Step 7: 7. Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or handle. Turn the faucet on and off several times, checking for drips from the spout or around the handle. If no leaks are present, congratulations, you've successfully fixed your leaky bathtub faucet!


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone during disassembly to help remember the order of parts for reassembly.
- Keep a towel or bucket handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and threaded connections to ensure a good seal and ease future repairs.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely turned off before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter stubborn parts or are unsure about any step, it's best to consult a professional plumber to avoid further damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle and trim, remove the internal cartridge or stem, replace worn-out O-rings or the entire cartridge, and then reassemble the faucet. This usually resolves most common leaks.

**What commonly causes a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The most common causes for a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber O-rings, washers, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet body. Over time, these components can degrade, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a proper seal and leading to drips or leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that drips constantly?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is usually a sign of a worn-out washer or cartridge that isn't fully sealing the water flow. The primary solution is to replace these internal components, following the steps of turning off water, disassembling, replacing parts, and reassembling the faucet.

**What tools are essential to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Essential tools for fixing a leaky bathtub faucet include screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, and a utility knife. You will also need the correct replacement O-rings, washers, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

If your faucet is old, severely corroded, or the leak persists despite replacing common wear parts, a full replacement might be more cost-effective in the long run. You can find detailed instructions on replacement here: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's still leaking after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet is still leaking after replacing parts, double-check that you used the correct replacement components for your specific faucet model. Ensure all parts are seated correctly and tightened appropriately, and that there are no cracks in the faucet body itself. Sometimes, professional help is needed for unusual leaks.

**Is fixing a leaky bathtub faucet a DIY project for beginners?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is generally considered an easy DIY project suitable for beginners, especially if the issue is a simple worn-out O-ring or washer. The key is to carefully follow instructions, have the right tools, and ensure the water supply is off.
