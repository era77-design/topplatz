A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can be surprisingly easy to fix. Most leaks stem from worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge, all of which can be replaced with basic tools and a little patience. This guide will walk you through the process to silence that drip.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags or towels
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning threads)
- Utility knife (optional, for scraping old sealant)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house, or the shut-off valves for the bathtub specifically if they exist. This prevents accidental flooding once you begin disassembling the faucet. Turn on the faucet briefly after shutting off the water to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a towel over the drain to catch any small parts that might fall during the repair process.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap or a small button. Carefully pry off the cap with a utility knife or a flathead screwdriver, then unscrew the handle. Some handles might simply pull off once the screw is removed, while others may require a gentle wiggle.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all removed screws and parts in a small container to avoid losing them.

### Step 3: Remove the Escutcheon and Valve Stem/Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll likely see a decorative escutcheon or trim plate. Unscrew or pry this off to expose the faucet's inner workings. Depending on your faucet type, you'll then need to remove the valve stem or cartridge. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut and pull out the stem. For cartridge faucets, there might be a retaining clip or nut to remove before pulling out the cartridge.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the assembly before you start removing parts; this will be helpful for reassembly.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Carefully examine the removed valve stem or cartridge. For compression faucets, the rubber washer at the end of the stem is usually the culprit. Pry it off and replace it with an identical new one. Also, check the O-rings along the stem and replace any that appear worn or brittle. For cartridge faucets, you'll typically replace the entire cartridge with a new, matching one. Take the old part to a hardware store to ensure you get the correct replacement.

> 💡 **Tip:** Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body and threads using a wire brush or old toothbrush.

### Step 5: Lubricate and Reassemble the Faucet

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new washers, O-rings, or the new cartridge before reinserting them. This helps create a better seal and makes future repairs easier. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly aligned. Hand-tighten the packing nut or retaining clip, then use your wrench for a snug fit, being careful not to overtighten.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure all components are seated correctly to prevent further leaks or damage.

### Step 6: Reattach Handle and Test for Leaks

Replace the escutcheon, handle, and any decorative caps in reverse order of removal. Once everything is securely reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check the faucet for any drips immediately. If the leak persists, you may need to tighten components slightly or re-examine the replacement parts. If you find the faucet needs a full replacement, consider consulting our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't overtighten screws or nuts when reassembling, as this can strip threads or crack components.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use genuine replacement parts or high-quality equivalents for the best results and longevity.
- If you're unsure about your faucet type, take a photo of it or the disassembled parts to your local hardware store for assistance.
- Regularly clean your faucet components to prevent mineral buildup, which can lead to leaks.
- Keep a small kit of common plumbing repair parts (washers, O-rings) on hand for quick fixes.
- When working with older plumbing, be gentle to avoid damaging brittle pipes or fixtures.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when dealing with hot water lines; they can cause burns.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or damage the faucet body.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or are unsure about any step, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet that's constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is typically caused by worn-out internal components like rubber washers or a faulty cartridge. The most common fix involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet handle and trim, removing the valve stem or cartridge, and replacing the degraded part with a new one. Reassemble the faucet, and the drip should stop.

**What are the most common reasons a bathtub faucet leaks?**

The primary reasons a bathtub faucet leaks include worn-out rubber washers (in compression faucets), degraded O-rings that no longer create a tight seal, or a faulty internal cartridge (in single-handle faucets). Mineral buildup can also prevent components from seating properly, leading to drips.

**How do I identify what type of faucet I have before starting repairs?**

Most bathtub faucets are either compression (two handles, one for hot, one for cold) or cartridge/disc (single handle). Knowing your faucet type helps you purchase the correct replacement parts. If you're unsure, take a photo of your faucet or the disassembled internal parts to a hardware store for identification assistance.

**What tools do I need to fix a bathtub faucet?**

To fix most bathtub faucets, you'll need basic tools such as a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, and potentially a utility knife. You will also need the specific replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a cartridge) for your faucet model, and plumber's grease.

**When should I call a professional plumber instead of trying to fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

While fixing a leaky faucet is often a DIY task, you should call a plumber if you encounter significant resistance, cannot identify the source of the leak, or if the leak persists after replacing the common components. Also, if your plumbing is very old or you're uncomfortable with the process, a professional can ensure a proper and safe repair.

**How to fix bathtub faucet leaks if replacing a washer doesn't work?**

If replacing the washer doesn't stop the leak, especially in a compression faucet, the issue might be with the valve seat, which could be corroded or pitted. You might need a valve seat dresser tool to resurface it, or the entire valve stem might need replacement. For single-handle faucets, if a new cartridge doesn't work, the problem could be within the faucet body itself, suggesting a more complex repair or full faucet replacement.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a simple repair like replacing a washer or O-ring, the process usually takes about 30 minutes to an hour. If you need to identify parts, clean extensive mineral buildup, or encounter unexpected issues, it might take a bit longer. Having the correct replacement parts on hand significantly speeds up the process.
