Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge inside the handle assembly. This common plumbing issue can be resolved with basic tools and a bit of patience, preventing water waste and potential damage to your tub.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Plumber's grease
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to faucet type)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Rag or towel
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off for your bathroom, if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped to prevent flooding once you begin disassembling the faucet. After shutting off the water, open the faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test the faucet after turning off the water to ensure no water is flowing before proceeding with the repair.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Use a screwdriver to remove any decorative caps on the faucet handles, then unscrew the visible screws holding the handles in place. Gently pull the handles off the stem. You may also need to remove a trim plate or escutcheon to fully expose the faucet body and gain access to the internal components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all removed screws and parts in a small container to avoid losing them during the repair process.

### Step 3: Identify and Remove the Cartridge or Stem

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either see a valve stem or a cartridge. For stems, use an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew the packing nut, then pull out the stem. For cartridges, you might need to remove a retaining clip or nut before pulling it straight out. Take note of its orientation and position before removal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Taking a photo of the assembly before disassembling can be very helpful for correct reassembly later.

### Step 4: Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. Carefully remove the old, damaged components using a utility knife or a small screwdriver. If it's a cartridge, you'll likely need to replace the entire unit. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or washers before installing them.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take the old components to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements, as parts can vary significantly by faucet model.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Insert the new washers, O-rings, or cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring proper alignment with any grooves or clips. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip firmly. Reattach the handles, decorative caps, and any trim plates you removed. Make sure all connections are snug but do not overtighten, as this can damage components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Double-check that all parts are correctly seated before tightening to prevent future leaks or operational issues.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet handles and spout. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has been successfully stopped. Observe for any drips over a few minutes to confirm the repair is complete.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine your work or consider that other components might be failing. For persistent issues, or if the faucet is very old, you might consider [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always keep a bucket or towel handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If you're unsure about the type of faucet or replacement parts needed, consult your faucet's manual or take photos to show a hardware store expert.
- Regularly clean your faucet components to prevent mineral buildup, which can lead to premature wear of washers and O-rings.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads, crack porcelain, or damage plastic components, leading to more significant repair costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the handle, and replacing worn components like rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. These parts are usually the cause of drips and can be replaced with basic tools.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components due to regular use and mineral deposits in the water. Rubber washers, O-rings, or the ceramic cartridge can degrade over time, losing their ability to create a watertight seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that many homeowners can tackle. With the right tools and replacement parts, you can usually stop the drip without needing to call a professional plumber.

**What tools do I need to fix bathtub faucet?**

To fix a bathtub faucet, you'll generally need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, plumber's grease, and the appropriate replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model.

**How do I identify the correct replacement parts for my leaky faucet?**

The best way to identify correct replacement parts is to first determine your faucet's brand and model. Once you've disassembled the faucet and removed the faulty part (like a cartridge or washer), take it to a hardware store to match it, or consult the manufacturer's website.

**When should I consider replacing the entire faucet instead of just fixing it?**

If your faucet is very old, extensively corroded, or if you've attempted repairs multiple times without success, it might be more cost-effective and efficient to replace the entire unit. Persistent leaks or severe damage often indicate it's time for a full replacement.
