To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply to prevent flooding. Then, carefully disassemble the faucet handle and valve assembly to access the internal components. Replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which are usually the culprits behind leaks, and reassemble the faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or pick tool
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or faucet repair kit
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags or towels
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stuck screws)
- Bucket or small container

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub, if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped. Open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and ensure the water is off.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handle and Trim

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that can be pried off to reveal a screw. Use a flathead screwdriver or utility knife to gently remove this cap, then unscrew the handle screw with the appropriate screwdriver. Once the screw is removed, pull the handle straight off. You may also need to remove an escutcheon or trim plate, which is often held by screws or simply pulls off.

### Step 3: Access and Remove the Valve Stem

Beneath the handle and trim, you'll find the valve stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type, you might need an adjustable wrench to loosen a packing nut or a cartridge puller tool. Carefully unscrew or pull out the valve stem or cartridge. Place all removed parts in order on a clean rag to help remember the reassembly sequence.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed valve stem or cartridge for any visible wear, cracks, or deterioration, especially on the rubber washers and O-rings. These are the most common causes of leaks. Use a utility knife or pick tool to carefully remove the old, worn parts. Replace them with new ones from your faucet repair kit, ensuring they match the original size and type. For cartridge faucets, you'll typically replace the entire cartridge.

### Step 5: Lubricate and Reassemble the Valve Stem

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new washers, O-rings, or the new cartridge. This lubrication helps create a good seal and makes future repairs easier. Carefully insert the lubricated valve stem or cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Hand-tighten any nuts or screws, then use your wrench or pliers for a final snug fit, being careful not to overtighten.

### Step 6: Reinstall Handle and Trim

Once the valve stem is secure, reattach the trim plate or escutcheon, followed by the faucet handle. Screw the handle back into place and replace the decorative cap. Double-check that all components are firmly attached and properly aligned, but avoid forcing anything.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Carefully check the faucet for any signs of dripping or leaks around the handle and spout. If you still notice a leak, gently tighten the packing nut or re-check the installation of the washers. If the leak persists, you might need to inspect the parts again or consider replacing the entire faucet if it's old or severely damaged. For guidance on replacement, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Comprehensive DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during disassembly to help remember the order of parts for reassembly.
- Place a rag or stopper in the drain to prevent small parts from falling down.
- If your faucet is old, consider bringing the old washers/O-rings to a hardware store to ensure you buy the correct replacements.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts; this can strip threads or crack components.
- Use plumber's grease on all rubber components to extend their life and ensure a good seal.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when using tools; worn parts can be brittle and sharp.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or are unsure about a step, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the handle and valve, replacing worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, and then reassembling the faucet. These small, inexpensive parts are usually the cause of most drips.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components, such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these parts degrade from constant use and mineral deposits, losing their ability to create a watertight seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that many homeowners can successfully complete. With the right tools and a clear guide, you can identify and replace the faulty parts, saving money on professional plumbing services.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, a utility knife or pick tool, and a replacement faucet repair kit or individual washers/O-rings. Plumber's grease and rags are also very helpful.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that drips when off?**

A bathtub faucet that drips when off usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. The most common solution is to replace the worn rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire valve cartridge that is responsible for stopping the water flow.

**How do I know what replacement parts to buy for my faucet?**

The best way to identify replacement parts is to first determine the brand and model of your faucet. If that's not possible, carefully remove the old parts (washers, O-rings, or cartridge) and take them to a hardware store to find exact matches or a universal repair kit.

**Is it better to repair or replace a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For minor leaks caused by worn washers or O-rings, repairing is often the most cost-effective and easiest solution. However, if the faucet is old, severely corroded, or has multiple persistent issues, replacing the entire faucet might be a better long-term investment. Consider your comfort level and the faucet's age.
