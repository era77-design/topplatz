A persistent drip from your bathtub faucet can be annoying and wasteful. Fortunately, most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed with basic tools and a few common replacement parts. This guide will walk you through the steps to identify the problem and restore your faucet to proper working order.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Needle-nose pliers
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Rags or old towels
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease or silicone sealant
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Look for shut-off valves typically located on the wall behind the faucet or underneath the tub if accessible. If you can't find individual valves, you may need to turn off the main water supply to your house. Open the faucet to drain any remaining water after turning off the supply.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap. Carefully pry off the cap with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw and gently pull the handle straight off. If the handle is stuck, a little wiggling or a handle puller might be necessary.

### Step 3: Remove the Escutcheon and Cartridge/Stem Nut

Once the handle is off, you'll see a decorative trim plate, also known as an escutcheon. Unscrew any visible screws holding it in place and remove it. Beneath this, you'll find the cartridge or stem, often held in place by a large retaining nut. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove this nut, turning counter-clockwise.

### Step 4: Extract and Inspect the Cartridge or Stem

With the retaining nut removed, you can now pull out the cartridge or stem. This might require some force or the use of needle-nose pliers. Once removed, inspect it thoroughly for signs of wear, such as cracked plastic, worn rubber washers, or deteriorated O-rings. These are the most common culprits for leaks.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

If your faucet uses washers and O-rings, carefully remove the old ones and replace them with new, identical parts. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new rubber components to ensure a good seal and smooth operation. If your faucet uses a cartridge, it's best to replace the entire unit. Take the old cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match for your faucet model.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the new or repaired cartridge/stem back into the faucet body, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Tighten the retaining nut securely with your adjustable wrench, but avoid overtightening. Reattach the escutcheon and then the faucet handle, making sure the handle screw is snug. Double-check all connections are firm.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, either at the individual valves or the main house valve. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or handle. Operate the faucet through its full range (hot, cold, off) several times to ensure proper function and to flush out any air. If a leak persists, you may need to re-evaluate the replacement parts or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos at each step of disassembly to help with reassembly.
- Place a rag or towel over the drain to prevent small parts from falling in.
- If your faucet is old or severely corroded, consider replacing the entire unit for a lasting solution.
- Always use plumber's grease on O-rings and washers to ensure a good seal and ease of future repairs.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before starting any work on your faucet to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old components, as they can be brittle or stuck.
- ⚠️ Hot water can cause burns; ensure the water is off before handling faucet components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first, shut off the water supply. Then, disassemble the handle and escutcheon to access the internal cartridge or stem. Inspect and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**What commonly causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers or O-rings inside the faucet's compression valves or a faulty cartridge in a single-handle faucet. Over time, these components can degrade, losing their ability to create a watertight seal.

**How do I know what replacement parts to buy to fix my bathtub faucet?**

The best way to identify correct replacement parts is to first determine your faucet's brand and model. Once you've disassembled the faucet, take the old worn parts or the entire cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you purchase an exact match. Taking photos and measurements can also help.

**Is fixing a leaky bathtub faucet a difficult DIY project?**

For most common leaks caused by worn washers or cartridges, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a relatively straightforward DIY project. With basic tools and patience, many homeowners can successfully complete the repair. If the faucet body itself is damaged, it might be more involved.

**What should I do if my bathtub faucet still leaks after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet still leaks after replacing the parts, double-check that all components are correctly installed and that the replacement parts are the exact match for your faucet model. Ensure the retaining nut is snug. If the problem persists, the faucet body might be corroded or damaged, and a full [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) might be necessary.

**What tools are essential for fixing a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and possibly needle-nose pliers. Rags, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge, and plumber's grease are also essential. A utility knife can help remove decorative caps.
