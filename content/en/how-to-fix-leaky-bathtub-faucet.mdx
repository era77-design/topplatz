Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet often involves replacing a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge. This common DIY repair can stop annoying drips, save water, and prevent potential damage to your bathroom fixtures. With a few basic tools, you can usually resolve the issue quickly and efficiently.

## What You Need

- Adjustable wrench
- Flathead screwdriver
- Phillips head screwdriver
- Utility knife (optional)
- Replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge (match existing)
- Plumber's grease (optional)
- Towel or rag
- Small basin or cup

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, ideally, the shut-off valves for your bathroom. Turn them clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped. This is a critical first step to prevent flooding during the repair.

### Step 2: Drain Faucet and Cover Drain

Turn on the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes. Once the water stops, turn the faucet off. Place a cloth or stopper over the bathtub drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling down it during the repair.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucet handles are held in place by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap. Pry off the cap with a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife, then use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to remove the screw. Pull the handle straight off the stem.

### Step 4: Access and Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see a decorative escutcheon plate, which you may need to unscrew. Beneath this, you'll find the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type, you might need an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut holding the stem in place, or simply pull out the cartridge after removing a retaining clip or nut. Carefully note the orientation of the parts as you remove them.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed stem or cartridge for worn rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any brittle, cracked, or compressed rubber parts with new ones of the exact same size. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often easiest to replace the entire cartridge with a new, matching unit. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for smooth operation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the stem or new cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated and oriented. Tighten the packing nut or replace any retaining clips/nuts. Screw the escutcheon plate back on, then reattach the faucet handle, securing it with its screw and decorative cap. Avoid overtightening components, which can cause damage.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water is fully restored, check the faucet for any signs of leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it's functioning correctly and that the drip has stopped. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine your work or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos at each stage of disassembly to help with reassembly.
- Bring the old washers, O-rings, or cartridge to the hardware store to ensure you get exact matches.
- Keep a small container or cup handy for screws and small parts so they don't get lost.
- If your faucet is very old or corroded, a full replacement might be more cost-effective in the long run.
- Use plumber's tape on threaded connections if necessary, though new washers/O-rings usually suffice for stopping drips.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or removing screws, as older plastic or metal parts can be brittle.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts during reassembly, as this can strip threads or crack components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or can't identify the cause of the leak, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet handle and trim, and then replacing worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. These small parts are often the cause of persistent drips.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common cause of a leaky bathtub faucet is worn or damaged internal components, such as rubber washers, O-rings, or the faucet cartridge. Over time, these parts can degrade, harden, or become compressed, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to drip through. Mineral buildup can also contribute to leaks.

**How do I fix a bathtub faucet that drips when off?**

To fix a bathtub faucet that drips when off, you'll likely need to replace the internal sealing components. This usually means replacing the stem washers and O-rings in compression faucets or replacing the entire cartridge in single-handle or disc faucets. Ensure the water is completely off before starting the repair.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need basic tools like a Phillips head screwdriver, a flathead screwdriver, and an adjustable wrench. A utility knife might be useful for prying off decorative caps. You'll also need the appropriate replacement parts, such as new washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model.

**When should I replace my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if the leak persists after attempting to fix it, if the faucet body itself is corroded or cracked, or if replacement parts are difficult or impossible to find. Sometimes, an older, severely damaged faucet is simply beyond economical repair, and a full replacement offers better long-term value and updated aesthetics. If you decide to replace, you can find guides like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many minor leaks in bathtub faucets can be fixed by a confident DIYer with basic tools and some patience. Replacing washers or cartridges is a common repair. However, if you're uncomfortable with the process, encounter seized parts, or the leak persists after your attempts, it's best to call a professional plumber.
