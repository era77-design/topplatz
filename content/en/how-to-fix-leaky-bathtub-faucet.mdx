A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water supply, carefully disassembling the handle, and replacing the faulty part. This guide will walk you through the process step-by-step to restore your faucet to proper working order.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Allen wrench set (if applicable)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement faucet washer/O-ring kit or new cartridge
- Plumber's grease
- Rags or towels
- Penetrating oil (optional)
- Flashlight

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply and Prepare

Before starting any plumbing work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, ideally, the shut-off valves specifically for your bathroom. Turn the water off completely. Once the water is off, turn on the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes. Place a rag or towel over the tub drain to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handle and Trim

The method for removing the handle varies by faucet type. Look for a decorative cap that might hide a screw (often Phillips or Allen head). Pry off the cap gently with a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver, then unscrew and remove the handle. Once the handle is off, you may need to remove a decorative escutcheon plate or trim by unscrewing it or prying it off.

### Step 3: Access and Remove the Cartridge or Stem

Beneath the handle, you'll find the faucet's internal mechanism, which could be a cartridge, compression stem, or ball valve. For most modern single-handle faucets, this will be a cartridge held in place by a nut or clip. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the retaining nut, then pull out the cartridge or stem. For older compression faucets, you'll remove the packing nut and then the stem.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Carefully examine the removed cartridge or stem for signs of wear, such as cracked O-rings, hardened rubber washers, or mineral buildup. If you have a compression faucet, replace the rubber washer at the base of the stem and the packing washer around the stem. For cartridge faucets, you'll usually replace the entire cartridge. Take the old part to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match.

### Step 5: Lubricate and Reinstall New Parts

Before installing the new washer, O-rings, or cartridge, apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to all rubber components. This helps create a better seal and prolongs their life. Carefully insert the new cartridge or stem back into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Hand-tighten any retaining nuts, then use your wrench for a final snug tightening – avoid over-tightening.

### Step 6: Reassemble and Test the Faucet

Replace the escutcheon plate, then reattach the faucet handle, tightening its screw securely. Replace any decorative caps. Once everything is back in place, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any leaks around the handle or spout. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and the leak is completely stopped.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This will be invaluable when you're putting everything back together.
- If you encounter stubborn nuts or screws, a little penetrating oil can help loosen them. Allow it a few minutes to work before trying again.
- Always have a small container or cup handy to place removed screws and small parts, preventing them from rolling away or falling down the drain.
- If parts are severely corroded or you can't find a replacement, it might be time to consider a full faucet replacement. Learn more with our guide: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely shut off before attempting any repairs to avoid flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not over-tighten screws or nuts, especially plastic ones, as this can strip threads or crack components.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when prying off decorative caps or trim to avoid scratching your faucet's finish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the handle and trim, remove the internal cartridge or stem, and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty internal cartridge. These components naturally wear down over time due to constant use and exposure to water, leading to a compromised seal and dripping.

**How do I fix bathtub faucet handle leaks?**

Leaks specifically from the bathtub faucet handle usually indicate a problem with the internal seals, washers, or the cartridge directly beneath the handle. Disassembling the handle and replacing these worn components should resolve the issue, following the steps outlined in this guide.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish with basic tools and a little patience. The key is correctly identifying the type of faucet and the specific part that needs replacement.

**How do I stop my bathtub faucet from dripping?**

To stop a bathtub faucet from dripping, you must address the underlying cause, which is usually a worn-out internal seal. Replacing the rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge within the faucet's stem or body will restore the watertight seal and stop the drip.

**When should I replace my bathtub faucet instead of repairing it?**

If the faucet body itself is corroded, parts are obsolete and hard to find, or you desire a significant style upgrade, replacing the entire unit might be more cost-effective and beneficial in the long run.
