Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or the cartridge. This DIY task can usually be completed in under an hour with basic tools, saving water and preventing further damage.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or socket wrench set
- Allen wrench set (if applicable)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (match existing)
- Plumber's grease
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Rag or towel
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting any work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub if available. Turn the water off completely to prevent flooding. Once off, turn on the faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test the faucet after shutting off the water to ensure no water is flowing before proceeding.

### Step 2: Prepare the Faucet Area

Place a rag or towel over the bathtub drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling into it. If your faucet has decorative caps over the handle screws, carefully pry them off using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. This exposes the screws holding the handle in place.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle(s)

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either find a single screw on the front or side of the handle, or a set screw underneath. Use the appropriate screwdriver or Allen wrench to loosen and remove the screw. Once the screw is out, gently pull the handle straight off. If it's stuck, a little wiggling or penetrating oil might help.

### Step 4: Access the Valve Mechanism

After removing the handle, you'll likely see a trim plate or escutcheon. Unscrew any retaining screws holding this plate in place and remove it. This will expose the valve stem, cartridge, or ball assembly, which is the heart of your faucet's operation and usually where the leak originates.

### Step 5: Remove and Inspect Internal Components

Carefully remove the valve stem or cartridge. This might involve unscrewing a retaining nut or removing a clip. Once removed, inspect the washers, O-rings, and the cartridge itself for any signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These worn components are the most common cause of a leaky faucet.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the components before disassembly to aid in correct reassembly. If you can't identify the cause of the leak or the parts are seized, consider that a full replacement might be necessary. You can find guidance on this by checking out [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

### Step 6: Replace Worn Parts

Take the old washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you get exact replacements. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings before installing them. Carefully insert the new components back into the faucet body in the reverse order of removal, ensuring everything is seated correctly.

### Step 7: Reassemble and Test for Leaks

Reattach the retaining nut or clip, then the trim plate, and finally the handle, securing it with its screw. Turn the water supply back on slowly. Open the faucet and check for any drips or leaks. If the leak persists, you may need to re-check your work or consider replacing the entire faucet mechanism.


## Tips & Tricks

- Keep a small container or bag handy for screws and small parts to prevent them from getting lost.
- If any part is corroded or stuck, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying to loosen it.
- Always use the correct tools to avoid stripping screws or damaging faucet components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter lead pipes or feel unsure at any point, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet quickly?**

To quickly fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, remove the faucet handle and access the internal valve mechanism. Replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that are typically responsible for the leak, then reassemble and test.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

A bathtub faucet typically leaks due to worn-out internal components. Over time, rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire faucet cartridge can degrade, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to drip or stream out.

**How to stop a bathtub faucet from dripping?**

To stop a bathtub faucet from dripping, you usually need to replace the faulty internal parts. This involves shutting off the water, disassembling the faucet handle and trim, and then replacing the worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge with new ones.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's a single handle?**

To fix a single-handle bathtub faucet, the process is similar: turn off the water, remove the handle, and then access the cartridge. Single-handle faucets almost always use a cartridge, which you'll need to remove, inspect for damage, and replace if faulty to stop the leak.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or socket wrench, potentially an Allen wrench set, and replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge. Plumber's grease and a rag are also useful.

**Is fixing a leaky bathtub faucet a difficult DIY task?**

For most common leaks caused by worn washers or O-rings, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is considered an easy to moderate DIY task. With basic tools and a little patience, many homeowners can resolve the issue themselves, though replacing a full cartridge can be slightly more involved.

**When should I call a professional for a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you can't identify the source of the leak, if parts are seized or broken during your attempt, or if you suspect larger plumbing issues. Persistent leaks after your repair attempts also warrant professional assistance.
