Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. This common DIY repair can save water and prevent further damage, often requiring only basic tools and a little patience.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or channel locks
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Rag or towel
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, usually in the basement, utility closet, or near your water meter. Turn it completely off. If your bathtub has dedicated shut-off valves, use those first. This prevents water from gushing out during the repair.

### Step 2: Drain the Faucet and Protect the Drain

Open the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This relieves pressure and ensures a dry workspace. Place a rag or towel over the drain opening to prevent small screws or parts from falling down and getting lost.

### Step 3: Remove Faucet Handle and Trim

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap that pops off to reveal a screw underneath. Use a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife to pry off the cap, then unscrew the handle. Carefully pull off the handle and any decorative escutcheon plate behind it, exposing the valve assembly.

### Step 4: Remove the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either see a valve stem held in place by a packing nut or a cartridge that slides out. Use your adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut. For cartridges, you might need to remove a retaining clip or nut before pulling it straight out.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any damaged washers or O-rings with new ones of the exact same size. If you have a cartridge faucet, replace the entire cartridge with a new, matching one. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new rubber components.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reverse the disassembly process. Insert the new washers, O-rings, or cartridge. Tighten the packing nut or retaining clip, ensuring it's snug but not overtightened, which can damage new parts. Reattach the escutcheon plate, handle, and decorative cap.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base. Open the faucet to test its operation and ensure the leak has stopped. If a drip persists, you may need to re-tighten components slightly or re-inspect for another worn part.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly. This creates a visual guide for reassembly, especially useful for first-timers.
- Keep all removed screws, nuts, and parts organized in a small container or on a clean cloth to prevent losing them.
- When buying replacement parts, it's best to bring the old washer, O-ring, or cartridge to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match.
- If your faucet is old, severely corroded, or replacement parts are difficult to find, it might be more cost-effective in the long run to replace the entire faucet. You can find a guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to avoid flooding.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when working with older plumbing fixtures, as components can be brittle or corroded. Avoid forcing parts to prevent damage.
- ⚠️ If you encounter extreme difficulty or cannot stop the leak, do not hesitate to call a licensed plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these parts degrade and no longer create a watertight seal, leading to drips.

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the valve stem or cartridge, replace the worn internal parts, and then carefully reassemble everything. It's a common DIY plumbing task.

**What tools do I need to fix a bathtub faucet?**

Typically, you'll need a Phillips and flathead screwdriver, an adjustable wrench or channel locks, and the appropriate replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge) specific to your faucet model. Plumber's grease is also recommended.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most DIYers, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and how easily the faucet disassembles. Gathering the correct parts beforehand can speed up the process.

**When should I call a professional to fix my bathtub faucet?**

If you've attempted the repair and the leak persists, if you encounter seized parts that won't budge, or if you're simply uncomfortable with the plumbing process, it's always best to call a licensed plumber to ensure the job is done correctly.

**What if my bathtub faucet still leaks after fixing it?**

If the leak continues, double-check that all components are correctly seated and tightened. The issue might be a different worn part you overlooked, or you may need to replace the entire faucet if the valve body itself is damaged or corroded beyond repair.

**Is it difficult to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For many, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a straightforward DIY task that requires basic tools and a step-by-step approach. With patience and the right parts, most homeowners can successfully tackle this common plumbing issue themselves.
