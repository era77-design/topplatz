A leaky bathtub faucet can be annoying and wasteful, but often it's a simple fix you can do yourself. Most leaks are caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges inside the faucet assembly. By following a few straightforward steps, you can quickly stop the drip and restore your faucet's function.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or ceramic cartridges (matching existing ones)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or old towel
- White vinegar and old toothbrush (for cleaning mineral deposits)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before beginning any repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific valve for your bathroom. Turn it completely off to prevent water flow during the repair. It's also a good idea to open the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes, ensuring no water pressure is left.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles

Most bathtub faucet handles have decorative caps that hide a screw. Pry these caps off gently with a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to loosen and remove the screws, then pull the handles straight off. If they are stuck, gently wiggle them or apply a penetrating oil to help loosen them.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handles are removed, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and unscrew the packing nut. For single-handle cartridge faucets, remove the retaining clip or nut. Gently twist or pull the stem or cartridge straight out of the faucet body. Be careful, as old parts can be brittle or corroded.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracked ceramic discs. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Remove the old, damaged parts and replace them with new ones of the exact same size and type. It's often helpful to take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you get a perfect match.

### Step 5: Clean and Lubricate Components

While the faucet is disassembled, take the opportunity to clean away any mineral deposits or grime from the stem, cartridge, and inside the faucet body. Use white vinegar and an old toothbrush for this. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new washers, O-rings, and the threads of the packing nut to ensure smooth operation and a good, lasting seal.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the newly repaired or replaced stem or cartridge back into the faucet body. Hand-tighten the packing nut or retaining nut, then use your adjustable wrench to tighten it snugly, but do not overtighten. Replace the faucet handles, reattach the screws, and snap the decorative caps back into place.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks and Adjust

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the bathtub faucet to a moderate flow and check for any drips or leaks around the handles or spout. If you notice a minor drip, you might need to slightly tighten the packing nut or retaining nut. If the leak persists after tightening, you may need to re-evaluate the parts or consider replacing the entire faucet.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during each stage of disassembly to help you remember how to reassemble the faucet correctly.
- Always have a small container or rag ready to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- When buying replacement parts, consider purchasing a small assortment of common washer and O-ring sizes, as these are inexpensive and useful to have on hand for future repairs.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely turned off before attempting any faucet repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Avoid overtightening nuts and screws, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or damage the faucet body.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when prying off decorative caps or removing old components, as they can be brittle and easily broken.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handles and stem to access and replace worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Reassemble the faucet, turn the water back on, and test for leaks to ensure the repair is successful.

**What are the most common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The most common causes are worn-out rubber washers or O-rings inside compression faucet stems, or a damaged ceramic disc cartridge in newer single-handle faucets. Mineral deposits can also build up on internal components, preventing them from forming a tight seal and leading to drips.

**How do I fix a bathtub faucet that drips constantly?**

A constantly dripping faucet typically indicates a faulty washer, O-ring, or cartridge that isn't sealing properly. Follow the steps of turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, inspecting and replacing the worn internal components, and then carefully reassembling everything to stop the drip.

**Do I need special tools to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most repairs, you'll need basic tools like a screwdriver set, an adjustable wrench, and possibly pliers. For some cartridge-style faucets, a specialized cartridge puller might be helpful, but often isn't strictly necessary if you can carefully remove the cartridge with pliers.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a DIY enthusiast, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet usually takes between 30 minutes to an hour, assuming you have the correct replacement parts on hand. If you need to purchase parts or encounter seized components, it might take longer.

**What should I do if replacing the washers or cartridge doesn't stop the leak?**

If replacing the internal components doesn't resolve the leak, the faucet body itself might be corroded or cracked, or you might have missed a worn part. In such cases, it may be more cost-effective and reliable to replace the entire faucet fixture. **When should I call a professional plumber for a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're uncomfortable performing the repair yourself, if you encounter unexpected complications like seized components that won't budge, or if the leak persists after multiple repair attempts. They have specialized tools and expertise for complex or persistent issues.
