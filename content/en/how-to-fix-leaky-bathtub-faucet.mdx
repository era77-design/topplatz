A leaky bathtub faucet can be an irritating nuisance and a significant waste of water. Fortunately, most common leaks are simple to fix with basic tools and a little patience. This guide will walk you through the steps to identify and repair the problem, saving you money and conserving water.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife (optional)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge
- Plumber's grease
- Thread seal tape (Teflon tape)
- Rags or towels
- Small bucket or basin

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before you begin any work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or, if available, the shut-off valves for the bathroom itself. Turn the water off completely to prevent flooding. Once the water is off, turn on the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes, relieving pressure.

### Step 2: Cover the Drain and Prepare the Area

Place a rag or stopper over the bathtub drain opening. This crucial step prevents small screws, washers, or other components from falling down the drain during disassembly. Lay towels around the faucet area to catch any residual water.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap. Carefully pry off the cap with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the retaining screw and pull the handle straight off. Some older handles might be stuck and require a gentle wiggle or a tap with a rubber mallet.

### Step 4: Disassemble the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll expose the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), you'll need to unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem or cartridge in place. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers for this step. Carefully pull out the entire stem or cartridge assembly.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a cracked cartridge. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any damaged parts with new ones of the exact same size and type. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for better sealing and smoother operation. If the entire cartridge is faulty, replace it with a new, matching cartridge.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new components or the repaired stem/cartridge back into the faucet body. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip, but do not overtighten, as this can damage the new parts. Reattach the faucet handle, ensuring the screw is snug, and replace any decorative caps.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check the faucet for any immediate leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water, to ensure everything is working correctly and there are no drips. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full faucet replacement. For guidance on a full replacement, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Before purchasing replacement parts, take the old components (washers, O-rings, or cartridge) to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match. Faucet designs vary widely.
- Keep all removed screws and small parts in a cup or on a clean cloth to prevent them from getting lost.
- If you encounter stubborn nuts or screws, a penetrating oil can help loosen them, but be sure to clean up any residue before reassembly.
- Use thread seal tape (Teflon tape) on the threads of any pipe connections, if applicable, to create a watertight seal, wrapping it clockwise 2-3 times.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely turned off before attempting any repairs to avoid flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to strip screws or overtighten nuts, as this can cause further damage to the faucet or pipes.
- ⚠️ If you are unsure about any step or encounter difficulties, it's always safer to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet body. Over time, these parts degrade from continuous use and water exposure, leading to drips and leaks.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll typically need a screwdriver (flathead and Phillips), an adjustable wrench, pliers, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge, plumber's grease, and thread seal tape. Rags and a small bucket are also useful for cleanup.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common bathtub faucet leaks are relatively simple DIY repairs that can be done with basic tools and a little patience. If you're comfortable following instructions and identifying the faulty part, you can likely fix it yourself. However, if the issue is complex or you're unsure, calling a professional plumber is always a safe option.

**How do I know what replacement parts I need to fix my bathtub faucet?**

The best way to identify the correct replacement parts is to carefully disassemble your faucet and remove the worn components (washers, O-rings, or cartridge). Take these parts to a hardware store to match them exactly. Knowing your faucet's brand and model can also help significantly.

**How long does it take to fix a bathtub faucet?**

For a typical DIY repair involving replacing washers or an O-ring, fixing a bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience and how easily the faucet disassembles. More complex issues or cartridge replacements might take a bit longer.

**What should I do if my bathtub faucet is still leaking after I try to fix it?**

If your bathtub faucet is still leaking after your repair, double-check that all components were installed correctly and are the exact match. The issue might be a different component, or the faucet body could be corroded, indicating a more significant problem. If the leak persists, consider consulting a professional plumber or replacing the entire faucet, for which you can find guidance in our [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Step-by-Step DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).
