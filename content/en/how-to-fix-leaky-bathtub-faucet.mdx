A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers or O-rings inside the faucet handle or a faulty cartridge. To fix it, you'll need to turn off the water supply, carefully disassemble the handle, replace the faulty components, and then reassemble the faucet. This simple DIY repair can save water, reduce your utility bills, and prevent further water damage.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge kit (specific to faucet brand/model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or towel
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the handle clockwise until the water flow stops completely. This crucial first step prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process.

> 💡 **Tip:** Test the faucet after shutting off the valve to ensure no water is flowing before proceeding.

### Step 2: Drain Faucet and Cover Drain

Open the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines. This releases pressure and clears out residual water. Place a rag or a stopper over the drain opening to prevent small screws, washers, or other parts from falling down and getting lost while you work.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucets have a decorative cap covering a screw. Carefully pry off this cap with a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw and pull the handle straight off the stem. Some older or different models might have a set screw on the side or underside of the handle that needs to be loosened with an Allen wrench.

### Step 4: Disassemble the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either see a stem held in place by a packing nut or a cartridge. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut. If it's a cartridge-style faucet, you might need a cartridge puller to remove it. Observe the order of all parts as you remove them for easier reassembly.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the old rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are typically the culprits behind leaks. Replace these with new, identical parts. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and threads to ensure a smooth, watertight seal and prolong their life.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reverse the disassembly process. Insert the new components, tighten the packing nut (but don't overtighten, as this can compress washers too much and cause new issues), reattach the handle, and secure it with its screw and decorative cap. Ensure all connections are snug but not excessively tight.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Open the faucet to allow any trapped air to escape, then close it. Carefully check around the faucet body, handles, and spout for any signs of dripping or leakage. If you still see a drip, you may need to tighten a connection slightly or re-inspect the replaced parts. If the leak persists, you might need to consider a full faucet replacement. ## Tips & Tricks

- Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent accidents.
- Take the old washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge to your local hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements for your specific faucet model.
- Keep a bucket and old towels handy to catch any residual water during the repair process.
- Clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet components with a wire brush before reassembly to ensure a better seal.
- If you're unsure about your faucet type or the correct replacement parts, take photos of your faucet and the disassembled components to a plumbing expert for advice.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the water supply completely before starting work to prevent flooding and potential injury.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads, crack components, or damage washers, potentially leading to new leaks or more costly repairs.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant rust, corrosion, or components that are seized and won't budge, avoid forcing them, as this could damage your plumbing. Consider calling a professional plumber in such cases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle and stem, replace worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, and then reassemble the unit. This process usually resolves common drips and leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these parts can deteriorate, crack, or lose their flexibility, failing to create a watertight seal and allowing water to drip or flow continuously.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish with basic tools and a little patience. The repair often involves replacing inexpensive internal parts, making it a cost-effective solution.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that keeps dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet almost always indicates an issue with the internal sealing components. You'll need to turn off the water, open the faucet, and replace the worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that is no longer creating a proper seal.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Essential tools include a screwdriver (Phillips or flathead), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife or small flathead for prying caps, and replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge kit specific to your faucet. Plumber's grease and a rag are also very helpful.

**How do I identify the type of faucet I have for replacement parts?**

To identify your faucet type, look for brand names on the faucet body. When you disassemble it, take photos of the internal components like the stem or cartridge. Bringing these photos or the old parts to a hardware store will help you find the correct replacements.

**When should I call a professional for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you can't locate the water shut-off, if the leak persists after you've replaced the internal parts, if components are rusted or seized and won't come apart, or if you're uncomfortable with the repair process. They can diagnose complex issues or replace the entire faucet if necessary.
