A leaky bathtub faucet is not only annoying but can also waste a significant amount of water over time. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which are relatively simple to replace with basic tools. This guide will walk you through the steps to diagnose and repair common bathtub faucet leaks.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for decorative caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rag or towel
- Flashlight
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or, if available, the shut-off valves specifically for your bathroom. Turn the water off completely to prevent flooding. Open the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handles and Trim

Carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles to expose the retaining screws. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove these screws, then pull the handles straight off. Next, remove any escutcheon plates or trim that cover the valve body by unscrewing them or prying them gently.

### Step 3: Access the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Once the handles and trim are off, you'll see the valve stems or cartridges. Depending on your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc), you'll need to use an adjustable wrench or pliers to unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip that holds the stem/cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed valve stem or cartridge for signs of wear. For compression faucets, look for a worn rubber washer at the end of the stem and O-rings around the stem body. For cartridge faucets, the entire cartridge may need replacement. Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact matches. Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings or washers before installation.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the new washer, O-rings, or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Tighten the packing nut or retaining clip by hand, then use your wrench or pliers for a final snug turn, being careful not to overtighten. Reattach the escutcheon plates, handles, and decorative caps in reverse order of removal.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, listening for any rushing water or immediate leaks. Check the faucet thoroughly for drips or leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure proper operation. If the leak persists, you may need to re-check your work or consider replacing the entire faucet.


## Tips & Tricks

- Before disassembling, take a photo of your faucet to help with reassembly and identifying parts.
- Identify your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc) before buying replacement parts. This information is often found in your faucet's manual or by searching your faucet's brand and model online.
- Keep a bucket or towel handy to catch any residual water when you remove the valve components.
- When tightening, make sure parts are snug but avoid overtightening, which can damage threads or components.
- If you find extensive corrosion or damage to the faucet body, or if replacement parts are hard to find, it might be time to consider a full replacement. See our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet – DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not force parts. If something feels stuck, try applying penetrating oil or check for hidden screws/clips.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using sharp tools like screwdrivers or utility knives to avoid injury.
- ⚠️ Ensure all connections are tight but do not overtighten, which can strip threads or crack components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks occur due to worn-out internal components. Common culprits include rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that controls water flow, which degrade over time with use and mineral deposits.

**How do I identify the type of bathtub faucet I have to fix it?**

You can usually identify your faucet type by its operation and appearance. Compression faucets have separate hot and cold handles that you turn repeatedly. Cartridge faucets have a single handle that moves up/down and side-to-side. Ball and ceramic disc faucets also typically have single handles. Knowing the type helps you get the correct replacement parts.

**What are the common steps to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, remove the faucet handles and trim, access the internal valve stem or cartridge, replace any worn washers, O-rings, or the cartridge itself, and then reassemble the faucet. Finally, turn the water back on and test for leaks.

**How do I fix a bathtub faucet if it's a compression type?**

To fix a bathtub faucet of the compression type, you'll need to replace the rubber washer at the end of the valve stem and possibly the O-rings around the stem. After turning off the water, remove the handle and packing nut to pull out the stem, then simply replace the worn components with new ones.

**What tools will I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most leaky bathtub faucet repairs, you'll need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, a utility knife, and replacement parts specific to your faucet. Plumber's grease and a rag are also very useful.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of trying to fix the leak?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if the leak persists after attempting repairs, if replacement parts are unavailable or difficult to find, or if the faucet body itself is corroded or extensively damaged. Sometimes, an old or severely damaged faucet is simply beyond economical repair. For guidance, refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

**Can a leaky bathtub faucet cause significant water waste?**

Yes, even a small drip can lead to significant water waste over time. A faucet dripping just one drop per second can waste thousands of gallons of water per year, potentially increasing your water bill and impacting the environment.
