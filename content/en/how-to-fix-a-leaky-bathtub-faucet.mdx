A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance, wasting water and causing annoying drips. Most leaks stem from worn-out internal components like washers or cartridges, which can often be fixed with basic tools and a little DIY effort. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and repairing your leaky faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags or towels
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn them clockwise until the water is completely off. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and ensure the water is off.

### Step 2: Identify Faucet Type and Disassemble Handle

Bathtub faucets come in several types (compression, cartridge, ball, ceramic disc). Carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handle to expose the screw. Use your screwdriver to remove the screw and pull off the handle. You may need to remove a decorative escutcheon or trim plate next.

### Step 3: Access the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll typically see a packing nut or a retaining clip securing the faucet stem or cartridge. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut. For cartridge faucets, you might need to pull the cartridge straight out after removing a retaining clip.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

For compression faucets, examine the rubber washer at the end of the stem; it's usually the culprit. Replace it with a new one of the exact same size. Also, check the O-rings on the stem. For cartridge faucets, the entire cartridge likely needs replacement. Take the old part to a hardware store to ensure you get an identical replacement.

### Step 5: Clean and Lubricate

Before reassembling, use a wire brush or old toothbrush to clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the moving parts of a new cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a better seal.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the new washer/O-rings onto the stem or slide the new cartridge back into place. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Reattach the handle, screw, and any decorative caps or trim. Ensure everything is snug but do not overtighten.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet body. Turn the faucet on and off several times, checking for drips from the spout. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine your work or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Place a cloth over the drain opening to prevent small parts from falling down during disassembly.
- Take photos at each stage of disassembly to help with reassembly.
- If you're unsure about your faucet type or replacement parts, take the removed components to a hardware store for expert advice.
- Keep a bucket or rags handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If the leak persists after replacing washers or cartridges, the faucet body itself might be corroded or damaged, potentially requiring a new faucet. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet) offers a step-by-step guide for replacement.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when handling old plumbing parts, as they can be sharp or corroded.
- ⚠️ Avoid overtightening nuts and screws, as this can strip threads or crack components.
- ⚠️ Hot water can scald; ensure the water is off and cooled before working on hot water lines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first shut off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle and access the internal components, typically the stem or cartridge. Replace worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble and test.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common cause of a leaky bathtub faucet is worn-out internal components. Over time, rubber washers, O-rings, or cartridges can degrade, stiffen, or crack due to constant use and mineral deposits, preventing a proper seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, in many cases, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a straightforward DIY task that requires basic tools and replacement parts. By following careful instructions, you can often resolve the issue without needing to hire a professional plumber.

**How do I know what type of bathtub faucet I have?**

You can often identify your faucet type by its appearance and how it operates. Compression faucets have separate hot and cold handles, while cartridge, ball, and ceramic disc faucets typically have a single handle that controls both temperature and flow.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a typical DIY repair, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 15 to 60 minutes, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet. Locating the correct replacement parts might add extra time.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're unable to identify the source of the leak, if the repair involves complex plumbing, or if the leak persists after you've attempted to replace the common components. Extensive corrosion or damage to the faucet body might also necessitate professional help or a full replacement.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Basic tools for fixing a leaky bathtub faucet usually include a screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife, and clean rags. You'll also need the specific replacement parts for your faucet, such as washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge.
