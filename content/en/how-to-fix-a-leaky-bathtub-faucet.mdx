A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by a worn-out washer or O-ring inside the faucet handle or valve. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, replacing the faulty component, and reassembling the faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife (optional, for stubborn parts)
- Replacement O-rings/washers (specific to faucet brand/type)
- Plumber's grease
- Rag/towel
- White vinegar (for cleaning mineral deposits)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, if available, the isolation valves for your bathroom. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped. Test the faucet by turning it on to ensure no water comes out.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you can't find a bathroom-specific shut-off, turn off the main house supply. This is crucial for safety and to prevent flooding.

### Step 2: Drain Remaining Water & Protect the Drain

Open the faucet handles to drain any residual water from the pipes. Place a rag or towel over the bathtub drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling into it during the repair process. This simple step can save you a lot of frustration.

> 💡 **Tip:** A small bucket or container can also be useful to catch any drips as you work.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Depending on your faucet type, you'll need to remove decorative caps or screws to access the handle mechanism. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove any visible screws, then carefully pull off the handle(s). You may need to gently pry it if it's stuck.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This will be invaluable when it's time to put everything back together correctly.

### Step 4: Remove and Inspect the Valve Stem/Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see a retaining nut or clip holding the valve stem or cartridge in place. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully remove it. Pull out the valve stem or cartridge and inspect the rubber washers, O-rings, or ceramic discs for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are typically the culprits behind leaks.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you find mineral deposits, clean the components thoroughly with white vinegar and a small brush before reassembly or replacement.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Take the worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge to a hardware store to find exact matches. Carefully remove the old components using a utility knife or screwdriver, then install the new ones. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and moving parts to ensure smooth operation and a better seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** It's often wise to replace all accessible washers and O-rings at once, even if only one looks worn, as they tend to age at a similar rate.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the valve stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly aligned. Tighten the retaining nut or clip, but do not overtighten, as this can damage the new components. Reattach the faucet handle(s) and secure any screws or decorative caps you removed earlier.

> 💡 **Tip:** Refer to the photos you took during disassembly to ensure every part goes back in its correct position.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks around the faucet base. Once the water is fully restored, turn on the faucet to test its operation and ensure the leak has stopped. If you notice any drips, you may need to tighten a connection or re-examine the installed components.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak persists or the faucet is severely damaged, you might consider a full replacement. Learn [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Before starting, identify your faucet's brand and model if possible. This makes finding replacement parts much easier.
- Keep a small container or cup handy for organizing screws and small parts as you disassemble the faucet.
- Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to new leaks.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding and potential damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle, remove and inspect the internal valve or cartridge for worn components like washers or O-rings, replace them, and then reassemble the faucet.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. Mineral deposits can also build up, preventing a tight seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish with basic tools and a little patience. Our step-by-step guide provides all the necessary instructions.

**How do I fix my bathtub faucet if it's still leaking after replacing the washer?**

If your bathtub faucet still leaks after replacing the washer, it might indicate that another component, such as an O-ring or the entire cartridge, is faulty. Double-check all seals, ensure components are properly seated, and consider replacing the full cartridge unit.

**What tools do I need to fix a bathtub faucet?**

To fix a bathtub faucet, you'll generally need a screwdriver set, an adjustable wrench, pliers, and potentially a utility knife. Having replacement O-rings or washers specific to your faucet model is also essential.

**When should I call a professional for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're unable to locate the source of the leak, if the repair requires specialized tools or knowledge you don't possess, or if the leak persists after multiple repair attempts. They can diagnose complex issues or recommend a full faucet replacement.
