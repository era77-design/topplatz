A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out internal components like washers or cartridges. Fixing it yourself can save money and prevent water waste. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and repairing common bathtub faucet leaks.

## What You Need

- Screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rag or towel
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's critical to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the isolation valves for the bathroom or specific tub. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes, ensuring no unexpected sprays during disassembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a towel over the drain to catch any dropped screws or small parts during the repair process.

### Step 2: Identify Your Faucet Type and Gather Parts

Bathtub faucets typically use compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc mechanisms. Visually inspect your faucet for screws, handles, and overall design to help determine its type. You'll need specific replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge, which can often be found at hardware stores. Taking a photo of your faucet or the old part can help when buying replacements.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you're unsure about your faucet type, taking a picture of the disassembled components to the hardware store can help staff identify the correct replacement parts.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle and Trim

Carefully remove any decorative caps on the faucet handle to expose the retaining screw. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove this screw, then pull the handle straight off. You may also need to remove an escutcheon (decorative cover plate) or other trim pieces to access the faucet's internal components. Use a utility knife to gently score around the escutcheon if it's sealed with caulk.

> 💡 **Tip:** Keep all screws and small parts organized in a cup or on a magnetic tray to prevent loss.

### Step 4: Remove and Inspect the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle and trim are off, you'll see the faucet stem (for compression faucets) or cartridge (for most other types). Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut or retaining nut that holds the stem/cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge. Inspect it for worn rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks in the plastic, as these are common causes of leaks.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo before removing the stem or cartridge to remember its orientation for reassembly.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

For compression faucets, replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and the packing washer around the stem. For cartridge faucets, replace the entire cartridge with a new one that matches your faucet model. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease or silicone lubricant to new O-rings or the new cartridge before installation. This helps with sealing and smooth operation.

> 💡 **Tip:** Clean any mineral deposits from the faucet body using a wire brush before installing new parts for a better seal.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated and oriented. Tighten the packing nut or retaining nut with your adjustable wrench, but avoid over-tightening, which can damage parts. Reattach the escutcheon, handle, and any decorative caps in reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all connections are snug.

> 💡 **Tip:** Double-check that all screws are tightened securely, but don't strip them.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or spout. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has stopped. If the leak persists or if the faucet is beyond repair, you might consider a full replacement. For guidance on this, refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

> 💡 **Tip:** If you notice a slight drip after reassembly, try tightening the packing nut or cartridge nut a quarter turn at a time until the drip stops.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use the correct tools for the job to avoid damaging faucet components.
- If hard water deposits are an issue, consider cleaning the faucet components with vinegar before reassembly.
- Keep spare washers and O-rings on hand if you know your faucet type, for quicker future repairs.
- Take pictures at each step of disassembly to aid in reassembly.
- If the leak persists after replacing parts, the faucet body itself might be cracked, requiring a full replacement.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply completely before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding or scalding.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to over-tighten nuts and screws, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ Hot water can cause severe burns; ensure the water is off and cool before handling any parts.
- ⚠️ If you're uncomfortable with any step or encounter unexpected issues, it's best to call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle and trim to access the internal stem or cartridge. Replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble and test.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. Mineral deposits can also build up and prevent a proper seal, leading to drips.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast. The repair typically involves simple tools and replacing inexpensive parts like washers or a cartridge. However, identifying the correct replacement part is key.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's still leaking after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet is still leaking after replacing parts, double-check that the new components are correctly installed and tightened. Ensure you used the exact right parts for your faucet model. If the leak persists, the faucet body itself might be damaged, or you may need professional assistance.

**When should I call a professional plumber for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're unable to identify the cause of the leak, if the repair seems too complex, or if the leak persists despite your best efforts. Also, if you suspect damage to the pipes behind the wall or the faucet body, a professional can provide an expert diagnosis.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes between 30 minutes to an hour for a DIYer, depending on your experience level and the type of faucet. Gathering the correct replacement parts beforehand can significantly speed up the process.
