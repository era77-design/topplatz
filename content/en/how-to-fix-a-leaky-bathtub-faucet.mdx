A persistent drip from your bathtub faucet can be annoying and wasteful, often indicating a simple repair is needed. Most leaky bathtub faucets are caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which are relatively inexpensive to replace. This guide will walk you through the steps to fix it yourself, saving water and the cost of a plumber.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or flathead screwdriver (for prying)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge
- Plumber's grease
- Rags or towels
- Bucket (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, or the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water supply is completely off to prevent flooding during the repair. Test the faucet to ensure no water is running before proceeding.

### Step 2: Drain Remaining Water and Protect the Drain

Open the faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes. This will relieve pressure and prevent unexpected drips. Place a cloth or stopper over the drain opening to prevent small screws, washers, or other parts from falling down the drain during disassembly, which can be a common frustration.

### Step 3: Remove Faucet Handles and Escutcheons

Use a screwdriver to remove the screw(s) holding the faucet handles in place; these are often hidden under decorative caps that can be gently pried off. Once the handles are off, you may need to pry off the escutcheon plates, which are decorative covers that conceal the valve stems, using a utility knife or flathead screwdriver.

### Step 4: Extract the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), you'll need to remove the internal mechanism. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut and pull out the valve stem. For cartridge faucets, there's usually a retaining clip or nut to remove before pulling out the cartridge.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed valve stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, deteriorating O-rings, or damaged plastic components. For compression faucets, typically the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem is the culprit. Carefully remove the old, worn parts and replace them with new, identical ones. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for better sealing and smoother operation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Reverse the disassembly steps to put your faucet back together, ensuring all components are correctly aligned. Tighten all nuts and screws securely, but be careful not to overtighten, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components. Reattach the escutcheons and faucet handles, making sure they are properly aligned and functional.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on, listening for any unusual sounds and checking for immediate leaks. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has been successfully stopped. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly to help you remember how to reassemble the faucet correctly.
- Bring the old washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge to your local hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements.
- Apply a small amount of plumber's grease to new O-rings and threads before reassembly to ensure a smooth operation and better seal.
- If you're replacing parts on one handle, it's often a good idea to replace the corresponding parts on the other handle (hot or cold) simultaneously, as they are likely of similar age and wear.
- Sometimes, a simple leak can indicate a larger issue or an aging faucet. If repairs are frequent or complex, consider if it's time for a new fixture. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Step-by-Step DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) offers guidance on full replacement.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the main water supply is completely shut off before starting any plumbing work to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to further leaks or damage.
- ⚠️ If you encounter resistance or are unsure about any step, it's best to consult a professional plumber to avoid causing more significant damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the handle and valve, identifying and replacing worn components like washers or O-rings, and then carefully reassembling the faucet. This process usually resolves most common drips effectively.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorating O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet valve. Over time, these parts can harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a proper seal.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, in most cases, a leaky bathtub faucet is a straightforward DIY repair that can be completed with basic tools and some patience. Identifying the type of faucet and the specific worn part is key to a successful fix.

**How do I identify the part causing the leak when I want to fix bathtub faucet?**

After disassembling the faucet, carefully inspect the valve stem or cartridge for signs of wear such as flattened, cracked, or hardened rubber washers and O-rings. These small, often rubber, components are typically responsible for the persistent drip.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips or flathead, depending on your faucet), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife, rags, and replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge. Plumber's grease can also be very helpful for reassembly.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most DIY enthusiasts, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on the faucet's complexity and your experience level. Gathering the correct replacement parts might add extra time.

**When should I call a professional for a leaky faucet?**

If you're uncomfortable with plumbing work, can't identify the cause of the leak, or if the leak persists after attempting repairs, it's best to call a professional plumber. They have the expertise and specialized tools for complex issues.
