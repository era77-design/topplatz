A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by a worn-out washer or cartridge, leading to annoying drips and wasted water. Fixing it yourself is usually a straightforward process that can save you money and prevent further issues. This guide will walk you through the essential steps to identify the problem and repair your faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife (optional, for caulk)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet)
- Plumber's grease
- Wire brush or old toothbrush
- White vinegar (for cleaning mineral deposits)
- Rag or towel
- Small bucket

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the isolation valves for your bathroom or bathtub specifically. Once turned off, open the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines.

> 💡 **Tip:** After turning off the water, briefly turn on the faucet to confirm that the water flow has stopped completely before proceeding.

### Step 2: Identify Faucet Type and Disassemble Handle

Bathtub faucets typically come in two main types: compression or cartridge. Remove any decorative caps or screws on the faucet handle to expose the mounting screw. Use a screwdriver to remove this screw, then gently pull the handle off. You might need to pry off the escutcheon plate or trim to fully access the internal components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This visual reference will be invaluable when it's time to reassemble the faucet correctly.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

For compression faucets, you'll see a stem that screws into the faucet body. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew and remove the stem. For cartridge faucets, there will be a retaining nut or clip holding the cartridge in place; remove this and pull the cartridge straight out. If the faucet is heavily corroded or damaged, a full replacement might be more effective. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet)

> 💡 **Tip:** If the stem or cartridge is stuck, apply a penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again.

### Step 4: Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. If you have a compression faucet, replace the old washers and O-rings with new ones of the exact same size. For a cartridge faucet, replace the entire cartridge unit with a new one specific to your faucet model. Lubricate new rubber parts with plumber's grease to ensure smooth operation and a good seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Bring the old washers, O-rings, or cartridge to your local hardware store to ensure you purchase exact matching replacements.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, use a wire brush or old toothbrush and white vinegar to clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet body. This helps ensure new components seat properly. Carefully insert the new washers, O-rings, or cartridge, then screw the stem or secure the cartridge back into place. Reattach the escutcheon plate and the faucet handle, ensuring all screws are snug but not overtightened.

> 💡 **Tip:** Ensure all components are aligned correctly during reassembly to prevent future leaks or operational issues.

### Step 6: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water is fully restored, check the faucet for any signs of dripping or leaking around the handle or spout. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and the leak has stopped. If the leak persists, especially from the spout, you might need to check the diverter or consider replacing the spout. > 💡 **Tip:** Watch closely for several minutes after turning the water back on, as small leaks might not appear immediately.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a small bucket or rag handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If you're unsure about your faucet type or parts, consult your faucet's user manual or search for your faucet model online.
- Avoid using excessive force when tightening components; overtightening can strip threads or crack plastic parts.
- Regularly clean your faucet with a mild cleaner to prevent mineral buildup that can lead to premature wear of internal parts.
- If the leak is coming from the base of the spout and not the handle, it might be an issue with the O-rings inside the spout itself, which requires a different approach.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply to your house before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when using tools; ensure a firm grip to avoid stripping screws or damaging faucet finishes.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can damage components and make future repairs more difficult.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply, then disassemble the handle to access the faucet stem or cartridge. Replace any worn washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge with new parts, clean any mineral buildup, and reassemble.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's constantly dripping?**

A constant drip from your bathtub faucet usually indicates a worn-out washer or a faulty cartridge. The repair involves shutting off the water, removing the faucet handle and trim, then replacing the compromised internal component that's failing to create a proper seal.

**What are the common causes of a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The most common causes include worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, a damaged or old cartridge, or mineral deposits accumulating on internal components. These issues prevent a complete seal, leading to drips and leaks.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, pliers, and possibly a utility knife. You'll also need the appropriate replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. However, if you encounter difficulties identifying parts, extensive corrosion, or if the leak persists after your repair, it might be best to consult a professional plumber.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For common issues like replacing a washer or cartridge, the repair can often be completed within 30 minutes to an hour. This time includes turning off the water, disassembling, replacing parts, cleaning, and reassembling the faucet.

**What should I do if my faucet continues to leak after replacing parts?**

If your faucet continues to leak after replacing the washers or cartridge, double-check that all components are correctly seated and tightened. Ensure you used the exact replacement parts for your specific faucet model. If the problem persists, the faucet body itself might be damaged, or it could be time to consider a full faucet replacement.
