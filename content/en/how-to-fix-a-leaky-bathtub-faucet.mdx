A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can be surprisingly irritating. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which can be replaced with basic tools and a little know-how. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and fixing your dripping faucet.

## What You Need

- Adjustable wrench
- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Utility knife or flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (match your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rags or towels
- Flashlight (optional)
- Basin wrench (for specific faucet types)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub fixtures. Once the water is off, turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and relieve pressure, then plug the drain to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucets have a decorative cap on the handle that conceals a screw. Carefully pry off this cap using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Use the appropriate screwdriver to remove the screw, then pull the handle straight off the faucet stem. You may need to gently wiggle it if it's stuck due to mineral buildup.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Trim and Cartridge/Stem

After the handle is off, you'll typically see a trim plate or escutcheon. Remove any screws holding it in place and carefully pull it away. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), you'll then need to remove the retaining nut or clip holding the cartridge or stem in place. An adjustable wrench or basin wrench may be needed for this step. If your leak is from the spout itself, you may need to remove the spout at this stage. See [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet Spout: A Quick DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-spout) for more details.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Once the cartridge or stem is removed, carefully examine the washers, O-rings, and the cartridge itself for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Take the worn parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact matches. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and washers before installation, as this helps create a better seal and prolongs their life.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new components, ensuring they are properly seated. Screw the retaining nut or clip back into place, tightening it securely but without overtightening, which can damage the new parts. Reattach the trim plate, then the handle, and finally the decorative cap. Make sure all screws are snug.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, listening for any hissing sounds that might indicate a loose connection. Once the water is fully on, check for leaks around the faucet handle, spout, and base. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has been resolved. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine your work or consider a full faucet replacement.

### Step 7: Clean Up and Enjoy a Drip-Free Faucet

Wipe down any water or plumber's grease from the faucet and surrounding area. Dispose of the old, worn parts responsibly. With your bathtub faucet now fixed, you can enjoy a quiet, drip-free bathroom and save on your water bill. Regular maintenance checks can help prevent future leaks.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always plug the drain before disassembling your faucet to prevent small screws, O-rings, or other parts from falling down the drain.
- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly. These can be invaluable when it comes to reassembling the faucet correctly.
- If you're unsure about the type of replacement part needed, take the old part with you to the hardware store for an exact match. Faucet repair kits are also available for common brands.
- Avoid using excessive force when tightening components; finger-tight plus a quarter turn with a wrench is often sufficient to prevent damage to plastic or brass parts.
- Consider replacing both hot and cold side washers/O-rings even if only one side is leaking, as the other is likely to fail soon.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old components, as they can be brittle or stuck. Use appropriate tools and gentle force.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack faucet components, leading to worse leaks or costly replacements.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle and trim, remove the internal cartridge or stem, and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the cartridge itself. Reassemble the faucet, turn the water back on, and test for leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a bathtub faucet generally involves identifying the source of the leak, which is often a worn internal component. You'll need to shut off the water, take the faucet apart, replace the faulty part (like a washer or cartridge), and then put it back together. Always ensure you have the correct replacement parts for your specific faucet model.

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks occur due to worn-out internal components. Over time, rubber washers, O-rings, or the ceramic disc cartridge can deteriorate, crack, or become stiff, preventing a proper seal and allowing water to drip or stream out.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need an adjustable wrench, a set of screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), a utility knife, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge, and plumber's grease. Depending on your faucet, a basin wrench might also be useful.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself or do I need a plumber?**

Most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. The process usually involves replacing inexpensive internal parts. However, if you're uncomfortable with plumbing, encounter seized components, or the leak persists after your repair attempts, calling a plumber is advisable.

**What if replacing the washer/o-ring doesn't fix the leak?**

If new washers or O-rings don't stop the leak, the issue might be a faulty cartridge (for single-handle faucets) or a damaged valve seat. In some cases, the faucet body itself might be corroded. If repeated repairs fail, you might need to consider replacing the entire faucet. For guidance, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**How do I identify my bathtub faucet type for replacement parts?**

There are several types: compression, cartridge, ceramic disc, and ball faucets. The best way to identify yours is by disassembling it. Take the removed cartridge or stem to a hardware store; they can usually help you find the correct replacement based on the part's appearance and any markings.
