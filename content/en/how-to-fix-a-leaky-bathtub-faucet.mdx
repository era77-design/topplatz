A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can be quite annoying. Often, the fix is simpler than you think, usually involving replacing a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge. This guide will walk you through the essential steps to stop that drip and restore peace to your bathroom.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and/or flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement O-rings and washers (specific to your faucet type)
- New faucet cartridge (if applicable, specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone lubricant
- Rag or old towel
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning mineral deposits)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to turn off the water supply to prevent flooding. Locate the main shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the specific shut-off valves for your bathroom. Once the water is off, open the bathtub faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes.

### Step 2: Cover the Drain

To prevent small screws, washers, or other faucet components from falling down the drain, place a rag or a drain stopper over the tub drain opening. This simple step can save you a lot of frustration and time searching for lost parts.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles have a decorative cap covering a screw. Carefully pry off this cap using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Once the cap is removed, use the appropriate screwdriver to unscrew the handle screw and pull the handle straight off. Some handles may simply pull off without a screw.

### Step 4: Remove Escutcheon and Access Stem/Cartridge

If your faucet has an escutcheon (trim plate), unscrew it to expose the faucet body. Depending on your faucet type, you'll then need to remove a retaining nut, packing nut, or clips to access the faucet stem or cartridge. Use an adjustable wrench for nuts, turning counter-clockwise, and gently pull out the stem or cartridge.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Carefully examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any brittle, cracked, or flattened components with new ones that match your faucet's specifications. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often best to replace the entire cartridge unit.

### Step 6: Apply Plumber's Grease and Reassemble

Before reassembling, apply a thin layer of plumber's grease or silicone lubricant to the new O-rings, washers, and the cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a better seal. Carefully reverse the disassembly steps, making sure all parts are seated correctly. Tighten screws and nuts securely, but avoid overtightening, which can damage components.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Once the water is fully restored, test the faucet by turning it on and off several times, checking for any drips or leaks. If the leak persists, you may need to recheck your work or consider replacing the entire faucet unit. For guidance on full replacement, refer to our article: [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy).


## Tips & Tricks

- Before purchasing replacement parts, take photos of your faucet and the disassembled components, or even bring the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you get the correct replacements.
- Take pictures at each step of disassembly. This visual guide will be invaluable when you're putting everything back together.
- If screws are stubborn, apply a small amount of penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying again.
- Clean any mineral deposits or corrosion from the faucet body and components using a wire brush or vinegar solution before reassembly for better performance.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts; this can strip threads or crack plastic components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent water damage or flooding.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when prying off decorative caps or removing old components, as they can be brittle and break easily.
- ⚠️ Never use harsh chemical cleaners on faucet parts, as they can damage rubber or plastic components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle and stem, and replace worn-out washers, O-rings, or the cartridge. Reassembling the faucet and testing for leaks completes the repair, often resolving the issue quickly.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that is constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is usually caused by a worn-out rubber washer, O-ring, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet mechanism. Replacing these internal components, which are specific to your faucet's model, will most often resolve the persistent drip.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by normal wear and tear on internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or the ceramic disc cartridge. Over time, these parts harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a tight seal and allowing water to escape.

**Do I need special tools to fix a leaky faucet?**

For most common leaky faucet repairs, you'll need basic household tools such as screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and pliers. Some specific faucet types might require a specialized cartridge tool, but this is less common for simple leaks.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes between 15 to 45 minutes, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet. Gathering tools and identifying the correct replacement parts might add a little extra time to the overall process.

**What if my bathtub faucet still leaks after I try to fix it?**

If your bathtub faucet still leaks after attempting a repair, double-check that all components were reassembled correctly and that the replacement parts are the exact match for your faucet model. If the leak persists, the faucet body itself might be damaged, or a more complex issue may require professional help or full faucet replacement.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet without turning off the main water supply?**

No, it is essential to turn off the water supply to your house or at least the bathroom before attempting to fix a leaky bathtub faucet. Failing to do so can result in significant water leaks, damage to your bathroom, and potential injury.
