Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out rubber washers or O-rings within the faucet's handles or spout. This common DIY repair can stop annoying drips, conserve water, and prevent potential water damage to your home. With basic tools and a little patience, most homeowners can tackle this task in under an hour.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to faucet type)
- Plumber's grease
- Old towels or rags
- Utility knife (optional)
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the main water supply to your house or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub. This prevents unexpected water flow and potential flooding during the repair. Once the water is off, open the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

### Step 2: Protect the Tub and Gather Tools

Place a towel or a rubber mat over the tub drain to prevent small parts from falling into it. Gather all your necessary tools, including screwdrivers, wrenches, and your replacement parts. Having everything organized will make the repair process smoother and more efficient.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handles

Most bathtub faucet handles have a decorative cap that can be pried off with a flathead screwdriver or utility knife to reveal a screw. Unscrew this screw and pull the handle straight off. For some faucets, there might be a escutcheon plate or trim that also needs to be removed to access the underlying components.

### Step 4: Remove the Valve Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see the valve stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type, this might be held in place by a packing nut, retaining clip, or a threaded housing. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully unscrew or remove the component. Pay attention to how it's oriented, as proper reinstallation is key.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Carefully inspect the removed valve stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or springs. These are typically the culprits behind leaks. Remove the old, deteriorated parts and replace them with new ones that precisely match the originals. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new O-rings and threads to ensure a good seal and smooth operation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Reverse the disassembly process to put the faucet back together. Carefully reinsert the valve stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated and tightened. Reattach the handle and secure it with its screw, then replace any decorative caps or trim. Make sure all connections are snug but avoid overtightening, which can damage plastic or brass components.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or spout. Once the water is fully restored, turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water. Watch for any drips or leaks from the spout or handles. If a leak persists, you may need to recheck your connections or replace more components.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly; this will be invaluable when reassembling the faucet.
- Always use exact replacement parts. If you can't find them, take the old parts to a hardware store for a match.
- Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings and threads to ensure a smooth fit and prevent future sticking.
- Keep a bucket or a small container handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- Consider replacing components on both the hot and cold sides if your faucet has separate handles, as they often wear out at similar rates.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely turned off before attempting any repairs to avoid water damage or injury.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to more significant leaks.
- ⚠️ If dealing with very old plumbing, be extra gentle, as old pipes and fixtures can be brittle and prone to breaking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I identify the cause of a leaky bathtub faucet?**

A leaky bathtub faucet usually indicates worn-out internal components. If it's dripping from the spout, the issue is often with the washers, O-rings, or cartridge inside the valve. Leaks around the handle typically point to a faulty O-ring or packing nut on the valve stem itself.

**What tools are essential to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll generally need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and the correct replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge specific to your faucet model. Old towels and plumber's grease are also very helpful.

**How do you fix a bathtub faucet that is constantly dripping from the spout?**

To fix a bathtub faucet dripping from the spout, you'll need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle, and remove the valve stem or cartridge. The most common fix is replacing the worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that controls water flow through the spout.

**Is it possible to fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or should I call a professional?**

Many leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a homeowner with basic tools and some patience, especially if it's just a matter of replacing worn washers or O-rings. However, if the issue is complex, the faucet is very old, or you're uncomfortable with plumbing work, calling a professional is always a good option. Sometimes, a full faucet replacement might be more cost-effective if parts are hard to find or the faucet is severely damaged; you can learn more about that process in our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**What should I do if my bathtub faucet leaks around the handle?**

If your bathtub faucet leaks around the handle, the problem is usually a worn O-ring or packing washer on the valve stem directly beneath the handle. You'll need to turn off the water, remove the handle, and then replace these specific components, applying plumber's grease for a better seal.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically takes between 30 to 60 minutes for most DIYers. This estimate can vary depending on your experience level, the type of faucet, and how easily you can access and replace the necessary parts.

**What are the common replacement parts needed when I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

When you fix a leaky bathtub faucet, the most common replacement parts are rubber washers, O-rings, springs, and sometimes an entire valve cartridge. These components wear down over time, leading to drips and leaks, and are usually specific to your faucet's brand and model.
