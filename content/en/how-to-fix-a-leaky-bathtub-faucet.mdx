Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. This common plumbing issue can be resolved with basic tools and a bit of patience, saving water and preventing further damage.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for decorative caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or faucet cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or towel
- Wire brush or old toothbrush (for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Shut Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or, if available, the shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow is completely stopped. Open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure, ensuring no unexpected sprays during the repair.

### Step 2: Access the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucet handles have a decorative cap covering a screw. Carefully pry off this cap using a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw with the appropriate screwdriver (usually Phillips) and gently pull the handle straight off the stem. If the handle is stuck, a slight wiggle or a penetrating lubricant might help loosen it.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Beneath the handle, you'll find the faucet stem or cartridge, often secured by a retaining nut or clip. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut or retaining clip. Once loosened, gently pull out the stem or cartridge from the faucet body. Note its orientation for easier reassembly.

### Step 4: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cracks. For compression faucets, replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and the O-rings around the stem. For cartridge faucets, you'll typically replace the entire cartridge unit. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the new cartridge before installation to ensure smooth operation and a better seal.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body using a wire brush or old toothbrush. Insert the new components back into the faucet body, ensuring they are correctly aligned. Secure the retaining nut or clip, then reattach the faucet handle, screw, and decorative cap. Do not overtighten, as this can damage components.

### Step 6: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on, listening for any hissing sounds that might indicate a leak. Open the bathtub faucet and check for any drips or leaks around the handle or spout. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure proper functionality. If a leak persists, you may need to recheck your work or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos at each disassembly step to help with reassembly, especially if you're unfamiliar with your faucet's internal workings.
- When purchasing replacement parts, bring the old components with you to ensure you get an exact match for your specific faucet model.
- Avoid overtightening screws and nuts during reassembly, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- If your faucet is old or severely corroded, or if simple repairs don't stop the leak, it might be more cost-effective in the long run to replace the entire faucet. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy) can help with that.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely shut off before beginning any work to prevent water damage or injury.
- ⚠️ Do not force stubborn parts; use penetrating oil or gentle persuasion to avoid stripping screws or breaking components.
- ⚠️ Use only appropriate replacement parts for your specific faucet model; generic parts may not fit correctly and could cause further leaks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to identify the type of faucet you have (e.g., compression, cartridge) and replace the worn internal components. This usually involves swapping out a faulty washer, O-ring, or the entire cartridge unit, which are common causes of drips.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. These components degrade over time due to normal wear and tear, mineral buildup from hard water, or constant pressure, leading to water escaping when the faucet is closed.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, in many cases, you can fix a leaky bathtub faucet yourself with basic tools and some patience. It's a common DIY plumbing repair that often requires replacing inexpensive parts specific to your faucet's make and model. If you're unsure, online tutorials or your faucet's manual can provide guidance.

**What tools do I need to fix bathtub faucet leaks?**

To fix bathtub faucet leaks, you'll generally need a few basic tools. These include Phillips and flathead screwdrivers, an adjustable wrench or pliers, and potentially a utility knife. The most important 'tool' will be the correct replacement parts, such as washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge.

**How do I know what type of faucet I have?**

The type of faucet (compression, cartridge, ceramic disc, or ball) is often determined by its internal mechanism. Compression faucets have separate hot and cold handles that you turn to tighten, while cartridge faucets usually have a single handle that moves up/down and side-to-side. Disassembly will often reveal the specific internal components.

**When should I call a professional plumber?**

You should call a professional plumber if you encounter significant issues like heavily corroded or seized components, stripped screws you cannot remove, or if the leak persists after multiple repair attempts. Additionally, if you're uncomfortable working with plumbing or suspect a more complex problem, a professional can diagnose and fix it safely.
