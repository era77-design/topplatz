A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance, wasting water and potentially causing damage over time. Most leaks stem from worn-out internal components like washers or cartridges, which are typically straightforward to replace. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and fixing your leaky faucet, saving you money and hassle.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Rag or old towel
- Pliers (optional)
- Wire brush or old toothbrush (for cleaning)
- Seat wrench (for compression faucets)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or the specific valves for the bathroom if available. Turn the handles clockwise until the water flow stops completely. Open the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes before starting work, ensuring no unexpected sprays occur.

### Step 2: Identify Your Faucet Type

Bathtub faucets typically fall into two main categories: compression faucets (older style, use rubber washers) or cartridge/disc faucets (newer style, use a ceramic cartridge or disc assembly). Knowing your faucet type will dictate the specific parts you need to replace. If you're unsure, take photos of the disassembled parts to an expert or hardware store for identification.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Carefully pry off any decorative caps or covers on the faucet handle using a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. Unscrew the handle screw, then pull the handle straight off. You may need to use a bit of force or gently pry it if it's stuck due to mineral buildup. Remove any escutcheon plates or trim pieces to expose the faucet body and internal components.

### Step 4: Replace the Worn Component (Washer or Cartridge)

For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the packing nut (or bonnet nut) and remove the stem assembly. Replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and the O-rings if present, and clean the valve seat. For cartridge faucets, remove the retaining clip or nut holding the cartridge in place, then pull the old cartridge straight out with pliers, noting its orientation. Insert the new cartridge, aligning it correctly, and secure it with the clip or nut.

### Step 5: Clean and Lubricate Parts

While the faucet is disassembled, thoroughly clean any mineral deposits, rust, or debris from the internal components using a wire brush or old toothbrush. This helps prevent future issues and ensures smooth operation. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease (silicone-based) to new washers, O-rings, and the cartridge to prolong their lifespan and create a better seal.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully put all the faucet components back together in reverse order of disassembly. Ensure screws are snug but do not overtighten, as this can strip threads or crack plastic parts. Double-check that all parts are correctly seated and aligned before proceeding to the final step.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Once the faucet is fully reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any leaks around the handle, spout, or faucet body. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure smooth operation and verify that the leak has been resolved. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or towel handy to catch any residual water during disassembly.
- Take photos at each step of disassembly to aid in accurate reassembly, especially with complex cartridge systems.
- When purchasing replacement parts, bring the old ones with you to the hardware store to ensure a perfect match, or note the faucet brand and model number.
- If the leak is coming from the spout itself rather than the handles, it might be an issue with the diverter or the spout's O-rings. Consider checking [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet Spout: A Quick DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-spout) for guidance.
- Keep a small container for screws and small parts to prevent them from getting lost during the repair process.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding and water damage.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads, crack plastic components, or damage the faucet body.
- ⚠️ If you encounter rust or corrosion that prevents easy disassembly, stop and consider calling a professional plumber or replacing the entire faucet to avoid further damage.
- ⚠️ Wear appropriate safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris, especially when prying off stubborn caps or components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the handle, identify the type of faucet (compression or cartridge), and replace the worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully and then test for leaks.

**What usually causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal parts. For older compression faucets, this is often a deteriorated rubber washer or a corroded valve seat. For newer cartridge or disc faucets, the ceramic cartridge or the O-rings within it usually wear out over time, leading to drips.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY task for most homeowners. With basic tools, the right replacement parts, and a little patience, you can often diagnose and repair the issue yourself, saving on plumber costs. This guide provides the steps you need to get started.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's a cartridge type?**

If your bathtub faucet is a cartridge type, you'll need to remove the handle and any retaining clips or nuts holding the cartridge in place. Carefully pull out the old cartridge, ensuring you note its orientation, then insert a new, matching cartridge. Secure it with the clip or nut, and then reassemble the faucet.

**What tools are essential for fixing a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Essential tools include a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, and potentially pliers. You'll also need replacement parts specific to your faucet, such as new washers, O-rings, or a cartridge, and plumber's grease to ensure a good seal and smooth operation.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

If your faucet is very old, extensively corroded, or if replacement parts are difficult to find or discontinued, it might be more cost-effective to replace the entire unit. Persistent leaks after multiple repair attempts or significant damage to the faucet body are also good indicators for replacement.
