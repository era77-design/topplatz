A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household nuisance that wastes water and can be surprisingly simple to fix. Most leaks stem from worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet handle assembly. By replacing these inexpensive components, you can often stop the drip, conserve water, and restore your faucet to proper working order without needing a professional.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge/stem (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying)
- Clean rags or towels
- Wire brush (for cleaning mineral deposits)
- Flashlight

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any repair work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or if available, the isolation valves for the bathroom or individual tub. Turn both the hot and cold water completely off, then open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the lines and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap or a small plug. Carefully pry off any caps with a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to expose the screw. Use the appropriate screwdriver to remove the screw, then gently pull the handle straight off. If it's stuck, a little wiggling or penetrating oil might help loosen it.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), this component will vary. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem or cartridge in place. Slowly pull out the stem or cartridge, being mindful of any small parts that might fall out.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact matches. If you have a cartridge faucet, it's often easier and more effective to replace the entire cartridge. Clean any mineral deposits from the faucet body using a wire brush or vinegar solution.

### Step 5: Apply Plumber's Grease and Reassemble

Lubricate new washers and O-rings with plumber's grease before installing them. This helps create a better seal and prolongs their life. Carefully insert the new components or the new cartridge back into the faucet body. Reattach the packing nut or retaining clip, tightening it snugly with your wrench, but avoid over-tightening which can damage parts.

### Step 6: Reattach the Handle and Test for Leaks

Place the faucet handle back onto the stem, secure it with its screw, and replace any decorative caps. Once everything is reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check the faucet thoroughly for any drips or leaks. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure smooth operation and no further leaks.

### Step 7: Final Adjustments and Clean Up

If you notice any minor drips, a slight adjustment to the packing nut might be needed – a quarter turn can sometimes make a difference. Clean up your workspace and properly dispose of old parts. Enjoy your now leak-free bathtub faucet and the savings on your water bill!


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This visual guide will be invaluable when reassembling the faucet, especially for complex models.
- Keep all removed screws, washers, and small parts organized in a container or on a clean rag. Small components can easily get lost, making reassembly difficult.
- When buying replacement parts, it's best to bring the old ones with you to the hardware store to ensure a perfect match. Many faucets use proprietary components.
- Apply a thin, even layer of silicone-based plumber's grease to all rubber washers, O-rings, and threads. This helps create a better seal, prevents future leaks, and makes future repairs easier.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts. Snug is sufficient; overtightening can strip threads, crack plastic components, or deform rubber seals, leading to new leaks.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent accidental floods and water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old, seized parts, as they can break, strip, or scratch the faucet's finish. Use appropriate tools and gentle force.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical cleaners or abrasive tools on your faucet, as this can damage its finish and potentially corrode internal components over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these parts degrade from constant use, mineral deposits in the water, or changes in water pressure, leading to drips and leaks that need addressing.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, in most cases, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a straightforward DIY project that many homeowners can tackle with basic tools. With the right replacement parts and a bit of patience, you can often resolve the issue without needing to call a professional plumber.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's a constant drip?**

A constant drip usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components, such as a worn washer or a damaged cartridge. You'll need to disassemble the faucet, identify the specific worn-out part, and replace it. Thoroughly cleaning any mineral buildup during this process can also help ensure a tight, lasting seal.

**What tools do I need to fix bathtub faucet leaks?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, pliers, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge (specific to your faucet), plumber's grease, and a utility knife. Having a flashlight and clean rags is also very helpful for the task.

**How much does it cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet yourself is usually minimal, often just the price of replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge, which typically ranges from $5 to $30. Hiring a plumber, however, could cost significantly more due to labor charges, so DIY can save money.

**When should I consider replacing the entire faucet instead of fixing it?**

Consider replacing the faucet if the leak persists after multiple repair attempts, if the faucet body is corroded or cracked, if replacement parts are unavailable, or if you simply want to upgrade your bathroom's look. For a full replacement guide, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it leaks from the base or spout?**

Leaks from the base or spout often indicate issues with the O-rings or seals around the spout itself, or possibly a worn diverter if it's a shower/tub combo. You would typically need to remove the spout, replace its internal O-rings, and ensure it's properly seated and sealed. If the spout itself is damaged, you might need to replace just the spout. For spout replacement, you can refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet Spout: A Quick DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-spout).
