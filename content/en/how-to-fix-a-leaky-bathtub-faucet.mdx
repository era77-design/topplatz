A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge, leading to wasted water and annoying drips. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet handle, and replacing the faulty internal components. This DIY guide will walk you through the process to restore your faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Cleaning cloth
- White vinegar (optional, for mineral deposits)
- Flashlight
- Safety glasses

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or the specific valves for your bathroom if available. Turn the valve clockwise until the water flow completely stops. This crucial step prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process. Always double-check that the water is off before proceeding.

> 💡 **Tip:** After shutting off the water, open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Remove Faucet Handle and Trim

Use a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver to carefully pry off any decorative caps on the faucet handles. Beneath these caps, you'll usually find screws (often Phillips-head) holding the handles in place. Unscrew these and pull the handles off. Next, remove any escutcheon plates or trim that cover the faucet body, typically by unscrewing them or prying them off gently.

### Step 3: Access and Disassemble the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handles and trim are removed, you'll expose the internal components, which are usually a stem or a cartridge. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), you'll need an adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully unscrew the retaining nut or clip holding the stem or cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge from the faucet body. Note its orientation before removal.

### Step 4: Identify and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for any signs of wear, such as cracked O-rings, deteriorated rubber washers, or a damaged plastic cartridge body. For compression faucets, the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem is often the culprit. Replace these worn parts with new ones, ensuring they are the correct size and type for your specific faucet model. Take the old parts to a hardware store to find exact matches if needed.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembling, thoroughly clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body and internal components using a brush and white vinegar solution. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease (silicone-based) to new O-rings and moving parts to ensure smooth operation and a better seal. Carefully insert the new or repaired stem/cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly seated. Reinstall the retaining nut or clip, tightening it snugly but not excessively.

### Step 6: Reattach Handles and Test for Leaks

Reattach the escutcheon plates, handles, and decorative caps in reverse order of removal. Ensure all screws are tightened securely. Slowly turn the main water supply back on and check the faucet for any leaks or drips. Turn both the hot and cold water on and off several times to ensure proper function and to flush out any air in the lines. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider a full replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly to help with reassembly.
- Keep all removed parts organized in the order they were taken off to avoid confusion.
- When buying replacement parts, always try to use manufacturer-specific components for the best fit and performance.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- If mineral deposits are severe, let parts soak in white vinegar overnight before cleaning.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying or unscrewing old parts, as they can be brittle or corroded and may break easily.
- ⚠️ Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from debris or splashes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the most common reasons for a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks stem from worn-out internal components. This commonly includes deteriorated rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge that has lost its sealing integrity. Mineral buildup can also prevent parts from sealing properly, leading to persistent drips.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that drips constantly?**

To fix a constantly dripping bathtub faucet, you typically need to replace the worn internal parts. This usually involves shutting off the water, disassembling the handle, and replacing the washer, O-rings, or the entire cartridge that is causing the drip. Following the steps in this guide will help you address the issue effectively.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. Common repairs like replacing washers or cartridges are straightforward. However, if you encounter extensive corrosion, stripped screws, or complex internal mechanisms, calling a professional plumber might be a better option.

**What tools will I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Essential tools include screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and possibly a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver for prying. You will also need the appropriate replacement parts, such as new washers, O-rings, or a cartridge specific to your faucet model. Plumber's grease and a cleaning cloth are also helpful.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

A standard repair for a leaky bathtub faucet, such as replacing a washer or cartridge, usually takes between 30 to 60 minutes. This time can vary depending on your experience, the complexity of your faucet, and whether you have the necessary replacement parts readily available.

**When should I consider replacing the entire faucet instead of just repairing it?**

You should consider replacing the entire faucet if it's very old, heavily corroded, or if replacement parts are difficult to find. Persistent leaks after multiple repair attempts or significant damage to the faucet body also indicate it's time for a full replacement. For guidance on this, see [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet - Your DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

**Are there different types of bathtub faucets, and do they all fix the same way?**

Yes, there are several types, including compression, cartridge, disc, and ball faucets, each with slightly different internal mechanisms. While the general principle of replacing worn parts remains, the disassembly and specific components will vary. Identifying your faucet type is key to finding the correct replacement parts.
