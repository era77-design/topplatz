A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household annoyance that can waste water and drive up utility bills. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which can often be fixed with basic tools and a little DIY effort. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and repairing your dripping faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers (slip-joint or needle-nose)
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Rags or towels
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or new cartridge, specific to your faucet model)
- Flashlight or headlamp

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any work, locate the main water shut-off valve for your house or the specific valve for the bathroom. Turn it off completely to prevent water from gushing out when you disassemble the faucet. Open the bathtub faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Access the Faucet Handles

Look for decorative caps on the top or front of the faucet handles; these usually pry off with a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife. Underneath, you'll find screws holding the handles in place. Remove these screws and carefully pull off the handles.

### Step 3: Remove the Trim and Escutcheon

Once the handles are off, you may see an escutcheon plate or trim covering the faucet body. This might be held by additional screws or simply threaded on. Remove any screws and carefully pry off or unscrew the trim to expose the faucet's internal components, such as the cartridge or stem assembly.

### Step 4: Extract the Cartridge or Stem Assembly

The method for removing the internal mechanism varies by faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc). For most cartridge faucets, you'll need to remove a retaining nut or clip with pliers or an adjustable wrench. Carefully pull out the cartridge or stem assembly; it might require a firm tug or a specialized cartridge puller if it's stuck.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the removed cartridge or stem for signs of wear, such as cracked O-rings, hardened washers, or corroded springs. Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you get exact matches. If it's a cartridge faucet, often replacing the entire cartridge is the simplest and most effective solution. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or moving parts before installation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the new cartridge or stem assembly, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Secure it with the retaining nut or clip you removed earlier. Replace the escutcheon plate, handles, and decorative caps in reverse order of disassembly. Make sure all screws are tightened snugly, but avoid overtightening, which can strip threads or crack plastic components.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet body and handles. Turn the faucet on and off several times, checking for drips and ensuring proper water flow and temperature control. If a leak persists, you may need to re-check your assembly or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during each step of disassembly; this will be a valuable reference for reassembly.
- Place a rag or stopper in the drain to prevent small parts from falling down the drain.
- Bring the old parts to the hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacements for your specific faucet model.
- Don't force parts during assembly; if something isn't fitting, re-check its orientation or ensure you have the correct part.
- Keep all screws and small components organized in a tray or small containers as you remove them.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply completely before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old components, as they can be brittle and break easily.
- ⚠️ Avoid using harsh chemical cleaners on your faucet, as they can damage finishes and internal components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant resistance or are unsure about a step, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks occur due to worn-out internal components. Common culprits include deteriorated rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Over time, these parts harden, crack, or lose their seal, allowing water to drip through.

**How to fix bathtub faucet that drips continuously?**

A continuously dripping bathtub faucet usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. The most common fix involves replacing the worn washers, O-rings, or the entire faucet cartridge. Following the steps in this guide will help you identify and replace the faulty part.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll typically need basic hand tools like screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and a utility knife. You'll also need replacement parts specific to your faucet model, such as new washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common bathtub faucet leaks can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. If you can identify your faucet type and are comfortable disassembling and reassembling components, you can likely handle it yourself. However, if the leak persists, the faucet is severely corroded, or you're unsure, calling a plumber is recommended.

**How do I identify my bathtub faucet type?**

Bathtub faucets typically fall into four categories: compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc. You can often identify the type by how the handle operates and the internal components you find after removing the handle. Knowing the type helps in purchasing the correct replacement parts.

**What if replacing the washer or cartridge doesn't stop the leak?**

If basic repairs like replacing washers or cartridges don't resolve the leak, the issue might be more complex, such as damage to the faucet body itself, corroded valve seats, or issues with the piping. In such cases, it might be more cost-effective to replace the entire faucet or consult a professional plumber. You can find guides on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet-diy) if you decide to replace it yourself.

**How much does it cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet yourself is usually minimal, typically just the price of replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge, which can range from a few dollars to $30-$50. Hiring a plumber, however, could cost anywhere from $100 to $300 or more, depending on the complexity and location.
