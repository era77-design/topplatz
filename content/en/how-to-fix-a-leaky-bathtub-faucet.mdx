Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or cartridges within the faucet mechanism. This common DIY repair can save water, prevent stains, and eliminate irritating dripping sounds.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Old towel or rag
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, if available, the shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the handle clockwise until the water supply is completely off. Turn on the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

Depending on your faucet type, you'll usually find a decorative cap (often plastic) covering a screw on the handle. Carefully pry off this cap with a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife, then use a screwdriver to remove the screw. Pull the handle straight off.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Body

Once the handle is off, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. For two-handle faucets, there's often a decorative escutcheon or trim piece that may need to be unscrewed. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut (bonnet nut) that holds the stem in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or springs. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Note their position and condition, then remove the old, worn parts and replace them with new, identical ones. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings for smooth operation and a better seal.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements.

### Step 5: Clean and Reassemble the Faucet

Before reassembly, use a wire brush or rag to clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body's interior. Carefully insert the new or repaired stem/cartridge back into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Hand-tighten the packing nut, then use the wrench to gently tighten it further, but don't overtighten.

### Step 6: Reattach the Handle and Test

Place the faucet handle back onto the stem, secure it with its screw, and replace the decorative cap. Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any leaks around the faucet base or handle, then turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and the leak is resolved.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak persists or the faucet is severely damaged, it might be time for a full replacement. Consider reading [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) for detailed instructions.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each step of disassembly. This will be invaluable when putting everything back together.
- Keep all removed screws and parts in a small container or on a towel so they don't get lost down the drain.
- When buying replacement parts, it's often helpful to bring the old parts or know your faucet's brand and model number.
- Avoid overtightening nuts and screws, especially on plastic components, as this can cause damage.
- If you're replacing parts on one handle (e.g., hot), consider replacing the corresponding parts on the other handle (cold) at the same time, as they likely have similar wear.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely turned off before starting any work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off decorative caps or loosening old components, as they can be brittle or stuck.
- ⚠️ Do not use excessive force when tightening parts, as this can strip threads or crack the faucet body.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Bathtub faucets typically leak due to worn-out internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Over time, these parts degrade, losing their ability to create a watertight seal, leading to drips.

**How can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

You can often fix a leaky bathtub faucet yourself by turning off the water, disassembling the faucet handle and body, and replacing the worn washers, O-rings, or cartridge. Reassembly follows the reverse steps.

**What tools do I need to fix bathtub faucet leaks?**

Essential tools include screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and potentially a utility knife. You'll also need replacement parts specific to your faucet model and plumber's grease.

**How do I identify the correct replacement parts for my leaky faucet?**

The best way to identify correct parts is to remove the old, worn components (washers, O-rings, or cartridge) and take them to a hardware store. Many stores can match them to new ones or help you find a universal repair kit.

**How long does it typically take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most DIYers, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, assuming you have the right tools and replacement parts readily available.

**What if my bathtub faucet is still leaking after I've tried to fix it?**

If the leak persists, double-check that all components are correctly seated and tightened. The issue might be a more complex problem, such as a cracked faucet body or valve seat, which may require professional plumbing assistance or a full faucet replacement.

**Is it really worth the effort to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

Absolutely! A constantly dripping faucet wastes a significant amount of water over time, leading to higher water bills. Fixing it also prevents unsightly mineral stains in your tub and eliminates an annoying noise.
