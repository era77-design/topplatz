A leaky bathtub faucet is commonly caused by worn-out internal components, such as rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Fixing it typically involves turning off the main water supply, carefully disassembling the faucet handle, and replacing the degraded part to restore a drip-free flow.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (matching your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife or flat scraper (optional)
- Towels or rags
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, or the specific valve for your bathroom if available. Turn it off completely to prevent flooding when you begin working on the faucet. It's crucial to ensure no water can flow to the bathtub before proceeding with any disassembly.

### Step 2: Drain Remaining Water and Protect the Tub

Once the water supply is off, open the leaky faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This relieves pressure and prevents unexpected drips. Place a towel or rag over the drain to catch any small parts that might accidentally fall, preventing them from going down the drain.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap. Carefully pry off the cap with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver, then use the appropriate screwdriver to remove the screw. Once the screw is out, pull the handle straight off to expose the underlying components.

### Step 4: Disassemble the Faucet Body

After removing the handle, you'll typically see a decorative escutcheon or cover plate, which might be screwed in or simply threaded on. Remove this to expose the faucet stem or cartridge. You may need an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip that holds the stem or cartridge in place.

### Step 5: Identify and Replace Worn Parts

Carefully extract the faucet stem or cartridge. Examine the rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. Replace any worn rubber components with new ones of the exact size and type. If your faucet uses a cartridge, replace it with an identical new one. For more complex replacements or a full faucet overhaul, refer to our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet).

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new washers, O-rings, or the cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a good seal. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Reattach the packing nut or retaining clip, then the escutcheon, and finally the faucet handle, securing it with its screw and decorative cap.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check the faucet for any leaks around the handle or spout. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure it operates smoothly and the leak has been completely stopped. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or tighten connections slightly.


## Tips & Tricks

- Keep all removed parts organized in the order you took them off to make reassembly easier.
- Use plumber's grease on new rubber washers and O-rings to help create a better seal and prevent future wear.
- Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to avoid water damage.
- ⚠️ Be gentle when prying off decorative caps or loosening old components, as they can be brittle and break easily.
- ⚠️ Ensure replacement parts are identical to the originals for proper fit and function.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle and body, and replace worn internal components like rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully and then restore the water supply.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out or deteriorated internal parts. These include rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge that no longer creates a watertight seal, allowing water to drip or run continuously.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that many homeowners can accomplish. With the right tools and replacement parts, it's a straightforward process that can save you money on professional plumbing services.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need basic tools like screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and the specific replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a cartridge) that match your faucet's make and model.

**How do you fix a bathtub faucet that won't stop dripping?**

To fix a bathtub faucet that won't stop dripping, the most common solution is to replace the internal sealing components. This usually involves identifying if your faucet uses washers, O-rings, or a cartridge, and then replacing the faulty part to restore a tight seal.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most common leaks, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and the specific type of faucet you have. Gathering the correct replacement parts beforehand can significantly speed up the process.

**When should I call a professional for a leaky faucet?**

You should call a professional plumber if you're uncomfortable with DIY repairs, can't identify the source of the leak, or if the leak persists after attempting to fix it. Additionally, if the faucet is old, corroded, or requires specialized tools, a professional can ensure the job is done correctly.
