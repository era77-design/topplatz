A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Fixing it typically involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet handle, replacing the faulty components, and then reassembling the faucet. This DIY repair can save water, prevent further damage, and eliminate irritating drips.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (flathead and Phillips)
- Adjustable wrench
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (match your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease or silicone sealant
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying)
- Old towel or rag
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)
- Bucket (optional, to catch drips)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Look for the main shut-off valve for your house, or individual shut-off valves for the bathtub if they are present. Turning off the water prevents floods and ensures a safe working environment.

> 💡 **Tip:** Always test the faucet after turning off the water to confirm it's completely off before proceeding.

### Step 2: 2. Prepare Your Workspace

Place an old towel or rag in the bathtub drain to prevent small parts from falling in. Gather all your tools and replacement parts. Having everything organized will make the repair process smoother and prevent frustration.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take a photo of the faucet before disassembly; it can be a helpful reference during reassembly.

### Step 3: 3. Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most bathtub faucet handles have a decorative cap or screw cover that needs to be removed first. Use a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to gently pry off the cap, revealing the screw underneath. Use the appropriate screwdriver to loosen and remove this screw, then pull the handle straight off. Some handles may just pull off or unscrew.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the handle is stuck, a little penetrating oil applied to the screw and allowed to sit for a few minutes can help.

### Step 4: 4. Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. If it's a stem, you'll likely need an adjustable wrench to loosen the packing nut that holds it in place. Turn counter-clockwise to remove. For cartridge-style faucets, there might be a retaining clip or nut that needs to be removed before the cartridge can be pulled out. Carefully extract the stem or cartridge.

> 💡 **Tip:** Note the orientation of the stem or cartridge as you remove it to ensure correct reinstallation.

### Step 5: 5. Replace Worn Components

Examine the old washers, O-rings, or the cartridge for signs of wear, cracking, or deterioration. Replace these with new, matching parts. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to the new O-rings and rubber washers before installing them. If replacing a cartridge, ensure the new one is an exact match for your faucet model.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take the old parts to a hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements.

### Step 6: 6. Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Place the handle back on, align it correctly, and re-tighten the screw. Finally, snap the decorative cap back into place.

> 💡 **Tip:** Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can damage components or strip threads.

### Step 7: 7. Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check for any drips or leaks around the faucet handle and spout. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure smooth operation and that the leak has been resolved. If the leak persists, you may need to re-check your work or consider replacing the entire faucet.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak persists after replacing parts, the faucet body itself might be corroded, or you might need a different type of repair. Sometimes, a full replacement is the most effective solution. For guidance on that, check out [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use the correct tools for the job to avoid stripping screws or damaging parts.
- Keep a small container handy to hold screws and tiny parts during disassembly.
- If you have an older faucet, consider replacing both the hot and cold water components at the same time, as they are likely to wear out around the same rate.
- If the leak is from the spout, it's typically a washer or O-ring issue. If it's from the handle, it's often the stem or cartridge.
- Consult your faucet's manufacturer website for specific diagrams or part numbers if you're unsure.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to scratch or damage the finish of your faucet or tub during the repair process.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten screws or nuts, as this can lead to stripped threads or cracked components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the stem or cartridge, replace worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, and then reassemble the faucet. This usually resolves most common leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a bathtub faucet to leak are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty internal cartridge. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their seal, allowing water to escape.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY task that most homeowners can accomplish with basic tools and some patience. Identifying the type of faucet and getting the correct replacement parts are key to a successful repair.

**What tools do I need to fix my bathtub faucet?**

To fix a bathtub faucet, you will typically need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge, plumber's grease, and possibly a utility knife. Having an old towel and a bucket is also helpful.

**How do I know if I need to replace the washer or the cartridge?**

If your faucet is an older compression style, it likely uses washers. Newer single-handle faucets or some two-handle models use a cartridge. You'll determine which by disassembling the handle and observing the internal mechanism.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For most DIYers, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience and the complexity of your faucet. Gathering the right parts beforehand can significantly speed up the process.

**When should I call a professional to fix a bathtub faucet?**

You should call a professional if you're uncomfortable with the repair, if the leak persists after multiple attempts, or if you encounter complex issues like stripped pipes or inaccessible components. Professionals can quickly diagnose and resolve persistent problems.
