A dripping bathtub faucet can be annoying and wasteful, but often, it's a simple fix. Most leaks are caused by worn-out internal components like washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. You can usually resolve this issue yourself with basic tools and some replacement parts.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or pliers
- Utility knife or putty knife
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit (specific to your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease
- Rag or towel
- Wire brush (optional, for cleaning)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the faucet on briefly after shutting off the water to drain any remaining water from the pipes and relieve pressure.

### Step 2: Remove the Faucet Handle

The method for removing the faucet handle varies by model. Look for a decorative cap or screw cover that you can pry off with a utility knife or flathead screwdriver. Underneath, you'll typically find a screw that holds the handle in place. Unscrew it and carefully pull the handle straight off.

### Step 3: Access the Faucet Cartridge or Stem

Once the handle is off, you'll usually see an escutcheon plate or trim covering the wall opening. Remove any screws holding this in place and gently pry it off. This will expose the faucet's valve body, where you'll find the cartridge or stem assembly. Note how it's oriented before removal.

### Step 4: Remove and Inspect the Cartridge/Stem

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either have a cartridge or a stem. For a cartridge, there might be a retaining nut or clip holding it. Use an adjustable wrench or pliers to loosen and remove the nut, then carefully pull out the cartridge. For a stem, unscrew the packing nut and remove the stem. Inspect the O-rings, washers, and the cartridge itself for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. Take pictures if needed to remember the correct orientation.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Parts

Take your old cartridge, stem, washers, or O-rings to a hardware store to find exact replacements. New parts should match the old ones precisely. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the moving parts of the cartridge to ensure smooth operation and a good seal. This lubrication helps extend the life of the components.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully insert the new cartridge or stem back into the valve body, ensuring it's oriented correctly. Tighten any retaining nuts or clips. Reattach the escutcheon plate, then the handle, making sure all screws are snug but not overtightened. Overtightening can damage the plastic components.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks. Turn on the bathtub faucet to test its operation and ensure the leak has stopped. If the leak persists, you may need to re-check your work or consider that the faucet body itself might be damaged. In such cases, replacing the entire faucet might be necessary. You can find guides like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-bathtub-faucet) if you decide to go that route.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always have a bucket or rag handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- Take photos during disassembly to help you remember the order and orientation of parts when reassembling.
- If you can't find exact replacement parts, consider contacting the faucet manufacturer with your model number for assistance.
- Clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body while it's disassembled using a wire brush or old toothbrush.
- Apply plumber's grease to new O-rings and threads to ensure a good seal and make future repairs easier.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when using tools like screwdrivers and wrenches to avoid stripping screws or damaging faucet finishes.
- ⚠️ If you are unsure about any step or uncomfortable working with plumbing, it's best to consult a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you fix a leaky bathtub faucet that drips constantly?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is most often caused by a worn-out washer, O-ring, or cartridge inside the faucet handle assembly. To fix it, you'll need to shut off the water, disassemble the faucet handle, and replace the faulty component with a new one specific to your faucet model.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you'll typically need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench or pliers, and a utility knife. You'll also need the correct replacement parts, such as new washers, O-rings, or a cartridge, which you can usually find at a hardware store.

**Can I really fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?**

Many common bathtub faucet leaks can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast with basic tools and some patience. The process usually involves replacing a worn internal part. However, if the leak persists after replacing components, or if you encounter significant difficulty, calling a professional plumber is advisable.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak from the base?**

If your bathtub faucet is leaking from its base rather than the spout, it could indicate a problem with the seals or connections where the faucet body meets the wall or tub deck. This often points to faulty O-rings, a loose mounting nut, or issues with the spout's internal diverter if it's a shower/tub combination faucet.

**How to fix bathtub faucet issues if replacing parts doesn't work?**

If replacing the washers, O-rings, or cartridge doesn't resolve your bathtub faucet leak, the issue might be more complex, such as damage to the faucet body itself or pipe connections. In such cases, the best solution might be to replace the entire faucet unit. Consider checking out guides like [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A Complete DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) for detailed instructions.

**How long does it take to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

For a typical DIY repair, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet can take anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour, depending on your experience level and the specific faucet type. This estimate includes the time to gather tools, disassemble, replace parts, and reassemble the faucet.
