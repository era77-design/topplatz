A dripping bathtub faucet can be an irritating noise and a waste of water. Most leaks are caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or cartridges, which are relatively simple and inexpensive to replace. This guide will walk you through the steps to diagnose and fix your leaky faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or flat-blade screwdriver (for prying)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (match your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags
- Flashlight (optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: 1. Shut Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to turn off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home, or if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn on the faucet briefly after shutting off the water to relieve any residual pressure in the pipes.

### Step 2: 2. Remove the Faucet Handle and Trim

Most faucet handles are held in place by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap or button. Carefully pry off the cap with a utility knife or small flat-blade screwdriver, then unscrew and remove the handle. Next, remove any decorative escutcheon or trim plates to expose the faucet body and components underneath.

### Step 3: 3. Disassemble the Faucet Body

Depending on your faucet type (compression, ball, disc, or cartridge), you'll need to remove additional components. For compression faucets, you'll see a packing nut and a stem. For cartridge faucets, you'll typically find a retaining clip or nut holding the cartridge in place. Use your adjustable wrench or pliers to carefully loosen and remove these parts, taking note of their order.

### Step 4: 4. Identify and Replace Worn Components

Once the internal components are accessible, inspect them for wear and tear. For compression faucets, the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem is usually the culprit. Replace it with a new one. For cartridge faucets, the entire cartridge might need to be replaced. For ball or disc faucets, look for worn O-rings or seals. Take the old part to a hardware store to ensure you get an exact match.

### Step 5: 5. Reassemble the Faucet

Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the new cartridge to ensure a smooth seal and easier installation. Carefully reassemble the faucet components in the reverse order of disassembly. Make sure all parts are seated correctly and tighten nuts and screws snugly, but avoid overtightening, which can damage parts.

### Step 6: 6. Restore Water Supply and Test

Once the faucet is fully reassembled, slowly turn the water supply back on. Check for any leaks around the handle, spout, and trim. Turn the faucet on and off several times to ensure it operates smoothly and that the leak has stopped. If a drip persists, you may need to recheck your work or consider replacing more components.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during disassembly to help remember the order of parts when reassembling.
- Keep a bucket or rags handy to catch any residual water when disassembling the faucet.
- If you have an older faucet, consider replacing both hot and cold-side washers/cartridges at the same time, as they are likely to wear out around the same time.
- Always use a non-abrasive cleaner for your faucet's finish to prevent damage.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely shut off before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Do not overtighten nuts or screws, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter a ceramic cartridge, handle it with care as it can be brittle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first shut off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle and trim to access the internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Replace the worn-out part with a new one, reassemble the faucet, and restore the water supply to test for leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's constantly dripping?**

A constantly dripping bathtub faucet is typically fixed by replacing worn internal seals. This often involves replacing the rubber washer in a compression faucet or the entire cartridge in a single-handle faucet. Ensure you match the replacement part to your specific faucet model for a lasting repair.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a bathtub faucet leak are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge. Mineral buildup can also contribute to leaks by preventing seals from closing properly.

**What tools are needed to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set, an adjustable wrench or basin wrench, pliers, and possibly a utility knife to pry off decorative caps. Having the correct replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a cartridge) is also essential.

**Can I repair a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common DIY plumbing task that most homeowners can accomplish. With the right tools and replacement parts, you can usually resolve the issue without needing to call a professional plumber.

**How do I know if I need a new cartridge or just a washer?**

Compression faucets typically use washers, while single-handle faucets often use cartridges. If you have a two-handle faucet and the leak stops when you tighten the handle, it's likely a washer. If you have a single-handle faucet that drips or is hard to turn, a new cartridge is usually needed.

**When should I consider replacing the entire bathtub faucet?**

You should consider replacing the entire faucet if it's very old, if replacement parts are difficult to find, or if the faucet body itself is corroded or damaged beyond simple repair. Sometimes, a full replacement can be more cost-effective and provide a long-term solution.
