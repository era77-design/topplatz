A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge, leading to annoying drips and wasted water. Fixing it is a common DIY task that typically involves turning off the water supply, carefully disassembling the faucet handle, and replacing the faulty internal components. This guide will walk you through the process step-by-step.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (flathead and Phillips)
- Adjustable wrench
- Penetrating oil (optional)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit
- Plumber's grease
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Rags or towels
- Bucket or small container

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main shut-off valve for your home or the specific valve for the bathroom, usually found under sinks or near the water heater. Turn it clockwise until the water flow stops completely to prevent flooding.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a rag over the drain to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: Drain Remaining Water and Protect the Area

Once the water supply is off, open the bathtub faucet handles to drain any remaining water from the pipes. This relieves pressure and prevents unexpected drips during disassembly. Lay down towels or rags in the tub to protect the surface and catch any stray water or small components.

> 💡 **Tip:** Have a bucket nearby for any water that might still come out.

### Step 3: Remove Handles and Trim

Carefully remove the decorative caps on top of the faucet handles, usually by prying them off with a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife. Unscrew the retaining screws underneath the caps using the appropriate screwdriver. Once the screws are out, pull off the handles and then remove any decorative escutcheon plates or trim that cover the faucet body.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take photos with your phone as you disassemble the faucet; they will be helpful during reassembly.

### Step 4: Access and Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Beneath the handles and trim, you'll find the faucet stem or cartridge, which controls water flow. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem or cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge; it might require a slight twist or wiggle if it's stubborn. Note its orientation for reassembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** If parts are corroded, apply penetrating oil and let it sit for a few minutes before trying to loosen them.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for any visible wear, cracks, or deterioration on the rubber washers and O-rings. If it's a compression faucet, replace the worn-out rubber washer at the end of the stem. For cartridge faucets, replace the entire cartridge with a new, identical one. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and washers before installation.

> 💡 **Tip:** Take the old parts to the hardware store to ensure you purchase exact replacements.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new stem or cartridge, ensuring it's properly seated and oriented the same way it was removed. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip, but avoid over-tightening. Replace the decorative trim, faucet handles, and their caps in the reverse order of removal. Ensure all screws are snug.

> 💡 **Tip:** Refer to your disassembly photos if you get stuck during reassembly.

### Step 7: Test for Leaks

Once everything is reassembled, slowly turn the main water supply back on. Open the bathtub faucet handles and check for any drips or leaks around the handles and spout. Let the water run for a minute to flush out any air, then turn it off and observe for several minutes to confirm the leak is gone.

> 💡 **Tip:** If the leak persists, you might need to re-tighten components or re-evaluate the replacement parts.


## Tips & Tricks

- Always use the correct tools to avoid stripping screws or damaging components.
- Keep a small container or bag handy for organizing removed screws and small parts.
- Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body while it's disassembled.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts, as this can damage threads or crack plastic components.
- If you encounter extreme difficulty removing parts, consult your faucet's manufacturer manual or a professional plumber.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the main water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to lose small screws or washers down the drain.
- ⚠️ Do not use excessive force when disassembling or reassembling, as you could damage the faucet or pipes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handles, remove the internal stem or cartridge, and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge. Reassemble the faucet carefully and test for leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty internal cartridge. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their seal, allowing water to drip.

**How do you fix a bathtub faucet that won't stop dripping?**

A bathtub faucet that won't stop dripping usually indicates a problem with the internal sealing components. You'll need to identify whether it's a compression faucet (requiring new washers) or a cartridge faucet (requiring a new cartridge) and replace the faulty part.

**What tools are needed to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need a screwdriver (flathead and Phillips), an adjustable wrench, replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge, plumber's grease, and rags. Penetrating oil can be helpful for stubborn parts.

**When should I consider replacing my bathtub faucet instead of fixing it?**

You should consider replacing your bathtub faucet if the body is corroded, parts are difficult to find, or if the repairs are becoming frequent and costly. Sometimes, a full replacement is more cost-effective and provides a longer-term solution. **Can a leaky faucet increase my water bill?**

Yes, even a small drip can waste a significant amount of water over time. A faucet dripping just one drop per second can waste thousands of gallons of water per year, leading to a noticeable increase in your water bill.
