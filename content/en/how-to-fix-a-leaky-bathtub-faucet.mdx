A leaky bathtub faucet is a common household problem that can waste water and cause unsightly stains. Fortunately, most leaks are caused by worn-out washers or O-rings and can be fixed with basic tools and a little DIY effort. This guide will walk you through the process of diagnosing and repairing your dripping faucet.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge kit
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver (for prying)
- Rags or towels
- Bucket

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house or, preferably, the shut-off valves for the bathtub specifically if they exist. This crucial first step prevents flooding once you begin disassembling the faucet. After turning off the water, open the faucet to drain any remaining water from the pipes, ensuring no pressure is built up.

### Step 2: Remove Decorative Caps and Handles

Most bathtub faucets have decorative caps covering the screws that hold the handles in place. Carefully pry off these caps using a small flathead screwdriver or utility knife. Once the caps are removed, use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver to loosen and remove the screws, then gently pull the handles straight off the faucet body. Set all removed parts aside in a safe place.

### Step 3: Access the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

With the handles removed, you'll likely see an escutcheon plate or a packing nut that holds the faucet stem or cartridge in place. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and remove the packing nut or retaining clip. If your faucet uses a cartridge, it will usually pull straight out or require a specific tool for removal, depending on the model. Note the orientation of the cartridge before removal.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Once the stem or cartridge is out, inspect the rubber washers, O-rings, or the cartridge itself for signs of wear, cracks, or deterioration. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any worn-out components with new ones that match the old parts exactly. If you're replacing a cartridge, ensure the new one is the correct model for your faucet.

### Step 5: Apply Plumber's Grease and Reassemble

Before reassembling, apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings and moving parts to ensure smooth operation and a good seal. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge, making sure it's properly seated. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip, then replace the escutcheon plate, handles, and decorative caps in reverse order of removal. Do not overtighten screws or nuts.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test

Once everything is securely reassembled, slowly turn the water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base and handles. Turn the faucet on and off several times, checking both hot and cold settings, to ensure proper function and that the leak has been resolved. If the leak persists or if you encounter issues beyond a simple washer replacement, you might consider professional help or replacing the entire faucet. For guidance on a full replacement, see our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during disassembly to help remember the order of parts for reassembly.
- Bring the old washers, O-rings, or cartridge to your local hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacements.
- Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet components while they are disassembled.
- Avoid using excessive force when tightening parts; hand-tightening followed by a quarter turn with a wrench is usually sufficient.
- Keep a bucket or towel handy under the faucet during disassembly to catch any drips or dropped parts.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to strip screws or overtighten nuts, as this can cause further damage to the faucet components.
- ⚠️ If you encounter stubborn parts that won't budge, avoid forcing them, as this could break the faucet. Consider using a penetrating oil or seeking professional help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handles and access the internal stem or cartridge. Inspect and replace worn-out rubber washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble the faucet and restore the water supply.

**How to fix bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a bathtub faucet generally involves identifying the source of the problem, which is often a worn internal component. After shutting off the water, you'll typically remove the handles, access the valve mechanism, replace faulty parts like washers or cartridges, and then reassemble the faucet.

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out or deteriorated rubber washers, O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet valve. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their elasticity, preventing a proper seal and leading to drips.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky faucet?**

You'll typically need a Phillips and flathead screwdriver, an adjustable wrench, pliers, a utility knife, and rags. For specific repairs, you might also need plumber's grease and replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge kit.

**Can I fix a leaky faucet myself?**

Yes, most leaky bathtub faucets can be fixed by a DIY enthusiast. The process usually involves basic tools and replacing inexpensive parts. However, if you're uncomfortable with plumbing tasks or the leak persists, it's best to call a professional.

**How do I know if I need a new cartridge or just a washer?**

If your faucet drips continuously from the spout, it's often a washer or O-ring issue. If the leak is persistent, originates from the handle base, or the handle is stiff/difficult to turn, a full cartridge replacement is more likely needed. Inspecting the disassembled parts will confirm the culprit.

**How often should I check my bathtub faucet for leaks?**

It's a good practice to visually inspect your bathtub faucet for drips or signs of corrosion regularly, perhaps once every few months. Addressing small leaks promptly can prevent wasted water, higher utility bills, and potential damage to your tub or surrounding area.
