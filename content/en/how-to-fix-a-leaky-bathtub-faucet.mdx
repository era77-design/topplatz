A leaky bathtub faucet is not only annoying but can also waste water and increase your utility bills. Fortunately, most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out washers or O-rings and can be fixed with basic tools and a little DIY effort, saving you the cost of a plumber.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Utility knife or small flathead screwdriver
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge (matching your faucet model)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Old rags or towels
- Bucket
- Penetrating oil (optional, for stubborn screws)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the water supply to your bathtub. Locate the main water shut-off valve for your house, or if available, individual shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn the faucet on briefly to drain any remaining water from the pipes after turning off the supply.

### Step 2: Cover the Drain and Prepare Your Workspace

Place a rag or towel over the bathtub drain to prevent small screws or parts from falling into it during disassembly. This simple step can save you a lot of frustration. Lay out your tools and replacement parts neatly so they are easily accessible.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Most faucet handles are secured by a screw, often hidden under a decorative cap or behind the handle itself. Use a utility knife or small flathead screwdriver to gently pry off any caps. Then, use the appropriate screwdriver to remove the screw and pull the handle straight off. You may need to use some gentle wiggling if it's stuck.

### Step 4: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Once the handle is off, you'll expose the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type (compression, disc, ball, or cartridge), this component will vary. Use an adjustable wrench to carefully loosen and unscrew the packing nut or retaining clip holding the stem/cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for any visible wear, cracks, or deterioration on the rubber washers, O-rings, or seals. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Replace any worn parts with new ones that precisely match your faucet model. Apply a thin coat of plumber's grease to new O-rings for better sealing and easier installation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the new stem or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip with your adjustable wrench. Reattach the faucet handle, making sure the screw is snug but not overtightened, and replace any decorative caps.

### Step 7: Restore Water and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on. Check the faucet for any immediate drips or leaks. Turn the faucet on and off a few times to ensure proper operation and that the leak has been resolved. If the leak persists, you might need to recheck your work or consider a full faucet replacement.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos with your phone at each stage of disassembly. This visual guide will be invaluable when you reassemble the faucet.
- When purchasing replacement parts, bring the old components with you to the hardware store to ensure you get an exact match for your faucet's make and model.
- If after replacing the washers and O-rings, your faucet still leaks, the entire faucet body or cartridge might be faulty. In this case, consider replacing the entire unit. [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet) offers a comprehensive walkthrough.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply completely before attempting any repairs to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components, leading to further damage.
- ⚠️ Be cautious when working with hot water pipes, as residual hot water can cause burns even after the supply is off.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out internal components, such as rubber washers, O-rings, or seals within the faucet's stem or cartridge. Over time, these parts can degrade, causing water to escape and create a drip.

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water supply, disassemble the faucet handle, remove the stem or cartridge, replace the worn washers or O-rings, and then reassemble the faucet. This process usually resolves most common leaks.

**What tools do I need to fix a bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, a utility knife, and replacement parts like washers or a new cartridge. Plumber's grease and old rags are also helpful for the repair process.

**How can I stop a bathtub faucet from dripping?**

Stopping a bathtub faucet from dripping almost always involves replacing the internal rubber components that have worn out. These include the washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge assembly, which are responsible for sealing the water flow when the faucet is off.

**Is it difficult to fix a bathtub faucet myself?**

For most common leaks caused by worn washers or O-rings, fixing a bathtub faucet is considered an easy DIY task that can be completed by homeowners with basic tools and minimal plumbing experience. However, complex issues might require professional help.

**What if my bathtub faucet still leaks after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet continues to leak after you've replaced the washers and O-rings, the problem might be with the faucet body itself, a damaged valve seat, or a faulty cartridge that needs complete replacement. In some cases, replacing the entire faucet might be the most effective solution.
