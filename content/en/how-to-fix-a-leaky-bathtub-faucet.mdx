A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out washers or O-rings inside the faucet handle. Fixing it usually involves turning off the water supply, disassembling the faucet, replacing the faulty parts, and reassembling it. This simple DIY repair can save water and prevent further damage.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Replacement washers/O-rings/cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Utility knife (optional, for stubborn gaskets)
- Clean cloths or rags
- Bucket or small container

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off Water Supply

Locate the main water shut-off valve for your home or the specific shut-off valves for the bathroom. Turn them off completely to prevent water flow during the repair. Test the leaky bathtub faucet to ensure no water comes out before proceeding with any disassembly.

### Step 2: Drain Remaining Water

Open the bathtub faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes. This step helps relieve pressure and prevents water from gushing out when you open the faucet body. It's also a good idea to place a cloth over the drain to prevent small parts from falling into the plumbing.

### Step 3: Disassemble the Faucet Handle

Carefully remove the decorative cap or screw cover from the faucet handle. Use a screwdriver to loosen and remove the screw holding the handle in place. Gently pull the handle off, exposing the faucet stem or cartridge beneath. If the handle is stuck, a gentle rocking motion might help.

### Step 4: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

Depending on your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc), you'll need to remove the stem or cartridge. For compression faucets, use an adjustable wrench to loosen the packing nut and pull out the stem. For cartridge faucets, there might be a retaining nut or clip to remove before pulling out the cartridge. Note the orientation of the parts as you remove them.

### Step 5: Inspect and Replace Worn Parts

Examine the washers, O-rings, and gaskets on the stem or cartridge. These are usually the culprits for leaks. Replace any rubber parts that appear worn, cracked, or hardened with new ones. Apply a thin layer of plumber's grease to new O-rings before installation to ensure a good seal and prolong their life.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge into the faucet body, ensuring it's properly aligned with any grooves or keys. Tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip firmly but without overtightening. Reattach the faucet handle, tightening its screw, and replace the decorative cap.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the main water supply back on. Check for any immediate leaks around the faucet base or handle. Turn the faucet on and off several times, observing closely for drips or persistent leaks. If the leak persists, you may need to recheck your work or consider replacing the entire faucet. For more extensive repairs or full replacement, refer to our guide on [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Replacement Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Take a photo of the faucet assembly before disassembling it to help with correct reassembly.
- If you're unsure about the type of faucet you have, take the old parts to a hardware store for help finding exact replacements.
- Consider replacing both hot and cold-side washers/O-rings even if only one is leaking, as they likely have similar wear.
- Keep all small parts organized in a container or on a clean cloth to prevent losing them during the repair.
- If the faucet seat is corroded, you might need a faucet seat wrench to replace or resurface it.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always shut off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repair to prevent flooding and water damage.
- ⚠️ Be careful not to overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack components, leading to further damage.
- ⚠️ If you encounter significant difficulty, cannot identify the problem, or cause further damage, it's best to stop and call a professional plumber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handle, replace worn-out washers or O-rings, and then reassemble the faucet. This process usually resolves most common leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

Most bathtub faucet leaks are caused by worn-out rubber components like washers, O-rings, or gaskets inside the faucet's stem or cartridge. These parts harden, crack, or lose their seal over time, allowing water to drip out.

**Can I fix a leaky bathtub faucet myself?**

Yes, fixing a leaky bathtub faucet is a common and achievable DIY project for many homeowners. With basic tools and a bit of patience, you can often replace the faulty parts yourself and successfully stop the leak.

**How to fix bathtub faucet if it's still leaking after replacing parts?**

If your bathtub faucet is still leaking after replacing the washers or O-rings, the faucet seat might be corroded or damaged. You may need a specialized faucet seat wrench to remove and replace or resurface the faucet seat.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll typically need a screwdriver set (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, replacement washers/O-rings specific to your faucet, and possibly plumber's grease. A utility knife can also be helpful for removing stubborn old gaskets.

**How do I determine the correct replacement parts for my leaky faucet?**

The best way to ensure you buy the correct replacement parts is to first disassemble your faucet and remove the old components, such as the stem or cartridge. Take these parts to a hardware store, where staff can help you match them to the exact replacements needed for your specific faucet model.
