Fixing a leaky bathtub faucet typically involves replacing worn-out components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. This common DIY repair can save water and prevent further damage. Most leaks are caused by these small, inexpensive parts failing over time, and the repair is often straightforward.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench
- Pliers
- Utility knife or flathead screwdriver (for caps)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Plumber's grease (silicone-based)
- Clean rags or towels
- Bucket or bowl
- Flashlight (optional)
- Penetrating oil (for stubborn screws, optional)

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing work, it's crucial to shut off the main water supply to your house or, if available, the specific shut-off valves for the bathtub. Turn on the faucet to drain any residual water from the pipes. Place a rag over the drain to prevent small parts from falling in.

### Step 2: Identify Faucet Type and Access Screws

Bathtub faucets come in various types (compression, cartridge, ball, disc). Most have decorative caps covering the handle screws. Use a utility knife or a small flathead screwdriver to carefully pry off these caps. Once exposed, use the appropriate screwdriver to loosen and remove the handle screws.

### Step 3: Remove Handles and Trim

After removing the screws, carefully pull off the faucet handles. If they are stubborn, a gentle wiggle might help. Next, remove any decorative escutcheon plates or trim that cover the faucet body. This usually involves unscrewing them or prying them off, revealing the internal components.

### Step 4: Extract the Cartridge or Stem Assembly

Depending on your faucet type, you'll either find a cartridge, a stem assembly with washers, or a ball valve. For cartridge faucets, remove the retaining nut or clip, then pull out the old cartridge with pliers. For compression faucets, unscrew the packing nut and then the stem assembly. Carefully note the orientation of parts as you remove them.

### Step 5: Replace Worn Components

Inspect the removed components for wear. For compression faucets, replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem and the O-rings or packing washer. For cartridge faucets, replace the entire cartridge with a new one that matches your faucet model. Apply a thin coat of plumber's grease to new O-rings or the cartridge before installation.

### Step 6: Reassemble the Faucet

Carefully reverse the disassembly process. Insert the new cartridge or stem assembly, tighten the retaining nut or clip, and replace any packing nuts. Reattach the trim plate and handles, ensuring screws are snug but not overtightened. Make sure the handles are aligned correctly.

### Step 7: Restore Water Supply and Test

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks. Turn on the faucet to test its operation and check for drips. If the leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider replacing the entire faucet if the body is corroded or damaged beyond simple repair.


## Tips & Tricks

- Take photos during disassembly to help remember the order of parts when reassembling.
- Bring the old cartridge, washer, or O-rings to the hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacements.
- Clean any mineral deposits or grime from the faucet body parts while disassembled to improve performance.
- Don't overtighten screws or nuts, as this can strip threads or crack plastic components.
- If the leak persists after replacing parts, the faucet body itself might be cracked or corroded, suggesting a full faucet replacement might be necessary. For a complete guide, consider reading [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: Step-by-Step Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always ensure the water supply is completely shut off before starting work to prevent flooding.
- ⚠️ Be careful when prying off caps or handles to avoid scratching the faucet finish.
- ⚠️ Do not force stubborn components; use penetrating oil if screws are stuck, or consult a professional.
- ⚠️ Hot water pipes can be extremely hot; allow them to cool if you've recently used the faucet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, you typically need to turn off the water, disassemble the faucet handles and trim, identify the worn-out component (usually a washer, O-ring, or cartridge), replace it, and then reassemble the faucet. This process usually resolves most common leaks.

**What causes a bathtub faucet to leak?**

The most common causes for a leaky bathtub faucet are worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge inside the faucet body. Over time, these components can harden, crack, or lose their seal, allowing water to drip or flow when it shouldn't.

**Can I fix bathtub faucet leaks myself?**

Yes, most bathtub faucet leaks are simple DIY fixes that homeowners can tackle with basic tools. Identifying the type of faucet and getting the correct replacement parts are the most critical steps to a successful repair. Follow a reliable guide to ensure you complete the repair safely and effectively.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, pliers, and a utility knife. You'll also need the specific replacement parts like washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge that match your faucet model. Plumber's grease and clean rags are also helpful.

**How much does it cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

The cost to fix a leaky bathtub faucet yourself is usually minimal, often just the price of replacement washers, O-rings, or a cartridge, which can range from a few dollars to $30-$50. If you hire a professional plumber, the cost will include labor, typically ranging from $100 to $300 or more depending on the complexity.

**When should I call a professional to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You should call a professional if you're unable to identify the source of the leak, if components are severely corroded and stuck, or if you're uncomfortable performing the repair yourself. Also, if the leak persists after multiple attempts or if you suspect a more serious plumbing issue, a plumber can diagnose and fix the problem safely.
