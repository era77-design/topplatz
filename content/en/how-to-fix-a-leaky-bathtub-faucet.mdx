A leaky bathtub faucet is often caused by worn-out internal components like washers or O-rings, leading to annoying drips and wasted water. Fixing it is a common DIY task that typically involves turning off the water, disassembling the faucet, replacing the faulty parts, and reassembling. This guide will walk you through the process.

## What You Need

- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Adjustable wrench or basin wrench
- Plumber's tape (Teflon tape)
- Replacement washers, O-rings, or cartridge (specific to your faucet type)
- Utility knife or wire brush (for cleaning)
- Rags or towels
- Penetrating oil (optional, for seized screws)
- Small bucket or container

## Steps

### Step 1: Turn Off the Water Supply

Before starting any plumbing repair, locate and turn off the main water supply to your house or, ideally, the shut-off valve for the bathtub specifically. This prevents flooding and ensures safety during the repair process. Turn on the faucet briefly to drain any remaining water from the pipes, relieving any residual pressure.

### Step 2: Access the Faucet Handle and Trim

Carefully remove any decorative caps or screws on the faucet handle. Use a Phillips or flathead screwdriver as needed. Once the screw is exposed, loosen it and pull the handle straight off. You may need to gently pry off escutcheon plates or trim rings to expose the faucet body and stem, which are often held in place by additional screws.

### Step 3: Remove the Faucet Stem or Cartridge

With the handle removed, you'll see the faucet stem or cartridge. Depending on your faucet type (compression, cartridge, ball, or ceramic disc), this might be held by a packing nut or a retaining clip. Use an adjustable wrench to loosen and remove the packing nut, or carefully remove the clip holding the cartridge in place. Gently pull out the stem or cartridge assembly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Place a rag over the drain to prevent small parts from falling into it during disassembly.

### Step 4: Inspect and Replace Worn Components

Examine the removed stem or cartridge for worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or springs. These are the most common culprits for leaks. Carefully remove the old, deteriorated parts. If your faucet uses a cartridge, you might need to replace the entire unit. Clean any mineral deposits or debris from the faucet body using a utility knife or wire brush before installing new parts. Ensure the new washers/O-rings match the old ones in size and shape.

### Step 5: Reassemble the Faucet

Install the new washers, O-rings, or cartridge into the faucet body. Apply plumber's tape clockwise to the threads of the packing nut if applicable, ensuring a tight seal. Carefully reinsert the stem or cartridge and tighten the packing nut or secure the retaining clip. Reattach the handle, decorative caps, and any trim pieces, making sure everything is snug but not overtightened.

### Step 6: Restore Water Supply and Test for Leaks

Slowly turn the water supply back on, checking for any immediate leaks around the faucet. Turn the faucet on and off several times, testing both hot and cold water, to ensure the new components are functioning correctly and that the leak has stopped. If a leak persists, you may need to re-examine the components or consider replacing the entire faucet if the body is damaged or parts are unavailable. For guidance on replacement, refer to [How to Change a Bathtub Faucet: A DIY Guide](/en/how-to-change-a-bathtub-faucet).


## Tips & Tricks

- Before disassembling, take photos of each step to aid in reassembly, especially for complex faucet designs.
- Take the old washers, O-rings, or cartridge to a hardware store to ensure you purchase the correct replacement parts.
- Avoid overtightening screws and nuts, as this can strip threads or crack components, leading to new leaks or damage.
- Clean any mineral buildup or corrosion from all parts as you work to ensure a better seal and smoother operation of the faucet.

## Warnings

- ⚠️ Always turn off the water supply before attempting any plumbing repairs to prevent flooding and ensure safety.
- ⚠️ Be careful when handling hot water pipes; residual hot water can cause burns even after the supply is off.
- ⚠️ Do not force stubborn parts, as this can cause damage to the faucet or plumbing. Use penetrating oil if screws or nuts are seized.
- ⚠️ Ensure all small parts are kept in a safe place, like a small container, to avoid losing them down the drain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

To fix a leaky bathtub faucet, first turn off the water supply. Then, disassemble the faucet handle to access the internal components, typically the stem or cartridge. Inspect and replace any worn-out washers, O-rings, or the entire cartridge, then reassemble the faucet and test for leaks.

**How to fix bathtub faucet?**

Fixing a bathtub faucet generally involves identifying the source of the problem, which is often a drip from the spout or a leak around the handle. The process usually requires shutting off the water, disassembling the faucet to replace internal seals or a cartridge, and then putting it back together.

**Why is my bathtub faucet leaking?**

Bathtub faucets typically leak due to worn-out rubber washers, deteriorated O-rings, or a faulty cartridge that can no longer create a tight seal. Corrosion on the valve seat or loose connections can also contribute to a persistent drip.

**What tools do I need to fix a leaky bathtub faucet?**

You'll generally need a screwdriver (Phillips and flathead), an adjustable wrench, plumber's tape, and the appropriate replacement parts (washers, O-rings, or a new cartridge) specific to your faucet model. Rags and a utility knife for cleaning are also helpful.

**How do I know what type of replacement parts to buy for my faucet?**

The best way to ensure you get the correct replacement parts is to remove the old washer, O-ring, or cartridge and take it to a hardware store for an exact match. Knowing your faucet's brand and model number can also help you find the right components.

**Can I fix a leaky faucet without turning off the main water supply?**

No, it is crucial to turn off the water supply before attempting any faucet repair. Failing to do so can result in significant water damage and make the repair impossible due to continuous water flow and pressure.

**When should I replace the entire bathtub faucet instead of repairing it?**

Consider replacing the entire faucet if the faucet body is cracked, parts are obsolete or unavailable, or if repeated repairs fail to stop the leak. Sometimes, an older, corroded faucet may be beyond simple repair, making replacement a more cost-effective long-term solution.
